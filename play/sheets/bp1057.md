# BP1057

Researchers conducted a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of the novel antihypertensive agent, Hypotensol, in adults aged 40–65 with stage 1 hypertension. Eighty participants were randomly assigned to receive either 10 mg of Hypotensol daily or a matching placebo for 12 weeks. Blood pressure (BP) was measured at baseline and at week 12 using a validated automated cuff. The study also recorded adverse events and serum creatinine levels. The investigators reported that Hypotensol significantly lowered systolic BP (SBP) and diastolic BP (DBP) compared to placebo, with a mean SBP reduction of 12 mm Hg in the treatment group versus 3 mm Hg in the placebo group. Adverse events were mild and comparable between groups. The authors concluded that Hypotensol is a safe and effective option for managing mild hypertension.

## bp1057_q2 Which of the following best describes a potential limitation of the study’s design?
Correct: B
While the study was randomized, double‑blind, and placebo‑controlled, the relatively small sample size (n = 80) reduces the ability to detect infrequent adverse events.

## bp1057_q3 If 40 participants received Hypotensol, what is the total reduction in systolic BP across all treated participants?
Correct: A
Mean reduction per participant is 12 mm Hg. 12 mm Hg × 40 participants = 480 mm Hg total reduction.

## bp1057_q4 Which of the following statements is a common misinterpretation of the study’s results?
Correct: A
The mean reduction of 12 mm Hg applies to the sample studied; it cannot be generalized to all patients with different baseline values.

## bp1057_q5 Which pharmacologic concept best explains the mechanism of action of Hypotensol?
Correct: C
Hypotensol is described as a novel antihypertensive that reduces BP by inhibiting calcium influx in vascular smooth muscle, leading to vasodilation.
