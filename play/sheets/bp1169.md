# BP1169

Researchers conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive agent, Hypertensol, in adults with stage 1 hypertension. A total of 200 participants were enrolled and randomly assigned to either Hypertensol (n = 100) or placebo (n = 100). Baseline systolic blood pressure (SBP) was measured after a 5‑minute seated rest. After 4 weeks of daily dosing, SBP was reassessed. The investigators reported the following mean SBP values and standard deviations (SD): Hypertensol group: baseline 138 mmHg (SD 10), 4‑week follow‑up 120 mmHg (SD 9); Placebo group: baseline 139 mmHg (SD 11), 4‑week follow‑up 136 mmHg (SD 10). The study concluded that Hypertensol significantly lowered SBP compared to placebo, with a mean reduction of 18 mmHg versus 3 mmHg. The authors suggested that the drug’s mechanism involves selective blockade of the angiotensin II type 1 receptor.

## 1 Based on the table, what is the mean reduction in systolic blood pressure for the Hypertensol group after 4 weeks?
Correct: B
The mean SBP decreased from 138 mmHg to 120 mmHg, a reduction of 18 mmHg. This matches option B.

## 2 Which of the following best describes the experimental design used in this study?
Correct: C
Participants were randomly assigned to Hypertensol or placebo, which is the hallmark of a randomized controlled trial.

## 3 What is the percent reduction in systolic blood pressure for the Hypertensol group?
Correct: A
Percent reduction = (138 – 120) / 138 × 100 ≈ 12.9%. Option A is the closest.

## 4 Which statement is a potential trap in interpreting the study’s results?
Correct: A
The placebo group’s baseline SBP (139 mmHg) is slightly higher than the Hypertensol group (138 mmHg). This small difference could bias the apparent treatment effect if not properly adjusted.
