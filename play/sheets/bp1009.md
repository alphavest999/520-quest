# BP1009

Researchers studied the impact of caffeine on reaction time in a controlled laboratory setting. Thirty healthy adults (15 male, 15 female) were randomly assigned to one of three groups: placebo (0 mg), moderate caffeine (200 mg), or high caffeine (400 mg). Each participant completed a computerized simple reaction time task consisting of 100 trials, and their average reaction time (RT) was recorded. Heart rate was also measured before and after the task to assess physiological arousal. The study aimed to determine whether caffeine dosage correlates with faster RT and whether increased arousal mediates this effect. Results indicated a dose-dependent improvement: the high caffeine group had the lowest mean RT (215 ms), followed by moderate caffeine (240 ms), and placebo (260 ms). Heart rate increased significantly in both caffeine groups compared to placebo, suggesting a physiological link. The authors concluded that caffeine enhances cognitive performance at moderate doses, with potential applications for occupations requiring rapid responses.
