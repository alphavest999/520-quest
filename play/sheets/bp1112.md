Researchers at the University of Greenfield investigated the effects of a novel dietary supplement, NutriBoost, on resting metabolic rate (RMR) in adults with a BMI between 25 and 30. A randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial enrolled 120 participants who were assigned to either the NutriBoost group (n = 60) or the placebo group (n = 60). After 12 weeks of daily supplementation, RMR was measured using indirect calorimetry. The researchers also recorded changes in body composition, serum lipid profiles, and self‑reported energy intake. Participants were instructed to maintain their usual diet and physical activity levels throughout the study. The primary outcome was the mean change in RMR from baseline to week 12. Secondary outcomes included changes in fat mass, lean mass, LDL‑cholesterol, and total energy intake. The study aimed to determine whether NutriBoost could safely enhance metabolic rate and support weight management in overweight adults.

Group | N | Mean RMR change (kcal/day) | p-value
--- | --- | --- | ---
NutriBoost | 60 | +12.5 ± 4.3 | 0.001
Placebo | 60 | +3.2 ± 3.8 | 0.12

## 2 Which design feature best ensures that the observed difference in RMR is attributable to NutriBoost?
Correct: B
Double‑blind design prevents bias from participants and investigators, making it more likely that differences are due to the supplement itself.

## 4 Which statement is a potential limitation of the study?
Correct: C
Requiring participants to maintain their usual diet may limit generalizability and does not control for dietary changes that could influence RMR.

Correct: B
The significant increase in RMR with NutriBoost indicates potential as an adjunct for weight management, warranting further studies.
