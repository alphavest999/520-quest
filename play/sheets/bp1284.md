# BP1284

Researchers studied the effect of a new antihypertensive drug on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in 300 adults with stage‑1 hypertension. Participants were enrolled from three community clinics and followed for 12 weeks. Baseline SBP was measured after a 5‑minute seated rest, and the drug was administered once daily at 8:00 a.m. Blood pressure readings were taken at baseline and at 12 weeks by the same trained technician using a calibrated oscillometric cuff. The study was designed as a single‑group, pre‑post intervention without a control arm. Demographic data were collected, and participants were stratified into three age groups: 40–50, 51–60, and 61–70 years. The investigators reported mean SBP reductions for each group and performed paired t‑tests to assess statistical significance. No serious adverse events were reported, and the drug was well tolerated. The authors concluded that the medication effectively lowered SBP across all age strata, with the greatest absolute reduction observed in the oldest cohort.

Age Group | N | Mean SBP Pre (mmHg) | Mean SBP Post (mmHg)
40‑50 | 120 | 140 | 135
51‑60 | 130 | 145 | 133
61‑70 | 140 | 150 | 135

## bp1284-1 Which age group showed the greatest reduction in mean systolic blood pressure?
Correct: C
The 61–70 age group had a mean SBP reduction from 150 to 135 mmHg, a 15‑mmHg drop, which is larger than the 5‑mmHg drop in the 40–50 group and the 12‑mmHg drop in the 51–60 group.

## bp1284-2 Which of the following best describes the study design used by the researchers?
Correct: B
The study followed the same participants over time, measuring SBP before and after the intervention; this is a prospective cohort (pre‑post) design. It was not randomized, controlled, or cross‑sectional.

## bp1284-3 What is the percent reduction in mean SBP for the 61‑70 age group?
Correct: B
Percent reduction = (150 – 135)/150 × 100 = 15/150 × 100 = 10%. The closest option is 10%.

## bp1284-4 Which of the following is a potential source of bias in this study?
Correct: A
Participants were recruited from clinics and may not represent the general hypertensive population, creating selection bias. The study was not randomized or blinded, so observer bias is unlikely. Confounding by age is a risk factor but not a bias in the traditional sense, and publication bias refers to selective reporting of results.

## bp1284-5 Which statement best links the study findings to clinical practice?
Correct: C
The data show the greatest absolute reduction in the 61‑70 age group, suggesting particular benefit for older adults. The study does not support replacing all antihypertensives or contraindicating use in younger patients.
