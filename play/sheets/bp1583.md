# BP1583

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel cognitive training program on reaction time among healthy adults. A randomized controlled trial enrolled 120 participants aged 18–35, who were randomly assigned to either the training group (n = 60) or a wait‑list control group (n = 60). Over 8 weeks, the training group completed daily 30‑minute sessions of computer‑based tasks designed to improve working memory and attentional control. The control group received no intervention during this period. At baseline and after 8 weeks, all participants performed a standardized reaction‑time task (simple visual stimulus, button press). The researchers recorded mean reaction time (ms) and standard deviation for each group at both time points. The data are summarized in Table 1 below.

## 1 Based on Table 1, which statement accurately reflects the data presented for the training group?
Correct: B
The training group’s mean reaction time dropped from 350 ms to 310 ms, a decrease of 40 ms, indicating improved performance.

## 2 Which experimental design element best ensures that the observed improvement in reaction time is attributable to the training program?
Correct: B
Random assignment balances known and unknown confounders across groups, allowing causal inference that changes are due to the intervention.

## 3 Calculate the absolute change in mean reaction time for the control group from baseline to 8 weeks.
Correct: B
Control group mean increased from 345 ms to 340 ms, a change of 5 ms. However, because reaction time is faster when lower, the absolute change is 5 ms. Option B mistakenly states 10 ms; the correct answer is 5 ms, but since 5 ms is not listed, the intended correct answer is 5 ms (Option A).

## 4 Which of the following is a potential confounding variable that could bias the observed improvement in the training group?
Correct: B
Regular exercise can independently improve reaction time; if more participants in the training group exercised, the improvement could be due to exercise rather than the training program.

## 5 Which statistical concept best explains why the researchers used a randomized controlled design rather than a simple before‑after comparison?
Correct: C
Randomization ensures that potential confounders are evenly distributed between groups, thereby reducing selection bias and allowing causal inference.
