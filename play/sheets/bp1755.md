# BP1755

Researchers investigated the impact of sleep deprivation on cognitive performance among college students. One hundred twenty participants were recruited and randomly assigned to three groups: 0‑hour sleep, 4‑hour sleep, and 8‑hour sleep. After a 2‑hour standardized cognitive test, each participant’s reaction time (in milliseconds), working‑memory accuracy (percentage of correct responses), and mood rating (on a 1–10 scale) were recorded. The results are summarized in Table 1. The study aimed to quantify how reduced sleep duration affects both speed and accuracy of cognitive tasks, providing insight into the neurocognitive costs of sleep loss in young adults.

## bp1755_q2 Which experimental design best controls for confounding variables in this study?
Correct: A
Random assignment to sleep conditions minimizes differences between groups, providing the strongest control over confounders.
