# BP1096

Researchers investigated the impact of acute sleep deprivation on cognitive performance in a sample of 120 healthy adults aged 20–35. Participants were randomly assigned to one of four conditions: (1) normal sleep (8 h), (2) 6 h sleep, (3) 4 h sleep, or (4) no sleep (0 h). After the assigned sleep period, all participants completed a computerized reaction time (RT) task measuring the time to press a button in response to a visual stimulus. The mean RTs (±SD) were recorded for each group. The study aimed to quantify how reduced sleep duration affects processing speed and to assess whether a dose–response relationship exists. Findings suggested a monotonic increase in RT with decreasing sleep, implying that even moderate sleep loss can impair rapid decision making. The authors discussed implications for occupations requiring quick reflexes, such as driving and shift work, and recommended interventions to mitigate sleep loss in high‑risk environments.

## bp1096-3 Calculate the percent increase in mean reaction time from the normal‑sleep condition to the 4‑hour sleep condition.
Correct: C
Percent increase = [(320 – 250)/250] × 100 = 70/250 × 100 = 28 %.

Correct: B
