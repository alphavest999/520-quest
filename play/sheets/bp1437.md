# BP1437

Researchers studied the effect of a brief mindfulness exercise on cognitive performance in 120 adults. Participants were randomly assigned to one of four groups: (1) a 5‑minute guided meditation, (2) a 5‑minute breathing exercise, (3) a 5‑minute reading task, and (4) a no‑intervention control. After the intervention, all participants completed a computerized reaction‑time task measuring response latency to visual stimuli. The study aimed to determine whether brief mindfulness practices could improve attentional focus and reduce reaction times compared to control and other active conditions. The sample was balanced for age (mean 35.2 ± 9.8 years) and gender (58% female). The researchers hypothesized that the meditation group would show the greatest reduction in mean reaction time, followed by breathing, reading, and control. Data were analyzed using ANOVA and post‑hoc Tukey tests. The findings suggested a statistically significant difference between groups, with the meditation group exhibiting the fastest responses. These results support the notion that even short mindfulness interventions can enhance cognitive efficiency in everyday settings.

## bp1437-1 Based on the table, which group exhibited the fastest mean reaction time?
Correct: A
The table shows the lowest mean response time (0.412 s) for Group 1, the guided meditation group, indicating the fastest reactions.

## bp1437-2 Which experimental design best isolates the effect of the mindfulness intervention?
Correct: A
A randomized controlled trial with parallel groups ensures each participant experiences only one intervention, controlling for confounding variables and allowing causal inference.

## bp1437-3 Calculate the mean difference in reaction time between the meditation group and the control group.
Correct: A
Mean difference = 0.498 s (control) – 0.412 s (meditation) = 0.086 s.

## bp1437-4 Which of the following statements is incorrect regarding the study’s findings?
Correct: B
The study reported significant differences between groups, but not all pairwise comparisons were significant; only the meditation group differed from the others.

## bp1437-5 Which broader psychological principle is illustrated by the study’s finding that brief mindfulness improves reaction time?
Correct: A
Mindfulness likely reduces over‑arousal and enhances attentional focus, aligning with the Yerkes–Dodson law, which posits an inverted‑U relationship between arousal and performance.
