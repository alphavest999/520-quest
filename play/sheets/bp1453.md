# BP1453

Researchers studied the effect of a novel antihypertensive drug on adults with stage 1 hypertension. In a double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial, 100 participants were randomly assigned to receive either the drug (n = 50) or placebo (n = 50). Baseline measurements of systolic blood pressure (SBP), diastolic blood pressure (DBP), and heart rate (HR) were recorded, followed by a 12‑week treatment period. Table 1 summarizes the mean values (± SD) for each variable before and after treatment. The drug group showed a mean SBP reduction of 10 mmHg, a DBP drop of 5 mmHg, and a slight HR decrease of 1 bpm. The placebo group had negligible changes. The study concluded that the drug effectively lowered SBP and DBP with minimal impact on HR.

## bp1453_q1 Which variable showed the largest absolute decrease from baseline to post‑treatment?
Correct: A
The absolute decrease for SBP is 10 mmHg, for DBP 5 mmHg, and for HR 1 bpm. Thus SBP had the greatest change.

## bp1453_q2 What type of study design was used in this research?
Correct: A
The study randomly assigned participants to drug or placebo and was double‑blind, characteristic of a randomized controlled trial.

Correct: C

## bp1453_q5 Which of the following concepts is most closely related to the study’s primary outcome?
Correct: A
The primary outcome is the reduction in blood pressure, a key component of hypertension management.
