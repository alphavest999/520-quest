# BP1507

Researchers studied the effects of a novel anti-inflammatory drug on joint pain in patients with rheumatoid arthritis. The study was a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial conducted over 12 weeks. A total of 200 participants were enrolled and assigned to one of four groups: placebo, low dose (25 mg), medium dose (50 mg), or high dose (100 mg). Pain was assessed weekly using a standardized visual analog scale (VAS) ranging from 0 (no pain) to 100 (worst pain). At the end of the trial, mean VAS scores and standard deviations were recorded for each group. The data are summarized in Table 1 below. The investigators hypothesized that higher doses would produce greater reductions in pain scores. They also planned to analyze the safety profile by recording adverse events. The results showed a clear dose-response trend, with the high-dose group reporting the greatest average pain reduction. However, the incidence of mild gastrointestinal upset increased with dose. These findings suggest that while the drug is effective, dosing must balance efficacy with tolerability.
