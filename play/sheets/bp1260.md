Researchers studied the effect of varying doses of a novel analgesic on pain‑relief response times in healthy volunteers. Forty‑two participants were randomly assigned to one of four groups: a control group receiving placebo, and three treatment groups receiving low (10 mg), medium (20 mg), and high (40 mg) doses of the drug. Each participant completed a standardized pain‑stimulus task while their response times were recorded in milliseconds. The study aimed to determine whether increasing the drug dose would proportionally reduce response times, thereby indicating enhanced analgesic efficacy. Data were collected over a single session, and participants were blinded to their group assignment. The results suggested a dose‑dependent improvement, with the highest dose group exhibiting the fastest mean response time. ## bp1260-01 Which of the following best describes the trend in mean response time across conditions?
Correct: A
The mean response time declines from 350 ms in the control group to 250 ms in the high‑dose group, indicating a dose‑dependent reduction.

## bp1260-02 Which of the following best describes the experimental design used in this study?
Correct: A
Participants were randomly assigned to placebo or drug doses, and both participants and investigators were blinded, which characterizes a randomized controlled trial.
