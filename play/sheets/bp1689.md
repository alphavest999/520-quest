# BP1689

Researchers studied the pharmacokinetics of two novel antibiotics, Drug A and Drug B, in a controlled laboratory setting. A cohort of 20 healthy volunteers received a single oral dose of each drug on separate days. Blood samples were collected at 1, 2, 3, and 4 days post‑dose. The concentrations (in mg/L) were measured using high‑performance liquid chromatography. The data revealed a rapid decline in both drugs, with Drug A consistently maintaining higher plasma levels than Drug B. The study aimed to compare the elimination rates and assess whether Drug A could provide a longer therapeutic window. The researchers noted that the half‑life of Drug A appeared to be approximately 2.5 days, whereas Drug B’s half‑life was closer to 1.5 days. These findings suggest potential clinical advantages for Drug A in treating infections that require sustained drug exposure. The investigators plan to extend the study to include patients with renal impairment to evaluate dose adjustments.

Time (days) | Drug A (mg/L) | Drug B (mg/L)
1 | 12 | 8
2 | 9 | 6
3 | 6 | 4
4 | 3 | 2

## 1 Which day had the highest combined concentration of Drug A and Drug B?
Correct: A
Adding the concentrations for each day: Day 1 = 12 + 8 = 20 mg/L, Day 2 = 9 + 6 = 15 mg/L, Day 3 = 6 + 4 = 10 mg/L, Day 4 = 3 + 2 = 5 mg/L. The highest total is on Day 1.

## 2 Which experimental design would best test whether Drug A reduces bacterial load faster than Drug B?
Correct: A
A randomized controlled trial assigns participants to receive either Drug A or Drug B under controlled conditions, allowing direct comparison of their effects on bacterial load while minimizing bias.

## 3 What is the average concentration of Drug A over the 4‑day period?
Correct: A
Sum of Drug A concentrations = 12 + 9 + 6 + 3 = 30 mg/L. Divide by 4 days = 30 / 4 = 7.5 mg/L.

## 4 Which statement is NOT supported by the data presented?
Correct: D
The table shows Drug A concentrations (12, 9, 6, 3) are consistently higher than Drug B concentrations (8, 6, 4, 2). Thus, statement D is false.
