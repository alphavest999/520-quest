# BP789

Researchers at the University of BioChem conducted a titration experiment to characterize the ionization behavior of glycine, the simplest amino acid, across a wide pH range. Using a calibrated pH meter, they added incremental amounts of 0.1 M HCl to a 1.0 mM glycine solution and recorded the resulting pH after each addition. Simultaneously, they monitored the degree of protonation of the α‑amino group and the deprotonation of the α‑carboxyl group by measuring the absorbance at 280 nm, which correlates with the protonated state of the amino group. The data were plotted as a function of pH, yielding a characteristic sigmoidal curve with two inflection points corresponding to the pKa values of the carboxyl (2.34) and amino (9.60) functional groups. The table below summarizes the fraction of each group that is protonated or deprotonated at selected pH values and the resulting net charge on the glycine molecule. The experiment demonstrates how the net charge of an amino acid changes with pH, a concept that is essential for understanding protein structure and function.

| pH | % α‑amino protonated | % carboxyl deprotonated | Net charge |
|----|-----------------------|-------------------------|------------|
| 1  | 100% | 0% | +1 |
| 3  | 100% | 0% | +1 |
| 5  | 100% | 0% | +1 |
| 7  | 100% | 100% | 0 |
| 9  | 80%  | 100% | -1 |
| 11 | 20%  | 100% | -1 |

## bp789-1 Which variable in this experiment is the independent variable?
Correct: A
Analogy (10‑yr‑old): The independent variable is like the knob you turn on a stove; you decide what to change. Official: The independent variable is the one you manipulate (pH). Wrong: The dependent variable is the one that changes in response.

## bp789-2 If the researchers increased the ionic strength of the solution while keeping the pH constant, what would most likely occur?
Correct: A
Analogy (10‑yr‑old): Adding salt is like adding more people to a crowded room; the room’s temperature (pH) stays the same, but the way people interact (activity coefficients) changes. Official: Ionic strength alters activity coefficients, shifting pKa values slightly; the net charge at a fixed pH stays the same. Wrong: Thinking the net charge changes with ionic strength.

## bp789-3 Using the pKa values of 2.34 (COOH) and 9.60 (NH3+), what is the isoelectric point (pI) of glycine?
Correct: B
Analogy (10‑yr‑old): To find the middle point, you take the average of the two numbers. Official: pI = (pKa1 + pKa2)/2 = (2.34 + 9.60)/2 = 6.97. Wrong: Mis‑adding or mis‑averaging the numbers.
