# BP1103

Researchers at the University of Greenfield investigated the effect of light intensity on the photosynthetic rate of the aquatic plant *Elodea densa*. The study employed a controlled laboratory setup where plants were exposed to four different light intensities: 50, 150, 300, and 600 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹. For each intensity, the oxygen evolution rate was measured over a 10‑minute period, and the average rate was recorded. The researchers also measured the chlorophyll a concentration in each plant to assess whether changes in photosynthetic output were correlated with pigment levels. Results indicated a nonlinear relationship: rates increased sharply up to 300 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹ and then plateaued. Chlorophyll a concentrations rose modestly with light intensity but did not fully explain the plateau. The authors concluded that *E. densa* reaches a saturation point where additional light does not enhance photosynthesis, suggesting a limit imposed by the photosynthetic machinery rather than pigment availability.

Correct: B
The data show a rapid increase in oxygen evolution from 50 to 300 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹, after which the rate levels off (3.5 to 3.6 µmol O₂ min⁻¹), indicating a saturation point.

## bp1103-02 If you were to design a follow‑up experiment to test whether the plateau in photosynthetic rate is due to a limitation in the electron transport chain, which of the following experimental modifications would be most appropriate?
Correct: C
A proton‑gradient uncoupler would directly test the capacity of the electron transport chain; if the plateau is due to this limitation, uncoupling should alter the rate at high light intensities.
