# BP1211

Researchers studied the effect of a novel dietary supplement on exercise performance in healthy adults. Twenty-four participants were randomly assigned to either the supplement group (n=12) or a placebo group (n=12). Each participant completed a 5-kilometer time trial before and after a 4-week intervention. The mean time (minutes) and standard deviation (SD) for each group are shown in Table 1. The supplement group demonstrated a mean improvement of 1.8 minutes (SD 0.6) while the placebo group improved by 0.5 minutes (SD 0.4). The researchers concluded that the supplement significantly enhanced endurance performance. The study design, statistical analysis, and clinical relevance of these findings are discussed in the article.

Table 1. Mean 5‑km Time Trial Performance

| Group        | Pre‑Intervention (min) | Post‑Intervention (min) | Mean Change (min) | SD |
|--------------|------------------------|------------------------|-------------------|----|
| Supplement   | 20.5                   | 18.7                   | 1.8               | 0.6 |
| Placebo      | 20.4                   | 19.9                   | 0.5               | 0.4 |
| Total        | 20.45                  | 19.35                  | 1.1               | 0.5 |


## bp1211_q1 Based on Table 1, what is the average improvement in time trial performance for participants in the supplement group?
Correct: A
The mean change for the supplement group is 1.8 minutes, as shown directly in the table.

## bp1211_q2 Which study design best supports the authors’ conclusion that the supplement improves endurance?
Correct: B
The participants were randomly assigned to supplement or placebo groups, a hallmark of a randomized controlled trial, which is the gold standard for establishing causality.

## bp1211_q3 Assuming a two‑sample t‑test, what is the approximate t‑statistic for the difference in mean change between groups?
Correct: B
Using pooled SD ≈0.55, difference = 1.3 minutes, SE = 0.55*sqrt(1/12+1/12)=0.42, t = 1.3/0.42 ≈3.1.

## bp1211_q4 Which of the following is a common trap when interpreting the study’s results?
Correct: A
The reported mean improvement may appear large, but without context on minimal clinically important difference, it could be statistically significant yet not clinically relevant.

## bp1211_q5 How does this study illustrate the broader principle of the placebo effect in clinical research?
Correct: A
Even the placebo group improved by 0.5 minutes, indicating that participants’ expectations and the act of taking a pill can influence performance outcomes.
