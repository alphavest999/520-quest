# BP1066

Researchers investigated the acute effects of caffeine on cognitive performance in healthy adults. A randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled crossover trial was conducted with 60 participants (30 male, 30 female). Each subject received four interventions in random order: placebo (0 mg), low dose (50 mg), moderate dose (100 mg), and high dose (200 mg) of caffeine. After a 30‑minute absorption period, participants completed a computerized psychomotor vigilance task (PVT). The primary outcome was mean reaction time (RT) in milliseconds (ms). The study also recorded subjective alertness scores on a 10‑point visual analog scale. The authors reported that caffeine reduced mean RT compared to placebo, with the greatest improvement at 200 mg. They concluded that caffeine may enhance short‑term alertness and reaction speed in healthy adults.
