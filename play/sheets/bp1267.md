# BP1267

Researchers conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the effect of a high‑protein diet on weight loss and metabolic health in overweight adults. One hundred and twenty participants were randomly assigned to either a high‑protein intervention group (n = 60) or a standard‑protein control group (n = 60). The intervention lasted 12 weeks, during which participants received individualized meal plans and weekly counseling. Baseline characteristics were similar across groups. At the end of the study, the intervention group lost a mean of 6.2 kg (SD = 1.8 kg) and exhibited a 12 % reduction in fasting insulin levels, whereas the control group lost 3.1 kg (SD = 2.0 kg) and had a 4 % reduction in fasting insulin. Blood pressure, lipid profiles, and body composition were also measured. The authors concluded that a high‑protein diet promotes greater weight loss and improves insulin sensitivity compared with a standard‑protein diet, suggesting potential clinical benefits for overweight adults.
