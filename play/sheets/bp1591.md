Researchers investigated how caffeine dosage influences cognitive performance in healthy adults. A double‑blind, placebo‑controlled study enrolled 90 participants who were randomly assigned to one of three groups: placebo (0 mg caffeine), low‑dose caffeine (50 mg), and high‑dose caffeine (200 mg). Each participant completed 30 trials of a visual‑reaction time task after ingestion of their assigned beverage. The researchers recorded mean reaction time (RT) in milliseconds, standard deviation (SD), and sample size (n) for each group, as shown in Table 1. Additionally, participants rated their subjective alertness on a 10‑point scale. The results indicated that the high‑dose group had the fastest mean RT but also the greatest variability, while the low‑dose group showed modest improvement over placebo without increased SD. The study aimed to map a dose‑response curve for caffeine’s acute effects on attention.
