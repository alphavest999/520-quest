# BP1011

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel antihypertensive drug on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults with stage 1 hypertension. Eighty patients were randomly assigned to receive either the drug (n = 40) or a placebo (n = 40) for four weeks. Baseline SBP was measured in all participants, and SBP was reassessed after the treatment period. The study found that patients receiving the drug experienced a mean reduction of 30 mmHg in SBP, whereas those receiving placebo had a mean reduction of only 3 mmHg. The researchers concluded that the drug produced a clinically meaningful reduction in SBP compared with placebo, suggesting potential benefit for hypertensive patients.
