# BP1307

Researchers conducted a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial to evaluate the antihypertensive efficacy of two novel agents, Drug A and Drug B, in adults with stage‑1 hypertension. One hundred and twenty participants were enrolled and randomly assigned to one of three groups: placebo (n=40), Drug A (n=40), or Drug B (n=40). Baseline systolic blood pressure (SBP) was measured after a 24‑hour ambulatory monitoring period. After a 4‑week treatment interval, SBP was reassessed. The investigators recorded the mean SBP and the mean change from baseline for each group. In addition, a two‑tailed t‑test was performed to compare the mean change between each drug group and placebo, yielding p‑values for statistical significance. The table below summarizes the key findings. These results suggest that both Drug A and Drug B significantly lower SBP compared with placebo, with Drug A producing the greatest mean reduction. The study also monitored adverse events, noting a mild headache in 5% of Drug A participants and no serious adverse events in any group.

Group,Baseline SBP (mmHg),Week 4 SBP (mmHg),Change (mmHg),p-value
Placebo,145,140,5,0.08
Drug A,146,130,16,0.001
Drug B,147,135,12,0.003

## bp1307-01 Which group experienced the greatest mean reduction in systolic blood pressure after 4 weeks?
Correct: B
The table shows Drug A had a mean reduction of 16 mmHg, Drug B 12 mmHg, and placebo 5 mmHg, so Drug A had the greatest reduction.

## bp1307-02 Which experimental design best controls for confounding variables in this study?
Correct: C
Randomization and blinding minimize confounding and bias, making this design the gold standard for comparing drug efficacy.

## bp1307-03 What is the percent reduction in SBP for Drug A?
Correct: C
Percent reduction = (16 mmHg ÷ 146 mmHg) × 100 ≈ 10.96%, rounded to 11%.

## bp1307-04 Which of the following statements is NOT supported by the data?
Correct: D
The data show no serious adverse events in any group; mild headache was reported only in 5% of Drug A participants, not placebo.
