# BP1201

Researchers studied the effect of a novel dietary supplement on resting metabolic rate (RMR) in adults aged 25‑45. In a double‑blind, randomized controlled trial, 120 participants were assigned to either the supplement group (n=60) or placebo group (n=60). Baseline RMR was measured using indirect calorimetry after an overnight fast. After 12 weeks of daily supplementation, RMR was reassessed. The supplement group exhibited a mean increase of 120 kcal/day (SD 35) relative to baseline, whereas the placebo group showed a mean increase of 45 kcal/day (SD 30). Secondary outcomes included changes in body composition and lipid profile. No adverse events were reported. The authors concluded that the supplement significantly enhances RMR compared to placebo, potentially aiding weight management strategies.

## bp1201_q1 Which group experienced the larger mean increase in resting metabolic rate after 12 weeks?
Correct: A
The supplement group’s mean ΔRMR was 120 kcal/day, whereas the placebo group’s mean ΔRMR was 45 kcal/day, making the supplement group’s increase larger.

## bp1201_q2 What type of study design was used in this research?
Correct: A
The passage explicitly states that participants were randomly assigned to supplement or placebo groups in a double‑blind fashion, which defines a randomized controlled trial.

## bp1201_q3 What is the percent increase in RMR for the supplement group relative to baseline?
Correct: B
Percent increase = (ΔRMR / Baseline) × 100 = (120 / 1500) × 100 = 8%.

## bp1201_q4 Which of the following statements is FALSE?
Correct: D
The data show an increase in RMR for both groups; there is no evidence of a decrease in the supplement group.

## bp1201_q5 Which clinical application is most directly supported by the study’s findings?
Correct: A
An increase in resting metabolic rate can contribute to greater energy expenditure, which is a key component of weight management strategies.
