## bp1221_q1 Which light intensity produced the highest rate of oxygen evolution?
Correct: D
The rate of O₂ evolution increases with light intensity until it reaches a plateau. The highest rate recorded was 43.7 µmol O₂ g⁻¹ leaf h⁻¹ at 800 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹, indicating saturation of the photosynthetic machinery.

## bp1221_q2 Which of the following controls would best account for potential confounding variables in this experiment?
Correct: A
A dark control controls for non-light-driven oxygen evolution and confirms that observed O₂ production is due to photosynthesis. Temperature and species changes introduce new variables, while a constant light intensity does not serve as a control for the variable being tested.

## bp1221_q3 What is the percent increase in O₂ evolution rate when light intensity increases from 200 to 400 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹?

Percent increase = [(42.1‑35.6)/35.6] × 100 = 18.5 % ≈ 17.7 % when rounded to one decimal place. The closest answer is A.

Correct: A
The data show a rapid increase up to 200 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹, but the relationship is not perfectly linear; the slope changes, indicating a nonlinear response. Statements B, C, and D are directly supported by the table.

## bp1221_q5 How does this light–response relationship illustrate the concept of photosynthetic regulation?
Correct: C
The plateau at high light intensities reflects the saturation of the photosynthetic machinery; beyond this point, further increases in light do not raise the rate, illustrating a key regulatory mechanism.
