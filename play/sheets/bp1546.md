# BP1546

Researchers studied the effect of a novel antioxidant compound on oxidative stress markers in cultured human fibroblasts. The experiment involved five treatment groups: a control group receiving vehicle only, and four groups receiving the antioxidant at concentrations of 0.1, 0.5, 1.0, and 5.0 µM. Each group consisted of 10 replicates. After 48 hours of exposure, intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels were measured using a fluorescent probe, and the expression of the antioxidant response gene Nrf2 was quantified by qPCR. Results indicated a dose-dependent decrease in ROS levels, with the 5.0 µM group showing a 60% reduction relative to control. Nrf2 expression increased by 2.5-fold at 1.0 µM and 4.8-fold at 5.0 µM. The study concluded that the antioxidant effectively mitigates oxidative stress in a concentration-dependent manner, suggesting therapeutic potential for age‑related cellular damage.
