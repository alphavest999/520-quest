Researchers studied the effect of caffeine on cognitive performance in a randomized controlled trial. 120 healthy adults aged 18‑35 were assigned to four groups: placebo, 50 mg caffeine, 100 mg caffeine, and 200 mg caffeine. The authors also collected subjective alertness ratings on a 10‑point Likert scale. Data analysis involved repeated‑measures ANOVA with post‑hoc Tukey tests. The researchers hypothesized that 100 mg caffeine would yield the fastest responses, while 200 mg might produce fatigue effects. Results indicated a significant main effect of dose (p < .01), with 100 mg outperforming placebo (p < .001) and 200 mg not significantly different from placebo. The authors concluded that moderate caffeine improves cognitive speed, but higher doses do not confer additional benefits and may impair performance.
