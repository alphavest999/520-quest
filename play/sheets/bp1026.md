# BP1026

Researchers studied the effect of a novel cognitive training program on working memory performance in adults aged 25-45. Participants were randomly assigned to one of four groups: (1) training with adaptive difficulty, (2) training with fixed difficulty, (3) active control using non-cognitive games, and (4) passive control with no intervention. Over an eight-week period, each participant completed a battery of working memory tasks at baseline, mid-intervention, and post-intervention. The primary outcome was the change in digit span scores. Table 1 summarizes the mean digit span improvements (± SD) for each group at post-intervention. The researchers hypothesized that the adaptive training group would show the greatest improvement, followed by the fixed training group, with the active and passive controls showing minimal change. Secondary analyses examined reaction times and error rates. The study also explored whether baseline working memory capacity moderated training effects. The authors concluded that adaptive training yields significant gains in working memory and recommend its implementation in workplace settings to enhance cognitive performance.
