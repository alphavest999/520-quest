# BP1263

Researchers studied the effects of three different cognitive training programs on working memory performance in adults aged 25–35. A total of 120 participants were randomly assigned to one of four groups: a control group (no training) and three experimental groups that received either a visuospatial training program (Group V), a verbal training program (Group W), or a combined visuospatial–verbal program (Group C). Each training program consisted of 30‑minute sessions, three times per week, over eight weeks. Working memory was assessed before and after the intervention using the Digit Span and Corsi Block‑Tapping tasks. Table 1 summarizes the mean post‑intervention scores and standard deviations for each group. The researchers hypothesized that the combined program would yield the greatest improvement, followed by the visuospatial and verbal programs, with the control group showing minimal change. The results indicated that Group C achieved the highest mean score, while Group V and Group W performed similarly, and the control group had the lowest scores.
