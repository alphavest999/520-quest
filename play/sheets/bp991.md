# BP991

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel dietary supplement on glucose tolerance in adult mice. Twenty-four male C57BL/6 mice were randomly assigned to one of four groups: (1) control diet, (2) control diet plus placebo, (3) high‑fat diet, and (4) high‑fat diet plus supplement. The study lasted 12 weeks, during which weekly blood glucose levels were measured following an intraperitoneal glucose tolerance test. At the end of the trial, the researchers recorded the area under the curve (AUC) for each mouse and performed a two‑way ANOVA to assess the interaction between diet type and supplement. The data suggested that the supplement reduced AUC values in the high‑fat diet group but had no significant effect in the control diet group. The authors concluded that the supplement may ameliorate diet‑induced glucose intolerance, though they noted the need for a larger sample size to confirm the interaction effect.

## bp991-1 Which of the following best describes the main finding of the study?
Correct: B
The data show a reduction in AUC only for the high‑fat diet group receiving the supplement, indicating a diet‑specific effect.

## bp991-2 If the researchers wanted to test whether the supplement alone (without a high‑fat diet) improves glucose tolerance, which experimental design adjustment would be most appropriate?
Correct: A
Adding a group that receives the supplement on a control diet isolates the supplement’s effect independent of diet type.

## bp991-3 Calculate the percent reduction in mean AUC for the high‑fat + supplement group relative to the high‑fat group.
Correct: B
Percent reduction = (1900 – 1550) / 1900 × 100 ≈ 18.4%; the closest option is 15.8%.

## bp991-4 Which of the following statements is a potential trap when interpreting the study’s results?
Correct: A
The data show the supplement only affected the high‑fat group; claiming independence from diet is misleading.

## bp991-5 Which of the following best links the study’s findings to potential clinical implications?
Correct: B
The data suggest a diet‑specific benefit; translating this to humans would focus on high‑fat dietary contexts.
