# BP1709

Researchers conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive agent, HypertensoX, in adults with stage 1 hypertension. Two hundred participants were enrolled and randomly assigned to receive either HypertensoX (n = 100) or a matching placebo (n = 100) for 12 weeks. Baseline systolic blood pressure (SBP) was measured using a calibrated sphygmomanometer and recorded in millimeters of mercury (mmHg). After the intervention period, SBP was reassessed. The study also collected demographic data to assess baseline comparability between groups. Table 1 summarizes the key findings. The HypertensoX group showed a mean SBP reduction of 30 mmHg, whereas the placebo group’s mean reduction was 13 mmHg. These results suggest that HypertensoX produces a clinically meaningful reduction in SBP compared with placebo. The investigators concluded that HypertensoX could be a valuable addition to current antihypertensive therapy, pending further long‑term safety studies.
