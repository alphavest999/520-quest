# BP1312

Researchers at the University of Midvale investigated how acute sleep deprivation affects cognitive performance in healthy adults. Twenty participants (10 males, 10 females, aged 20–35) were randomly assigned to either a sleep-deprived group (n=10) or a well-rested control group (n=10). The sleep-deprived group was restricted to 4 hours of sleep the night before testing, while the control group slept a normal 8 hours. On the following morning, all participants completed a battery of neuropsychological tests, including a simple reaction time task and a word list recall task. Reaction times were recorded in milliseconds, and memory scores were the number of words correctly recalled out of 20. The researchers hypothesized that sleep deprivation would lead to slower reaction times and poorer memory performance. The study’s design allowed for direct comparison between the two groups, controlling for potential confounding variables such as caffeine intake and baseline sleep patterns. The data collected provide insight into the acute effects of sleep loss on cognitive functions that are critical for daily activities and workplace safety.
