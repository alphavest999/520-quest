# BP1093

Researchers at the University of Greenfield investigated the effect of a new antihypertensive compound, Hypertensol, on systolic blood pressure in adults with mild hypertension. The study enrolled 120 participants who were randomly assigned to one of three groups: placebo (n = 40), low-dose Hypertensol (25 mg/day, n = 40), or high-dose Hypertensol (50 mg/day, n = 40). Blood pressure readings were taken at baseline and after 12 weeks of treatment. The investigators also recorded adverse events and compliance rates. The primary endpoint was the mean change in systolic blood pressure from baseline to week 12. Secondary endpoints included diastolic pressure change, incidence of side effects, and adherence to medication. The study was double‑blind and the data were analyzed using ANOVA with post‑hoc Tukey tests. The authors concluded that Hypertensol significantly lowered systolic blood pressure in a dose‑dependent manner, with the high‑dose group showing the greatest reduction and a tolerable safety profile.

The table shows a dose‑dependent decrease: 8.4 mmHg for 25 mg and 12.7 mmHg for 50 mg, both statistically significant, whereas placebo shows a negligible change.

Correct: B
Randomization and blinding minimize confounding and bias, establishing a causal relationship between dosage and outcome.
