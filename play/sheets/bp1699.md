# BP1699

Researchers investigated the effects of different cognitive training protocols on reaction time in a controlled laboratory setting. A total of 120 participants, aged 18-35, were randomly assigned to one of four groups: a control group that received no training, and three experimental groups that received distinct training regimens (A, B, and C). Each training protocol consisted of 30 minutes of computer-based exercises over five consecutive days. Reaction time was measured using a standardized computerized task before the first training session and immediately after the final session. The results, summarized in Table 1, indicate that all experimental groups exhibited reductions in mean reaction time relative to the control group. Group B showed the largest mean reduction, followed by Groups A and C. Standard deviations varied modestly across groups, suggesting comparable variability in performance. These findings suggest that specific training protocols may differentially enhance cognitive processing speed, with potential implications for occupational performance and clinical rehabilitation. Future studies should examine long-term retention and transfer effects to real-world tasks.
