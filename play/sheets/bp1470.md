Researchers investigated the relationship between light intensity and photosynthetic oxygen production in the aquatic plant Lemna minor. They measured O2 production rates at five different photon flux densities (PFD) under controlled temperature and CO2 conditions. The experiment used a Clark-type electrode to record O2 evolution over a 10‑minute interval, and data were averaged over three replicates. The results, summarized in Table 1, show a sigmoidal response with a plateau at high light levels. The authors concluded that photosynthetic capacity saturates at ~800 µmol photons m‑2 s‑1, suggesting that light is not limiting for this species under typical pond conditions. They also noted that the initial linear phase indicates a high quantum yield, while the inflection point marks the transition to light‑saturated metabolism. These findings have implications for optimizing growth conditions in bioreactors and for modeling primary productivity in shallow freshwater ecosystems.
