# BP1155

Researchers studied the effect of two different dosages of a novel anti‑inflammatory drug on joint pain in a randomized controlled trial. A total of 120 patients with osteoarthritis were enrolled and randomly assigned to either Treatment A (low dose) or Treatment B (high dose). Pain scores were recorded on a 0‑10 visual analog scale at baseline and after 14 days of therapy. The study also included a placebo group for comparison. The following table summarizes the mean pain scores and percent reduction from baseline for each group after 14 days.

| Group          | Baseline Pain | 14‑Day Pain | % Reduction |
|----------------|---------------|-------------|-------------|
| Placebo        | 7.2           | 6.8         | 5.6%        |
| Treatment A    | 7.1           | 4.3         | 39.6%       |
| Treatment B    | 7.3           | 2.9         | 60.3%       |

The researchers concluded that both doses significantly reduced pain compared with placebo, with the high dose showing a greater effect.

Group,Baseline Pain,14‑Day Pain,% Reduction
Placebo,7.2,6.8,5.6%
Treatment A,7.1,4.3,39.6%
Treatment B,7.3,2.9,60.3%

## 1 Which of the following statements best summarizes the data presented in the table?
Correct: A
The table shows that Treatment B achieved a 60.3% reduction in pain, which is higher than the 39.6% reduction seen with Treatment A. Therefore, Treatment B produced a greater percent reduction.

## 2 What is the most appropriate control group for this study?
Correct: B
The placebo group serves as a negative control, allowing the investigators to determine whether the observed pain reduction is due to the drug or to placebo effect. A higher dose group would not serve as a control, and a different drug or no treatment would not provide the same baseline comparison.

## 3 Calculate the average percent reduction in pain for Treatment A.
Correct: B
The table directly lists the percent reduction for Treatment A as 39.6%.

## 4 Which of the following is a potential trap when interpreting the study’s results?
Correct: A
A common trap is to equate greater efficacy with greater safety; higher doses may increase adverse events. The study does not provide safety data, so safety cannot be inferred from efficacy alone.

## 5 How could the findings of this study be applied in clinical practice?
Correct: B
Given the greater efficacy of the high dose but potential for increased adverse effects, a prudent approach is to start with the low dose and increase only if pain persists. The study does not provide safety data, so caution is advised.
