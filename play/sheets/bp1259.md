Researchers at the University of Northbridge examined the impact of caffeine on working‑memory performance in healthy adults. A double‑blind, randomized crossover design was employed, with 40 participants (age 20–35, 22 females) receiving either a 200‑mg caffeine dose or a placebo on separate test days. Each session began with a 30‑minute acclimation period, followed by a 20‑item N‑back task. Reaction times (RT) and accuracy were recorded. The study aimed to determine whether caffeine could enhance cognitive speed and whether any benefits were moderated by baseline sleep quality. Participants also completed a sleep diary the week prior to each session.
