# BP1695

Researchers studied the effect of a novel antioxidant on oxidative stress markers in cultured human fibroblasts. Cells were divided into four groups: A (control, no treatment), B (low-dose antioxidant), C (high-dose antioxidant), and D (antioxidant plus a known pro-oxidant). After 48 hours, the levels of reactive oxygen species (ROS) were quantified by fluorescence intensity, and the expression of the antioxidant enzyme superoxide dismutase (SOD) was measured via qPCR. The data are summarized in Table 1. The authors concluded that the antioxidant dose-dependently reduced ROS and upregulated SOD, but the addition of the pro-oxidant in group D partially reversed these effects. They suggested that the antioxidant could be beneficial in conditions of oxidative stress, pending further in vivo validation.

## bp1695_q1 Based on Table 1, which statement most accurately reflects the observed data?
Correct: C
The table shows ROS decreasing from 100 in group A to 45 in group C, indicating a dose-dependent reduction. Group D’s ROS (80) is higher than group C but lower than group A, demonstrating partial mitigation by the pro-oxidant. SOD expression rises with antioxidant treatment, not decreases.

## bp1695_q2 If the researchers wanted to eliminate the potential confounding effect of cell passage number, which experimental design modification would be most appropriate?
Correct: A
Using cells from a single passage ensures that passage-related changes (e.g., senescence, altered gene expression) affect all groups equally, thereby controlling for this confounder. Random assignment (C) would still allow passage differences to influence results.

## bp1695_q3 What is the percent decrease in ROS from group A to group C?
Correct: C
Percent decrease = [(100 - 45)/100] × 100 = 55%.

## bp1695_q4 Which of the following statements is a common pitfall when interpreting the data from this experiment?
Correct: A
In vitro correlations do not guarantee causation in vivo; the study’s design cannot establish causality beyond the observed associations.

## bp1695_q5 Which future study would most directly build on these findings?
Correct: A
An in vivo mouse model would directly test the antioxidant’s efficacy under physiological oxidative stress, bridging the gap between in vitro and clinical relevance.
