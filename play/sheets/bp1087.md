# BP1087

Researchers investigated the effect of caffeine dosage on reaction time in a controlled laboratory setting. The study recruited 120 healthy adults aged 18–35, randomly assigned to one of four groups: placebo, 50 mg, 100 mg, and 200 mg caffeine. Each participant completed a computerized simple reaction time task, and their mean response times were recorded. The results are summarized in Table 1. The researchers hypothesized that higher caffeine doses would reduce reaction time, but they also considered potential fatigue effects at the highest dose. Data analysis revealed a dose‑dependent improvement up to 100 mg, after which the benefit plateaued. The study’s design included counterbalancing to mitigate order effects and a double‑blind procedure to control expectancy. These findings suggest an optimal caffeine window for cognitive performance, with implications for both athletic training and occupational safety.

Placebo | 350 | 30
50 mg | 320 | 25
100 mg | 310 | 20
200 mg | 305 | 22

## bp1087-1 Which group had the lowest mean reaction time?
Correct: C
The table shows the mean reaction times: Placebo = 350 ms, 50 mg = 320 ms, 100 mg = 310 ms, 200 mg = 305 ms. The 100 mg group has the smallest mean.

## bp1087-2 Which factor was manipulated by the researchers?
Correct: C
The independent variable is the caffeine dosage; participants were randomly assigned to placebo, 50 mg, 100 mg, or 200 mg groups.

## bp1087-3 What is the overall mean reaction time across all groups?
Correct: A
Sum of means = 350 + 320 + 310 + 305 = 1 285 ms. Divide by 4 groups = 321.25 ms, rounded to 321 ms.

## bp1087-4 Which statement is NOT supported by the data?
Correct: B
The table shows 200 mg mean = 305 ms, which is slower than 100 mg (310 ms). Thus statement B is false.

## bp1087-5 What is the most logical implication for future research based on the plateau observed at 200 mg?
Correct: C
The plateau at 200 mg suggests that higher doses might introduce fatigue; thus future studies should examine doses above 200 mg to determine the onset of fatigue effects.
