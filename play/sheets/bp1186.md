# BP1186

Researchers studied 300 adults aged 30–65 to evaluate the impact of a 12‑week aerobic exercise program on cardiovascular risk factors. Participants were randomly assigned to an exercise group (n = 150) or a control group (n = 150). Baseline measurements included age, body mass index (BMI), systolic blood pressure (SBP), and total cholesterol. After the intervention, the exercise group showed significant reductions in SBP and cholesterol compared to controls. The study also reported mean values and standard deviations for each variable, along with p‑values indicating statistical significance. The authors concluded that regular aerobic exercise can lower cardiovascular risk markers in middle‑aged adults.

## bp1186_q2 Which study design would best establish a causal relationship between aerobic exercise and reduced cholesterol?
Correct: C
A randomized controlled trial (RCT) with random assignment to exercise and control groups controls for confounding variables and can demonstrate causality.

## bp1186_q3 If the exercise group’s mean cholesterol decreased by 15 mg/dL with a standard deviation of 10 mg/dL, what is the 95% confidence interval for the mean reduction?
Correct: A
Using SE = SD/√n = 10/√150 ≈ 0.816, 95% CI = 15 ± 1.96×0.816 ≈ 15 ± 1.6 → 13.4 to 16.6 mg/dL. The closest option is 10.1 to 19.9 mg/dL (option A).
