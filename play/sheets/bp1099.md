# BP1099

Researchers studied the effect of a novel dietary supplement on exercise performance in healthy adults. A randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial enrolled 120 participants aged 18‑35 years. Participants were randomly assigned to receive either the supplement (n=60) or a matched placebo (n=60) for 8 weeks. Baseline measurements included body mass index (BMI), resting heart rate, and VO2max. After the intervention, participants performed a graded treadmill test to exhaustion, and time to exhaustion (TTE) was recorded. The supplement group exhibited a mean TTE increase of 12.5% (95% CI 8.3–16.7%) compared with the placebo group, which showed a 2.1% increase (95% CI –1.4–5.6%). Secondary outcomes included changes in lactate threshold and perceived exertion. The supplement was well tolerated, with no serious adverse events reported. These findings suggest that the supplement may enhance aerobic endurance in young adults, potentially through improved mitochondrial efficiency and glycogen sparing.

## bp1099_q1 Which of the following best describes the change in time to exhaustion (TTE) observed in the supplement group compared with the placebo group?
Correct: A
The supplement group’s mean TTE increased by 12.5% with a 95% confidence interval of 8.3–16.7%, whereas the placebo group’s increase was only 2.1% with a CI that includes zero.

## bp1099_q2 Which of the following best describes the design of this study?
Correct: A
The study used randomization, double‑blinding, and a placebo control, which are hallmarks of a randomized controlled trial.

## bp1099_q3 If the mean VO2max of the supplement group increased from 45.2 to 47.0 ml/kg/min, what is the percent change?
Correct: A
Percent change = (47.0 – 45.2) / 45.2 × 100 ≈ 3.98%, which rounds to 3.9%.

Correct: B
The study did not report controlling participants’ dietary intake, which could influence performance outcomes. Baseline VO2max was measured, randomization and placebo use were implemented.

An increase in lactate threshold suggests improved glycogen utilization and mitochondrial efficiency, which aligns with the supplement’s proposed mechanism of enhancing endurance.
