# BP1040

Researchers investigated the effect of varying concentrations of a novel antimicrobial peptide (AMP) on the growth of Pseudomonas aeruginosa in vitro. Twenty-four bacterial cultures were inoculated in 96-well plates and exposed to AMP concentrations of 0, 2, 4, 8, 16, and 32 µg/mL for 24 hours. After incubation, optical density at 600 nm (OD600) was measured to assess bacterial growth. The results, summarized in Table 1, show a dose-dependent decrease in OD600, with complete inhibition observed at 32 µg/mL. In addition, the study measured the minimal inhibitory concentration (MIC) by serial dilution and determined the MIC to be 16 µg/mL. The researchers also performed a time‑kill assay at 8 µg/mL, revealing a 3‑log reduction in colony‑forming units after 6 hours. Statistical analysis (ANOVA) indicated significant differences between concentrations (p < 0.01). The authors concluded that the AMP has potent antibacterial activity and could serve as a lead compound for new antibiotics.

## bp1040_q1 Which concentration shows the highest % inhibition?
Correct: D
The table indicates that 32 µg/mL achieves 100% inhibition, the highest among the tested concentrations.

## bp1040_q2 Which experimental approach best determines the minimal inhibitory concentration (MIC) of the AMP?
Correct: C
MIC is defined as the lowest concentration that prevents visible growth; it is determined by serial dilution and assessing growth, not by OD alone or statistical tests.

## bp1040_q3 If the OD600 at 8 µg/mL is 0.40, what is the percent inhibition relative to the control?
Correct: A
Percent inhibition = [(1.00 – 0.40)/1.00] × 100 = 60%.

## bp1040_q4 Which of the following statements is NOT supported by the data?
Correct: D
The table lists the OD600 for 0 µg/mL as 1.00, not 0.85.
