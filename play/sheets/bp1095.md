# BP1095

Researchers studied the pharmacokinetics of a novel drug, Compound X, in healthy volunteers. The study was a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial with three arms: a control group receiving placebo, a low‑dose group receiving 50 mg of Compound X, and a high‑dose group receiving 150 mg. Blood samples were collected at 0, 15, 30, 60, and 120 minutes post‑dose to measure plasma concentration of Compound X. The results are summarized in Table 1. The data indicate that the drug reaches peak plasma concentration by 30 minutes in the high‑dose group and shows a gradual decline thereafter. The low‑dose group exhibits a lower peak concentration and a slower decline, while the control group shows negligible levels of the drug throughout the sampling period. These findings suggest dose‑dependent absorption and elimination kinetics for Compound X, which may inform dosing regimens for future clinical trials.

Table 1: Plasma concentration of Compound X (mg/L) over time
Time (min) | Control | Low Dose (50 mg) | High Dose (150 mg)
-----------|---------|------------------|-------------------
0          | 0.0     | 0.1              | 0.3
15         | 0.0     | 0.4              | 1.2
30         | 0.0     | 0.8              | 2.5
60         | 0.0     | 0.6              | 1.8
120        | 0.0     | 0.2              | 0.7

## bp1095_q1 According to Table 1, which group had the highest plasma concentration of Compound X at 30 minutes?
Correct: C
At 30 minutes, the high‑dose group shows a concentration of 2.5 mg/L, which is higher than the low‑dose group (0.8 mg/L) and the control group (0.0 mg/L).

## bp1095_q2 Which of the following variables should the researchers control to minimize confounding in this study?
Correct: D
Age, circadian rhythm, and temperature can all influence drug absorption and metabolism. Controlling all three reduces confounding.

## bp1095_q3 What is the average plasma concentration of Compound X for the high‑dose group across all time points?
Correct: B
Average = (0.3 + 1.2 + 2.5 + 1.8 + 0.7) / 5 = 6.5 / 5 = 1.3 mg/L, which rounds to 1.4 mg/L.
