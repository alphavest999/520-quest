# BP1478

Researchers studied the effect of a new dietary supplement on resting heart rate among adults aged 30-50. 120 participants were randomly assigned to either the supplement group (n=60) or a placebo group (n=60). After a 12-week intervention, resting heart rates were measured. Table 1 summarizes the mean resting heart rates and standard deviations for each group. The supplement group showed a mean resting heart rate of 68 bpm (SD 5.2), while the placebo group had a mean of 72 bpm (SD 4.8). Baseline heart rates were similar between groups, with means of 70.5 bpm (SD 4.9) for the supplement group and 70.2 bpm (SD 5.1) for placebo. The investigators also reported a 95% confidence interval for the mean difference of –3.8 to –0.2 bpm. The investigators performed a two-sample t-test to evaluate the difference between groups and reported a p-value of 0.02. They also calculated the effect size (Cohen’s d) to assess clinical relevance. The study concluded that the supplement significantly reduced resting heart rate compared to placebo.

## bp1478-1 What is the mean difference in resting heart rate between the supplement and placebo groups?
Correct: A
The mean resting heart rate in the supplement group is 68 bpm and in the placebo group is 72 bpm; the difference is 72 − 68 = 4 bpm.

## bp1478-2 What type of study design was employed?
Correct: A
Participants were randomly assigned to supplement or placebo groups, which is the hallmark of a randomized controlled trial.

## bp1478-3 Calculate Cohen’s d for the difference in resting heart rates.
Correct: A
Pooled SD ≈ sqrt(((59*5.2²)+(59*4.8²))/118) ≈ 5.0. Difference = 4 bpm. Cohen’s d = 4/5.0 ≈ 0.8.

## bp1478-4 Which statement about the reported p‑value is correct?
Correct: A
A p‑value of 0.02 means that if the null hypothesis were true, there would be a 2% probability of observing a difference as extreme as the one seen.

## bp1478-5 Which statement best links the study findings to clinical practice?
Correct: A
A 4‑bpm reduction in resting heart rate is clinically meaningful and could translate to lower cardiovascular risk.
