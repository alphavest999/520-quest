# BP1038

Researchers investigated the influence of light wavelength on the rate of photosynthesis in the green alga *Chlorella vulgaris*. Cultures were grown under controlled conditions and exposed to monochromatic light at five wavelengths (400, 450, 500, 550, and 600 nm) at a constant photon flux density of 200 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹. Oxygen evolution was measured over a 30‑minute interval using a Clark-type electrode. The data revealed a bell‑shaped response curve, with peak oxygen production at 500 nm. The study also examined the effect of varying light intensity at 500 nm, demonstrating a linear increase in oxygen evolution up to 400 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ before plateauing. The authors concluded that *C. vulgaris* possesses photoreceptors that optimally absorb blue light, aligning with the absorption spectra of chlorophyll‑a and chlorophyll‑b. This finding has implications for optimizing algal biofuel production, as maximizing light utilization can enhance biomass yield. The experiment’s design incorporated both wavelength and intensity variables, allowing for a comprehensive analysis of light quality and quantity on photosynthetic efficiency.
