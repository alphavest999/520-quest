# BP1552

Researchers studied the effects of a novel dietary supplement on the insulin sensitivity of 120 adults aged 30–55 years. Participants were randomly assigned to either the supplement group (n = 60) or a placebo group (n = 60). Over 12 weeks, fasting glucose, insulin levels, and HOMA-IR scores were measured biweekly. The study aimed to determine whether the supplement could reduce insulin resistance compared to placebo. Baseline characteristics were similar across groups, with mean age 42.3 years, mean BMI 27.8 kg/m², and mean fasting glucose 95.6 mg/dL. The supplement group received 500 mg of the active compound daily, while the placebo group received an identical inert capsule. Compliance was monitored via pill counts and weekly phone check‑ins. At week 12, the supplement group exhibited a mean HOMA-IR reduction of 1.8 units, whereas the placebo group showed a reduction of 0.4 units. The researchers concluded that the supplement significantly improved insulin sensitivity, suggesting potential therapeutic benefits for prediabetic individuals.

## bp1552-01 Which of the following best describes the primary outcome of the study?
Correct: B
The study’s primary aim was to assess improvement in insulin sensitivity, operationalized by the change in HOMA‑IR scores. The reported reduction of 1.8 units in the supplement group versus 0.4 units in placebo directly addresses this outcome.

## bp1552-02 Which experimental design feature best supports the validity of the study’s conclusions?
Correct: B
Random assignment minimizes selection bias and balances confounding variables between groups, strengthening causal inference about the supplement’s effect.

## bp1552-03 If the mean HOMA‑IR for the supplement group decreased from 4.4 to 2.6, what is the percent reduction?
Correct: A
Percent reduction = (4.4 – 2.6) / 4.4 × 100 = 1.8 / 4.4 × 100 ≈ 40.9%.

## bp1552-04 Which of the following statements is a potential trap for interpreting the study’s results?
Correct: B
Without baseline HOMA‑IR values, it is unclear whether the groups started at comparable levels, which could bias the observed change.

## bp1552-05 Which resource would best help a clinician apply these findings to practice?
Correct: B
A systematic review aggregates multiple studies, providing a broader evidence base that is more directly translatable to clinical decision‑making.
