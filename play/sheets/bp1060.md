# BP1060

Researchers investigated the effects of a novel dietary supplement on exercise performance in healthy adults. A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial enrolled 120 participants aged 18‑35. Participants were randomly assigned to either the supplement group (n=60) or the placebo group (n=60). Over 12 weeks, both groups followed identical exercise regimens consisting of 3 resistance training sessions per week and 2 aerobic sessions. The primary outcome was peak oxygen uptake (VO2max) measured by a graded exercise test at baseline and after 12 weeks. Secondary outcomes included muscular strength (1RM bench press), body composition (DXA), and self‑reported fatigue (VAS). Table 1 summarizes the mean changes in VO2max and 1RM for both groups. The supplement group showed a statistically significant greater increase in VO2max (+3.2 mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹ vs. +1.5 in placebo, p<0.01) and 1RM (+8.5 kg vs. +4.2 kg, p<0.05). No adverse events were reported. These findings suggest the supplement may enhance aerobic and strength adaptations when combined with resistance training.

## bp1060-1 Which group experienced the greater increase in VO2max after the 12‑week intervention?
Correct: A
The supplement group’s mean VO2max increased by +3.2 mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹, whereas the placebo group’s increase was +1.5 mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹, indicating a greater improvement in the supplement group.

## bp1060-2 What type of study design was employed in this research?
Correct: A
The passage explicitly states the study was a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial, which is the definition of a randomized controlled trial.

## bp1060-3 What is the absolute difference in mean change of 1RM between the supplement and placebo groups?
Correct: A
Subtract the placebo mean change (4.2 kg) from the supplement mean change (8.5 kg) to obtain 4.3 kg.

## bp1060-4 Which of the following could be a potential confounder in this study?
Correct: B
Although the study used randomization, gender distribution was not specified; unequal gender proportions could influence performance outcomes and thus act as a confounder.

## bp1060-5 To whom are the study’s findings most directly applicable?
Correct: A
The inclusion criteria limited participants to healthy adults aged 18‑35, so the results are most relevant to that age group.
