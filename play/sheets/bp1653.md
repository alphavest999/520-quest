# BP1653

Researchers studied the effect of a novel cognitive training program on working memory performance in adults aged 25–35. A randomized controlled trial enrolled 120 participants, who were randomly assigned to either the training group (n = 60) or a wait‑list control group (n = 60). Over a 6‑week period, the training group completed 45 minutes of adaptive n‑back tasks daily, while the control group received no intervention. Working memory was assessed at baseline and after the intervention using the Digit Span and Spatial Span subtests of the Wechsler Adult Intelligence Scale. The table below summarizes the mean scores and standard deviations for each group at both time points. The researchers hypothesized that the training group would show a statistically significant improvement in working memory scores relative to the control group.

Condition | Time Point | Mean Digit Span | SD | n
--- | --- | --- | --- | ---
Training | Baseline | 7.2 | 1.1 | 60
Training | Post | 9.1 | 1.0 | 60
Control | Baseline | 7.3 | 1.2 | 60
Control | Post | 7.5 | 1.1 | 60

## 1 Which of the following statements best describes the observed change in Digit Span scores for the training group?
Correct: A
The mean Digit Span for the training group rose from 7.2 to 9.1, a difference of 1.9 points, reflecting a moderate improvement.

## 2 Which experimental design feature best controls for placebo effects in this study?
Correct: A
Random assignment ensures that any placebo or expectation effects are distributed equally between groups, allowing attribution of differences to the intervention.

## 3 Calculate the effect size (Cohen’s d) for the training group’s improvement from baseline to post‑intervention.
Correct: A
Cohen’s d = (9.1 – 7.2) / pooled SD ≈ 1.9 / 1.05 ≈ 0.86.

## 4 Which of the following is a potential flaw in the study’s design that could bias the results?
Correct: A
Because participants knew whether they were training or not, expectancy effects could influence performance, especially in a self‑report or performance‑based outcome.

## 5 Which external resource would best help a clinician interpret the clinical significance of the observed improvement?
Correct: A
A meta‑analysis provides context for effect sizes across studies, helping clinicians gauge whether a 1.9‑point gain is clinically meaningful.
