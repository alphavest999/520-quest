# BP1359

Researchers studied the effects of a novel dietary supplement on resting metabolic rate (RMR) in healthy adults. The study recruited 90 participants, randomly assigned to three groups: Group A received the supplement, Group B received a placebo, and Group C received no intervention. After a 4‑week intervention period, RMR was measured using indirect calorimetry. The data are summarized in Table 1. The researchers reported that Group A had a mean RMR of 5.2 kcal/min (SD = 0.8, N = 30), Group B had a mean RMR of 6.1 kcal/min (SD = 0.9, N = 30), and Group C had a mean RMR of 4.9 kcal/min (SD = 0.7, N = 30). The authors concluded that the supplement significantly increased RMR compared to placebo and no‑intervention controls.

Sample | Mean | SD | N
A | 5.2 | 0.8 | 30
B | 6.1 | 0.9 | 30
C | 4.9 | 0.7 | 30

## bp1359-1 Which sample shows the greatest variability?
Correct: B
Sample B has the largest standard deviation (0.9) relative to its mean, indicating the greatest variability.

## bp1359-2 Which experimental design would best test whether the difference between Sample A and Sample B is statistically significant?
Correct: B
The groups are independent; an independent samples t‑test is appropriate to compare their means.

## bp1359-3 Calculate the Cohen’s d effect size for the difference between Sample A and Sample B.
Correct: C
Pooled SD ≈ 0.851; mean difference = 0.9; Cohen’s d = 0.9/0.851 ≈ 1.06, closest to 1.02.

## bp1359-4 Which of the following is a common mistake when interpreting the results of this study?
Correct: A
The study is observational in terms of outcome measurement; causation cannot be inferred solely from the observed differences.

## bp1359-5 How could the findings of this study be applied in a real‑world context?
Correct: B
The data suggest the supplement may increase metabolic rate, informing regulatory guidance and consumer safety policies.
