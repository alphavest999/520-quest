# BP1611

Researchers studied the impact of sleep deprivation on cognitive performance among university students. Eight volunteers were recruited and assigned to one of four sleep conditions: 4, 6, 8, or 10 hours of sleep prior to a standardized reaction time and accuracy test. The study measured reaction time (in milliseconds) and accuracy (percentage correct) on a computerized Stroop task. Results indicated that participants who slept fewer hours exhibited slower reaction times and lower accuracy scores, whereas those with longer sleep durations performed better. The data suggest a dose–response relationship between sleep duration and cognitive function, but the study design limits causal inference. The researchers plan to conduct a follow-up randomized controlled trial to further examine these effects.

## bp1611_q1 Which participant achieved the highest accuracy score?
Correct: D
Accuracy scores increase with sleep duration: 78% (A), 85% (B), 92% (C), 95% (D). Thus, Participant D had the highest accuracy.

## bp1611_q2 Which study design would best establish a causal relationship between sleep duration and cognitive performance?
Correct: B
Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) assign participants to conditions randomly, controlling for confounders and allowing causal inference. The current design is observational, so an RCT would be the best next step.

## bp1611_q3 What is the mean reaction time across all participants?
Correct: A
Sum of reaction times = 420 + 390 + 350 + 310 = 1470 ms. Mean = 1470 / 4 = 367.5 ms.

## bp1611_q4 Which statement is NOT supported by the data presented?
Correct: D
The study is observational; it shows association but cannot prove causation. Statements A–C are directly supported by the data.

## bp1611_q5 Which concept best describes the limitation of the current study?
Correct: A
Because the study is observational, other variables (e.g., stress, caffeine) could confound the relationship between sleep and performance. Randomization and placebo control would address this, but they are not present.
