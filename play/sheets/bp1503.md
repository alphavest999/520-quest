# BP1503

Researchers studied the effects of a novel dietary supplement on glucose metabolism in adult mice. Two hundred thirty-two male C57BL/6J mice were randomly assigned to one of four groups (n = 58 per group): (1) control diet, (2) high‑fat diet, (3) high‑fat diet plus low‑dose supplement (10 mg/kg/day), and (4) high‑fat diet plus high‑dose supplement (30 mg/kg/day). Over 12 weeks, weekly measurements of fasting blood glucose, insulin tolerance test (ITT) area under the curve (AUC), and body weight were recorded. At week 12, mice were sacrificed, and liver tissues were harvested for glycogen content and expression of key gluconeogenic enzymes (PEPCK, G6Pase). The data revealed a dose‑dependent improvement in ITT AUC and liver glycogen storage, with the high‑dose group showing a 35% reduction in fasting glucose compared to the high‑fat group. Gene expression analysis indicated significant down‑regulation of PEPCK and G6Pase in the high‑dose group, suggesting enhanced insulin sensitivity. These findings support the potential of the supplement to mitigate diet‑induced insulin resistance.
