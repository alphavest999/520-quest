# BP981

Researchers at the University of Health Sciences conducted a study to examine how varying degrees of sleep deprivation affect cognitive performance in adolescents. Fifty students aged 14–17 were recruited and randomly assigned to one of three sleep conditions: normal sleep (8 hours), moderate sleep deprivation (4 hours), and severe sleep deprivation (2 hours). After the assigned sleep period, each participant completed a computerized reaction time task designed to assess attention and processing speed. The mean reaction times (RT) and standard deviations (SD) for each group were recorded. The results indicated a clear dose–response relationship: adolescents who slept 8 hours had the fastest reaction times, while those who slept only 2 hours exhibited the slowest responses. These findings suggest that sleep deprivation significantly impairs cognitive function in this age group, with potential implications for academic performance and safety.
