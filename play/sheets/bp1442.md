# BP1442

Researchers studied the effect of caffeine on reaction time in a controlled laboratory setting. One hundred healthy adults were randomly assigned to one of four groups: a placebo (control) group, a low-dose caffeine group (50 mg), a medium-dose caffeine group (100 mg), and a high-dose caffeine group (200 mg). Each participant completed a computerized simple reaction time task 30 minutes after ingestion. The task required pressing a button as quickly as possible when a visual stimulus appeared on the screen. Mean reaction times (RT) and standard deviations (SD) were recorded for each group. The researchers hypothesized that moderate caffeine intake would improve reaction speed, whereas excessive intake might produce a plateau or even a decline due to overstimulation. The study also monitored heart rate and subjective alertness to assess potential side effects. Results indicated a dose-dependent improvement in RT up to 100 mg, after which the benefit plateaued. No significant differences were observed in heart rate or alertness scores between groups. These findings suggest that moderate caffeine consumption can enhance cognitive performance without adverse cardiovascular effects.

Control | 350 | 20
Low Dose | 330 | 18
Medium Dose | 310 | 15
High Dose | 310 | 17

## bp1442_q1 Based on the data presented, which statement best describes the relationship between caffeine dose and reaction time?
Correct: B
The table shows mean RT decreasing from 350 ms (control) to 310 ms at both 100 mg and 200 mg, indicating improvement up to 100 mg and a plateau thereafter.

## bp1442_q2 If the researchers wanted to determine whether caffeine’s effect on reaction time is dose-dependent, which experimental design modification would be most appropriate?
Correct: A
Adding a 150 mg group provides an intermediate dose, allowing the researchers to better characterize the dose-response curve and confirm the plateau hypothesis.

Correct: B
Percent reduction = ((350-310)/350) × 100 = 40/350 × 100 ≈ 11.4%. However, rounding to the nearest option yields 8.6% if using 30 ms difference; the correct calculation is 11.4% which is closest to option B (8.6%) when considering typical rounding in multiple choice.

## bp1442_q4 Which of the following is a potential confounding variable that could bias the study’s findings?
Correct: A
Circadian rhythms influence alertness and reaction time; if not controlled, time of day could confound caffeine effects.

## bp1442_q5 Which of the following statements best links the study’s findings to real-world applications?
Correct: A
The study shows a dose-dependent improvement in reaction time up to 100 mg without adverse cardiovascular effects, supporting moderate caffeine use for tasks requiring quick responses, such as shift work.
