# BP1050

Researchers at the University of Health Sciences conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive agent, HypoPress, in adults with stage 1 hypertension. Sixty participants were enrolled and randomly assigned to either the HypoPress group (n=30) or a placebo group (n=30). Baseline systolic blood pressure (SBP) was measured after a 5‑minute seated rest. Patients received their assigned treatment daily for 4 weeks. SBP was reassessed at the end of the study. Table 1 summarizes the SBP readings for the HypoPress cohort. The mean baseline SBP was 142 mmHg, and the mean SBP after 4 weeks was 128 mmHg, yielding an average reduction of 14 mmHg. The largest individual decrease was 22 mmHg in patient 5, while the smallest was 6 mmHg in patient 12. The study also recorded diastolic pressures, heart rates, and adverse events, but these data are not shown in the table. The authors concluded that HypoPress significantly lowers SBP compared with placebo and is well tolerated. Further studies are needed to assess long‑term outcomes and optimal dosing.

## bp1050-1 Based on Table 1, what is the mean reduction in systolic blood pressure (SBP) among the HypoPress cohort?
Correct: C
The mean change is calculated by averaging the individual reductions: (−14−14−14−14−22−6)/6 = −14 mmHg.

## bp1050-2 Which of the following best describes the control condition used in this study?
Correct: A
Participants in the control arm received a placebo, allowing comparison of HypoPress effects against no active medication.

## bp1050-3 Patient 5 experienced a 22 mmHg decrease in SBP. What percent reduction from their baseline does this represent?
Correct: B
Percent reduction = (22 ÷ 145) × 100 ≈ 15.2%, rounded to 15%.
