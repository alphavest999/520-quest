# BP1210

Researchers at the University of Health Sciences conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the effect of a novel antihypertensive drug, Hypertenso, on systolic blood pressure (SBP). A total of 120 participants with stage‑1 hypertension were enrolled and randomly assigned to either the treatment group (Hypertenso) or a placebo control group. Baseline SBP was measured after a 5‑minute seated rest, and follow‑up measurements were taken at 4 weeks. The study aimed to determine whether Hypertenso could produce a clinically meaningful reduction in SBP compared to placebo. In addition to SBP, researchers recorded heart rate, serum potassium, and patient-reported side effects. The investigators hypothesized that the drug would lower SBP by at least 8 mmHg, a threshold considered clinically significant for reducing cardiovascular risk. The trial was double‑blind, and adherence was monitored via pill counts and electronic medication event monitoring systems. Results were analyzed using intention‑to‑treat principles and a two‑sided alpha of 0.05.
