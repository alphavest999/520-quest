# BP1046

Researchers at the Institute of Cognitive Science investigated how different visual stimulus patterns influence reaction times in a simple choice task. Twenty-four adult volunteers were randomly assigned to one of four experimental groups: a Control group presented with a neutral gray square, and three experimental groups presented with either a flickering red square (Condition A), a static blue square (Condition B), or a moving green square (Condition C). Each participant completed 60 trials, and the mean response time (in milliseconds) along with the standard deviation was recorded. The results, summarized in Table 1, show that the flickering red square (Condition A) yielded the fastest responses, while the moving green square (Condition C) produced the slowest. The researchers hypothesize that the dynamic properties of the stimuli modulate attentional capture and motor preparation. They plan to extend the study by varying stimulus duration and adding a dual-task component to assess cognitive load. The findings have potential implications for interface design, where rapid user responses are critical, and for understanding how visual dynamics affect perceptual processing.

Table 1: Mean response times (ms) and standard deviations for each group.

| Group | Mean Response Time (ms) | Standard Deviation (ms) |
|-------|------------------------|------------------------|
| Control | 1200 | 100 |
| Condition A | 900 | 90 |
| Condition B | 1100 | 110 |
| Condition C | 1300 | 120 |

## bp1046_q1 Which group had the fastest mean response time?
Correct: B
The table shows that Condition A had a mean response time of 900 ms, which is lower (faster) than Control (1200 ms), Condition B (1100 ms), and Condition C (1300 ms).

## bp1046_q2 Which variable was manipulated in this experiment?
Correct: B
The experiment varied the visual stimulus (color and motion) across the four groups; other factors were held constant.

## bp1046_q3 What percent improvement in mean response time does Condition A show relative to Control?
Correct: D
Improvement = (1200 - 900) / 1200 = 300 / 1200 = 0.25 = 25%.

## bp1046_q4 Which of the following is NOT a potential source of bias in this study?
Correct: D
Randomization is a method to reduce bias, not a source of bias itself.

## bp1046_q5 Which real‑world application could most directly benefit from these findings?
Correct: A
The study shows that dynamic visual cues can speed up user responses, a principle useful for interface designers aiming for rapid interaction.
