# BP1538

Researchers studied the effect of varying concentrations of a novel antimicrobial peptide on bacterial growth rates in vitro. They cultured Escherichia coli in 96‑well plates, adding peptide at concentrations of 0, 5, 10, 20, and 40 µg/mL. After 24 hours, optical density at 600 nm (OD600) was measured. The results were plotted in Table 1. Additionally, they assessed the peptide’s cytotoxicity on mammalian fibroblasts using a lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) release assay, with concentrations ranging from 0 to 40 µg/mL. The LDH data are shown in Table 2. The study aimed to determine the therapeutic window where bacterial inhibition is maximized while mammalian cell toxicity remains below 10%.

Concentration (µg/mL) | OD600 (E. coli) | % LDH release (fibroblasts)
0 | 1.00 | 5
5 | 0.85 | 6
10 | 0.60 | 8
20 | 0.30 | 12
40 | 0.10 | 18

## bp1538-1 Which concentration of peptide results in the lowest bacterial OD600?
Correct: D
The lowest OD600 value (0.30) corresponds to 20 µg/mL, indicating the greatest reduction in bacterial growth.

## bp1538-2 To further assess the peptide’s efficacy, which of the following modifications would best strengthen the study’s conclusions?
Correct: D
All listed modifications would provide more robust data: more replicates reduce random error, testing another species assesses spectrum, and an alternative viability assay confirms the OD600 findings.

## bp1538-3 Assuming linear LDH release between 10 and 20 µg/mL, estimate the % LDH release at 15 µg/mL.
Correct: B
Linear interpolation: 8 % at 10 µg/mL to 12 % at 20 µg/mL gives 10 % at 15 µg/mL.

## bp1538-4 Which of the following statements is NOT supported by the data?
Correct: D
LDH release is not linear beyond 20 µg/mL; it accelerates, so the linear assumption is incorrect.

## bp1538-5 Which concept best explains the observed therapeutic window?
Correct: B
The therapeutic index, the ratio of toxic to effective dose, defines the window where bacterial inhibition is achieved without exceeding 10 % mammalian toxicity.
