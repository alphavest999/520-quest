# BP1753

Researchers studied the effect of a novel antioxidant compound on oxidative stress markers in cultured human fibroblasts. Twenty-one fibroblast cultures were divided into three groups: a control group receiving vehicle only, a low‑dose antioxidant group (10 µM), and a high‑dose antioxidant group (50 µM). After 24 hours of exposure, reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels were quantified using a fluorescent probe. The control group exhibited mean ROS levels of 120 ± 5 units, the low‑dose group 80 ± 4 units, and the high‑dose group 78 ± 3 units. The antioxidant treatment significantly reduced ROS production in a dose‑dependent manner, with the high‑dose group showing a 35% reduction compared to control (p < 0.01). These findings suggest the compound’s potential to mitigate oxidative damage in fibroblasts, which may be relevant for skin aging and wound healing applications. Future studies will evaluate long‑term effects and underlying signaling pathways.

## bp1753-01 Which sample had the highest measured ROS level?
Correct: A
The highest ROS measurement reported in the table is 120 units for control sample C1, indicating the greatest oxidative stress among the groups.

## bp1753-02 Which experimental design element is essential for attributing the observed ROS reduction to the antioxidant compound?
Correct: D
Randomization reduces selection bias, a vehicle‑only control distinguishes treatment effects from baseline, and blinding prevents observer bias. All three strengthen causal inference.

## bp1753-03 Calculate the percent reduction in mean ROS level from the control group to the low‑dose antioxidant group.
Correct: C
Mean control = (120+115+118)/3 = 117.67. Mean low‑dose = (80+85+78)/3 = 81. Reduction = (117.67-81)/117.67 ≈ 0.367 or 36.7%, rounded to 35%.
