# BP1235

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel anti‑inflammatory compound on interleukin‑6 (IL‑6) production by human macrophages. Four experimental groups were used: a vehicle control and three concentrations of the compound (0.5, 1.0, and 2.0 µM). Macrophages were incubated for 24 hours, after which the culture supernatant was collected and IL‑6 quantified by ELISA. The mean IL‑6 concentrations (pg/mL) ± standard deviation (SD) for each group are shown in Table 1. The study aimed to determine whether increasing the compound concentration would suppress IL‑6 secretion, thereby indicating anti‑inflammatory activity. All assays were performed in triplicate, and the data were analyzed using one‑way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post‑hoc test. The researchers reported a statistically significant dose‑dependent decrease in IL‑6 levels compared to control, with the highest concentration yielding the lowest IL‑6 production.

## Q1 Which of the following best describes the trend observed in IL‑6 production across the experimental groups?
Correct: C
The table shows a clear dose‑dependent reduction in IL‑6: 1200 pg/mL (control) → 950 pg/mL (0.5 µM) → 720 pg/mL (1.0 µM) → 480 pg/mL (2.0 µM). Thus IL‑6 decreases with higher concentrations.

## Q2 Which variable was most appropriately controlled to ensure that changes in IL‑6 were attributable to the compound concentration?
Correct: A
All groups were incubated for the same 24 hours, controlling for time‑dependent changes in cytokine production. This ensures that observed differences are due to compound concentration rather than incubation duration.
