# BP1189

Researchers studied the effect of a new dietary supplement on exercise performance in healthy adults. A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial enrolled 120 participants aged 18–35. Participants were randomly assigned to either the supplement group (n=60) or placebo group (n=60). Over 8 weeks, all participants performed a standardized treadmill test at baseline and after the intervention. The primary outcome was peak oxygen uptake (VO2peak). Table 1 summarizes the mean VO2peak values and standard deviations for each group. In addition, secondary outcomes included time to exhaustion and perceived exertion scores. The supplement group showed a mean increase of 3.2 mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹ in VO2peak compared to 1.1 mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹ in the placebo group. Statistical analysis revealed a p-value of 0.004 for the between-group difference. No adverse events were reported. The authors concluded that the supplement may enhance aerobic capacity in young adults.

Table 1. VO2peak changes

| Group | Mean VO2peak (mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹) | SD |
|-------|------------------------------|----|
| Placebo | 42.5 | 4.3 |
| Supplement | 45.7 | 3.9 |

## 3 If the supplement group had a mean VO2peak of 45.7 with SD 3.9, what is the approximate 95% confidence interval for the mean, assuming n=60?
Correct: A
SE = SD/√n = 3.9/√60 ≈ 0.503. 95% CI = 45.7 ± 1.96×0.503 ≈ 45.7 ± 0.99 → 44.71 to 46.69.
