# BP1371

Researchers studied the effect of a novel dietary supplement on the recovery of muscle strength after resistance training in healthy adults. Twenty-eight participants were randomly assigned to either the supplement group (n=14) or a placebo group (n=14). Over a 6‑week period, all subjects performed a standardized resistance‑training regimen three times per week. Muscle strength was assessed by measuring one‑repetition maximum (1RM) for the bench press and leg press at baseline, week 3, and week 6. In addition, serum creatine kinase (CK) levels were measured at the same time points to evaluate muscle damage. The supplement group exhibited a 12% greater increase in bench‑press 1RM from baseline to week 6 compared with the placebo group (p = 0.02). CK levels rose modestly in both groups but were significantly lower in the supplement group at week 6 (p = 0.04). These findings suggest that the supplement may enhance strength gains and reduce exercise‑induced muscle damage.
