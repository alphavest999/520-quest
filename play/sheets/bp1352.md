# BP1352

Researchers studied the effects of a novel cognitive training program on working memory performance in adults aged 25 to 45. The study recruited 120 participants who were randomly assigned to either a control group (n=60) that received no intervention or an experimental group (n=60) that completed a 12-week training regimen consisting of computer-based working memory tasks. Baseline assessments measured working memory capacity using a standardized digit span test. After the intervention period, post‑intervention assessments were conducted. The results, summarized in Table 1, indicate that the experimental group showed a statistically significant improvement in mean digit span scores compared to the control group. Specifically, the control group’s mean score increased from 7.2 to 7.5, whereas the experimental group’s mean score rose from 7.3 to 9.1. The study also reported secondary outcomes such as reaction time and self‑reported stress levels, but these were not the primary focus. The authors concluded that the cognitive training program effectively enhances working memory capacity in the studied population. Future research should examine long‑term retention and potential transfer effects to daily functioning.
