Researchers studied the growth of two bacterial strains, A and B, under identical laboratory conditions over a 30‑day period. The experiment was conducted in triplicate to ensure reproducibility. At days 0, 5, 10, 20, and 30, samples were taken, serially diluted, and plated on agar to count colony‑forming units (CFU). The data are summarized in Table 1. The goal was to determine which strain proliferates more rapidly and whether the growth rates differ significantly over time. The researchers hypothesized that strain A would exhibit a faster exponential phase due to a higher metabolic rate. They also planned to calculate the doubling time for each strain and assess the statistical significance using a two‑sample t‑test at each time point. This information could inform future applications in industrial fermentation where rapid biomass accumulation is desirable.

## bp1258-01 At day 10, which bacterial strain has a higher mean population count?
Correct: A
The table shows that at day 10, strain A has 1.9×10⁶ CFU/mL while strain B has 1.6×10⁶ CFU/mL. Therefore, strain A has the higher mean population count.

## bp1258-02 Which experimental design best tests the hypothesis that strain A has a faster exponential growth phase?
Correct: C
A parallel group design keeps both strains under identical conditions, allowing direct comparison of their growth curves. The other designs introduce additional variables that could confound the comparison.
