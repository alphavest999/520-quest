# BP1265

Researchers studied the effect of a novel dietary supplement on blood pressure in adults aged 40–60. A randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial enrolled 120 participants, who were assigned to either the supplement group (n = 60) or the placebo group (n = 60). Baseline systolic blood pressure (SBP) was measured after a 30‑minute rest period. Participants received their assigned intervention daily for 12 weeks. SBP was reassessed at weeks 4, 8, and 12. The study aimed to determine whether the supplement could reduce SBP compared to placebo. The researchers also recorded adverse events and compliance rates. Table 3‑4 summarizes the mean SBP values (± SD) at each time point for both groups.

## bp1265-1 Which statement best describes the observed trend in systolic blood pressure for the supplement group?
Correct: C
The table shows a consistent decline in mean SBP for the supplement group from 132 mmHg at baseline to 120 mmHg at week 12, a 12‑mmHg reduction. The placebo group shows minimal change. Thus, the supplement produced a progressive decrease.

## bp1265-2 Which experimental design best supports the conclusion that the supplement caused the observed SBP reduction?
Correct: B
A randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial minimizes bias and confounding, allowing causal inference that the supplement caused the SBP reduction.

## bp1265-3 Calculate the percent reduction in mean SBP for the supplement group from baseline to week 12.
Correct: A
Percent reduction = (12 mmHg / 132 mmHg) × 100% ≈ 9.1%. The 12‑mmHg drop represents a 9.1% decrease from baseline.

## bp1265-4 Which of the following statements is a common misconception about interpreting the study’s results?
Correct: C
While the supplement group showed a greater reduction, other factors (e.g., lifestyle changes) could also contribute. The study design controls for many confounders, but attributing the effect solely to the supplement ignores potential residual confounding.

## bp1265-5 Which concept is most directly linked to the study’s use of a placebo control?
Correct: A
A placebo control allows the investigators to assess the placebo effect—changes in outcomes due to participants’ expectations rather than the active intervention.
