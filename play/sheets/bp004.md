# bp004 – Glycolysis Regulation

**Passage**  
Glycolysis is tightly controlled at the level of three rate‑limiting enzymes—hexokinase (HK), phosphofructokinase‑1 (PFK‑1), and pyruvate kinase (PK).  Allosteric effectors and hormonal signals integrate to ensure that glucose oxidation matches the cell’s energetic and biosynthetic needs.  ATP, a high‑energy nucleotide, acts as a negative allosteric regulator of both HK and PFK‑1, whereas ADP and AMP relieve this inhibition, thereby promoting glycolysis when the cell is energy‑depleted.  Citrate, a TCA‑cycle intermediate, feeds back to inhibit PFK‑1, preventing futile cycling when the citric‑acid cycle is saturated.  Fructose‑2,6‑bisphosphate (F‑2,6‑BP), synthesized by the bifunctional enzyme PFK‑2/FBPase‑2, is a potent activator of PFK‑1 and an inhibitor of fructose‑1,6‑bisphosphatase, thereby shifting the balance toward glycolysis.  Hormonal regulation further modulates these pathways: insulin promotes the synthesis of F‑2,6‑BP in adipose and liver cells, enhancing glycolysis; glucagon and epinephrine stimulate phosphofructokinase‑2 activity in liver, increasing F‑2,6‑BP and glycolytic flux during fasting or stress.  Finally, PK is regulated by phosphorylation; in the liver, protein kinase A (PKA) phosphorylates PK, rendering it less active and favoring gluconeogenesis, whereas dephosphorylation by protein phosphatase 1 (PP1) restores its activity during feeding.  Thus, glycolysis is coordinated by a network of allosteric modulators and hormonal signals that respond to the cell’s metabolic state.

| Enzyme | Primary Activator(s) | Primary Inhibitor(s) | Hormonal Influence |
|--------|----------------------|----------------------|--------------------|
| Hexokinase | ADP, AMP | ATP | Insulin ↑ activity |
| Phosphofructokinase‑1 | F‑2,6‑BP, AMP | ATP, citrate | Glucagon/epinephrine ↑ F‑2,6‑BP |
| Pyruvate kinase | Fructose‑1,6‑bisphosphate | PKA‑phosphorylation | Epinephrine ↓ activity in liver |
| PFK‑2/FBPase‑2 | PKA (phosphorylation) | PP1 (dephosphorylation) | Glucagon ↑ PFK‑2 activity |

## Questions

**Q1.** Which of the following best describes the effect of ATP on hexokinase activity in a cell that has recently completed glycolysis?  
A. Activates hexokinase, increasing glucose phosphorylation  
B. Inhibits hexokinase, decreasing glucose phosphorylation  
C. Has no effect on hexokinase activity  
D. Activates hexokinase only when ATP is below 1 mM  
E. Inhibits hexokinase only in the presence of citrate  

**Answer:** B  
**Explanation:** ATP is a negative allosteric regulator of hexokinase.  When glycolysis has just produced ATP, the high ATP concentration binds to hexokinase and inhibits its activity, preventing further glucose phosphorylation until the cell’s energy demand subsides.

---

**Q2.** In the liver, activation of the β‑adrenergic receptor during exercise leads to increased levels of which metabolite that stimulates phosphofructokinase‑1?  
A. Citrate  
B. AMP  
C. Fructose‑2,6‑bisphosphate  
D. Pyruvate  
E. Glucose‑6‑phosphate  

**Answer:** C  
**Explanation:** β‑adrenergic stimulation activates protein kinase A, which phosphorylates PFK‑2/FBPase‑2, shifting the enzyme toward its PFK‑2 activity and producing fructose‑2,6‑bisphosphate.  F‑2,6‑BP is a potent activator of PFK‑1, thereby enhancing glycolytic flux during exercise.

**Q3.** Which statement best explains why citrate inhibits phosphofructokinase‑1 in the presence of a high ATP concentration?  
A. Citrate competes with ATP for the allosteric site on PFK‑1.  
B. Citrate binds to the catalytic site, preventing substrate binding.  
C. Citrate and ATP bind to distinct sites, causing a conformational change that reduces enzyme activity.  
D. Citrate is phosphorylated by PFK‑1, depleting the enzyme’s active form.  
E. Citrate activates phosphofructokinase‑2, which in turn inhibits PFK‑1.  

**Answer:** C  
**Explanation:** Citrate and ATP bind to separate allosteric sites on PFK‑1.  Their simultaneous binding induces a conformational change that reduces the enzyme’s affinity for its substrate, thereby inhibiting glycolysis when the TCA cycle is saturated.

---

**Q4.** Which hormonal signal is most likely to decrease pyruvate kinase activity in the liver during fasting?  
A. Insulin  
B. Glucagon  
C. Epinephrine  
D. Cortisol  
E. Growth hormone  

**Answer:** B  
**Explanation:** Glucagon activates PKA, which phosphorylates liver pyruvate kinase, rendering it less active.  This inhibition favors gluconeogenesis during fasting, when glucose must be generated rather than consumed.

**Q5.** (Not passage‑based) Which of the following best describes the role of AMP in regulating glycolysis?  
A. It activates hexokinase and inhibits phosphofructokinase‑1.  
B. It inhibits both hexokinase and pyruvate kinase.  
C. It activates phosphofructokinase‑1 and pyruvate kinase.  
D. It has no effect on glycolysis but activates gluconeogenesis.  
E. It inhibits phosphofructokinase‑2, lowering fructose‑2,6‑bisphosphate levels.  

**Answer:** C  
**Explanation:** AMP, an indicator of low cellular energy, activates phosphofructokinase‑1 (by relieving ATP inhibition) and pyruvate kinase, thereby accelerating glycolysis to restore ATP levels.
