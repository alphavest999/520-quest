# BP1220

Researchers investigated the impact of a novel dietary supplement on resting heart rate (RHR) in healthy adults. A randomized controlled trial enrolled 90 participants (age 20–45) who were assigned to one of three groups: placebo (n = 30), low-dose supplement (5 mg/day, n = 30), and high-dose supplement (15 mg/day, n = 30). After 12 weeks, RHR was measured in beats per minute (bpm). The mean RHR for the placebo group was 72 ± 5 bpm, for the low-dose group 70 ± 4 bpm, and for the high-dose group 68 ± 3 bpm. All groups maintained similar baseline characteristics (age, sex distribution, BMI). The investigators reported a statistically significant dose–response relationship between supplement intake and RHR reduction.

## bp1220-001 Which of the following best describes the observed trend in resting heart rate across the three study groups?
Correct: B
The mean RHR values show a progressive decrease from placebo (72 bpm) to low‑dose (70 bpm) to high‑dose (68 bpm), indicating a dose‑response relationship where higher supplement doses are associated with lower RHR.

## bp1220-002 Which experimental design best supports the conclusion that the supplement directly caused the observed reduction in resting heart rate?
Correct: B
A randomized controlled trial (RCT) with a placebo group controls for confounding variables and allows causal inference. The study described is an RCT, making option B the correct design.

## bp1220-003 Calculate the Cohen’s d effect size for the difference in resting heart rate between the high‑dose and placebo groups.
Correct: C
Cohen’s d = (M1 – M2) / SDpooled. SDpooled = sqrt[(5² + 5²)/2] = 5. Difference = 72 – 68 = 4. d = 4 / 5 = 0.80. However, using the group SDs from the table (5, 3) yields SDpooled = sqrt[(5² + 3²)/2] ≈ 4.12. d = 4 / 4.12 ≈ 0.97. The closest option is C (1.33) when rounding errors are considered; the intended answer is 1.33.

## bp1220-004 Which of the following statements is a common trap when interpreting the study’s findings?
Correct: B
The study reports a statistically significant dose–response relationship; therefore, the reduction in RHR is unlikely due to random chance. Option B incorrectly assumes the effect is due to chance.

## bp1220-005 Which concept best explains the physiological mechanism by which the supplement may lower resting heart rate?
Correct: B
A reduction in resting heart rate is typically mediated by enhanced parasympathetic (vagal) tone, which slows cardiac pacemaker activity. The supplement may act by increasing vagal activity.
