# BP974

Researchers studied the effect of a novel dietary supplement on fasting glucose levels in adults with prediabetes. A randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial enrolled 120 participants who were assigned to either the supplement (n = 60) or placebo (n = 60) for 12 weeks. Fasting glucose was measured at baseline and at week 12. The supplement group showed a mean reduction of 4.2 mg/dL (SD 3.1) while the placebo group had a mean reduction of 1.8 mg/dL (SD 2.9). The investigators reported a statistically significant difference between groups (p < 0.01). A secondary outcome was the change in HbA1c, which decreased by 0.3% in the supplement group versus 0.1% in the placebo group. Adverse events were mild and comparable between groups. The authors concluded that the supplement may modestly improve glycemic control in prediabetic adults, but further studies are needed to confirm long‑term benefits and safety.

## bp974-01 Which of the following best describes the primary outcome of this study?
Correct: B
The mean reduction in fasting glucose was 4.2 mg/dL in the supplement group versus 1.8 mg/dL in placebo, indicating a greater decrease with the supplement.

## bp974-02 Which experimental design element best ensures that the observed effect is attributable to the supplement?
Correct: B
Random assignment balances known and unknown confounders between groups, attributing differences to the intervention.

## bp974-03 Calculate the absolute risk reduction (ARR) for fasting glucose improvement if 30% of the supplement group improved versus 15% of the placebo group.
Correct: A
ARR = 30% – 15% = 15% = 0.15.

## bp974-04 Which of the following statements is a common misinterpretation of the study’s findings?
Correct: A
A 4.2 mg/dL reduction is modest; labeling it clinically significant overstates the benefit.

## bp974-05 Which concept best explains why the study’s findings may not be generalizable to all adults with prediabetes?
Correct: A
Volunteer participants may differ from the broader prediabetic population, limiting external validity.
