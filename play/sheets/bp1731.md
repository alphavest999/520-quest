# BP1731

Researchers studied the effect of varying glucose concentrations on the rate of glycolysis in isolated yeast cells. The experiment involved incubating equal volumes of yeast suspension in buffers containing 0.1 M, 0.5 M, 1.0 M, and 2.0 M glucose, respectively, at 30 °C for 10 minutes. After incubation, the amount of pyruvate produced was quantified using a spectrophotometric assay at 340 nm. The data revealed a sigmoidal relationship between glucose concentration and pyruvate production, suggesting cooperative enzyme kinetics. The authors proposed that the observed plateau at higher glucose levels indicates saturation of hexokinase activity. They also noted that the initial rate of pyruvate formation increased by approximately 2.5-fold when glucose concentration rose from 0.1 M to 0.5 M, but only by 1.2-fold when increased from 1.0 M to 2.0 M. The study concluded that yeast glycolytic flux is tightly regulated by substrate availability and enzyme cooperativity, and that these findings have implications for industrial fermentation processes where glucose concentrations vary widely.
