# BP1305

Researchers studied the effect of a novel dietary supplement on the endurance performance of trained cyclists. A randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial was conducted with 80 participants divided equally into two groups: Supplement (S) and Placebo (P). Over a 12‑week intervention period, participants performed a standardized 30‑minute time trial on a stationary bike at the start and end of the study. Performance was measured by total distance covered. The study also recorded heart rate recovery and lactate concentration at 5 minutes post‑exercise. Table 1 summarizes the key outcome measures. The researchers concluded that the supplement significantly improved endurance, as evidenced by a 7.5% increase in distance and a 12% faster heart rate recovery compared to placebo. However, lactate levels did not differ significantly between groups, suggesting the supplement’s effect may be mediated through cardiovascular efficiency rather than metabolic pathways.

## bp1305-01 Based on the data presented in Table 1, which of the following statements best describes the effect of the supplement on the cyclists’ endurance performance?
Correct: B
The table shows a 7.5% increase in distance (3200 vs. 2950 m) and a faster heart rate recovery (15 vs. 17 bpm/min) in the supplement group, while lactate concentrations are essentially unchanged (4.2 vs. 4.3 mmol/L). Therefore, option B accurately reflects the findings.

## bp1305-02 If you were to design a follow‑up study to investigate whether the supplement’s effect on endurance is mediated by improved cardiovascular efficiency, which of the following experimental designs would be most appropriate?
Correct: A
A crossover design controls for inter‑subject variability by having each participant serve as their own control, allowing precise assessment of the supplement’s effect on cardiovascular parameters. The washout period minimizes carry‑over effects.

## bp1305-03 Using the data from Table 1, calculate the percent change in heart rate recovery from placebo to supplement group.
Correct: A
Percent change = ((Placebo – Supplement) / Placebo) × 100 = ((17 – 15) / 17) × 100 ≈ 11.8%. Option A is the closest to the calculated value.

## bp1305-04 Which of the following statements is a common misinterpretation of the study’s findings?
Correct: A
The data show no significant lactate difference, so attributing the endurance benefit to lactate reduction is a trap. The correct interpretation focuses on cardiovascular efficiency.

## bp1305-05 How does the study’s conclusion about cardiovascular efficiency relate to the broader concept of the lactate threshold in endurance training?
Correct: B
The study found improved endurance and heart rate recovery without changes in lactate, implying that cardiovascular adaptations (e.g., improved oxygen delivery) can raise endurance independent of lactate threshold shifts.
