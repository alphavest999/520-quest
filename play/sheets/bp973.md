# BP973

Researchers investigated the effect of varying doses of a novel drug on plasma concentration in healthy volunteers. Ten participants were randomly assigned to one of three groups: a control group receiving a placebo, a low‑dose group receiving 10 mg, and a high‑dose group receiving 30 mg. Blood samples were collected 2 hours post‑dose and analyzed for drug concentration (µg/mL). The study aimed to assess dose‑dependent pharmacokinetics while minimizing inter‑individual variability through randomization. The results are summarized in Table 1 below. The researchers plan to use these findings to inform dose‑finding studies in patients with the target disease.

Condition | N | Mean (µg/mL) | SD
Control | 10 | 5.2 | 0.8
Low Dose | 10 | 7.5 | 1.1
High Dose | 10 | 12.3 | 1.4

## bp973-1 Which experimental condition shows the greatest increase in mean plasma concentration relative to the control group?
Correct: C
The high‑dose group has a mean concentration of 12.3 µg/mL, which is higher than the control mean of 5.2 µg/mL, representing the largest absolute increase among the groups.

## bp973-2 Which experimental design best controls for inter‑individual variability in this study?
Correct: B
A crossover design allows each participant to receive multiple treatments in a randomized order, thereby controlling for inter‑individual differences by using participants as their own controls.

## bp973-3 Calculate the percent increase in mean plasma concentration from the control group to the high‑dose group.
Correct: B
Percent increase = ((12.3 – 5.2) / 5.2) × 100 ≈ 136.5%, which is closest to 150% among the given options.

## bp973-4 Which of the following statements is incorrect regarding the study design?
Correct: C
A sample size of 10 is generally considered small; it may not reliably satisfy the central limit theorem for normality, especially with potential outliers.

## bp973-5 Which statistical concept is most directly relevant to interpreting the study’s findings?
Correct: C
Hypothesis testing is used to determine whether the observed differences in mean concentrations between groups are statistically significant.
