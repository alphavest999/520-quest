# BP1069

Researchers studied the effect of two novel cognitive training programs on reaction time in adults aged 20–35. Participants were randomly assigned to one of four groups: a control group receiving no training, Treatment A (visual‑spatial drills), Treatment B (verbal memory tasks), or a combined group that performed both treatments in sequence. Over a 4‑week period, participants completed a computerized simple reaction time task at baseline, mid‑intervention, and post‑intervention. Mean reaction times (in milliseconds) and standard deviations were recorded at each assessment point. The table below summarizes the post‑intervention results for the four groups. The researchers hypothesized that the combined training would produce the greatest reduction in reaction time, reflecting synergistic effects of multimodal practice. Statistical analysis included one‑way ANOVA followed by post‑hoc Tukey tests to compare group means. Findings suggested a significant difference between the combined group and the single‑treatment groups, but not between the two single‑treatment groups. The study contributes to the literature on cognitive enhancement and informs future experimental designs in this domain.

## 1 Which group had the lowest mean reaction time?
Correct: D
The combined group had a mean of 320 ms, the lowest among all groups.

## 2 Which experimental design best tests whether the combined treatment is more effective than the single treatments?
Correct: A
A randomized controlled trial with separate groups isolates the effect of each treatment and the combination, allowing direct comparison.

## 3 If the combined group mean is 320 ms with SD = 40 ms and n = 30, what is the 95% confidence interval for the mean?
Correct: C
SE = 40/√30 ≈ 7.3; 95% CI = 320 ± 1.96×7.3 ≈ 320 ± 14.3 → 305.7–334.3, which rounds to 308–332.

## 4 Which of the following is NOT a potential source of bias in this study?
Correct: D
Randomization reduces bias; it is not a source of bias itself.

## 5 Which concept links this study to the broader field of cognitive psychology?
Correct: A
Reaction time improvements are often interpreted as enhanced working memory capacity, a core concept in cognitive psychology.
