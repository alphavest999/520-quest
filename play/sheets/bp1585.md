# BP1585

Researchers studied the effect of a novel antihypertensive drug on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults with mild hypertension. A randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial enrolled 200 participants (100 per arm). Baseline SBP was measured after a 5‑minute seated rest. Participants were assigned to either the drug (treatment) or a matching placebo (control). SBP was recorded at baseline, after 1 week, and after 4 weeks. Table 1 summarizes the mean SBP (± SD) for each group. The treatment group showed a progressive decline in SBP, whereas the control group remained relatively unchanged. At 4 weeks, the mean SBP in the treatment group was 115 mm Hg (± 10) compared with 130 mm Hg (± 12) in the control group. The study reported a statistically significant difference (p < 0.01) between groups at week 4. The investigators concluded that the drug effectively lowers SBP within 4 weeks of initiation.

## 1 Which group experienced the greater reduction in mean systolic blood pressure from baseline to week 4?
Correct: B
The treatment group’s mean SBP decreased from 130 mm Hg to 115 mm Hg (a 15 mm Hg drop), whereas the control group remained at 130 mm Hg. Thus, the treatment group had the greater reduction.

## 2 What type of study design was employed in this research?
Correct: A
The study randomized participants to treatment or placebo, used blinding, and had a control group, which are hallmarks of a randomized controlled trial.

## 3 What is the percent reduction in mean systolic blood pressure in the treatment group from baseline to week 4?
Correct: D
Percent reduction = [(130 – 115)/130] × 100 = 15.4%, which rounds to 15.0%.

## 4 Which of the following statements is FALSE regarding this study?
Correct: B
While the control group’s mean SBP remained close to baseline, the study did not report a statistically significant change; the statement incorrectly implies a change that was not observed.

## 5 Which concept is most directly linked to the study’s use of a placebo group?
Correct: A
A placebo group allows assessment of the placebo effect, which is the improvement in symptoms due to expectation rather than the active treatment.
