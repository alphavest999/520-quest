# BP1089

Researchers at the Cognitive Neuroscience Institute conducted a study to examine the effect of sleep deprivation on working memory performance. Participants were randomly assigned to one of four groups: (1) 8 hours of sleep, (2) 4 hours of sleep, (3) 8 hours of sleep with caffeine, and (4) 4 hours of sleep with caffeine. All participants completed the same 2-back task, and reaction times (RT) were recorded. The study aimed to determine whether caffeine could mitigate the adverse effects of sleep loss. Data were collected over a single night, and participants were tested in the morning. The researchers controlled for age, gender, and baseline cognitive ability by matching participants across groups. The results indicated a clear trend: participants who received adequate sleep performed faster than those who were sleep deprived, and caffeine improved performance only in the sleep-deprived groups. The study’s findings suggest that while caffeine can partially offset the impact of sleep loss, it does not fully restore working memory performance to the level of well-rested individuals. Future research should investigate longer-term effects and potential interactions with other stimulants.

## bp1089_q1 According to the table, which group had the fastest mean reaction time?
Correct: C
The mean reaction time for the 8h sleep + caffeine group is 410 ms, which is lower (faster) than the other groups. The 8h sleep group has 420 ms, 4h sleep 520 ms, and 4h sleep + caffeine 470 ms.

## bp1089_q2 Which of the following best describes how the researchers controlled for confounding variables in this study?
Correct: B
The passage states that participants were matched across groups for age, gender, and baseline cognitive ability, which controls for these potential confounders. The study was not double‑blind, participants did not choose their sleep duration, and there was no restriction to caffeine users.

## bp1089_q3 What is the percent difference in mean reaction time between the 4h sleep group and the 4h sleep + caffeine group?
Correct: A
Percent difference = (520 - 470) / 520 × 100% = 50 / 520 × 100% ≈ 9.6%. The other options are larger than the correct value.

## bp1089_q4 Which of the following statements is NOT supported by the data presented in the passage?
Correct: B
The fastest mean reaction time is observed in the 8h sleep + caffeine group (410 ms), not the 8h sleep group alone (420 ms). Thus, adequate sleep alone does not produce the fastest times. The other statements are directly supported by the passage.

## bp1089_q5 The findings of this study most directly relate to which broader concept in cognitive psychology?
Correct: B
The study examines how sleep deprivation and caffeine affect working memory performance, which involves attentional resources. It does not address long‑term memory, socioeconomic status, or procedural memory.
