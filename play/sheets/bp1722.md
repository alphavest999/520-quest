# BP1722

Researchers at the University of Midvale conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive drug, Hypotensol, in adults with stage 1 hypertension. The study enrolled 200 participants who were randomly assigned to receive either Hypotensol or a placebo for 12 weeks. Baseline systolic blood pressure (SBP) was measured after a 5‑minute seated rest. After the treatment period, SBP was reassessed using the same protocol. The investigators collected demographic data, medication adherence, and adverse events. They reported that the mean SBP reduction in the Hypotensol group was 12 mmHg (SD 4.5), compared to 2 mmHg (SD 3.8) in the placebo group, yielding a statistically significant difference (p < 0.001). Subgroup analysis suggested greater benefit in patients with baseline SBP > 150 mmHg. The study concluded that Hypotensol effectively lowers SBP in mild‑to‑moderate hypertensive adults and has a favorable safety profile.

## q2 Which of the following control conditions would best isolate the pharmacologic effect of Hypotensol?
Correct: B
A placebo control allows for blinding and accounts for the placebo effect, thereby isolating the drug’s pharmacologic effect. A no‑treatment group would not control for placebo response, and an active comparator would test relative efficacy rather than absolute effect. Historical controls lack randomization and blinding.
