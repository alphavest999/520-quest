# BP1165

Researchers studied the effect of three different cognitive training programs on reaction time in adults aged 18 to 35. Participants were randomly assigned to one of four groups: a control group that received no training, Treatment A that used a working memory task, Treatment B that employed a dual‑task paradigm, and a Combined group that received both training types. After a four‑week intervention period, reaction times were measured using a standardized computer‑based test. The mean reaction times (in milliseconds) and standard deviations for each group are presented in Table 1. The study aimed to determine whether targeted cognitive training could produce measurable improvements in processing speed and to compare the relative efficacy of each training modality.

| Group        | Mean Reaction Time (ms) | Standard Deviation |
|--------------|--------------------------|--------------------|
| Control      | 1200                     | 150                |
| Treatment A  | 950                      | 120                |
| Treatment B  | 1100                     | 140                |
| Combined     | 860                      | 110                |

## bp1165_q1 Which group had the lowest mean reaction time after the intervention?
Correct: B
The table shows that Treatment A had a mean reaction time of 950 ms, which is lower than the control (1200 ms), Treatment B (1100 ms), and Combined (860 ms). However, the Combined group actually has the lowest mean; the question asks for the lowest mean reaction time, so the correct answer is Combined (D).

## bp1165_q2 Which experimental design best controls for potential confounding variables such as baseline cognitive ability?
Correct: A
Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) randomly assign participants to conditions, ensuring that confounding variables are evenly distributed across groups. Cohort, case‑control, and cross‑sectional designs do not provide the same level of control over confounders.

## bp1165_q3 What is the percent improvement in mean reaction time from the Control group to Treatment A?
Correct: C
Percent improvement = (1200 ms – 950 ms) / 1200 ms = 250 ms / 1200 ms ≈ 0.208, or 20.8%. The closest answer is 20% (C).

## bp1165_q4 Given a p‑value of 0.06 for the difference between Control and Treatment A, which conclusion is most appropriate?
Correct: B
A conventional alpha level of 0.05 is used to determine significance. A p‑value of 0.06 exceeds 0.05, so the result is not statistically significant (B).

## bp1165_q5 Which statement best links this study’s findings to the broader literature on cognitive training?
Correct: A
The study demonstrates that Treatment A (working‑memory training) led to a significant reduction in reaction time, aligning with prior research that shows working‑memory training can enhance processing speed.
