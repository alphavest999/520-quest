# BP1286

Researchers conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive drug (Drug A) compared to an existing therapy (Drug B) and a placebo. One hundred and twenty hypertensive adults were enrolled and randomly assigned to one of three groups (n=40 per group). Baseline systolic blood pressure (SBP) was measured for all participants. After a 4‑week treatment period, SBP was re‑measured. The study aimed to determine whether Drug A could produce a clinically significant reduction in SBP relative to Drug B and placebo while maintaining safety. The investigators collected additional data on heart rate, adverse events, and medication adherence, but these variables are not shown in the table below. The authors reported that the mean SBP decreased in all groups, with the largest mean reduction observed in the Drug A group. They concluded that Drug A was superior to both Drug B and placebo in lowering SBP over the short term. The study’s limitations included a short follow‑up period and lack of blinding for the participants.

Group | n | Mean SBP Baseline (mmHg) | Mean SBP After 4 Weeks (mmHg)
-------|---|--------------------------|----------------------------
Drug A | 40 | 158 | 132
Drug B | 40 | 156 | 140
Placebo | 40 | 157 | 149

## bp1286_q1 Which group experienced the greatest mean reduction in systolic blood pressure after 4 weeks?
Correct: A
Subtracting the 4‑week mean from the baseline mean gives reductions of 26 mmHg for Drug A, 16 mmHg for Drug B, and 8 mmHg for Placebo. Therefore Drug A had the greatest reduction.

## bp1286_q2 Which experimental design feature best controls for potential confounding variables in this study?
Correct: A
Random assignment distributes both known and unknown confounders evenly across groups, thereby controlling for them. Baseline measurement, treatment duration, and reporting are important but do not control confounding.

## bp1286_q3 What is the percent reduction in mean SBP for Drug A over the 4‑week period?
Correct: C
Percent reduction = ((158‑132)/158) × 100 ≈ 25.8%. The other options are incorrect calculations.

## bp1286_q4 Which of the following conclusions is not supported by the data presented?
Correct: B
Drug B’s reduction (16 mmHg) is smaller than placebo’s (8 mmHg), so it is not more effective than placebo. The other statements are supported by the table.

## bp1286_q5 Which of the following would be the most logical next step to build on these findings?
Correct: A
A longer, double‑blind trial would address the current study’s limitations (short follow‑up and lack of blinding) and provide stronger evidence for sustained benefit and safety.
