Researchers at the State University conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the impact of four distinct instructional approaches on high‑school students’ mathematics achievement. The study enrolled 240 students, randomly assigned to one of four groups: Traditional lecture (T), Interactive problem‑solving (I), Flipped classroom (F), and Hybrid (H). Each group received a 12‑week intervention, after which students completed a standardized math assessment. Table 1 summarizes the key outcome metrics. The researchers reported mean scores and standard deviations for each instructional method, noting that the Interactive group achieved the highest average score, followed by the Flipped, Hybrid, and Traditional groups. The authors concluded that incorporating interactive problem‑solving strategies significantly enhances mathematical performance, especially when combined with flipped classroom elements. They suggested that future curriculum designs should prioritize student engagement and active learning to close achievement gaps.
