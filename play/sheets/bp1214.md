# BP1214

Researchers studied the effect of a novel insulin analog on postprandial glucose levels in 120 adults with type 2 diabetes. Participants were randomized to receive either the analog or a standard insulin formulation. Blood samples were collected at baseline and at 0.5, 1, 2, and 3 hours after a standardized mixed‑meal challenge. The primary endpoint was the mean glucose concentration over the 3‑hour period. Secondary endpoints included insulin pharmacokinetics and safety outcomes. Table 1 summarizes the glucose and insulin concentrations observed in the two treatment groups. The analog group demonstrated a modest but statistically significant reduction in mean glucose levels compared with the standard insulin group, particularly at the 2‑hour mark. No serious adverse events were reported. The study suggests that the analog may improve postprandial glycemic control without increasing hypoglycemia risk.

## bp1214-1 Based on Table 1, what trend is observed in glucose levels over the 3‑hour period for the insulin analog group?
Correct: C
The table shows glucose rising from 180 mg/dL at 0 h to 210 mg/dL at 0.5 h, peaking at 210 mg/dL, then dropping to 190 mg/dL at 1 h, 165 mg/dL at 2 h, and 150 mg/dL at 3 h. This pattern indicates a peak at 0.5–1 hour followed by a decline.
