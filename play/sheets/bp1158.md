# BP1158

Researchers studied the effect of varying concentrations of a novel antioxidant compound on the oxidative stress levels in cultured human liver cells. The experiment was conducted over a 48‑hour period with five treatment groups: 0 µM (control), 10 µM, 25 µM, 50 µM, and 100 µM. Oxidative stress was measured by the fluorescence intensity of dichlorofluorescein (DCF) at 488 nm, expressed as relative fluorescence units (RFU). Table 1 summarizes the mean RFU values and standard deviations for each group. The researchers hypothesized that higher concentrations would reduce oxidative stress, reflected by lower RFU values. However, preliminary analysis suggested a biphasic response, with 25 µM showing the greatest reduction, while 50 µM and 100 µM did not differ significantly from the control. The data were plotted on a dose‑response curve to assess the optimal concentration.

Concentration (µM) | Mean RFU | SD | n
0 | 1200 | 50 | 6
10 | 950 | 45 | 6
25 | 650 | 40 | 6
50 | 1100 | 55 | 6
100 | 1150 | 60 | 6

## 1 Which concentration group had the lowest mean RFU?
Correct: C
The lowest mean RFU value (650) occurs at the 25 µM concentration, indicating the greatest reduction in oxidative stress.

## 2 Which of the following best describes the experimental design used in this study?
Correct: C
The study systematically varied the dose of the antioxidant and measured the response, characteristic of a dose‑response experiment.

## 3 What percent reduction in mean RFU does the 25 µM group achieve relative to the control?
Correct: C
Percent reduction = (1200 – 650) / 1200 × 100 ≈ 45.8%, rounded to 45%.

## 4 Which of the following statements is NOT supported by the data?
Correct: B
The 50 µM mean RFU (1100) is not significantly different from the control (1200), so it does not reduce oxidative stress more than control.

## 5 The observed biphasic response in this study is most similar to which pharmacological phenomenon?
Correct: A
A biphasic response, where an intermediate dose shows the greatest effect and higher doses lose efficacy, exemplifies a non‑linear dose‑response relationship.
