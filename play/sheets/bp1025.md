Researchers conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive agent, Hypertensol, in adults with stage 1 hypertension. One hundred thirty‑two participants were randomly assigned to receive either Hypertensol (n=66) or a matching placebo (n=66) for eight weeks. Baseline systolic and diastolic blood pressures were measured after a 24‑hour ambulatory monitoring period. At the end of the study, mean changes in blood pressure were recorded. The investigators also collected data on heart rate, serum potassium, and adverse events. A 3‑column table summarizes key outcomes: baseline values, mean change after 8 weeks, and the proportion of participants achieving target blood pressure (<140/90 mm Hg). The results show that Hypertensol produced a greater mean systolic reduction (−12.4 mm Hg) compared with placebo (−3.1 mm Hg). Diastolic pressure decreased by −7.8 mm Hg in the treatment group versus −2.0 mm Hg in the control group. The proportion of participants reaching the target was 58 % for Hypertensol and 21 % for placebo. Adverse events were mild and comparable between groups.
