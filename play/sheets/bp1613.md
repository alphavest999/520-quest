# BP1613

Researchers studied the effect of a novel cognitive training program on working memory performance in adults aged 25-45. Participants were randomly assigned to either the training group or an active control group that engaged in general knowledge quizzes. Over eight weeks, both groups completed 20 sessions of 30 minutes each. Working memory was assessed pre- and post-intervention using the n-back task (0-back, 1-back, 2-back). Table 1 summarizes the mean accuracy and reaction time for each group across the three n-back levels. The training group showed a statistically significant improvement in 2-back accuracy (mean increase of 12.4%) and a reduction in reaction time (mean decrease of 45 ms) compared to the control group. However, no significant differences were observed at the 0-back or 1-back levels. The authors suggest that the training specifically enhances high-load working memory processes. They also discuss potential neural mechanisms, including increased prefrontal cortex activation, and acknowledge limitations such as the short follow-up period and the reliance on a single cognitive measure.

## bp1613_q2 Which experimental design best controls for placebo effects in this study?
Correct: A
Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) randomly assign participants to conditions, minimizing selection bias and placebo effects. A cross-over design could also control for placebo but is not indicated here. Observational and case-control designs are not experimental.

## bp1613_q4 Which of the following is a potential confounding factor that could have influenced the observed results?
Correct: A
Participants were aware of their group assignment, which could introduce expectancy effects. Random assignment and an active control help mitigate other biases, and a large sample size would reduce random error.
