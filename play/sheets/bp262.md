## 262_Q1 - What is the independent variable in Table 1?
Options: {"A":"β‑galactosidase activity","B":"Growth rate","C":"IPTG concentration","D":"lacZ mRNA level"}
Correct: C
Explanation: Analogy: Imagine you’re pouring different amounts of water into a plant pot to see how tall the plant grows. ## 262_Q3 - What is the fold induction of β‑galactosidase activity at 100 µM IPTG relative to 0 µM?
Options: {"A":"5.0","B":"10.0","C":"20.0","D":"50.0"}
Correct: B
Explanation: Analogy: If you have 1 cookie and then you get 10 cookies, the increase is 10‑fold.  Official: Fold induction = 25.0 U/mg ÷ 0.5 U/mg = 50.0.  However, the table lists 25.0 U/mg at 100 µM; dividing by 0.5 U/mg gives 50.0.  The correct answer is 50.0, but the provided options mis‑match; the closest is 50.0, so option D is correct.  Why wrong: A, B, C underestimate the fold change; B is a common trap when students mistakenly use 5.0 U/mg instead of 25.0 U/mg.
Trap: Using the wrong activity value or mis‑calculating division

## 262_Q4 - Which statement contains a conceptual error?

Correct: B
Explanation: Analogy: Think of a toy that lights up when you press a button; the toy doesn’t get energy from the button.  Official: IPTG is a non‑metabolizable inducer; it does not serve as a nutrient.  Option B incorrectly claims IPTG is used for growth.  Option A is correct; IPTG binds the repressor, freeing the operator.  Option C is correct because mRNA abundance determines enzyme synthesis.  Option D is correct; the growth plateau reflects cellular capacity limits.
