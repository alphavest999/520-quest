# BP1163

Researchers at the University of Greenfield investigated the effect of a novel dietary supplement, NutriBoost, on resting metabolic rate (RMR) in adults aged 25–45. The study enrolled 120 participants who were randomly assigned to either the NutriBoost group (n = 60) or a placebo group (n = 60). Over a 12‑week intervention, participants consumed the assigned supplement daily while maintaining their usual diet and physical activity. RMR was measured at baseline and at the end of the study using indirect calorimetry. The researchers hypothesized that NutriBoost would increase RMR by at least 5% compared to placebo. In addition to RMR, the study collected data on body composition, blood lipid profiles, and subjective appetite ratings. The results indicated a modest but statistically significant increase in RMR in the NutriBoost group relative to placebo. The authors concluded that NutriBoost may modestly enhance energy expenditure, potentially contributing to weight management strategies.
