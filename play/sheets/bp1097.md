# BP1097

Researchers studied the effect of three novel therapeutic agents on the reduction of a biomarker associated with chronic inflammation. In a randomized controlled trial, 90 participants were assigned equally to one of three treatment groups (A, B, or C). Over a 12‑week period, serum biomarker levels were measured at baseline and at the end of the study. The mean change from baseline (units) and standard deviations (SD) were reported as follows: Treatment A showed a mean reduction of 12.4 units (SD = 1.2) with 30 participants; Treatment B exhibited a mean reduction of 15.6 units (SD = 1.5) with 30 participants; and Treatment C displayed a mean reduction of 9.8 units (SD = 0.9) with 30 participants. The study concluded that Treatment B produced the greatest biomarker reduction, suggesting it may be the most effective option for patients with elevated inflammatory markers.

| A         | 12.4                   | 1.2 | 30 |
| B         | 15.6                   | 1.5 | 30 |
| C         | 9.8                    | 0.9 | 30 |

Correct: B
