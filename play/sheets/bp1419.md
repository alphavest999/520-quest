# BP1419

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel antihypertensive drug, CardioX, on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults with mild hypertension. A randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial enrolled 120 participants (age 35–65) who had baseline SBP between 140–159 mmHg. Participants were assigned to either CardioX (n = 60) or placebo (n = 60). Over 12 weeks, SBP was measured weekly. The mean SBP reduction in the CardioX group was 12.5 mmHg (SD 4.2), whereas the placebo group showed a mean reduction of 3.1 mmHg (SD 3.8). The difference between groups was statistically significant (p < 0.001). Secondary outcomes included heart rate, serum potassium, and adverse events; no significant differences were observed except a slight increase in dizziness in the CardioX group (5% vs 1%). The study concluded that CardioX effectively lowers SBP with a favorable safety profile.

## bp1419-2 Which of the following experimental designs would best test whether CardioX’s effect on SBP is dose‑dependent?
Correct: B
A parallel‑group design with multiple dosage levels and a placebo arm allows direct comparison of dose‑response while controlling for confounding variables.

## bp1419-3 If the standard error of the mean (SEM) for the CardioX group’s SBP change is 0.54 mmHg, what is the 95% confidence interval for the mean reduction?
Correct: A
SEM = SD/√n = 4.2/√60 ≈ 0.54. 95% CI = mean ± 1.96*SEM ≈ -12.5 ± 1.06 mmHg.

## bp1419-4 Which of the following is a potential trap when interpreting the study’s results?
Correct: A
A 12.5 mmHg reduction is clinically meaningful, but the baseline SBP range (140–159 mmHg) must be considered; a smaller reduction might still be significant for some patients.
