# BP1429

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel cognitive training program on working memory performance in adults aged 25–35. Participants were randomly assigned to one of three groups: (1) the training group received 30 minutes of adaptive working‑memory tasks daily for four weeks; (2) an active control group completed 30 minutes of non‑adaptive memory tasks; and (3) a passive control group received no intervention. Pre‑ and post‑tests measured reaction times (RT) on a digit‑span task. The study aimed to determine whether the adaptive training would produce measurable improvements in RT compared to controls and to assess the magnitude of any effect. Data were collected by a double‑blind protocol, and participants were blinded to group assignment. The researchers hypothesized that the training group would show a statistically significant reduction in mean RT relative to both control groups, with the active control exhibiting a modest improvement due to general task engagement. The study’s implications could inform educational and clinical interventions targeting working‑memory deficits.

Correct: A
Random assignment distributes individual differences evenly across groups, reducing confounding. The passive control alone would not control for placebo or engagement effects, and single‑time‑point data would not allow pre‑post comparisons.
