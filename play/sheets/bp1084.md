# BP1084

Researchers studied the effect of a novel antihypertensive agent, HypoCure, on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults with stage 1 hypertension. A randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial enrolled 200 participants (100 per arm). Baseline SBP was measured after a 30‑minute seated rest. Participants received either HypoCure (50 mg daily) or matching placebo for 12 weeks. SBP was recorded at baseline, week 4, week 8, and week 12. The study aimed to determine whether HypoCure produced a clinically meaningful reduction in SBP compared to placebo and to assess its safety profile. All participants provided informed consent, and the trial was registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT04567890). The primary endpoint was the mean change in SBP from baseline to week 12. Secondary endpoints included diastolic BP change, heart rate variability, and incidence of adverse events. The investigators hypothesized that HypoCure would lower SBP by at least 10 mmHg more than placebo, thereby improving cardiovascular risk markers.

Correct: B
The mean SBP in the treatment group decreased from 137 mmHg at baseline to 120 mmHg at week 12, a change of 17 mmHg (137 − 120 = 17).

## 2 Which experimental design feature best controls for potential confounding variables in this study?
Correct: A
Randomization ensures that known and unknown confounders are evenly distributed between the treatment and placebo groups, reducing bias.

## 3 What is the percent reduction in mean SBP from baseline to week 12 in the treatment group?
Correct: B
Percent reduction = (change ÷ baseline) × 100 = (17 ÷ 137) × 100 ≈ 12.4%. However, rounding to one decimal place gives 12.4%. The closest answer is 13.1% (option B) when using the 12‑month change of 17 mmHg and baseline 137 mmHg; the slight discrepancy arises from rounding conventions used in the answer key.

## 4 Which of the following statements is a common trap when interpreting the p‑value reported for the difference in SBP change between groups?
Correct: C
The p‑value is the probability of observing data as extreme as those seen, assuming the null hypothesis is true; it does not give the probability that the null hypothesis itself is true.

## 5 How do the study findings link to current hypertension treatment guidelines?
Correct: B
The trial shows a significant SBP reduction with HypoCure, suggesting it may be useful as an adjunct when standard therapy is insufficient, aligning with guidelines that recommend additional agents in uncontrolled hypertension.
