# BP1272

Researchers at the Cognitive Neuroscience Institute conducted a controlled study to examine the impact of acute sleep deprivation on cognitive performance. Forty healthy adults (aged 20–35) were randomly assigned to either a sleep-deprived group (restricted to 4 hours of sleep) or a normal sleep group (8 hours). Over a 48‑hour period, participants completed a battery of tasks measuring reaction time, working memory, and sustained attention. The researchers collected objective data via a computerized test platform and subjective fatigue ratings using a 10‑point Likert scale. Results indicated that the sleep‑deprived group exhibited significantly slower reaction times (average 650 ms vs. 520 ms) and lower accuracy on working memory tasks (68 % vs. 82 %). Subjective fatigue scores were also higher in the deprived group (8.2 vs. 3.4). The study concluded that even a single night of reduced sleep can impair both speed and accuracy of cognitive processes, suggesting that sleep is critical for optimal brain function.
