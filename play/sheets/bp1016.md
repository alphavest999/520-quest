# BP1016

Researchers studied the effect of two different instructional methods on students’ reaction times during a cognitive task. Participants were randomly assigned to either the Traditional Lecture (TL) or the Interactive Multimedia (IM) condition. Each participant completed 50 trials of a simple reaction time task while their response times were recorded. The data were summarized in Table 1. The researchers hypothesized that the IM condition would yield faster reaction times due to increased engagement. The study also collected demographic information to ensure groups were comparable. The results were analyzed using descriptive statistics and a two‑sample t‑test to compare mean reaction times between conditions. The findings suggested a statistically significant difference, with the IM group showing a lower mean response time. The authors discussed the implications for instructional design and suggested further research to explore long‑term retention effects.

## bp1016-01 Based on Table 1, which instructional condition had the lowest mean reaction time?
Correct: B
The Interactive Multimedia (IM) condition shows a mean response time of 485 ms, which is lower than all other conditions listed.

## bp1016-02 Which variable was manipulated by the researchers to determine its effect on reaction time?
Correct: B
The researchers varied the instructional method (Traditional Lecture vs. Interactive Multimedia) to assess its impact on reaction times.

## bp1016-03 Calculate the absolute difference in mean reaction time between the Traditional Lecture and Interactive Multimedia conditions.
Correct: A
Mean for TL = 520 ms; mean for IM = 485 ms. Difference = 520 – 485 = 35 ms.

## bp1016-04 Which of the following statements is incorrect regarding the reported results?
Correct: C
The Control group had the highest mean response time (560 ms), not the fastest.

## bp1016-05 Which theoretical framework best explains why Interactive Multimedia may improve reaction times?
Correct: A
Cognitive Load Theory posits that multimedia can reduce extraneous load and enhance learning, which aligns with faster reaction times observed in the IM condition.
