# BP1029

Researchers studied the effect of light intensity on the photosynthetic rate in spinach leaves. The plants were grown under identical conditions and then exposed to four light intensities: 200, 400, 600, and 800 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹. Oxygen evolution was measured over a 10‑minute period. The table below lists the mean oxygen production (µmol O₂ g⁻¹ leaf⁻¹ h⁻¹) and its standard error for each intensity. The data reveal a clear increase in oxygen production with light intensity up to 800 µmol photons, suggesting a saturable response. The researchers noted that beyond 800 µmol photons, the rate plateaued, indicating a potential limitation in the light‑dependent reactions or the Calvin cycle.

Light Intensity (µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹) | Oxygen Production (µmol O₂ g⁻¹ leaf⁻¹ h⁻¹) | Standard Error
200 | 15 | 1.5
400 | 28 | 2.0
600 | 35 | 2.5
800 | 45 | 3.0

## bp1029-1 Which light intensity yielded the highest mean oxygen production?
Correct: D
The table shows the highest oxygen production (45 µmol O₂ g⁻¹ leaf⁻¹ h⁻¹) at 800 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹.

## bp1029-2 Which experimental control would best isolate the effect of light intensity on photosynthesis?
Correct: B
Temperature influences enzyme activity and gas diffusion; keeping it constant ensures that changes in oxygen production are due to light intensity alone.

## bp1029-3 What is the percent increase in mean oxygen production when light intensity rises from 200 to 800 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹?
Correct: A
Percent increase = [(45–15)/15] × 100 % = 200 %. Option A is correct.

## bp1029-4 Which statement reflects a common misconception about light intensity and photosynthesis?
Correct: C
The data show a plateau at 800 µmol photons, proving that beyond a certain point, increasing light does not increase the rate.

## bp1029-5 Which concept best explains the plateau observed at higher light intensities?
Correct: C
The plateau suggests that the Calvin cycle becomes rate‑limiting; once the light reactions produce enough ATP and NADPH, the cycle cannot process them faster.
