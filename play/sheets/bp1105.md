# BP1105

Researchers studied the effect of light intensity on the rate of photosynthesis in spinach leaves. Using a controlled laboratory setup, they measured the rate of oxygen evolution (µmol O₂/mg protein/min) at five different light intensities (lux). The experiment was performed in triplicate to ensure reproducibility. The data collected are summarized in Table 1 below. The researchers hypothesized that increasing light intensity would increase the rate of photosynthesis up to a saturation point, after which the rate would plateau. They also considered potential confounding factors such as temperature and leaf age, which were kept constant throughout the experiment. The results provide insight into how light availability influences plant metabolic activity and can inform agricultural practices in varying light environments.

Table 1: Light Intensity vs. Oxygen Evolution
Light Intensity (lux) | Oxygen Evolution (µmol O₂/mg protein/min)
--- | ---
200 | 12.5
400 | 18.3
600 | 24.1
800 | 27.9
1000 | 28.5

## bp1105_q1 Which light intensity produced the highest rate of oxygen evolution according to Table 1?
Correct: D
The table shows that the oxygen evolution rate increases with light intensity up to 1000 lux, where it reaches 28.5 µmol O₂/mg protein/min, the highest value among the data points.

## bp1105_q2 Which experimental design best isolates the effect of light intensity on photosynthesis rate?
Correct: A
To isolate the effect of light intensity, all other variables that influence photosynthesis (temperature, leaf age, CO₂ concentration) must be held constant. Option A accomplishes this.

## bp1105_q3 What is the percent increase in oxygen evolution when light intensity increases from 200 lux to 800 lux?
Correct: C
Percent increase = ((27.9 - 12.5) / 12.5) * 100 = 115.2%. Rounded to the nearest whole number, the closest option is 132.8% (option C).

## bp1105_q4 Which of the following statements is a common trap when interpreting the data?
Correct: A
The data actually plateau at 1000 lux, indicating that beyond a certain point, increasing light does not increase photosynthesis. Assuming a linear relationship (option B) is also a trap, but option A is the most common misconception.

## bp1105_q5 Which concept is most directly linked to the observed saturation of photosynthetic rate at high light intensities?
Correct: A
The plateau in oxygen evolution at high light intensities is indicative of photoinhibition, where excess light damages the photosynthetic apparatus.
