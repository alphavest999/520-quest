Researchers at the University of Health Sciences conducted a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of the novel antihypertensive agent, Hypotensol, in adults with stage 1 hypertension. Eighty participants aged 35–65 were enrolled and randomly assigned to receive either 10 mg of Hypotensol once daily or a matching placebo for 12 weeks. Baseline systolic (SBP) and diastolic blood pressure (DBP) readings were obtained using an automated cuff after a 5‑minute rest period. At the end of the study, mean SBP and DBP values were compared between groups. Table 1 summarizes the key findings. In addition, researchers recorded adverse events, heart rate, and serum creatinine to assess safety. The investigators hypothesized that Hypotensol would lower SBP by at least 10 mmHg more than placebo, with a corresponding reduction in DBP. The trial was registered under ClinicalTrials.gov identifier NCT12345678. Results indicated a mean SBP reduction of 12.5 mmHg in the treatment group versus 3.2 mmHg in the placebo group, yielding a between‑group difference of 9.3 mmHg (95% CI 6.1–12.5). DBP decreased by 7.8 mmHg in the treatment arm compared to 1.5 mmHg in controls. The drug was well tolerated, with mild headaches reported in 5% of participants.
