# BP1005

Researchers studied the effect of a novel cognitive training program on working memory performance in adults aged 25–35. Participants were randomly assigned to either the training group (n = 30) or a control group that received no intervention (n = 30). The training consisted of 12 sessions of 45 minutes each, delivered over six weeks. Working memory was assessed pre‑ and post‑intervention using the n‑back task (0‑back, 1‑back, 2‑back). The mean reaction time (RT) and accuracy were recorded. Results indicated that the training group showed a significant reduction in RT for the 2‑back condition (mean decrease of 120 ms, p < .01) and a modest improvement in accuracy (mean increase of 4.5%, p = .08). The control group exhibited no meaningful changes. These findings suggest that targeted cognitive training can enhance working memory speed, although accuracy gains were less pronounced. The authors discuss potential neural mechanisms and the importance of task difficulty in maximizing training benefits.

## bp1005-01 Which of the following best describes the primary outcome measured in this study?
Correct: B
The study reports a significant reduction in reaction time (RT) for the 2‑back condition as the main effect of the training program. Accuracy changes were modest and not statistically significant, and baseline RT differences were not the primary outcome.

## bp1005-02 Which experimental design element best supports the conclusion that the training caused the observed improvement?
Correct: A
Random assignment reduces confounding variables, allowing attribution of differences to the training intervention. Other options do not directly address causal inference.

## bp1005-03 What is the approximate percent reduction in mean reaction time for the 2‑back task after training?
Correct: B
Baseline mean RT for 2‑back = 520 ms; post‑intervention = 400 ms. Reduction = 120 ms. Percent reduction = (120/520) × 100 ≈ 23%, closest to 22% (option B).

## bp1005-04 Which of the following statements is a potential trap in interpreting the study’s results?
Correct: D
A p‑value indicates statistical significance but does not prove causality; other factors could influence results. Options A and C are reasonable interpretations, and B is a cautious inference.
