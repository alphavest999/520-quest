Researchers at the Cognitive Neuroscience Institute conducted a study to examine the effect of sleep deprivation on working memory performance. Participants were randomly assigned to one of four conditions: (1) 8 hours of sleep (control), (2) 4 hours of sleep, (3) 2 hours of sleep, and (4) no sleep for 24 hours. Each participant completed a 2-back task while their reaction times and accuracy were recorded. The study aimed to determine whether reduced sleep duration leads to measurable declines in cognitive function. The findings suggest a dose–response relationship: as sleep duration decreases, reaction times increase and accuracy decreases.
