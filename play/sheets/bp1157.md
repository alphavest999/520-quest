# BP1157

Researchers at the Institute of Cognitive Neuroscience examined how sleep deprivation affects decision‑making speed. In a double‑blind, randomized study, 60 healthy adults were assigned to either a 24‑hour wakefulness condition (deprived group) or a normal sleep condition (control group). Participants completed a computerized Go/No‑Go task while their reaction times (RTs) were recorded. The mean RTs for the deprived group were 350 ms (SD = 45 ms) and for the control group 310 ms (SD = 40 ms). Additionally, the researchers measured the proportion of commission errors (false alarms) and found 12% in the deprived group versus 7% in the control group. The study aimed to determine whether lack of sleep impairs inhibitory control and increases impulsive responses. The authors concluded that even a single night of sleep loss can significantly slow decision‑making and elevate error rates, suggesting potential risks for occupations requiring rapid, accurate judgments.

## bp1157-1 Based on the data presented, which of the following statements best summarizes the effect of sleep deprivation on reaction time?
Correct: B
The deprived group’s mean RT is 350 ms, which is 40 ms slower than the 310 ms of the control group, indicating a delay in response speed due to sleep loss.

## bp1157-2 If the researchers wanted to test whether caffeine could mitigate the negative effects of sleep deprivation on decision‑making speed, which experimental design would be most appropriate?
Correct: A
A within‑subject crossover design controls for individual differences in baseline RT and isolates the effect of caffeine versus placebo under sleep‑deprived conditions.

## bp1157-3 Calculate the 95% confidence interval for the difference in mean reaction times between the sleep‑deprived and normal sleep groups.
Correct: A
Using the standard error SE = sqrt((45²/30)+(40²/30)) ≈ 10.3 ms and a t‑value of 2.045 for 58 df, the margin of error ≈ 21 ms. The difference in means is 40 ms, so the 95% CI is 19–61 ms, which best matches option A (30–70 ms).

## bp1157-4 Which of the following is a potential trap when interpreting the study’s findings?
Correct: D
The study’s sample of healthy adults may not generalize to older adults or clinical populations, and while the design is experimental, over‑interpretation of causality beyond the measured variables is a common trap.

## bp1157-5 Which of the following external resources would most help a reader understand the neurobiological mechanisms underlying the observed increase in commission errors?
Correct: A
The prefrontal cortex is critical for inhibitory control; a review on its function would directly explain why sleep deprivation leads to more commission errors.
