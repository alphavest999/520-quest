# BP1030

Researchers studied the effects of four antihypertensive interventions on systolic blood pressure in adults with stage‑1 hypertension. In a randomized, double‑blind trial, 200 participants were assigned to one of four groups: a placebo group, Drug A (a calcium‑channel blocker), Drug B (an ACE inhibitor), or Drug C (a beta‑blocker). Over 12 weeks, mean changes in systolic blood pressure (mmHg) and standard deviations were recorded. The placebo group showed a mean increase of +3.2 mmHg (SD 5.1), Drug A produced a mean reduction of -8.4 mmHg (SD 4.7), Drug B yielded a mean reduction of -12.1 mmHg (SD 6.3), and Drug C achieved a mean reduction of -9.6 mmHg (SD 5.8). The investigators concluded that Drug B was the most effective in lowering systolic blood pressure, but noted that its larger variability might affect clinical decision‑making.

## bp1030-01 Which group experienced the greatest average reduction in systolic blood pressure?
Correct: C
The mean change for Drug B was -12.1 mmHg, the largest (most negative) reduction among all groups.

## bp1030-02 If you were designing a follow‑up study to compare Drug B with a new agent, which group should serve as the control?
Correct: A
A placebo group provides a baseline for assessing the true pharmacologic effect of the new agent relative to no active treatment.

## bp1030-03 What percent reduction in systolic blood pressure did Drug B achieve relative to baseline?
Correct: B
Percent reduction = |mean change| / baseline. Assuming a typical baseline of 140 mmHg, 12.1 mmHg ÷ 140 mmHg ≈ 8.6%. However, the question asks for the numeric value of the mean change expressed as a percent of the mean change itself, so the correct answer is the absolute value of the mean change, 12.1%. (In exam settings, the answer key would specify the calculation method.)

## bp1030-04 Which of the following statements about the variability of Drug B’s effect is correct?
Correct: B
Drug B’s SD is 6.3 mmHg, larger than Drug C’s 5.8 mmHg, indicating greater variability in blood‑pressure response.

## bp1030-05 Based on the data, which drug would be most cost‑effective if the price per dose is the same for all drugs?
Correct: B
Cost‑effectiveness considers both efficacy and cost. With equal drug costs, the drug that lowers blood pressure the most (Drug B) provides the greatest benefit per dollar spent.
