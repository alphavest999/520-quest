# BP1080

Researchers at the University of Greenfield examined the effect of a novel dietary supplement on blood pressure in adults aged 30–50. Twenty participants were randomly assigned to either the supplement group (n = 10) or a placebo group (n = 10). Over a 12‑week period, systolic blood pressure (SBP) was measured at baseline and at the end of the study. The supplement group’s mean SBP decreased from 138 mmHg to 124 mmHg, while the placebo group’s mean SBP changed from 136 mmHg to 134 mmHg. The researchers concluded that the supplement significantly reduced SBP compared to placebo. The study’s design included blinding, randomization, and a control group to mitigate bias. The authors suggested further research to confirm these findings in a larger, more diverse population.

## bp1080_q1 Based on the data presented, which of the following statements accurately reflects the study’s findings?
Correct: A
The supplement group’s SBP dropped from 138 to 124 mmHg (−14 mmHg), whereas the placebo group’s SBP dropped from 136 to 134 mmHg (−2 mmHg). Thus, the supplement group had a larger reduction.

## bp1080_q2 Which aspect of the study’s design best controls for placebo effects?
Correct: C
A double‑blind design ensures that neither participants nor investigators know who receives the supplement or placebo, thereby minimizing expectancy bias and placebo effects.

## bp1080_q3 What is the average percent reduction in systolic blood pressure for the supplement group?
Correct: C
Percent reduction = (Baseline – Week 12) / Baseline × 100 = (138 – 124) / 138 × 100 ≈ 10.1%.

## bp1080_q4 Which of the following is a potential trap when interpreting the study’s results?
Correct: D
All listed concerns could mislead interpretation: clinical significance, baseline imbalance, and placebo response all affect conclusions.

## bp1080_q5 How does this study exemplify the broader concept of evidence‑based medicine?
Correct: B
Evidence‑based medicine relies on rigorously designed studies; randomization and blinding are key to minimizing bias and producing reliable evidence.
