# BP975

Researchers investigated the pharmacokinetics of a novel combination therapy using Drug A and Drug B in a randomized, double-blind, crossover study involving 30 healthy volunteers. Each participant received a single oral dose of Drug A (50 mg) and Drug B (25 mg) separately, with a 7‑day washout period between administrations. Blood samples were collected at baseline and at 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8, 12, and 24 hours post‑dose. The primary endpoints were plasma concentration of each drug and a composite patient response score derived from a standardized symptom questionnaire administered at 4, 12, and 24 hours. Table 1 summarizes the mean concentrations and response scores across the two treatment arms. The investigators hypothesized that co‑administration would result in a synergistic reduction in symptom severity without altering the pharmacokinetic profiles of either drug. Secondary analyses included assessment of inter‑individual variability and correlation between drug levels and response scores. The study was approved by the institutional review board, and all volunteers provided written informed consent.
