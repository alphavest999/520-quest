# BP1185

Researchers studied the effect of a novel antihypertensive drug on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults with stage‑1 hypertension. Two hundred participants were randomly assigned to receive either the drug (n = 100) or a placebo (n = 100) for 12 weeks. Baseline mean SBP was 150 mm Hg in both groups. After 12 weeks, the drug group’s mean SBP fell to 120 mm Hg, while the placebo group’s mean SBP decreased to 140 mm Hg. The investigators reported a p‑value of <0.01 for the difference in mean change between groups. The study concluded that the drug significantly lowered SBP compared with placebo. ## bp1185-01 Which group experienced a greater mean reduction in systolic blood pressure?
Correct: A
The drug group’s mean SBP decreased by 30 mm Hg, whereas the placebo group’s mean SBP decreased by only 10 mm Hg, indicating a greater reduction in the drug group.

## bp1185-02 Which study design best controls for confounding variables in this investigation?
Correct: A
Randomization assigns participants to treatment or control groups by chance, balancing known and unknown confounders and providing the strongest evidence for causality.

## bp1185-03 What is the mean change in systolic blood pressure for the drug group?
Correct: C
Mean change = baseline (150) – week 12 (120) = 30 mm Hg.

## bp1185-04 Which statement is false regarding the study’s baseline data?
Correct: A
Baseline SBP was identical (150 mm Hg) in both groups; it was not higher in the drug group.

## bp1185-05 Which concept is most directly illustrated by this study’s design?
Correct: B
Randomization was used to allocate participants to drug or placebo groups, thereby controlling for confounding variables.
