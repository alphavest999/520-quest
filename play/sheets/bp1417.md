# BP1417

Researchers investigated how varying light intensity affects the growth of a common herbaceous plant, *Plantago major*. In a controlled greenhouse experiment, plants were exposed to four different light levels: 100, 200, 400, and 800 lux. Each group consisted of 10 replicates, and all other environmental conditions—soil type, watering schedule, temperature, and nutrient supply—were held constant. Over a 30‑day period, daily growth rates (in cm/day) were recorded, and at the end of the trial, total biomass (in grams) and seed yield (in units) were measured. The results, summarized in Table 1 below, reveal a clear trend: as light intensity increases, both growth rate and yield rise, but the rate of increase diminishes at the highest intensity. These findings suggest that *P. major* experiences light saturation around 600 lux, beyond which additional light yields diminishing returns. The data provide a basis for optimizing light conditions in both natural and artificial cultivation settings.

## bp1417-1 According to Table 1, what is the growth rate of *P. major* at 400 lux?
Correct: C
Table 1 lists the growth rate at 400 lux as 9 cm/day.

## bp1417-2 Which variable was controlled to isolate the effect of light intensity?
Correct: A
Soil type was held constant so that differences in growth could be attributed to light intensity.

## bp1417-3 Calculate the percent increase in seed yield from 200 lux to 800 lux.
Correct: A
Yield at 200 lux = 30 units; at 800 lux = 55 units. Percent increase = (55-30)/30 × 100 ≈ 83%.

## bp1417-4 Which of the following is NOT a factor that could confound the results if not controlled?
Correct: B
Light intensity is the variable being manipulated; it is not a confounding factor.

## bp1417-5 The observed light saturation point around 600 lux is most closely related to which physiological process?
Correct: A
Light saturation refers to the point at which photosynthetic light capture no longer limits growth.
