Researchers investigated the effect of two novel cognitive training programs on reaction time in healthy adults. A total of 180 participants (age 20‑35) were randomly assigned to one of three groups: a control group (no training), Treatment A (visual‑spatial training), or Treatment B (verbal‑linguistic training). After a 4‑week intervention, participants completed a computerized reaction‑time task. ## 1 According to the table, which group had the lowest mean reaction time after the intervention?
Correct: C
The table shows that Treatment B had a mean reaction time of 440 ms, which is lower than the 470 ms for Treatment A and the 520 ms for the Control group.

## 2 Which study design best controls for confounding variables in this experiment?
Correct: A
Randomization of participants into three groups ensures that confounding variables are evenly distributed across groups, making the randomized controlled trial the gold standard for controlling confounders.

## 3 Calculate the pooled standard deviation for Treatment A and Treatment B.
Correct: B
Pooled SD = sqrt(((nA-1)*sdA² + (nB-1)*sdB²)/(nA + nB - 2)) = sqrt(((59*38² + 59*35²)/118)) ≈ 37.2.

## 4 Which of the following is a potential source of bias in this study?
Correct: D
All three biases could affect the study: selection bias if randomization failed, publication bias if only positive results were reported, and confirmation bias if researchers interpreted data to fit expectations.

## 5 Which psychological concept is most closely related to the observed reduction in reaction time following training?
Correct: B
Operant conditioning explains how repeated practice (reinforcement) can strengthen a response—in this case, faster reaction times after training.
