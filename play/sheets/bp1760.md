# BP1760

Researchers studied the effect of caffeine on reaction time in a controlled laboratory setting. Thirty healthy adults were randomly assigned to one of three groups: a placebo group, a low‑dose caffeine group (100 mg), and a high‑dose caffeine group (200 mg). After ingesting their assigned beverage, participants completed a computerized Go/No‑Go task, and mean reaction times (RTs) were recorded. The study aimed to determine whether caffeine dose correlates with faster responses and to assess the variability of the effect across individuals. The researchers also measured heart rate and subjective alertness to explore potential mediating factors. Results suggested a dose‑dependent improvement in RT, with the high‑dose group exhibiting the fastest mean RT. However, variability increased with higher caffeine doses, indicating individual differences in sensitivity. These findings contribute to understanding how stimulants influence cognitive performance and may inform guidelines for safe caffeine consumption in high‑stakes environments such as aviation or military operations.
