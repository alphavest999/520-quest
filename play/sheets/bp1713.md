# BP1713

Researchers investigated the impact of a novel dietary supplement, NutriBoost, on insulin sensitivity in adults with prediabetes. A randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial enrolled 120 participants (age 35–65) who were randomly assigned to receive either NutriBoost (n=60) or a matching placebo (n=60) daily for 12 weeks. Fasting insulin levels and HOMA‑IR (homeostatic model assessment of insulin resistance) were measured at baseline and at the end of the intervention. The study also collected data on body mass index (BMI), fasting glucose, and adherence rates. Table 1 presents the mean changes (±SD) for each group. The investigators reported a statistically significant reduction in HOMA‑IR in the NutriBoost group compared with placebo (p<0.01). They concluded that NutriBoost improves insulin sensitivity independent of weight loss. However, the authors noted that the study was powered to detect a 0.5 unit difference in HOMA‑IR with 80% power at α=0.05. The authors also performed a secondary analysis adjusting for baseline BMI, which yielded a similar effect size.

## 1 Which of the following best describes the change in HOMA‑IR in the NutriBoost group?
Correct: B
The table shows a mean ΔHOMA‑IR of -1.2 for the NutriBoost group, indicating a 1.2‑unit decrease.

## 2 Which design would best test whether NutriBoost improves insulin sensitivity independent of weight loss?
Correct: B
A randomized controlled trial with a weight‑matched placebo controls for weight loss and isolates the effect of NutriBoost on insulin sensitivity.

## 3 What is the absolute difference in mean ΔHOMA‑IR between the NutriBoost and placebo groups?
Correct: A
NutriBoost ΔHOMA‑IR = -1.2; Placebo ΔHOMA‑IR = -0.3. Absolute difference = |-1.2 - (-0.3)| = 0.9.

## 4 Which statement is a common misinterpretation of the reported p‑value?
Correct: A
The p‑value does not give the probability that the null hypothesis is true; it gives the probability of obtaining the observed data (or more extreme) if the null were true.

## 5 Which concept is most directly related to the study's power calculation?
Correct: D
Statistical power is primarily determined by the sample size and the expected effect size; the authors powered the study to detect a 0.5 unit difference with 80% power.
