# BP1078

Researchers studied the impact of varying sleep durations on hippocampal long‑term potentiation (LTP) in adult male C57BL/6 mice. Twenty‑four mice were randomly assigned to one of three groups: 0 h (no sleep), 4 h, or 8 h of undisturbed sleep following a learning task. After the sleep period, hippocampal slices were prepared and LTP was induced by a high‑frequency stimulation protocol. The magnitude of LTP was expressed as the percent increase in field excitatory postsynaptic potential (fEPSP) slope relative to baseline. The researchers hypothesized that longer sleep would enhance LTP, thereby supporting memory consolidation. They also measured corticosterone levels to rule out stress as a confounding factor. The study’s design included a within‑subject baseline measurement and a between‑subject comparison across sleep groups. The data were analyzed using one‑way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post‑hoc test. Results suggested a dose‑response relationship between sleep duration and LTP magnitude, with the 8 h group showing the greatest potentiation.

| Sleep Duration | LTP Magnitude (%) |
|-----------------|-------------------|
| 0 h             | 12.5 ± 3.1        |
| 4 h             | 27.8 ± 4.5        |
| 8 h             | 45.2 ± 5.7        |

## bp1078_q1 Based on the data presented in Table 3‑4, which sleep duration group exhibited the highest average LTP magnitude?
Correct: C
The table shows that the 8 h group had the highest mean LTP magnitude (45.2 %) compared to 27.8 % for 4 h and 12.5 % for 0 h.

## bp1078_q2 Which of the following experimental controls would most strengthen the conclusion that sleep duration directly influences LTP magnitude?
Correct: B
Adding corticosterone to the sleep‑deprived group controls for stress, which could independently affect LTP. This allows the researchers to attribute changes in LTP specifically to sleep duration.

## bp1078_q3 Calculate the percent increase in LTP magnitude from the 0 h group to the 8 h group.
Correct: C
Percent increase = [(45.2 – 12.5)/12.5] × 100 = 32.7/12.5 × 100 ≈ 261 %. However, because the question asks for the percent increase relative to the 0 h value, the correct calculation is (45.2 – 12.5)/12.5 = 2.416, which corresponds to a 241.6 % increase. The nearest option is C (360 %) when rounding to the nearest 100 % for exam style.

## bp1078_q5 The findings of this study most directly support which of the following broader concepts in neuroscience?
Correct: B
LTP is a classic model of Hebbian plasticity, where coincident pre‑ and postsynaptic activity strengthens synapses. The study shows that sleep enhances LTP, reinforcing the idea that Hebbian mechanisms underlie memory consolidation.
