# BP1680

Researchers studied the effect of caffeine on reaction time in a controlled laboratory setting. Thirty healthy adults were randomly assigned to one of four groups: 0 mg (placebo), 50 mg, 100 mg, or 200 mg of caffeine. Each participant completed a computerized simple reaction time task after ingesting their assigned dose. Mean reaction times (RT) and standard deviations (SD) were recorded. The results indicated that caffeine dose was inversely related to RT: higher doses produced faster responses. The study controlled for age, sex, and baseline caffeine consumption, and was double‑blinded with a placebo group. These findings suggest that moderate caffeine intake may enhance performance in tasks requiring rapid responses.
