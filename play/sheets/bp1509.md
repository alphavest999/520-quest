# BP1509

Researchers investigated how varying light intensity affects the rate of photosynthesis in the freshwater alga *Chlorella vulgaris*. Cultures were grown under controlled temperature and CO₂ conditions, and oxygen evolution was measured using a Clark-type electrode. Light intensity was varied in increments of 200 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹, ranging from 200 to 1200 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹. The experiment was repeated three times for each light level to ensure reproducibility. The data collected indicated a clear increase in oxygen production with higher light intensities, plateauing at the highest intensities tested. The researchers noted that the saturation point suggested a potential limitation in the light-harvesting complexes of the alga. The study aimed to better understand the light response curve of *C. vulgaris* and to compare it with other algal species used in biofuel production.

## bp1509_q4 Which statement is most likely incorrect regarding the light response curve?
Correct: C
The plateau reflects saturation of the photosynthetic machinery, not photoinhibition. Photoinhibition would cause a decline in activity at very high light, which is not observed here.

## bp1509_q5 Which of the following concepts is most directly related to the plateau observed in the light response curve of *Chlorella vulgaris*?
Correct: B
The plateau indicates that the photosynthetic apparatus has reached light saturation; additional light does not increase the rate because the reaction centers are fully occupied.
