# BP1021

Researchers at the University of X performed a titration study to determine the pKa values and isoelectric points (pI) of several amino acids. In a series of buffer solutions ranging from pH 1.0 to 13.0, they measured the net charge of each amino acid using a microelectrophoresis setup. The experiment involved adding a small aliquot of the amino acid to 10 mL of buffer, stirring for 2 min, and recording the electrophoretic mobility at 25 °C. By plotting mobility versus pH, the researchers identified the pH at which the mobility changes sign—this corresponds to the pI. The data were then fitted to the Henderson–Hasselbalch equation to extract the pKa values for the α‑carboxyl, α‑amino, and side‑chain groups. The table below summarizes the key values obtained for lysine, aspartic acid, glutamine, and tyrosine. These results illustrate how side‑chain chemistry influences the overall charge profile and highlight the importance of accurate pKa determination for predicting protein behavior in different environments.

| Amino Acid | pKa (α‑COOH) | pKa (α‑NH3⁺) | pKa (Side‑chain) |
|------------|--------------|--------------|------------------|
| Lysine | 2.18 | 9.06 | 10.53 |
| Aspartic acid | 1.88 | 9.60 | 3.65 |
| Glutamine | 2.17 | 9.47 | N/A |
| Tyrosine | 2.20 | 9.10 | 10.07 |
