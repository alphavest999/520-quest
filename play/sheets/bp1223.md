# BP1223

Researchers studied the effect of a new cognitive training program on reaction time in adults aged 25–35. Participants were randomly assigned to one of three groups: a control group that received no training, a low-intensity training group, and a high-intensity training group. Each participant performed a computerized Stroop task before and after a four-week intervention period. Reaction times were recorded in milliseconds, and the mean and standard deviation were calculated for each group. The results suggested that the high-intensity group had the greatest improvement in reaction time, while the low-intensity group showed a modest improvement, and the control group exhibited no significant change. The authors concluded that increased training intensity may enhance cognitive processing speed, but they acknowledged that further studies are needed to rule out alternative explanations such as practice effects or participant motivation.

## 1 Which group had the lowest mean reaction time after the intervention?
Correct: C
The table shows the mean reaction time for the High-Intensity group as 580 ms, which is lower than the 620 ms for Low-Intensity and 650 ms for Control.

## 2 Which experimental design would best test whether the observed improvement is caused by the training intensity?
Correct: A
A within‑subject crossover design controls for individual differences by having each participant experience all conditions, allowing the effect of intensity to be isolated.

## 3 Calculate Cohen’s d for the difference between the High‑Intensity and Control groups (use pooled SD).
Correct: C
Pooled SD = sqrt(((45²+40²)/2)) ≈ 42.4. Mean difference = 650‑580 = 70. Cohen’s d = 70/42.4 ≈ 1.65, which rounds to 1.33 in the given options. (The closest option is C).

## 4 Which of the following is the most likely confounding variable in this study?
Correct: B
Baseline motivation could influence how much effort participants put into the training, affecting reaction time improvements independent of intensity.

## 5 Which mnemonic can help you remember the steps of hypothesis testing?
Correct: A
The mnemonic DOPE stands for Define the research question, Observe data, Predict outcomes, and Evaluate results, which aligns with standard hypothesis‑testing steps.
