# BP1555

Researchers studied the effect of a novel cognitive training program on working memory performance in adults aged 20–35. Twenty-eight participants were randomly assigned to either the training group (n = 14) or a control group (n = 14) that engaged in non‑cognitive activities. Over four weeks, participants completed daily sessions of 30 minutes. Working memory was assessed before and after the intervention using the n‑back task, recording reaction times (RT) and accuracy. The training group showed a significant reduction in mean RT from 650 ms to 530 ms (p < 0.01), whereas the control group’s RT changed from 655 ms to 650 ms (p = 0.78). Accuracy improved modestly in both groups, with the training group’s mean accuracy rising from 78 % to 85 % and the control group from 77 % to 79 %. The authors concluded that the training program enhances processing speed but has limited impact on accuracy.

## bp1555-1 Which of the following best describes the study’s experimental design?
Correct: B
Participants were randomly assigned to training or control groups and both groups were assessed before and after the intervention, fitting a randomized controlled trial with pre‑post measures.

## bp1555-2 If the researchers had included a third group that performed a different cognitive task, what would be the most appropriate justification for adding this group?
Correct: B
Adding a third group that engages in a different cognitive task would allow the researchers to determine whether improvements are specific to the training program or due to general cognitive engagement.

## bp1555-4 Which statement is a potential trap when interpreting the study’s findings?
Correct: A
Both groups performed the n‑back task pre‑ and post‑intervention, so practice or familiarity could contribute to faster reaction times; attributing all improvement to the training is a trap.
