# BP1759

Researchers at the University of Midvale investigated the effect of a novel dietary supplement on cognitive performance in adults aged 45–65. In a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial, 120 participants were assigned to either the supplement group (n = 60) or the placebo group (n = 60). Cognitive function was assessed at baseline and after 12 weeks using a standardized battery of tests, yielding composite scores ranging from 0 to 100. The researchers recorded the mean composite score and standard deviation for each group at the 12‑week mark. The study aimed to determine whether the supplement could produce a clinically meaningful improvement in cognition compared to placebo.

## 1 Which of the following best describes the change in mean composite score for the supplement group relative to the placebo group at 12 weeks?
Correct: A
The mean score for the supplement group is 78.4 and for placebo is 72.1. The difference is 78.4 – 72.1 = 6.3 points, indicating the supplement group improved by 6.3 points relative to placebo.

## 2 Which of the following best describes the experimental design used in the study?
Correct: C
The passage states that participants were randomized, the study was double‑blind, and there was a placebo control, which defines a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial.

## 3 Assuming the standard deviations are equal, which of the following is the approximate 95% confidence interval for the difference in mean scores between the supplement and placebo groups?
Correct: A
Using the formula for the 95% CI of the difference: 6.3 ± 1.96 × sqrt((6.2²/60)+(5.8²/60)) ≈ 6.3 ± 0.8 → 5.5 to 7.1.

## 4 Which of the following statements is a potential trap when interpreting the study’s findings?
Correct: C
With a 12‑week trial and 120 participants, the study may still be susceptible to a type I error; the reported difference could be due to chance if multiple comparisons were made.

## 5 Which of the following best links the study’s outcome to a broader public health implication?
Correct: B
The study demonstrates a modest improvement in cognitive scores with a dietary supplement, suggesting that such interventions could be part of a broader strategy to preserve cognition in adults aged 45–65.
