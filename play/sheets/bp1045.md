# BP1045

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel dietary supplement on resting metabolic rate (RMR) in healthy adults. In a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled study, 120 participants were assigned to one of four groups: placebo (n=30), low‑dose supplement (n=30), medium‑dose supplement (n=30), and high‑dose supplement (n=30). RMR was measured using indirect calorimetry after a 12‑hour fast, and results were recorded in kilocalories per day. The study also recorded body composition via dual‑energy X‑ray absorptiometry (DXA) to assess changes in lean mass. Participants were monitored for 12 weeks, with RMR measured at baseline, 6 weeks, and 12 weeks. The primary outcome was the change in RMR from baseline to 12 weeks. Secondary outcomes included changes in lean body mass and fat mass. The study aimed to determine whether the supplement could safely increase metabolic rate without adverse effects on body composition.

## bp1045-02 Which experimental design element is essential to attribute changes in RMR to the supplement rather than to other factors?
Correct: B
Randomization ensures that known and unknown confounders are evenly distributed across groups, allowing causal inference about the supplement’s effect.

## bp1045-03 Assuming the SD values represent standard deviations of the mean, calculate the 95% confidence interval for the mean RMR of the high‑dose group (n = 30).
Correct: A
Standard error = SD/√n = 105/√30 ≈ 19.2. 95% CI = mean ± 1.96×SE = 1820 ± 1.96×19.2 ≈ 1820 ± 37.6 → 1792–1858, rounded to 1795–1845.

Correct: D
Randomization was performed; the lack of blinding could affect behavior but does not invalidate the study’s design. Option D incorrectly attributes the flaw to randomization.

## bp1045-05 Which physiological concept best explains the observed increase in RMR with higher supplement doses?
Correct: A
The supplement likely stimulates thermogenesis and/or increases lean mass, both of which raise RMR. The other options would predict a decrease or no change in RMR.
