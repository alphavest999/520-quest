# BP1536

Researchers investigated how varying light intensity influences the photosynthetic rate of spinach leaves. Five light intensities (200, 400, 600, 800, and 1000 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹) were applied to detached leaves in a controlled chamber. For each intensity, the net CO₂ assimilation rate (µmol CO₂ m⁻² s⁻¹), chlorophyll a+b content (mg g⁻¹ fresh weight), and leaf temperature (°C) were recorded after a 30‑minute equilibration period. The data suggested a saturating relationship between light intensity and photosynthetic rate, with a modest decline in chlorophyll content at the highest light level, possibly due to photoinhibition. Leaf temperature remained relatively stable (24.5–25.8 °C) across treatments, indicating that temperature was not a confounding variable. These observations provide a foundation for exploring the mechanisms of light‑dependent regulation of photosynthesis and the role of chlorophyll in light capture.
