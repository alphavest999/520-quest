# BP1076

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel antihypertensive drug on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults aged 45–65. In a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial, 240 participants were assigned to one of four groups: placebo (n=60), low dose (10 mg, n=60), medium dose (20 mg, n=60), or high dose (40 mg, n=60). SBP was measured at baseline and after 12 weeks of treatment. The mean SBP reduction (± SD) for each group was: placebo 2.1 ± 5.3 mmHg, 10 mg 8.4 ± 6.1 mmHg, 20 mg 12.7 ± 5.9 mmHg, 40 mg 15.3 ± 6.4 mmHg. Table 1 summarizes these findings along with p‑values and 95 % confidence intervals for each dose comparison versus placebo.

Table 1: SBP Reduction by Dose
Dose (mg) | Mean Reduction (mmHg) | SD | p‑value vs placebo | 95% CI
--- | --- | --- | --- | ---
Placebo | 2.1 | 5.3 | — | —
10 | 8.4 | 6.1 | 0.001 | 5.2–11.6
20 | 12.7 | 5.9 | <0.0001 | 9.5–15.9
40 | 15.3 | 6.4 | <0.0001 | 12.1–18.5

## bp1076-01 Which statement best reflects the data presented in Table 1?
Correct: A
The 10 mg group’s mean reduction of 8.4 mmHg is statistically significant versus placebo (p = 0.001). The 20 mg dose had a larger mean reduction (12.7 mmHg) but its confidence interval (9.5–15.9) is narrower than that of the 40 mg dose, contradicting option B. Option C is incorrect because the 40 mg dose’s reduction (15.3 mmHg) is significantly greater than the 20 mg dose (p < 0.0001). Option D is incorrect because reductions >10 mmHg are considered clinically meaningful.

## bp1076-02 Which modification to the study design would best address the possibility that the observed SBP reductions are due to regression to the mean?
Correct: A
A crossover design allows each participant to serve as their own control, thereby reducing the influence of regression to the mean. Randomization by baseline SBP (option D) balances groups but does not eliminate regression to the mean. Extending follow‑up (option C) and changing baseline thresholds (option B) do not directly address the statistical artifact.

## bp1076-03 What is the absolute risk reduction (ARR) in systolic blood pressure for the 20 mg dose compared to placebo?
Correct: B
ARR is the difference in mean SBP reduction between the treatment and placebo groups: 12.7 mmHg – 2.1 mmHg = 10.6 mmHg. Option C reflects the 20 mg mean reduction itself, not the difference from placebo.

## bp1076-04 Which of the following statements is NOT supported by the data?
Correct: D
Only the 10, 20, and 40 mg groups are statistically significant versus placebo; the placebo group is not a comparison. Option D incorrectly states that all dose groups, including placebo, show significant reductions, which is false.

## bp1076-05 Which of the following best summarizes the clinical implication of the dose–response relationship observed in this study?
Correct: B
The 20 mg dose achieves a substantial reduction (12.7 mmHg) with a moderate SD and is statistically significant, suggesting an optimal balance of efficacy and safety. The 40 mg dose offers a slightly greater reduction but with a larger SD and potential for more adverse effects. Option A is incorrect because the 40 mg dose does provide additional benefit. Option C underestimates the benefit of higher doses, and option D overstates the recommendation without safety data.
