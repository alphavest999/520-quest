# BP1244

Researchers studied the effect of varying light intensity on photosynthetic performance in spinach (Spinacia oleracea) leaves. Leaf discs were exposed to four light intensities (200, 400, 600, and 800 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹) in a controlled growth chamber. Oxygen evolution, carbon dioxide uptake, and chlorophyll fluorescence (Fv/Fm) were measured after a 30‑minute acclimation period. The data show a progressive increase in O₂ production and CO₂ uptake with light intensity, while Fv/Fm values remained relatively stable, indicating that photoinhibition was not a limiting factor under the tested conditions. The researchers concluded that photosynthetic rate is strongly light‑dependent up to 800 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹, beyond which the response plateaus. These findings have implications for optimizing light conditions in controlled‑environment agriculture and for understanding the limits of photosynthetic efficiency in C₃ plants.
