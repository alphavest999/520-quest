# BP1327

Researchers studied the effects of four different dietary supplements on the endurance performance of trained cyclists. The study was conducted over a 12‑week period with 80 participants randomly assigned to one of four groups: placebo (Group A), carbohydrate supplement (Group B), protein supplement (Group C), and a combined carbohydrate‑protein supplement (Group D). Endurance performance was measured by time to complete a 40‑km time trial at baseline and after 12 weeks. Table 1 summarizes the mean time (in minutes) and standard deviation for each group at the end of the study. The researchers hypothesized that the combined supplement would yield the greatest improvement, followed by carbohydrate, protein, and placebo. Statistical analysis revealed a significant interaction between supplement type and time, with Group D showing a 12.5% reduction in completion time compared to baseline, whereas Group A increased by 2.1%. The authors concluded that a combined carbohydrate‑protein supplement enhances endurance performance more effectively than either macronutrient alone or placebo.

## bp1327_q1 Which of the following best describes the pattern of results observed in Table 1?
Correct: C
Table 1 shows that Group D (combined supplement) has the lowest mean time (39.7 min), indicating the greatest improvement relative to baseline. The carbohydrate group improved but not as much as Group D, and the protein group did not improve as much as the carbohydrate group.

## bp1327_q2 Which experimental design feature best supports the validity of the study’s conclusions?
Correct: B
Random assignment controls for confounding variables and ensures that differences in outcomes can be attributed to the supplements rather than pre‑existing differences among participants, thereby strengthening internal validity.
