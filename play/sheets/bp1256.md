# BP1256

Researchers conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of three novel antihypertensive agents (Drug A, Drug B, and Drug C) compared to a placebo in reducing systolic blood pressure (SBP) among adults aged 40–65 with stage 1 hypertension. A total of 400 participants were randomly assigned to one of four groups (n = 100 per group) and received their assigned treatment for 12 weeks. SBP was measured at baseline and at the end of the study using an automated cuff. The mean SBP at week 12 and the standard deviation (SD) for each group were recorded. The study aimed to determine which drug produced the greatest mean reduction in SBP while maintaining safety and tolerability.

Correct: C
Concurrent use of other antihypertensive drugs could influence SBP independently of the study drugs, acting as a confounder. Randomization, baseline measurement, and standardized protocol help control confounding.
