# BP853

Researchers investigated the ionization behavior of a set of small amino acids to better understand how side‑chain chemistry influences protein charge at physiological pH. Using a potentiometric titration apparatus, they measured the pH of aqueous solutions as they added standardized NaOH to each amino acid dissolved at 0.1 M. The titration curves were recorded in 0.05 M increments, and the inflection points corresponding to the α‑carboxyl, α‑amino, and side‑chain functional groups were noted. For each amino acid, the experiment yielded three pKa values (pKa1, pKa2, pKa3) and the isoelectric point (pI) calculated as the average of the two adjacent pKa values that bracket a net zero charge. The data set includes alanine, serine, lysine, and glutamic acid, chosen to represent non‑polar, polar, basic, and acidic side chains, respectively. The researchers also plotted the titration curves and highlighted the regions where the net charge changes from +1 to –1. These results provide a quantitative framework for predicting the charge state of amino acids under different pH conditions, which is essential for interpreting protein folding, stability, and interactions.

| Amino Acid | pKa1 | pKa2 | pI |
|------------|------|------|----|
| Alanine | 2.34 | 9.60 | 5.97 |
| Serine | 2.21 | 9.22 | 5.72 |
| Lysine | 2.18 | 9.06 | 8.79 |
| Glutamic Acid | 2.19 | 9.67 | 4.72 |

## 853-01 Which amino acid has the highest isoelectric point (pI) according to the data?
Correct: C
Analogy (10‑yr): Imagine a seesaw; the higher the pI, the more basic the amino acid sits on the right side. Official (AAMC): The pI is the average of the two pKa values that bracket a net zero charge; the highest pI among the list is Lysine (8.79). Why wrong (trap): The other amino acids have lower pI values, so they cannot be the correct answer.

## 853-02 If the researchers wanted to determine the pKa of serine’s side‑chain hydroxyl group, which experimental approach would be most appropriate?
Correct: A
Analogy (10‑yr): Think of each functional group as a stop on a road trip; the second stop is the side‑chain. Official (AAMC): The side‑chain pKa appears as the second inflection in a titration curve. Why wrong (trap): UV, NMR, and MS do not directly give pKa values in this context.

## 853-03 Calculate the isoelectric point (pI) of glycine if its pKa1 = 2.34 and pKa2 = 9.60.
Correct: A
Analogy (10‑yr): The pI is like the middle of a seesaw; you average the two numbers. Official (AAMC): pI = (pKa1 + pKa2)/2 = (2.34 + 9.60)/2 = 5.97. Why wrong (trap): The other options do not result from the average.
