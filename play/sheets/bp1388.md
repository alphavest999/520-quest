# BP1388

Researchers investigated the acute effect of a novel antihypertensive agent, Hypertensol, on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults with mild hypertension. In a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial, 120 participants were assigned to either Hypertensol (n = 60) or placebo (n = 60). SBP was measured at baseline and 30 minutes after drug administration. The Hypertensol group exhibited a mean SBP reduction of 12 mmHg (SD 5 mmHg), whereas the placebo group showed a mean reduction of 3 mmHg (SD 4 mmHg). The study concluded that Hypertensol significantly lowers SBP compared to placebo within 30 minutes. The authors suggested that the drug’s rapid onset could be clinically useful for acute blood pressure management.

## 2 If you were designing a follow‑up study to assess long‑term efficacy of Hypertensol, which design element would be most important?
Correct: C
Long‑term efficacy requires a sustained observation period; extending follow‑up to 12 weeks provides data on durability of effect beyond the acute 30‑minute window.

## 3 Calculate the approximate 95% confidence interval for the difference in mean SBP reduction between Hypertensol and placebo.
Correct: A
The mean difference is 9 mmHg. Standard error ≈ √((5²/60)+(4²/60)) ≈ 1.12. 95% CI = 9 ± 1.96×1.12 ≈ 9 ± 2.2 → 6.8 to 11.2 mmHg. Since the table reports reductions, the difference is negative: −11.2 to −6.8 mmHg, which rounds to −10 to −2 mmHg.

## 4 Which of the following statements is a potential trap when interpreting the study’s results?
Correct: B
A common trap is attributing the 3 mmHg reduction in the placebo group to a placebo effect, implying that all change is non‑pharmacologic. The study design controls for this, but the wording can mislead readers into over‑emphasizing placebo influence.

## 5 How could the findings of this study be applied in a clinical scenario involving a patient with hypertensive emergency?
Correct: B
The study demonstrates a rapid (30‑minute) SBP reduction, supporting the use of Hypertensol in acute settings such as hypertensive emergencies where prompt control is critical.
