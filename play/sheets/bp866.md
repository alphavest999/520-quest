# BP866

Researchers at the University of BioChem conducted a systematic titration study to determine the pKa values of the side chains of lysine and arginine across a temperature range of 4 °C to 60 °C. Using a calibrated pH electrode and a buffer system that maintained ionic strength at 0.1 M, they recorded the pH at which the first, second, and third protonation events occurred. The data were plotted as titration curves, and the inflection points were identified by the first derivative of the pH vs. added base curve. The resulting pKa values were compiled in Table 1. The experiment demonstrated that the pKa of the ε‑amino group of lysine decreased by 0.05 pH units per 10 °C increase, whereas the guanidinium group of arginine remained essentially unchanged. These observations provide insight into how temperature influences the ionization behavior of basic amino acids and help refine models of protein folding under physiological and non‑physiological conditions.
