# BP1205

Researchers investigated how varying light intensity affects photosynthetic performance in spinach (Spinacia oleracea) leaves. Spinach leaf discs were exposed to four controlled light regimes: 200, 400, 600, and 800 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹, using a calibrated LED light source. For each intensity, oxygen evolution (µmol O₂ m⁻² s⁻¹), stomatal conductance (mol m⁻² s⁻¹), and chlorophyll fluorescence (ΦPSII) were measured after a 10‑minute acclimation period. The data are summarized in Table 1. The results show a sigmoidal increase in oxygen evolution with light intensity, reaching a plateau near 700 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹. Stomatal conductance rises modestly across the range, while ΦPSII remains relatively stable, suggesting efficient photochemistry at higher light levels. These findings support the hypothesis that spinach photosynthesis is light‑limited up to ~600 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹, after which additional light does not markedly enhance electron transport.
