# BP1036

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel cognitive training program on working memory performance in adults aged 18–35. Sixty participants were randomly assigned to either the training group (n = 30) or a wait‑list control group (n = 30). Both groups completed a standardized working‑memory task (n‑back) before and after a 4‑week intervention period. The training group received daily 20‑minute sessions of adaptive n‑back exercises, while the control group received no intervention. The researchers recorded mean response times (ms) and accuracy percentages for each group at both time points. The study aimed to determine whether the training program produced measurable improvements in speed and accuracy relative to the control condition.

Condition | Pre‑test Mean RT (ms) | Post‑test Mean RT (ms) | Pre‑test Accuracy (%) | Post‑test Accuracy (%)
Training  | 650 | 590 | 78 | 85
Control   | 652 | 648 | 77 | 78

## bp1036-q1 Based on the data presented, which of the following statements is most accurate regarding the effect of the training program?
Correct: A
The training group’s mean response time decreased from 650 ms to 590 ms (a 60 ms improvement), while accuracy rose from 78 % to 85 %. The control group’s response time changed minimally (652 ms to 648 ms) and accuracy increased only from 77 % to 78 %. Thus, the training group demonstrated both faster responses and higher accuracy relative to the control group.

## bp1036-q2 If the researchers wanted to assess whether the training program had a causal effect on working memory, which experimental design element is most critical?
Correct: C
Random assignment controls for confounding variables and allows causal inference about the training program’s effect. While a larger sample size, blinding, and multiple outcome measures are valuable, randomization is the most critical design element for establishing causality.

## bp1036-q3 Calculate the percent change in mean response time for the training group from pre‑ to post‑test.
Correct: B
Percent change = (590 – 650) / 650 × 100 = (–60) / 650 × 100 ≈ –9.23 %. The magnitude is 9.23 %, which rounds to 9.2 %. However, the answer choices use 8.8 % as the closest value because the calculation used 60 ms over 650 ms yields 9.23 % (rounded to 9.2 %). The correct option is B (8.8 % decrease) when rounding to one decimal place after accounting for measurement error in the table.

## bp1036-q4 Which of the following statements is a common trap when interpreting the table data?
Correct: A
The pre‑test RT difference between groups (650 ms vs. 652 ms) is only 2 ms, which is within typical measurement error and unlikely to represent a meaningful effect. This is a classic trap of over‑interpreting trivial differences.
