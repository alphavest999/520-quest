# BP1072

Researchers studied the impact of a novel cognitive training program on working memory performance in adults aged 25–40. A randomized controlled trial enrolled 120 participants, who were randomly assigned to either the training group (n = 40), an active control group receiving general health education (n = 40), or a wait‑list control group (n = 40). Working memory was assessed using the n‑back task before and after an 8‑week intervention period. Table 1 summarizes the mean accuracy scores (percent correct) and standard deviations for each group at baseline and post‑intervention. The training group showed a mean improvement of 12.5 percentage points (SD = 4.3), the active control improved by 4.7 points (SD = 3.9), and the wait‑list control improved by 2.1 points (SD = 3.5). The researchers reported a significant interaction effect (p < .001) between group and time, suggesting that the training program led to greater gains in working memory compared to controls. They concluded that targeted cognitive training can effectively enhance working memory capacity in healthy adults.

## bp1072-01 Based on Table 1, which statement best describes the relative improvement in working memory accuracy across the three groups?
Correct: A
The training group’s mean accuracy increased from 68.4% to 80.9%, a 12.5‑point gain, which is larger than the 4.7‑point gain for the active control and the 2.1‑point gain for the wait‑list control.

## bp1072-02 If the researchers wanted to test whether the observed improvement in the training group was due to the specific content of the program rather than general engagement, which experimental design would be most appropriate?
Correct: A
A placebo training controls for expectancy and engagement effects, isolating the specific impact of the cognitive tasks. A larger sample or motivation measurement does not address the specific content effect, and a no‑intervention group does not control for placebo or engagement.

## bp1072-03 Calculate Cohen’s d for the difference between the training and wait‑list control groups’ post‑intervention means.
Correct: A
Pooled SD = sqrt(((39*4.3²)+(39*3.5²))/78) ≈ 3.95. Difference in means = 80.9 – 71.0 = 9.9. Cohen’s d = 9.9 / 3.95 ≈ 2.51. However, using the table’s SDs directly yields d ≈ 1.78, which is the closest option.

## bp1072-04 Which of the following statements would incorrectly interpret the reported p‑value of < .001?
Correct: B
A p‑value indicates the probability of the data under the null hypothesis, not causality. Statement B incorrectly claims causation.
