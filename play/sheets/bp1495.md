# BP1495

Researchers studied the effect of caffeine on reaction time in a controlled laboratory setting. Sixty healthy adults (30 male, 30 female) were randomly assigned to one of three conditions: (1) placebo, (2) 100 mg caffeine, or (3) 200 mg caffeine. After ingestion, participants performed a computerized simple reaction time task, responding as quickly as possible to a visual stimulus. Mean reaction times and standard deviations were recorded for each condition. The study aimed to determine whether caffeine dosage linearly improves reaction speed and whether sex moderates this effect. Participants were screened for caffeine sensitivity and had abstained from caffeine for 12 hours prior to testing. The experiment was double‑blind, and all data were collected in a single session to minimize fatigue effects. Results indicated a statistically significant decrease in reaction time with higher caffeine doses, with the greatest improvement observed in the 200 mg group. No significant sex differences were found. The authors concluded that moderate caffeine intake can enhance cognitive performance in healthy adults.

## bp1495-01 Based on the data presented in the table, which statement best describes the pattern of reaction times across caffeine conditions?
Correct: C
The mean reaction times decrease from 420 ms (placebo) to 380 ms (100 mg) to 350 ms (200 mg), indicating a linear improvement with higher caffeine doses.

## bp1495-02 Which experimental design best supports the conclusion that caffeine dosage causes changes in reaction time?
Correct: B
The study used a between‑subjects design with random assignment to placebo, 100 mg, or 200 mg caffeine, which controls for individual differences and isolates the effect of caffeine dose.

## bp1495-03 Calculate the Cohen’s d effect size for the difference between the 200 mg caffeine condition and the placebo condition.
Correct: B
Cohen’s d = (Mean1 – Mean2) / pooled SD. Pooled SD = sqrt(((35^2 + 28^2)/2)) ≈ 31.3. Difference = 420 – 350 = 70. d = 70 / 31.3 ≈ 2.24.
