# BP1141

Researchers conducted a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial to evaluate the effect of a novel antihypertensive agent, Hypertensol, on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults with stage 1 hypertension. Two hundred and forty patients were enrolled and randomly assigned to receive either Hypertensol (n=120) or a matching placebo (n=120) for 4 weeks. Baseline and 4‑week SBP and diastolic blood pressure (DBP) were measured in a standardized setting. The mean SBP at baseline was 148.2 mm Hg in the Hypertensol group and 147.9 mm Hg in the placebo group. After 4 weeks, the Hypertensol group’s mean SBP fell to 128.5 mm Hg, whereas the placebo group’s mean SBP was 146.3 mm Hg. Mean DBP decreased from 92.5 mm Hg to 84.7 mm Hg in the Hypertensol group and from 91.8 mm Hg to 90.9 mm Hg in the placebo group. The study concluded that Hypertensol significantly lowers both SBP and DBP compared with placebo, with a mean SBP reduction of 19.7 mm Hg in the treatment arm.

## 1 What is the mean reduction in systolic blood pressure observed in the Hypertensol group?
Correct: A
The mean SBP decreased from 148.2 mm Hg to 128.5 mm Hg, a reduction of 19.7 mm Hg.

## 2 Which experimental design best controls for potential confounding variables in this study?
Correct: B
Randomization and blinding minimize selection bias and placebo effect, making the double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial the gold standard for controlling confounders.

## 3 Calculate the percent reduction in mean systolic blood pressure in the Hypertensol group.
Correct: A
Percent reduction = (148.2 – 128.5) / 148.2 × 100 = 19.7 / 148.2 × 100 ≈ 13.3%.

## 4 Which of the following statements is FALSE regarding the study’s findings?
Correct: B
The placebo group’s SBP decreased from 147.9 to 146.3 mm Hg, a modest reduction, not an increase.

## 5 Which physiological mechanism most likely explains Hypertensol’s effect on blood pressure?
Correct: A
The drug’s name and effect suggest it is an ACE inhibitor, which reduces angiotensin II production, lowering vasoconstriction and aldosterone‑mediated volume expansion.
