# BP1355

Researchers investigated the influence of different monochromatic light wavelengths on the growth of the freshwater alga *Chlorella vulgaris*. Cultures were grown in identical photobioreactors under controlled temperature (25 °C) and CO₂ concentration (2 %). Five wavelengths (400 nm, 450 nm, 500 nm, 550 nm, 600 nm) were applied for 14 days, with a dark control. Growth was measured as linear increase in culture height (mm/day), photosynthetic efficiency (PE) via chlorophyll fluorescence, and final biomass concentration (g/L). The results suggested a peak in growth and PE at 500 nm, while 600 nm produced the lowest values. The study aimed to identify optimal illumination for maximizing algal productivity in biofuel production.

Wavelength (nm) | Growth Rate (mm/day) | Photosynthetic Efficiency (%) | Biomass (g/L)
400 | 1.2 | 45 | 0.85
450 | 1.5 | 55 | 1.10
500 | 1.9 | 68 | 1.35
550 | 1.6 | 60 | 1.20
600 | 0.9 | 35 | 0.70

## bp1355-01 Which wavelength produced the highest growth rate in the study?
Correct: C
The table shows a growth rate of 1.9 mm/day at 500 nm, which is higher than the rates for all other wavelengths.

## bp1355-02 Which of the following would be the most appropriate control for this experiment?
Correct: B
A dark control establishes the baseline growth without light, allowing comparison of light-induced effects.

## bp1355-03 What is the average growth rate across all five wavelengths?
Correct: B
Average = (1.2 + 1.5 + 1.9 + 1.6 + 0.9) / 5 = 7.1 / 5 = 1.42 mm/day ≈ 1.40 mm/day.
