# BP1032

Researchers studied the effect of three different light wavelengths on the photosynthetic rate of a common aquatic plant, Lemna minor. The experiment was conducted in a controlled laboratory setting where plants were exposed to red (660 nm), blue (450 nm), and white (full-spectrum) light for 8 hours per day over a 14-day period. The primary outcome measured was the increase in chlorophyll content, quantified via spectrophotometric absorbance at 645 nm. Secondary outcomes included growth rate (cm/day) and biomass accumulation (mg dry weight). The study employed a randomized block design with five replicates per treatment group. Data were collected weekly and analyzed using one‑way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post‑hoc test. The results indicated that blue light significantly increased chlorophyll content compared to red and white, while red light promoted faster biomass accumulation. However, the growth rate did not differ significantly among treatments. These findings suggest that light quality can differentially influence photosynthetic efficiency and growth in Lemna minor, with implications for optimizing aquatic plant cultivation.

## 1 Which treatment had the highest chlorophyll content?
Correct: B
The table shows the highest absorbance value (0.60) for the blue light treatment, indicating the greatest chlorophyll content.

## 2 Which experimental design best controls for variability across plants?
Correct: A
The study used a randomized block design, which accounts for block‑level variation and reduces error variance.

## 3 What is the percent increase in chlorophyll content of blue relative to red?
Correct: B
Percent increase = (0.60‑0.45)/0.45 × 100% ≈ 33%.

## 4 Which of the following is a potential confounding variable not controlled in the study?
Correct: A
The passage does not mention temperature control; temperature can affect photosynthetic rates and thus is a potential confounder.

## 5 Which mnemonic helps remember the order of light wavelengths from longest to shortest?

ROYGBIV (Red, Orange, Yellow, Green, Blue, Indigo, Violet) lists wavelengths from longest (red) to shortest (violet).
