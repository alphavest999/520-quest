# BP1493

Researchers studied the influence of light exposure on the circadian rhythm of laboratory mice. Twenty-four adult male C57BL/6 mice were randomly assigned to four groups (n = 6 per group). Group A was exposed to a 12:12 hour light-dark cycle (LD) throughout the experiment. Group B was kept in constant darkness (DD). Group C received a 6-hour phase advance of the light cycle each week for four weeks. Group D received a 6-hour phase delay of the light cycle each week for four weeks. Activity levels were recorded using infrared beam breaks, and total sleep duration was measured via EEG telemetry. The study spanned eight weeks, with data collected weekly. The researchers hypothesized that phase shifts would alter activity patterns and sleep architecture, potentially revealing adaptive mechanisms in circadian entrainment. Results indicated that both phase advance and delay groups exhibited increased nocturnal activity during the first two weeks, followed by a gradual return to baseline. Constant darkness led to a significant reduction in total sleep duration, whereas the LD group maintained stable activity and sleep profiles. The data suggest that mice can adapt to moderate phase shifts, but prolonged darkness disrupts sleep homeostasis.
