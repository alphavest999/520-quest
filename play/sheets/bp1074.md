# BP1074

Researchers at the University of Health Sciences conducted a double‑blind, randomized controlled trial to evaluate the antihypertensive efficacy of a novel angiotensin‑converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor, Hypertensol, compared to placebo. Eighty adult participants with mild to moderate essential hypertension were enrolled and randomly assigned to one of three groups: control (no active drug), low‑dose Hypertensol (50 mg daily), or high‑dose Hypertensol (100 mg daily). After 12 weeks of therapy, systolic blood pressure (SBP) was measured in all participants. The study aimed to determine whether Hypertensol could produce a clinically meaningful reduction in SBP and to assess dose‑response relationships. In addition, safety endpoints such as serum creatinine and potassium levels were monitored. The investigators hypothesized that the high‑dose group would exhibit the greatest SBP reduction, followed by the low‑dose group, with the control group showing minimal change.
