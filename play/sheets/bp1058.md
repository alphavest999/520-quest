# BP1058

Researchers studied the effect of a novel antibiotic on the growth of *Staphylococcus aureus* in vitro. The experiment involved culturing the bacteria in a nutrient broth and adding varying concentrations of the antibiotic. Samples were taken at 0, 1, 3, 5, and 7 days post-treatment. Bacterial counts were measured by serial dilution and plating, and the antibiotic concentration was confirmed by high-performance liquid chromatography. The goal was to determine the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) and the time-dependent bactericidal activity. The study also compared the novel antibiotic to vancomycin as a reference standard. The data collected provide insights into both the potency and the kinetics of bacterial killing, which are critical for informing dosing regimens in clinical settings. The researchers plan to use these findings to design subsequent in vivo studies and to evaluate potential resistance mechanisms.

## bp1058-02 Which experimental control would best determine whether the observed bacterial killing is due to the antibiotic rather than other variables?
Correct: A
A culture with no antibiotic serves as a negative control, showing bacterial growth in the absence of treatment. This allows comparison to antibiotic-treated cultures to attribute effects specifically to the drug.
