# BP1064

Researchers at the University of Greenfield examined the impact of a novel cognitive training program on working memory in adults aged 25–45. Sixty participants were randomly assigned to one of four groups: a control group (no training), Group A (30‑minute training sessions twice weekly), Group B (45‑minute sessions twice weekly), and Group C (30‑minute sessions three times weekly). Baseline working‑memory scores were obtained using the Digit Span task. After 12 weeks, post‑intervention scores were recorded. Table 1 summarizes the mean scores and changes for each group. The data suggest that increased session duration and frequency may correlate with greater improvement, but variability within groups indicates other factors may influence outcomes. The researchers plan to conduct a follow‑up study to explore the underlying neural mechanisms using fMRI. The current findings provide preliminary evidence that structured, moderate‑intensity training can enhance working memory, though the optimal dosage remains unclear. Future analyses will examine dose–response relationships and potential moderators.
