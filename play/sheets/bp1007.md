# BP1007

Researchers conducted a 12‑week, single‑arm, prospective study to evaluate the effect of the novel supplement 'Glucor' on fasting blood glucose (FBG) levels in adults with type 2 diabetes. Eighty‑two participants were enrolled; 40 completed the study. Baseline characteristics were similar across groups. FBG was measured at baseline and at the end of the 12‑week period. The primary outcome was the change in FBG from baseline to week 12. Participants received 500 mg of Glucor twice daily. Compliance was monitored via pill counts and electronic diaries. The study reported a mean reduction of 18 mg/dL in FBG, with a 95 % confidence interval of 12–24 mg/dL. No serious adverse events were reported. The investigators concluded that Glucor may improve glycemic control in type 2 diabetic patients.

## bp1007_q1 What is the average percent change in fasting blood glucose across the four participants?
Correct: A
The percent changes are -10.0%, -12.8%, -14.3%, and -5.7%. The average is (-10.0 - 12.8 - 14.3 - 5.7) / 4 = -10.7%.

## bp1007_q2 Which study design best describes this investigation?
Correct: B
The study followed participants forward in time (12 weeks) and measured outcomes, fitting a prospective cohort design. It was not randomized or controlled.

## bp1007_q3 Calculate the percent change in fasting blood glucose for Participant 3.
Correct: A
Percent change = (180 – 210) / 210 × 100 = –14.3%.

## bp1007_q4 Which participant experienced the largest absolute drop in fasting blood glucose?
Correct: C
Participant 3 dropped from 210 to 180 mg/dL, a 30 mg/dL decrease, which is the largest absolute change among the four.

## bp1007_q5 Which phenomenon could partially explain the observed improvement in fasting blood glucose in this single‑arm study?
Correct: B
In a single‑arm, non‑controlled study, improvements may arise from the placebo effect, where participants expect benefit and thus alter behavior or physiology.
