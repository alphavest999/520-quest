# BP1018

Researchers studied the effect of three different cognitive training programs on working memory performance in adults aged 25 to 45. Participants were randomly assigned to one of four groups: a control group that received no training, and three experimental groups that received distinct training protocols (A, B, and C). Each training session lasted 30 minutes and was conducted twice a week for eight weeks. Working memory was assessed using the n-back task before the intervention and immediately after the final session. The mean reaction times (in milliseconds) and standard deviations for each group are presented in Table 1. The researchers hypothesized that all training protocols would reduce reaction times compared to the control, with protocol C expected to produce the greatest improvement due to its emphasis on dual-task processing. Statistical analyses were performed using repeated-measures ANOVA, and post-hoc comparisons were adjusted with the Bonferroni correction.

Table 1: Mean Reaction Times (ms) and Standard Deviations

| Group   | Mean RT (ms) | SD (ms) |
|---------|--------------|---------|
| Control | 650          | 45      |
| A       | 590          | 50      |
| B       | 580          | 48      |
| C       | 530          | 42      |
