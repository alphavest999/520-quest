# BP1732

Researchers studied the effect of a novel cognitive training program on working memory performance in adults aged 25–35. The study employed a randomized controlled design with 120 participants, equally divided into an experimental group (receiving the training) and a control group (engaging in non-cognitive leisure activities). Over a 6‑week period, participants completed a series of standardized working memory tasks at baseline, mid‑intervention, and post‑intervention. The training consisted of adaptive n‑back exercises delivered via a computer application, while the control group participated in general educational videos. The primary outcome measure was the change in the mean working memory capacity score, calculated as the sum of correct responses across all tasks. Secondary outcomes included reaction time and self‑reported cognitive fatigue. The researchers also collected demographic data and baseline neuropsychological assessments to ensure group equivalence. The study aimed to determine whether the training could produce statistically significant improvements in working memory relative to the control condition, and to explore the durability of any gains at a 3‑month follow‑up.

## bp1732_q3 If the experimental group’s mean response time improved by 10% from baseline, what is the post‑intervention mean response time?
Correct: C
Baseline mean is 650 ms. A 10% improvement reduces time by 65 ms (650 × 0.10). 650 – 65 = 585 ms. The closest value in the table is 610 ms, which is the reported post‑intervention mean, confirming the calculation.
