# BP1357

Researchers at Green Valley University investigated how varying light intensity influences the rate of photosynthesis in the common bean (Phaseolus vulgaris). The experiment employed a controlled growth chamber where plants were grown under identical temperature (25 °C), humidity (60 %), and CO₂ concentration (400 ppm). Light intensity was adjusted using calibrated LED panels and measured in lux. After a 48‑hour acclimation period, oxygen evolution was recorded over a 10‑minute interval using a Clark-type electrode. The resulting data are summarized in Table 1. The researchers observed a sigmoidal relationship: photosynthetic rate increased rapidly at low light, plateaued at moderate intensity, and approached an asymptote at high light. These findings suggest that light is a limiting factor for photosynthesis up to a threshold, beyond which other factors (e.g., enzyme activity) constrain the process. The study’s implications extend to optimizing greenhouse lighting and understanding plant responses to changing solar irradiance.
