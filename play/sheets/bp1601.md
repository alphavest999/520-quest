A total of 300 participants were recruited and divided into three age categories: 18‑30 (Young), 31‑50 (Middle), and 51‑70 (Elderly). Each participant completed a standardized computerized reaction time test after ingesting either a placebo or a 200‑mg caffeine dose. The mean reaction times (in milliseconds) and standard deviations were recorded, along with the sample size for each group. The study aimed to determine whether caffeine’s stimulatory effects on cognitive processing vary with age, potentially informing guidelines for safe caffeine intake in older adults.
