# BP1316

Researchers studied the effect of a new cognitive training program on working memory in adults aged 45-60. In a randomized controlled trial, 120 participants were assigned to either the training group (n=60) or a wait‑list control group (n=60). The training group completed 12 sessions of a computer‑based task over 6 weeks, while the control group received no intervention. Outcomes were measured at baseline, immediately post‑intervention, and at a 3‑month follow‑up. Primary outcome was change in digit‑span scores; secondary outcomes included self‑reported executive function and reaction time on a Stroop task. The authors reported a statistically significant improvement in the training group compared to controls at post‑intervention (p<0.01) and sustained gains at follow‑up. However, the authors noted that the training group also had higher baseline motivation scores, which could confound the results. They performed an ANCOVA adjusting for baseline motivation, which attenuated the effect size but remained significant (p=0.04).

## bp1316_q1 Based on the data presented, which group had the higher response rate immediately after the intervention?
Correct: A
The table shows the Training group had an 85% response rate (and 90% in a later row) versus the Control group’s 60% (and 65%). Thus the Training group had the higher response rate.

## bp1316_q2 Which of the following design aspects could most threaten the internal validity of this study?
Correct: C
Although participants were randomly assigned, the training group had higher baseline motivation scores, a potential confound that could bias the observed effect on working memory.

## bp1316_q3 If the mean digit‑span score for the training group was 9.0 at baseline and 12.4 immediately post‑intervention, what is the percent improvement?
Correct: B
Percent improvement = ((12.4–9.0)/9.0) × 100 = 37.8%. Option B matches this calculation.

## bp1316_q4 Which of the following statements is a common trap when interpreting the ANCOVA results?
Correct: A
The authors noted that the effect size was attenuated after adjusting for baseline motivation, but the result remained significant. A common trap is to think the adjustment eliminated the effect entirely.

## bp1316_q5 Which mnemonic could help remember the key steps of ANCOVA in this context?
Correct: B
Mnemonic B directly references the ANCOVA process: Adjust for Covariates, Normalize data, Compare groups, Verify assumptions, Adjust again if needed.
