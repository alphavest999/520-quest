# BP1532

Researchers studied the effect of a novel dietary supplement on cognitive performance in adults aged 35-55. A randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial enrolled 120 participants who were randomly assigned to one of three groups: (A) supplement, (B) placebo, or (C) active control (a standard omega‑3 supplement). Over 12 weeks, participants completed a battery of neuropsychological tests, and mean scores were recorded. The study also measured serum levels of the supplement’s active compound and recorded adverse events. Results indicated that the supplement group achieved a statistically significant improvement in working memory scores compared to placebo, while the omega‑3 control showed modest gains. No serious adverse events were reported. The authors concluded that the supplement could be a viable option for enhancing cognitive function in middle‑aged adults, but further studies are needed to confirm long‑term safety and efficacy.
