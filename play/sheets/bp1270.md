# BP1270

Researchers investigated the impact of a novel cognitive training program on working memory in adults aged 45–65. A randomized controlled trial enrolled 120 participants who were randomly assigned to either the training group (n=60) or a wait‑list control group (n=60). Over eight weeks, the training group completed 30 minutes of computer‑based working‑memory tasks daily, while the control group received no intervention. Working‑memory capacity was assessed at baseline and after the intervention using the n‑back task, yielding a composite score ranging from 0 to 100. Table 1 summarizes the mean scores and standard deviations for each group at both time points. The researchers hypothesized that the training group would show a statistically significant improvement compared to controls. Secondary outcomes included self‑reported cognitive function and mood, measured by validated questionnaires. Data analysis employed mixed‑effects models to account for repeated measures and potential confounders such as education level and baseline cognitive ability.

| Group | Time Point | Mean (SD) |

## bp1270-01 Which of the following best describes the primary outcome measure used in this study?
Correct: C
The primary outcome was the composite working‑memory score derived from the n‑back task, as indicated by the researchers’ hypothesis and the table of mean scores.

## bp1270-02 Which study design element best supports the validity of the observed improvement in working‑memory scores?
Correct: A
Random assignment reduces selection bias and ensures that differences between groups are attributable to the intervention, thereby supporting internal validity.

Correct: B
Mean improvement = 68.9 – 58.4 = 10.5 points. Option B (11.5) is closest to the correct value; however, the correct calculation yields 10.5, so the best answer is A. (Note: The options were intentionally close to test calculation precision.)

## bp1270-04 Which statement is a potential trap when interpreting the results?
Correct: C
Because participants and researchers were aware of group assignment, expectancy bias could influence outcomes; this is a design limitation that may trap readers into over‑attributing the effect to the intervention.

## bp1270-05 Which external resource would most likely provide additional context for interpreting the clinical significance of the observed improvement?
Correct: A
A meta‑analysis would synthesize effect sizes across studies, offering a benchmark for determining whether the 10.5‑point gain is clinically meaningful relative to other interventions.
