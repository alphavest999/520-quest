# BP1730

Researchers studied the effect of different light wavelengths on the growth of the freshwater algae *Chlorella vulgaris*. In a controlled laboratory setting, cultures were exposed to monochromatic light at 450 nm (blue), 520 nm (green), 590 nm (yellow), and 660 nm (red) for 14 days. Growth was measured by daily increases in cell diameter, and statistical significance was assessed using a two‑tailed t‑test. The resulting growth rates and associated p‑values are summarized in Table 1. The researchers hypothesized that wavelengths within the photosynthetically active radiation (PAR) range (400–700 nm) would promote growth, with a peak expected around 520 nm. Their findings confirmed this trend, showing the highest growth at 520 nm and a gradual decline toward the red end of the spectrum. The study highlights the importance of spectral quality in algal cultivation and provides a foundation for optimizing light conditions in biofuel production.

Wavelength (nm) | Growth rate (mm/day) | p‑value
450 | 2.5 | 0.032
520 | 3.8 | <0.001
590 | 3.1 | 0.004
660 | 2.0 | 0.045

## bp1730-001 Which wavelength produced the highest growth rate in the algae cultures?
Correct: B
Table 1 shows that the growth rate at 520 nm (3.8 mm/day) is the highest among the tested wavelengths.

## bp1730-002 Which of the following is the most appropriate control for this experiment?
Correct: A
A broad‑spectrum white light control maintains the same intensity and duration as the experimental treatments but provides all wavelengths, allowing assessment of the specific effect of monochromatic light.

## bp1730-003 If the growth rate at 520 nm is 3.8 mm/day, what is the percent increase in growth rate compared to 450 nm?
Correct: A
Percent increase = ((3.8‑2.5)/2.5) × 100 % = 1.3/2.5 × 100 % = 52 %.
