# BP158 - 2B Ketone bodies

**Passage:** Researchers investigated how varying concentrations of free fatty acid (palmitate) influence ketone body synthesis in isolated rat liver mitochondria. Mitochondria were incubated with a fixed concentration of 3‑hydroxy‑3‑methylglutaryl‑CoA (HMG‑CoA, 1 mM) and 0.5 mM NAD⁺, and the production of β‑hydroxybutyrate (β‑HB) was measured over 30 min. Table 1 lists the rate of β‑HB formation (nmol min⁻¹ mg⁻¹ protein) at four palmitate concentrations (0, 0.25, 0.5, 1.0 mM). The data show a classic Michaelis‑Menten relationship: as palmitate increases, β‑HB production rises steeply and then plateaus near 2.5 nmol min⁻¹ mg⁻¹. The experiment was performed in triplicate, and error bars represent standard deviation. A control assay lacking palmitate confirmed that HMG‑CoA alone produced negligible β‑HB. These results suggest that fatty acid availability limits ketogenesis in the liver during fasting.

**Table:**
| Palmitate (mM) | β‑HB production (nmol min⁻¹ mg⁻¹) |
|----------------|-----------------------------------|
| 0.0 | 0.5 |
| 0.25 | 1.2 |
| 0.5 | 2.0 |
| 1.0 | 2.5 |
