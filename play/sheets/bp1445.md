# BP1445

Researchers studied the effect of three novel antimicrobial peptides on the growth of Staphylococcus aureus in vitro. A total of 120 bacterial cultures were divided into four equal groups: a control group receiving no peptide, and three experimental groups receiving peptide X, peptide Y, or peptide Z at a concentration of 10 µg/mL. After 24 hours of incubation at 37°C, the bacterial counts were enumerated using a standard plate count method. The results, summarized in Table 1, show the mean colony-forming units (CFU) per milliliter and the corresponding standard deviations for each group. Peptide X reduced the bacterial load by approximately 70% compared to the control, while peptide Y achieved a 50% reduction and peptide Z only a 20% reduction. The data suggest that peptide X is the most potent inhibitor among the three tested. However, the variability within the treatment groups indicates that additional experiments are needed to confirm these findings and to determine the optimal dosing regimen. The study provides a foundation for further investigation into the therapeutic potential of these peptides against resistant strains of S. aureus.
