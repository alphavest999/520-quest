# BP1397

Researchers investigated how varying light intensity influences the rate of photosynthetic oxygen evolution in the model plant Arabidopsis thaliana. Leaf discs (5 mm diameter) from 4‑week‑old plants were incubated in a 0.5 M CaCl₂ solution at 25 °C. Light intensity was varied from 50 to 500 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ using a calibrated LED array. Oxygen evolution was measured with a Clark-type electrode over a 10‑min period, and values were expressed as µmol O₂ g⁻¹ FW h⁻¹. Two genotypes were compared: wild‑type (WT) and a mutant lacking the PsbS subunit of the light‑harvesting complex. The data show a sigmoidal response in WT, with a maximum rate of 18.4 µmol O₂ g⁻¹ FW h⁻¹ at 400 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹, whereas the mutant reaches only 12.1 µmol O₂ g⁻¹ FW h⁻¹ under the same conditions. The mutant’s response curve is shifted to the right, indicating a higher light saturation point. These findings suggest that PsbS is essential for efficient photoprotection and optimal photosynthetic performance under high light.
