# BP1002

Researchers at the University of Greenfield investigated the effect of light intensity on the photosynthetic rate of *Arabidopsis thaliana*. The experiment was conducted in a controlled growth chamber where plants were exposed to four different light intensities: 100, 200, 400, and 800 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹. After a 48‑hour acclimation period, the net CO₂ assimilation rate was measured using a portable infrared gas analyzer. The data were summarized in Table 1 below. The researchers noted a nonlinear increase in photosynthetic rate with light intensity, suggesting the presence of a light saturation point. They also observed that the standard deviation increased at the highest light intensity, indicating greater variability among individual plants. The authors concluded that optimizing light intensity could enhance crop yield, but further studies are needed to determine the optimal intensity for different cultivars.

Table 1. Photosynthetic rates of *Arabidopsis thaliana* at four light intensities.

| Light Intensity (µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹) | Average Rate (µmol CO₂ m⁻² s⁻¹) | Standard Deviation |
|--------------------------------------|---------------------------------|--------------------|
| 100                                  | 3.2                             | 0.4                |
| 200                                  | 5.8                             | 0.6                |
| 400                                  | 9.1                             | 0.9                |
| 800                                  | 10.5                            | 1.5                |

## bp1002_q1 Based on Table 1, which of the following best describes the relationship between light intensity and photosynthetic rate?
Correct: B
The data show a rapid increase from 100 to 400 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹, after which the increase from 400 to 800 is smaller, indicating a plateau or saturation point. This pattern matches a nonlinear, saturating relationship.

## bp1002_q2 If the researchers wanted to determine whether light intensity has a statistically significant effect on photosynthetic rate, which experimental design element should they include?
Correct: A
Random assignment controls for confounding variables and ensures that differences in photosynthetic rate are attributable to light intensity rather than plant-to-plant variation.

## bp1002_q3 Calculate the percent increase in average photosynthetic rate from 100 to 200 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹.
Correct: A
Percent increase = [(5.8 – 3.2) / 3.2] × 100 = 2.6 / 3.2 × 100 ≈ 81.25%, which rounds to 80.6%.
