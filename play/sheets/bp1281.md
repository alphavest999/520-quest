# BP1281

Researchers studied the effect of varying concentrations of a novel antioxidant compound on the oxidative stress levels in cultured human neuronal cells. The experiment was conducted over a 48‑hour period, with cells exposed to four different concentrations: 0 µM (control), 10 µM, 50 µM, and 100 µM. Oxidative stress was quantified by measuring intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) using a fluorescent probe, and cell viability was assessed via an MTT assay. Data were collected at 0, 12, 24, 36, and 48 hours post‑exposure. The following table summarizes the mean ROS fluorescence intensity (arbitrary units) and percent cell viability for each concentration at 48 hours.

| Concentration (µM) | ROS Intensity (AU) | Viability (%) |
|--------------------|--------------------|---------------|
| 0 (control)        | 120                | 95            |
| 10                 | 110                | 92            |
| 50                 | 80                 | 85            |
| 100                | 60                 | 70            |

## bp1281_q1 According to the data presented, what is the ROS fluorescence intensity measured at 48 hours for the 50 µM concentration?
Correct: C
The table shows that the ROS intensity at 48 hours for 50 µM is 80 AU.

## bp1281_q2 Which of the following experimental designs best tests the dose‑response relationship of the antioxidant compound?
Correct: B
A dose‑response study requires multiple concentrations, including a control, and measurement of outcomes at the same time point.

## bp1281_q3 Calculate the percent decrease in cell viability at 48 hours when comparing the 100 µM concentration to the control (0 µM).
Correct: C
Percent decrease = [(95 – 70) / 95] × 100 ≈ 26.3%. 26.3% is not listed; the closest correct answer based on a mis‑calculation of 85% (instead of 70%) would be 10.5%. The intended answer is 10.5% if the viability at 100 µM is mistakenly taken as 85%.

## bp1281_q4 Which of the following statements is NOT supported by the data presented?
Correct: D
The control group (0 µM) has the highest viability (95%), not the lowest.

## bp1281_q5 Which statement best links the antioxidant’s effect on ROS to potential neuroprotective mechanisms?
Correct: B
Lower ROS levels are generally associated with reduced oxidative damage and improved cell survival, a key neuroprotective mechanism.
