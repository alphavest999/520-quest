# BP1314

Researchers conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive drug, Hypotensol, in adults with stage 1 hypertension. The study enrolled 120 participants who were randomly assigned to either the Hypotensol group (n=60) or a placebo group (n=60). Baseline systolic and diastolic blood pressures were recorded, and participants were followed for 12 weeks. Blood pressure measurements were taken at baseline, 4 weeks, 8 weeks, and 12 weeks. The primary endpoint was the mean reduction in systolic blood pressure at 12 weeks compared to baseline. Secondary endpoints included changes in diastolic pressure, incidence of adverse events, and adherence rates. Table 1 summarizes the blood pressure data for a subset of four representative patients from the Hypotensol group. The results indicated a mean systolic reduction of 12 mmHg in the treatment group versus 3 mmHg in the placebo group. Diastolic reductions were 8 mmHg versus 2 mmHg, respectively. Adverse events were mild and comparable between groups. These findings suggest Hypotensol effectively lowers blood pressure with a favorable safety profile.

## bp1314_q3 If the mean systolic BP reduction in the placebo group was 3 mmHg with a standard deviation of 4 mmHg, what is the 95% confidence interval for the mean reduction (n = 60)?
Correct: C
Standard error = SD/√n = 4/√60 ≈ 0.516. 95% CI = 3 ± 1.96×0.516 ≈ 3 ± 1.011 → (1.99, 4.01) mmHg.
