# BP1404

Researchers studied the effect of varying light intensity on photosynthetic oxygen evolution in the model plant *Arabidopsis thaliana*. Leaves were exposed to four light intensities (200, 400, 600, and 800 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹) while maintaining constant temperature (22 °C) and CO₂ concentration (400 µmol mol⁻¹). Oxygen evolution was measured using a Clark-type electrode, stomatal conductance was recorded with a porometer, and leaf temperature was monitored with a thermocouple. The experiment aimed to identify the light saturation point and to understand how stomatal behavior correlates with photosynthetic rate under different irradiance levels. The data suggest a rapid increase in oxygen evolution up to 600 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹, after which the rate plateaus, indicating light saturation. Stomatal conductance rises with light intensity but shows a slight decline at the highest intensity, possibly due to heat stress. These findings provide a baseline for subsequent experiments manipulating CO₂ levels or temperature to dissect the interplay between environmental factors and photosynthetic efficiency.

## bp1404_q1 Based on the table, which light intensity resulted in the highest rate of oxygen evolution?
Correct: C
The oxygen evolution values increase from 12.4 to 34.2 µmol O₂ min⁻¹ as light intensity rises to 600 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹. At 800 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ the rate only slightly increases to 35.1, indicating a plateau. Thus, 600 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ yields the highest rate before saturation.

## bp1404_q2 Design a follow‑up experiment to test the effect of CO₂ concentration on oxygen evolution while controlling for light intensity. Which of the following designs best isolates CO₂ as the independent variable?
Correct: A
To isolate CO₂ as the independent variable, all other conditions—including light intensity—must be held constant. Option A fixes light at 600 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ and varies CO₂, allowing direct assessment of CO₂ effects on oxygen evolution.

## bp1404_q4 Which variable is confounded with leaf temperature in this experiment?
Correct: C
Leaf temperature rises slightly at the highest light intensity (23.0 °C vs 22.1 °C at 200 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹). This temperature change can influence stomatal conductance, making stomatal conductance a confounded variable. Light intensity and CO₂ were held constant, and oxygen evolution is the response variable.

## bp1404_q5 Which concept is most directly linked to the observed plateau in oxygen evolution at 600 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹?
Correct: A
The plateau in oxygen evolution indicates that the photosynthetic apparatus has reached its maximum capacity for light utilization—this is the light saturation point. CO₂ compensation and stomatal closure are unrelated to the plateau in this dataset.
