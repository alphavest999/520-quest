# BP1391

Researchers investigated the relationship between light intensity and the rate of photosynthesis in the model plant Arabidopsis thaliana. The experiment was conducted in a controlled growth chamber where temperature, humidity, and CO₂ concentration were held constant at 22 °C, 60 % relative humidity, and 400 ppm, respectively. Leaves were exposed to four discrete light intensities: 100, 200, 400, and 800 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹, each for a 30‑minute acclimation period before measurements were taken. Net CO₂ assimilation rates were recorded using a portable infrared gas analyzer. The resulting data are summarized in Table 1 below. The data show a clear sigmoidal response, with a rapid increase in assimilation at low light levels that plateaus at higher intensities. This pattern is consistent with the light‑saturated phase of photosynthesis, where enzyme activity and electron transport become limiting factors. The researchers plan to extend the study by varying CO₂ concentrations and water availability to explore additional environmental influences on photosynthetic efficiency.
