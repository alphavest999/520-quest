# BP1678

Researchers conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive agent, Hypotensol, on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults with stage 1 hypertension. A total of 120 participants were enrolled and randomly assigned to either the Hypotensol group (n=60) or a placebo group (n=60). Baseline characteristics were comparable: mean age 52.3 ± 8.1 years, mean baseline SBP 138.4 ± 6.2 mmHg. Participants received daily oral Hypotensol 10 mg or matching placebo for 4 weeks. Blood pressure was measured in the seated position after a 5‑minute rest using an automated cuff at baseline and at the end of the study. Table 1 summarizes the mean SBP values and the mean change from baseline for each group. The Hypotensol group exhibited a mean SBP reduction of 12.3 mmHg, whereas the placebo group showed a modest 2.1 mmHg reduction. The difference between groups was statistically significant (p < 0.001). These findings suggest that Hypotensol effectively lowers SBP in this population.
