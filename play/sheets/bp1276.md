# BP1276

Researchers studied the effect of a novel anti-inflammatory compound on cytokine production in cultured human macrophages. Macrophages were isolated from peripheral blood mononuclear cells of 20 healthy volunteers and divided into four experimental groups: (1) untreated control, (2) vehicle-treated, (3) low-dose compound (10 µM), and (4) high-dose compound (50 µM). Cells were stimulated with lipopolysaccharide (LPS) for 24 h, after which supernatants were collected and analyzed for tumor necrosis factor‑α (TNF‑α) and interleukin‑6 (IL‑6) concentrations using ELISA. The study aimed to determine dose‑dependent suppression of pro‑inflammatory cytokines and to assess whether the compound’s effect differed from the vehicle. Data were expressed as mean ± SD, with n = 5 per group. The investigators also measured cell viability via MTT assay to ensure observed cytokine changes were not due to cytotoxicity. Results suggested a significant reduction in TNF‑α and IL‑6 at 50 µM, while 10 µM showed a modest, non‑significant trend. The findings support further investigation of the compound as a potential therapeutic for inflammatory disorders.
