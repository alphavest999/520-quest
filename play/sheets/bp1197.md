# BP1197

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel antihypertensive agent, Hypotensol, on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults with stage 1 hypertension. In a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial, 120 participants were assigned to either Hypotensol (n = 60) or placebo (n = 60) and followed for 12 weeks. Baseline characteristics were similar between groups (mean age 52 ± 9 years; mean baseline SBP 148 ± 8 mmHg). After 12 weeks, the Hypotensol group exhibited a mean SBP reduction of 12.4 mmHg (SD 5.2), whereas the placebo group showed a reduction of 3.1 mmHg (SD 4.7). The difference between groups was statistically significant (p < 0.001). Adverse events were mild and comparable across groups. These findings suggest that Hypotensol effectively lowers SBP in this population, with a favorable safety profile.

## bp1197_q1 Which of the following best describes the primary outcome of the study?
Correct: B
The study’s primary outcome was the change in systolic blood pressure after 12 weeks. The Hypotensol group had a mean reduction of 12.4 mmHg versus 3.1 mmHg in the placebo group, a statistically significant difference (p < 0.001).

## bp1197_q2 Which aspect of the study design best ensures that the observed effect is attributable to Hypotensol?
Correct: B
Random assignment minimizes selection bias and balances known and unknown confounders, allowing attribution of differences to the intervention. The double‑blind design and placebo control further reduce bias, but randomization is the key element for causal inference.

## bp1197_q3 What is the absolute risk reduction (ARR) in systolic blood pressure reduction of ≥10 mmHg between the Hypotensol and placebo groups, assuming 40% of the Hypotensol group and 15% of the placebo group achieved this threshold?
Correct: A
ARR = proportion in treatment group achieving ≥10 mmHg reduction – proportion in placebo group achieving ≥10 mmHg reduction = 0.40 – 0.15 = 0.25.

## bp1197_q5 The results of this study support which of the following clinical guidelines regarding antihypertensive therapy?
Correct: C
Guidelines recommend initiating pharmacologic therapy for stage 1 hypertension when lifestyle measures are insufficient. The study demonstrates that Hypotensol, a novel agent, is effective and safe, supporting its consideration as a treatment option.
