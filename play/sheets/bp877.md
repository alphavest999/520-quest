# BP877

Researchers at the University of Chemville investigated the acid–base properties of the amino acid lysine using a classical titration experiment. In a 25 mL solution of 0.1 M lysine, they added 0.1 M NaOH in 1 mL increments while continuously recording the pH with a calibrated electrode. The titration curve displayed three distinct inflection points corresponding to the deprotonation of the α‑carboxyl group, the α‑amino group, and the ε‑amino side chain. To aid interpretation, the investigators tabulated the volume of NaOH added, the measured pH, the change in pH relative to the previous increment, and a brief comment on the observed behavior. The data set is reproduced below for reference.

The experiment was performed at 25 °C, and all solutions were buffered with 10 mM phosphate to minimize temperature‑dependent pKa shifts. The resulting titration curve and table provide the necessary information to determine the pKa values of lysine and its isoelectric point (pI).

| Volume NaOH (mL) | pH | ΔpH | Comment |
|-------------------|----|------|---------|
| 0.0 | 2.70 | — | Initial acidic solution |
| 1.0 | 3.60 | +0.90 | First inflection |
| 2.0 | 4.55 | +0.95 | Second inflection |
| 3.0 | 5.45 | +0.90 | Third inflection |
| 4.0 | 6.30 | +0.85 | Near neutral |
| 5.0 | 7.10 | +0.80 | Buffering region |
| 6.0 | 7.90 | +0.80 | Near pI |
| 7.0 | 8.70 | +0.80 | Basic region |
| 8.0 | 9.50 | +0.80 | End of titration |


## bp877-01 Which volume of NaOH added corresponds to the first pKa of lysine?
Correct: B
Analogy 10‑yr: Think of a Lego tower that grows taller when you add blocks. The first time it reaches a new height is like the first pKa. Official: The first pKa is the volume where the first inflection point occurs, indicating the first deprotonation event. Why wrong: Choosing 0 mL or later volumes misidentifies the first equivalence point.

## bp877-02 If the researchers added a 0.1 M phosphate buffer to the lysine solution before titration, what would be the most likely effect on the titration curve?
Correct: A
Analogy 10‑yr: Adding a cushion to a mattress makes it softer, so the first bump appears higher. Official: A phosphate buffer stabilizes the pH, raising the apparent pKa values and shifting inflection points to higher pH. Why wrong: The buffer does not change the slope or the volume needed; it only shifts the pH axis.

## bp877-03 Using the data, calculate the isoelectric point (pI) of lysine.
Correct: C
Analogy 10‑yr: The pI is like the middle point of a seesaw; it’s the pH where the positive and negative charges balance. Official: pI ≈ (pKa1 + pKa3)/2 = (3.60 + 5.45)/2 ≈ 4.525, but for lysine the relevant pKa values are 2.18 (α‑COOH) and 10.53 (ε‑NH3+); using the table’s inflections gives 7.90. Why wrong: Choosing a single inflection ignores the two deprotonation events that define the net zero charge.

## bp877-04 Which of the following statements about the titration data is a common unit‑conversion trap?
Correct: A
Analogy 10‑yr: Mixing 1 cup of milk with 1 cup of water is not the same as mixing 1 mL with 1 mL; the sizes matter. Official: The volume of NaOH must be converted to liters to compute moles (0.001 L × 0.1 M = 0.0001 mol). Mis‑converting leads to wrong stoichiometry. Why wrong: The other options involve conceptual errors, not unit conversion.
