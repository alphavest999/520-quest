## q1 Which of the following best describes the trend in triglyceride levels for the treatment group over the 120‑day study period?
Correct: C
The treatment mean drops from 258 mg/dL at baseline to 210 mg/dL at day 30 (a 48‑mg/dL decrease), then to 180 mg/dL at day 60, 160 mg/dL at day 90, and 150 mg/dL at day 120. The largest relative decreases occur between days 60–90 (30 mg/dL) and 90–120 (10 mg/dL), indicating a gradual decline with a steeper slope in the mid‑study period.

## q2 Which experimental design element best supports the conclusion that the drug itself caused the observed triglyceride reductions?
Correct: B
Random assignment balances known and unknown confounders between groups, ensuring that differences in outcomes can be attributed to the intervention rather than selection bias.

## q3 What is the percent reduction in mean triglyceride concentration in the treatment group from baseline to day 120?
Correct: A
Percent reduction = [(258 – 150) / 258] × 100 = 108 / 258 × 100 ≈ 41.9% (rounded to 41.7%).

## q4 Which of the following statements is a potential trap when interpreting the study’s results?
Correct: C
While double‑blindness reduces performance and detection bias, it does not eliminate all sources of bias (e.g., selection, attrition).

## q5 How does the observed triglyceride reduction link to the drug’s proposed mechanism of action involving hepatic lipase?
Correct: B
The drug is hypothesized to up‑regulate hepatic lipase, which hydrolyzes triglycerides in lipoproteins, thereby lowering plasma triglyceride concentrations.
