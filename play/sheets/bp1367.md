# BP1367

Researchers studied the effect of four different dietary supplements on exercise performance in healthy adults. Forty-two volunteers were randomly assigned to one of four groups (A-D) and received the supplement daily for eight weeks. Performance was measured by a 5‑kilometer time trial at baseline and after the intervention. The mean time (in minutes) and standard deviation (SD) for each group are presented in Table 1. The study used a double‑blind, randomized controlled design to minimize bias. Compliance was monitored through weekly pill counts, and adverse events were recorded. The investigators hypothesized that Supplement B would produce the greatest improvement in performance due to its higher concentration of creatine monohydrate. Statistical analysis involved comparing mean changes between groups using one‑way ANOVA followed by post‑hoc Tukey tests. The authors reported a significant difference (p < 0.05) between groups B and C, but not between A and D. They concluded that Supplement B was most effective, though they acknowledged that the clinical relevance of a 1.6‑minute improvement remains uncertain.
