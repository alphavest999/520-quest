Researchers conducted a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial to evaluate the antihypertensive efficacy of a novel compound, Hypotensol, in adults with mild to moderate essential hypertension. Eighty participants were assigned to one of four groups: placebo (n=20), low-dose Hypotensol (10 mg daily, n=20), medium-dose (20 mg daily, n=20), and high-dose (40 mg daily, n=20). Baseline systolic blood pressure (SBP) was measured after a 5-minute seated rest, and follow-up readings were taken at 4 weeks. Table 1 summarizes the mean SBP values and the calculated mean reduction for each group. All participants completed the study, and adherence was verified by pill counts. The investigators also recorded adverse events, noting that dizziness and headache were most common in the high-dose group. Statistical analysis employed ANOVA with post-hoc Tukey tests to compare mean reductions among groups. The results suggested a dose-dependent decrease in SBP, with the high-dose group achieving the greatest mean reduction. These findings support further investigation of Hypotensol as a potential treatment for hypertension.
