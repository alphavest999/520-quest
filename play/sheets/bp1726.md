# BP1726

Researchers studied the effect of a novel dietary supplement on insulin sensitivity in adults with prediabetes. In a double-blind, randomized controlled trial, 120 participants were assigned to either the supplement group (n=60) or a placebo group (n=60) for 12 weeks. Baseline fasting glucose and insulin levels were measured, and the homeostatic model assessment of insulin resistance (HOMA-IR) was calculated. After the intervention, the supplement group showed a mean reduction in HOMA-IR of 1.2 units (SD 0.4), whereas the placebo group exhibited a mean reduction of 0.3 units (SD 0.5). The difference between groups was statistically significant (p=0.001). Secondary outcomes included changes in HbA1c, weight, and lipid profile. The supplement group lost an average of 2.5 kg (SD 1.1), while the placebo group lost 0.8 kg (SD 0.9). No serious adverse events were reported. The authors concluded that the supplement improves insulin sensitivity and modestly reduces body weight in prediabetic adults.
