Researchers studied the effect of a novel antihypertensive drug, Hypotensol, on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults with stage 1 hypertension. Twenty-five participants were enrolled in a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial. Each subject received either Hypotensol (50 mg daily) or a matching placebo for 12 weeks. Baseline SBP was measured after a 5‑minute seated rest. Follow‑up measurements were taken at weeks 4, 8, and 12. The investigators recorded SBP, diastolic BP, heart rate, and reported adverse events. A subset of patients (n=5) had additional laboratory tests (serum creatinine, electrolytes) to assess renal function. The primary outcome was the mean change in SBP from baseline to week 12. Secondary outcomes included the proportion of patients achieving target SBP <140 mmHg and the incidence of adverse events. The study aimed to determine whether Hypotensol could safely lower SBP and improve cardiovascular risk profiles compared to placebo.
