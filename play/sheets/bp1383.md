# BP1383

Researchers studied the effect of three different cognitive training protocols on reaction time in a sample of adult participants. Forty participants were randomly assigned to one of four groups: a control group that received no training, and three treatment groups that received distinct training protocols labeled A, B, and C. After a 4-week intervention, participants completed a standardized reaction time task. The mean response times (in milliseconds) and standard deviations for each group are presented in Table 1. The researchers hypothesized that the training protocols would reduce reaction times relative to the control group, with protocol B expected to produce the greatest improvement due to its emphasis on speeded processing. The data suggest a clear trend of decreasing mean reaction times across the treatment groups, but variability in performance also differed among protocols, raising questions about individual differences in responsiveness to training.

| Group | Mean Response Time (ms) | Standard Deviation (ms) |
|-------|------------------------|--------------------------|
| Control | 350 | 30 |
| Treatment A | 310 | 25 |
| Treatment B | 295 | 20 |
| Treatment C | 330 | 35 |

## bp1383-q1 Which group had the fastest mean reaction time according to Table 1?
Correct: C
Table 1 shows the mean reaction times for each group. Treatment B had the lowest mean (295 ms), indicating the fastest average response time among the groups.

## bp1383-q2 Which experimental design best controls for individual differences in reaction time?
Correct: B
A crossover design allows each participant to experience multiple conditions, thereby controlling for individual differences in baseline reaction time. Randomized controlled trials also control for differences, but a crossover design provides the strongest control for within‑subject variability.

## bp1383-q3 What is the percentage improvement in mean reaction time from the Control group to Treatment B?
Correct: B
Improvement = (350 – 295) / 350 = 55 / 350 ≈ 0.1571, or about 15.7%. The closest answer choice is 15%.

## bp1383-q4 Which statement is NOT supported by the data?
Correct: B
Treatment B actually had the fastest mean (295 ms). Treatment A’s mean was 310 ms, so the statement that Treatment A had the fastest mean is false.

## bp1383-q5 Which concept is illustrated by the reduction in reaction time across the treatment groups?
Correct: A
Habituation refers to a decrease in response to a repeated stimulus. The progressive reduction in reaction time across treatment groups reflects habituation to the task or training protocol.
