# BP1145

Researchers at the University of Northbridge investigated how varying amounts of sleep influence short‑term memory retention. A randomized controlled trial enrolled 120 healthy adults aged 20‑35, who were randomly assigned to one of four sleep‑duration groups: 4 hours, 6 hours, 8 hours, or 10 hours of sleep the night before a standardized memory test. After a 12‑hour retention interval, participants completed a 50‑item word‑list recall test. The mean recall scores (±SD) for each group were recorded. The study aimed to determine whether increasing sleep duration improves recall performance and to identify a potential threshold beyond which additional sleep offers diminishing returns. The researchers also collected subjective sleep quality ratings and controlled for caffeine intake and prior sleep patterns. The results were summarized in Table 1 below, which shows mean recall percentages and standard deviations for each sleep group.

Table 1: Mean Recall % (±SD) by Sleep Duration

| Sleep Duration | Mean Recall % | SD |
|----------------|---------------|----|
| 4 hours        | 52.4          | 8.3 |
| 6 hours        | 65.7          | 7.1 |
| 8 hours        | 78.9          | 6.4 |
| 10 hours       | 80.2          | 5.9 |
