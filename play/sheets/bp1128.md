# BP1128

Researchers studied the impact of light intensity on the growth of the model plant *Arabidopsis thaliana*. Four experimental groups were exposed to different light intensities (200, 400, 800, and 1600 lux) for 21 days. Measurements taken at the end of the period included average shoot height (cm), average root length (cm), and chlorophyll concentration (mg g⁻¹). The results, summarized in Table 1, show a positive correlation between light intensity and shoot height up to 800 lux, after which the growth plateaued. Root length increased steadily with light intensity, while chlorophyll concentration peaked at 800 lux and declined slightly at 1600 lux, suggesting photoinhibition at the highest intensity. These findings suggest that moderate light levels optimize both shoot and root development in *A. thaliana*.

## bp1128_q1 Which of the following best describes the relationship between light intensity and shoot height in *Arabidopsis thaliana* as reported in the study?
Correct: C
The data show shoot height rising from 12.4 cm at 200 lux to 26.3 cm at 800 lux, then only a slight increase to 27.0 cm at 1600 lux, indicating a plateau rather than a decline.

## bp1128_q2 Which experimental design change would best test whether the plateau in shoot height at 1600 lux is due to photoinhibition?
Correct: A
Increasing the light intensity beyond 1600 lux would test if the plateau is due to a threshold effect or photoinhibition. A higher intensity that still shows no further growth would support photoinhibition, whereas continued growth would suggest a different limiting factor.

Correct: B
Percent increase = ((26.3 – 12.4)/12.4) × 100 ≈ 68%.

## bp1128_q5 Which of the following best links the observed photoinhibition at 1600 lux to a broader ecological principle?
Correct: A
Photoinhibition at high light levels reflects the need for plants to adapt structurally (e.g., larger leaves, thicker cuticles) to prevent damage from excess light, a principle observed in high‑light ecosystems.
