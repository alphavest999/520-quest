# BP1589

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel cognitive training program on working memory performance in adults aged 25-45. Participants were randomly assigned to either the training group (n=60) or a control group (n=60) that engaged in non-cognitive leisure activities. Over a 6-week period, both groups completed a battery of standardized memory assessments at baseline, mid-intervention (week 3), and post-intervention (week 6). Table 1 summarizes the mean scores (±SD) for the primary outcome measure, the Digit Span Forward test, at each time point. The training group exhibited a statistically significant improvement from baseline to post-intervention (p<0.01), whereas the control group showed no appreciable change. Secondary analyses indicated that the magnitude of improvement was positively correlated with the number of training sessions attended (r=0.45, p<0.05). These findings suggest that the cognitive training program may enhance working memory capacity in this demographic.
