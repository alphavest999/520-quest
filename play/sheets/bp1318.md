# BP1318

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel probiotic supplement on gut microbiota composition and inflammatory markers in adults with irritable bowel syndrome (IBS). The study enrolled 120 participants randomized to probiotic or placebo for 12 weeks. Stool samples were collected at baseline, 6 weeks, and 12 weeks and analyzed via 16S rRNA sequencing to quantify relative abundance of key bacterial taxa. Serum C‑reactive protein (CRP) and interleukin‑6 (IL‑6) levels were measured at the same time points. Table 1 summarizes the primary outcomes for the probiotic group versus placebo at week 12. The probiotic group showed a 25 % increase in Bifidobacterium spp. relative abundance, a 15 % reduction in Proteobacteria, and a 30 % decrease in serum CRP compared to baseline. In contrast, the placebo group exhibited no significant changes. The authors concluded that the probiotic supplementation may modulate gut microbiota and reduce systemic inflammation in IBS patients.

Table 1: Primary outcomes at week 12
Group | Bifidobacterium % | Proteobacteria % | CRP (mg/L) | p‑value
Probiotic | 12.5 | 25.5 | 3.5 | <0.01
Placebo | 10.2 | 29.8 | 4.9 | 0.45
