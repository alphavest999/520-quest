# BP1344

Researchers investigated the antihypertensive effects of a novel compound, Hypertensol, in a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial. Eighty adults with stage‑1 hypertension were enrolled and randomly assigned to one of four groups: placebo (n = 20), low dose (10 mg/day, n = 20), medium dose (20 mg/day, n = 20), or high dose (40 mg/day, n = 20). Baseline systolic blood pressure (SBP) was measured after a 5‑minute seated rest. Patients received their assigned treatment for 12 weeks, after which SBP was reassessed. The study aimed to determine dose‑response relationships and identify the optimal dose that maximizes SBP reduction while minimizing adverse events. The investigators also collected data on diastolic BP, heart rate, and serum creatinine to evaluate safety. The trial was conducted at three tertiary care centers, and all participants provided informed consent. The primary outcome was the mean change in SBP from baseline to week 12.

## bp1344-01 Which of the following best describes the dose‑response relationship observed in the study?
Correct: B
The mean change in SBP increased progressively from low (-15 mmHg) to medium (-31 mmHg) to high (-40 mmHg) doses, indicating a linear dose‑response relationship.

## bp1344-02 Which study design feature best ensures that the observed SBP changes are attributable to Hypertensol rather than confounding variables?
Correct: A
Randomization balances known and unknown confounders across groups, attributing differences in SBP change to the intervention.

## bp1344-03 What is the absolute reduction in mean SBP for the high‑dose group compared to placebo?
Correct: A
High‑dose change: –40 mmHg; placebo change: –2 mmHg. Absolute difference = 40 – 2 = 38 mmHg.

## bp1344-04 Which of the following statements is a potential trap when interpreting the study’s results?
Correct: C
Although diastolic BP was collected, the passage does not provide those data; assuming they mirror SBP changes is a trap.
