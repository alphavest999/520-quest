# BP1168

Researchers investigated the effect of varying concentrations of the enzyme inhibitor X on the rate of the enzymatic reaction catalyzed by enzyme Y. In a controlled experiment, they prepared four reaction mixtures with inhibitor concentrations of 0 µM (control), 10 µM, 20 µM, and 40 µM. Each mixture contained 1 µM of enzyme Y and 100 µM of substrate Z, maintained at 37 °C in a phosphate buffer (pH 7.4). The reaction progress was monitored spectrophotometrically by measuring the decrease in absorbance at 340 nm, which corresponds to the oxidation of NADH. After a 10‑minute incubation, the final absorbance values were recorded. The data are summarized in Table 1. The researchers calculated the percent inhibition relative to the control and plotted the inhibition curve. They observed a dose‑dependent inhibition pattern, suggesting a competitive mechanism. The study aims to determine the IC50 value of inhibitor X and to compare its potency with that of known inhibitors.

Inhibitor concentration (µM) | Final absorbance (A340) | % change vs control | Reaction rate (µM/min)
0 | 1.00 | 0 | 0.10
10 | 0.90 | 10 | 0.09
20 | 0.75 | 25 | 0.075
40 | 0.50 | 50 | 0.05

## bp1168_q1 Which inhibitor concentration produced the highest percent inhibition?
Correct: C
Percent inhibition increases with inhibitor concentration; 40 µM yields a 50 % inhibition, the highest among the options.

## bp1168_q2 Which of the following modifications would best allow the researchers to determine whether inhibitor X acts competitively?
Correct: A
In a competitive inhibition assay, increasing substrate concentration should reduce the apparent inhibition, revealing a competitive mechanism.

## bp1168_q3 What is the percent inhibition at 20 µM inhibitor concentration?
Correct: B
Percent inhibition = (1 – (0.75/1.00)) × 100 % = 25 %. This matches option B.

## bp1168_q4 Which of the following statements is NOT supported by the data?
Correct: C
The data show 25 % inhibition at 20 µM, not 75 %. Thus option C is incorrect.

## bp1168_q5 Which concept best explains the observed dose‑dependent inhibition pattern?
Correct: A
A dose‑dependent inhibition curve that shifts the apparent Km is characteristic of competitive inhibition, which is described by Michaelis‑Menten kinetics.
