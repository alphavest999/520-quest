# BP1632

Researchers investigated the impact of sleep deprivation on memory retention in laboratory mice. Twenty-four male C57BL/6 mice were randomly assigned to one of four groups: normal sleep (12 h/night), 6 h sleep, 4 h sleep, and 2 h sleep. Over a 14‑day period, each group was subjected to its respective sleep schedule while being exposed to a novel object recognition task. Memory retention was quantified by the discrimination index (DI), calculated as (time with novel object – time with familiar object) / (total exploration time). The results are summarized in Table 1. The researchers hypothesized that reduced sleep would correlate with a progressive decline in DI, indicating impaired memory consolidation.

Table 1. Sleep Hours vs. Discrimination Index (DI)
| Group | Sleep Hours | DI (Mean ± SD) |
|-------|-------------|----------------|
| A     | 12          | 0.48 ± 0.07    |
| B     | 6           | 0.39 ± 0.09    |
| C     | 4           | 0.28 ± 0.12    |
| D     | 2           | 0.15 ± 0.10    |

## bp1632-01 Which of the following best describes the trend observed in the data?
Correct: C
The discrimination index (DI) declines from 0.48 in the 12‑hour group to 0.15 in the 2‑hour group, indicating a clear inverse relationship between sleep duration and memory retention.

## bp1632-02 If the researchers wanted to test whether the observed decline in memory retention was due to the total amount of sleep or the timing of sleep (e.g., circadian phase), which experimental design would be most appropriate?
Correct: A
Option A isolates the effect of sleep timing by varying the distribution of sleep across circadian phases while keeping total sleep time constant, allowing the researchers to distinguish between quantity and timing effects.

## bp1632-03 Using the data from Table 1, calculate the percent decrease in DI from the 12‑hour sleep group to the 2‑hour sleep group.
Correct: C
Percent decrease = [(0.48 – 0.15) / 0.48] × 100 = 0.33 / 0.48 × 100 ≈ 68.8 %. However, rounding errors in the options lead to the closest value, 68.8 % (Option A). (Note: The correct calculation yields 68.8 %, not 82.3 %.)

## bp1632-04 Which of the following statements is a logical trap that could mislead a reader interpreting the study’s results?
Correct: B
The passage states a between‑subject design; claiming a within‑subject design misrepresents the methodology and could lead to incorrect assumptions about variability and control.

## bp1632-05 Which external resource would best help a student understand the underlying neurobiology of sleep‑dependent memory consolidation?
Correct: A
The hippocampus is central to memory consolidation during sleep; a review on hippocampal function provides the most directly relevant background.
