# BP1694

Researchers investigated the effect of a new teaching method on high‑school students’ mathematics performance. They recruited 120 students from three schools and randomly assigned them to one of four groups: Traditional Lecture (TL), Interactive Workshop (IW), Flipped Classroom (FC), and Hybrid Model (HM). Over a 12‑week semester, each group received the same curriculum but differed in instructional approach. At the end of the semester, all students took a standardized math assessment. The results are summarized in Table 1 below.

Table 1. Summary of Assessment Scores

Group | Average Score | Standard Deviation
TL | 78.4 | 6.2
IW | 84.1 | 5.7
FC | 82.3 | 6.0
HM | 86.5 | 5.4

The researchers noted that the Hybrid Model group achieved the highest mean score, while the Interactive Workshop group had a slightly lower mean but a comparable standard deviation. These findings suggest that instructional approach may play a significant role in student achievement.

## bp1694-1 Which group had the highest average score?
Correct: D
The Hybrid Model (HM) group achieved the highest average score of 86.5, surpassing all other groups.

Correct: C
All groups received the same curriculum and assessment; the only systematic difference was the instructional approach, which explains the score variations.

The difference is 84.1 (IW) – 82.3 (FC) = 1.8 points.
