# BP1468

Researchers at the University of Midvale investigated how different visual cues influence reaction time in a simple go/no‑go task. A total of 120 undergraduate participants were randomly assigned to one of four experimental conditions: (1) a red square cue, (2) a blue square cue, (3) a red circle cue, and (4) a blue circle cue. Each participant completed 60 trials, and reaction times were recorded for correct responses. The study aimed to determine whether color or shape had a greater effect on processing speed. The researchers hypothesized that color would be the dominant factor, predicting faster reaction times for the red cues compared to the blue cues, regardless of shape. Data analysis involved comparing mean reaction times across conditions using one‑way ANOVA, followed by post‑hoc Tukey tests. The results showed significant differences between red and blue cues, with red cues yielding faster responses. No significant shape effect was observed, supporting the hypothesis that color modulates visual processing speed more strongly than shape. These findings suggest practical applications for interface design and safety signage where rapid detection is critical.
