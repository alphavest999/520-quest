# BP1381

Researchers at Greenfield University investigated how varying light intensity influences the rate of photosynthesis in spinach (Spinacia oleracea) leaves. The experiment employed a controlled environment chamber where spinach leaf discs were exposed to four discrete light intensities: 100, 200, 400, and 800 µmol photons m‑2 s‑1. Oxygen evolution, a direct proxy for photosynthetic activity, was measured using a Clark-type electrode over a 10‑minute interval at each intensity. The data, summarized in Table 1, show a clear increase in O₂ evolution with light intensity, reaching 73.5 µmol O₂ m‑2 s‑1 at the highest intensity. The researchers also maintained a dark control to confirm that no photosynthetic oxygen was produced in the absence of light. Temperature and CO₂ concentration were held constant at 25 °C and 400 ppm, respectively, to isolate the effect of light. The results suggest a non‑linear relationship, with diminishing returns at higher light levels, indicative of a saturation point in the light‑dependent reactions of photosynthesis. These findings reinforce the importance of optimal light conditions for maximizing photosynthetic efficiency in crop production.
