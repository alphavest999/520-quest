# BP1127

Researchers studied the effects of a novel antioxidant compound, Antiox-1, on cultured human fibroblasts exposed to oxidative stress. Fibroblasts were divided into four groups: a control group receiving no compound, a group treated with Antiox-1 at 10 µM (Treatment A), a group treated with Antiox-1 at 50 µM (Treatment B), and a group treated with a known antioxidant, N-acetylcysteine (NAC) at 20 µM (Treatment C). After 24 hours of exposure to 200 µM hydrogen peroxide, researchers measured cell viability, apoptosis rate, reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels, and mitochondrial membrane potential. Results indicated that Antiox-1 at 50 µM produced the highest cell viability and lowest apoptosis rate, while NAC showed intermediate effects. ROS levels were markedly reduced in both Antiox-1 treatments compared to control, with the 50 µM dose achieving the greatest reduction. Mitochondrial membrane potential was preserved in the Antiox-1 50 µM group, suggesting a protective effect on mitochondrial function. These findings suggest that Antiox-1, particularly at higher concentrations, may offer superior protection against oxidative damage in fibroblasts, warranting further investigation in vivo.
