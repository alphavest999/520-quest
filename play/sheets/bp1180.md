# BP1180

Researchers investigated how different light wavelengths influence the growth of the model plant *Arabidopsis thaliana*. In a controlled greenhouse experiment, plants were exposed to monochromatic light at 400 nm (violet), 500 nm (green), 600 nm (orange), and 700 nm (red) for 14 days. Growth was measured by average shoot length (mm) and chlorophyll a concentration (µg g⁻¹). The results indicated that 700 nm light produced the greatest average shoot length (28 mm) and chlorophyll content (12.5 µg g⁻¹), while 400 nm light yielded the lowest shoot length (15 mm) and chlorophyll content (4.2 µg g⁻¹). The intermediate wavelengths showed moderate values. The researchers concluded that longer wavelengths within the photosynthetically active radiation (PAR) spectrum more effectively promote photosynthetic activity and plant biomass accumulation. They suggested further studies on spectral combinations and intensity gradients to optimize growth conditions for crop species.

## bp1180-2 Which of the following would be the most appropriate control condition for this experiment?
Correct: C
A broad-spectrum white light serves as a baseline that provides all wavelengths within the PAR range, allowing comparison of the effect of individual wavelengths. Dark controls (B) would not isolate wavelength effects, and partial exposure (D) changes duration.

## bp1180-3 What is the percent increase in average shoot length from 400 nm to 700 nm?
Correct: A
Percent increase = ((28 mm – 15 mm) / 15 mm) × 100 % = 13 mm / 15 mm × 100 % ≈ 86.7 %. Option A is the closest.

Correct: C
Plants possess photoreceptors (phytochromes) that are highly sensitive to red light (~700 nm), which regulates growth and development.
