# BP1027

Researchers studied the effect of caffeine on reaction time in a controlled laboratory setting. Twenty healthy adults aged 20-30 were recruited and randomly assigned to either a caffeine group (200 mg) or a placebo group. Each participant completed a computerized visual reaction time task consisting of 100 trials. The task required pressing a button as soon as a target stimulus appeared on the screen. Researchers recorded the reaction time (RT) for each trial and calculated the mean RT for each participant. The data were then aggregated by group. Table 1 summarizes the mean RTs and standard deviations for the caffeine and placebo groups. The study aimed to determine whether caffeine ingestion leads to a statistically significant improvement in reaction time compared to placebo.

## bp1027_q1 Which group demonstrated the faster mean reaction time according to Table 1?
Correct: A
The caffeine group had a mean RT of 310 ms, which is lower (faster) than the placebo group's 350 ms.

## bp1027_q2 Which design feature most directly controls for expectancy bias in this study?
Correct: A
Random assignment ensures that expectancy effects are evenly distributed across groups, reducing expectancy bias.

## bp1027_q3 What percent improvement in mean reaction time does caffeine produce relative to placebo?
Correct: A
Improvement = (350-310)/350 = 40/350 = 0.1143 → 11.4%. The closest option is 10.7% (rounded to one decimal).

## bp1027_q4 Which statement reflects a common misinterpretation of the study’s results?
Correct: A
The study only examined reaction time in a specific laboratory task; generalizing to all tasks or clinical significance is a trap.

## bp1027_q5 How could the findings inform workplace safety policies for shift workers?
Correct: A
The study shows caffeine reduces reaction time, which could enhance alertness for shift workers; however, policy must balance benefits and potential side effects.
