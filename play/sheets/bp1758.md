# BP1758

Researchers studied the effect of a new dietary supplement on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults aged 40–60. A randomized controlled trial (RCT) was conducted with 100 participants, 50 assigned to the supplement group and 50 to a placebo. Baseline SBP was measured after an overnight fast, and participants were followed for 12 weeks. SBP was recorded at baseline, 6 weeks, and 12 weeks. The study aimed to determine whether the supplement could reduce SBP compared to placebo, and whether any reduction was clinically significant. The investigators also collected data on weight, heart rate, and adherence to the supplement regimen. Statistical analysis involved paired t-tests within groups and ANCOVA adjusting for baseline SBP and weight changes. The primary outcome was the mean change in SBP from baseline to 12 weeks.

The investigators randomly assigned participants to supplement or placebo groups and followed them prospectively, which is the hallmark of a randomized controlled trial.
