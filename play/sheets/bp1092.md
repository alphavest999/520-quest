# BP1092

Researchers conducted a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled study to evaluate the antihypertensive effect of a novel compound, Hypertensol, in adults with mild to moderate hypertension. A total of 300 participants were enrolled and randomly assigned to one of three groups: placebo (n = 100), low‑dose Hypertensol (10 mg daily, n = 100), or high‑dose Hypertensol (20 mg daily, n = 100). The primary endpoint was the change in systolic blood pressure (SBP) from baseline to 12 weeks. Baseline characteristics were similar across groups. At week 12, the mean SBP reduction was 2.3 mmHg (SD = 1.1) for placebo, 5.6 mmHg (SD = 1.4) for the low‑dose group, and 9.1 mmHg (SD = 1.7) for the high‑dose group. The study reported a statistically significant dose‑response relationship (p < 0.001). Secondary outcomes included diastolic BP reduction and incidence of adverse events, which were consistent with the primary findings. The investigators concluded that Hypertensol provides a clinically meaningful reduction in SBP, especially at the higher dose, with a favorable safety profile.

## bp1092_Q1 Which treatment group experienced the greatest mean reduction in systolic blood pressure at week 12?
Correct: C
The table shows the highest mean SBP reduction (9.1 mmHg) in the high‑dose Hypertensol group, which is greater than both the low‑dose and placebo groups.

Absolute difference = 9.1 mmHg (high‑dose) – 2.3 mmHg (placebo) = 6.8 mmHg.

## bp1092_Q4 Which of the following statements is a common misinterpretation of the study’s p‑value?
Correct: B
The p‑value indicates the probability of observing the data (or more extreme) if the null hypothesis is true; it does not convey effect size or clinical significance.

## bp1092_Q5 How do the study findings support the proposed mechanism of action of Hypertensol as a selective angiotensin‑II receptor blocker?
Correct: A
A selective angiotensin‑II receptor blocker would be expected to produce a dose‑dependent lowering of BP; the observed dose‑response supports this mechanism.
