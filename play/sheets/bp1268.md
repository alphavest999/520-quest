Researchers at the National Sleep Institute conducted a controlled study to examine the impact of acute sleep deprivation on cognitive performance, specifically reaction time. A total of 120 healthy adults (ages 20–35) were randomly assigned to one of two groups: the Sleep-Deprived Group (SDG) and the Well-Rested Group (WRG). Participants in the SDG were restricted to 4 hours of sleep the night before testing, while WRG participants slept 8 hours. On the morning of the experiment, all subjects completed a computerized simple reaction time task, which required pressing a button as quickly as possible in response to a visual stimulus. The mean reaction times (in milliseconds) and standard deviations for each group were recorded. The study aimed to quantify the decrement in performance attributable to sleep loss and to explore potential implications for occupations that demand rapid responses, such as air traffic control and emergency medicine. The authors also noted that individual variability was high, suggesting that some people may be more resilient to sleep deprivation than others.
