# BP1132

Researchers studied the effect of three different cognitive training programs on working memory performance in adults aged 20-30. The study was a randomized controlled trial with four groups: a control group that received no training, and three treatment groups that received Training A (visual-spatial), Training B (verbal), and Training C (mixed). Each group had 30 participants. After a 4-week intervention, participants completed a standardized working memory task. Table 1 shows the mean reaction times (in milliseconds) and standard deviations for each group. The researchers hypothesized that Training C would yield the greatest improvement, followed by Training A and Training B, with the control group showing the least improvement. The results indicated a significant reduction in reaction time for all training groups compared to control, with the largest effect observed in Training C. The authors concluded that mixed-modality training may be most effective for enhancing working memory in young adults.

Control | 30 | 650 | 45
Training A | 30 | 580 | 50
Training B | 30 | 600 | 48
Training C | 30 | 520 | 55
