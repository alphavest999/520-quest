# BP1617

Researchers at the University of Greenfield investigated the effect of a novel probiotic strain, L. plantarum 123, on the gut microbiome of adult mice. In a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled study, 60 mice were divided into two groups of 30. One group received a daily oral dose of 1 × 10^9 CFU of L. plantarum 123, while the control group received an identical volume of sterile saline. After 8 weeks, fecal samples were collected and analyzed by 16S rRNA sequencing. The probiotic group exhibited a significant increase in the relative abundance of Bifidobacterium spp. (from 12.3% to 28.7%) and a decrease in the Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio (from 3.2 to 1.8). Additionally, the probiotic group showed a 15% reduction in serum lipopolysaccharide (LPS) levels compared to baseline, whereas the control group had no significant change. These findings suggest that L. plantarum 123 may modulate gut microbial composition and reduce endotoxemia in mice.

## bp1617-q1 Based on the data presented, which of the following statements best reflects the effect of L. plantarum 123 on the gut microbiome of the mice?
Correct: C
The probiotic group showed a 15% reduction in serum LPS levels (from 5.2 to 4.4 ng/mL), indicating a decrease in endotoxemia. The other options are incorrect: the Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio decreased, not increased; Bifidobacterium spp. increased, not decreased; and there were significant changes observed.

## bp1617-q2 Which experimental design element best controls for potential confounding variables in this study?
Correct: B
Random assignment ensures that known and unknown confounders are evenly distributed between groups, minimizing bias. A larger sample size would increase power but not control confounding. Measuring LPS only at the end misses baseline differences. Flavoring the solution could introduce another variable.

## bp1617-q3 If the probiotic group’s serum LPS level decreased from 5.2 ng/mL to 4.4 ng/mL, what is the percent reduction in LPS?
Correct: C
Percent reduction = ((5.2 – 4.4) / 5.2) × 100 = (0.8 / 5.2) × 100 ≈ 15.38%, which rounds to 15%.

## bp1617-q4 Which of the following is a potential flaw in the study’s conclusion that L. plantarum 123 reduced endotoxemia?
Correct: A
A plausible alternative explanation for reduced serum LPS is increased gut barrier integrity; without measuring permeability, the causal link remains uncertain. The dose was adequate, diets were identical, and the duration exceeded 4 weeks.

## bp1617-q5 Which of the following best links the study’s findings to potential human health applications?
Correct: B
Reduced endotoxemia and increased beneficial Bifidobacteria are associated with improved gut barrier function and lower inflammation, which are relevant to inflammatory bowel disease. The other options are not directly supported by the data.
