# BP1492

Researchers at the University of Greenfield investigated the impact of a novel dietary supplement, NutriBoost, on the gut microbiome of adult mice. In a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial, 120 mice were divided into three groups: 40 received NutriBoost, 40 received a placebo, and 40 served as a no‑treatment control. Over 12 weeks, fecal samples were collected biweekly and analyzed via 16S rRNA sequencing to quantify relative abundances of major bacterial phyla. The primary outcome was the change in the Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio, a marker associated with metabolic health. Secondary outcomes included alpha diversity (Shannon index), short‑chain fatty acid production, and body weight gain. At week 12, the NutriBoost group exhibited a 15% reduction in the Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio compared to placebo (p < 0.01). Alpha diversity increased by 12% in the supplement group versus a 3% increase in placebo. No significant weight differences were observed among groups. The authors concluded that NutriBoost favorably modulates the gut microbiota, potentially offering metabolic benefits.

## 1 Which of the following best describes the primary outcome of the NutriBoost study?
Correct: B
The study’s primary outcome was the change in the Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio, which decreased by 15% in the NutriBoost group versus placebo. Alpha diversity (Shannon index) and weight gain were secondary outcomes.

## 2 Which experimental design element best ensures that the observed effects are attributable to NutriBoost rather than other variables?
Correct: A
Randomization balances known and unknown confounders across groups, making it more likely that differences are due to the intervention. The other options are aspects of the study but do not directly control for confounding.

## 3 What is the percent change in the Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio for the NutriBoost group from baseline to week 12?
Correct: C
Initial ratio = 1.80; final ratio = 1.53. Percent change = ((1.80‑1.53)/1.80) × 100 ≈ 15% decrease.

## 4 Which of the following statements is a potential trap for interpreting the study’s results?
Correct: A
The placebo group’s ratio actually decreased from 1.78 to 1.65 (≈7% decrease), which is less than the 15% decrease in the NutriBoost group. Some may mistakenly think the placebo had a larger change due to misreading the numbers.

## 5 Link this study to a broader concept in microbiome research: Which of the following best describes the significance of altering the Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio?
Correct: C
The Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio is widely used as an indicator of gut microbial community structure and has been associated with obesity, insulin resistance, and other metabolic disorders.
