Researchers studied the effect of a novel cognitive enhancer on reaction time in healthy adults. In a double‑blind, randomized controlled trial, 120 participants were assigned to one of four groups: placebo, low dose (10 mg), medium dose (20 mg), or high dose (30 mg). Each participant completed a computerized simple reaction time task at baseline and after a 4‑week intervention. The mean reaction times (in seconds) and standard deviations for each group are presented in Table 1. The study aimed to determine whether increasing doses would produce a dose‑response improvement in reaction speed. Secondary outcomes included subjective alertness and adverse events. The researchers hypothesized that higher doses would yield faster reaction times without increasing side effects. Data were analyzed using ANOVA followed by post‑hoc comparisons. The results suggested a statistically significant linear trend across doses, with the high dose group showing the fastest reaction times and the fewest reported adverse events.
