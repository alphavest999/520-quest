# BP1350

Researchers investigated the antimicrobial efficacy of a novel peptide, Peptidex, against *Escherichia coli* in vitro. The study employed a randomized, controlled design with four experimental groups: a negative control (no peptide), and three treatment groups receiving Peptidex at concentrations of 10 µM, 20 µM, and 40 µM. Bacterial growth was quantified after 24 hours by measuring optical density at 600 nm (OD600). The results, summarized in Table 1, show a dose‑dependent decrease in OD600 values. The 10 µM group exhibited a mean OD600 of 0.45 (SD 0.05), 20 µM yielded 0.30 (SD 0.04), and 40 µM produced 0.15 (SD 0.03). The negative control maintained a mean OD600 of 0.80 (SD 0.06). Statistical analysis revealed significant differences (p < 0.01) between each treatment group and the control, but not among the treatment groups themselves. The authors concluded that Peptidex effectively inhibits bacterial growth in a concentration‑dependent manner and suggested further studies to assess its mechanism of action and potential synergy with existing antibiotics.
