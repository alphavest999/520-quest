# BP1101

Researchers conducted a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study to evaluate the efficacy of the novel antihypertensive agent, Hypotensol, in adults with stage 1 hypertension. A total of 120 participants were enrolled and randomly assigned to one of four dosing groups: 10 mg, 20 mg, 40 mg, or placebo. Each participant received the assigned dose once daily for 12 weeks. The primary endpoint was the change from baseline in seated systolic blood pressure (SBP) measured at week 12. Secondary endpoints included diastolic BP change, heart rate, and incidence of adverse events. Baseline characteristics were comparable across groups. Table 1 summarizes the mean SBP reductions and standard deviations for each group. The results demonstrated a dose-dependent decrease in SBP, with the 40 mg group showing the greatest mean reduction of 12.5 mmHg (SD 4.3).
