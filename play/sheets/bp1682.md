Researchers studied the effect of different cognitive training protocols on working memory performance in adults aged 25‑35. In a randomized controlled trial, 120 participants were assigned to one of four groups: a control group receiving no training, a group receiving a 30‑minute working‑memory task daily for 4 weeks, a group receiving a 30‑minute dual‑task training daily for 4 weeks, and a group receiving a combined training (working‑memory plus dual‑task) daily for 4 weeks. Working‑memory performance was assessed using a digit‑span task before and after the intervention. The results, summarized in Table 1, show that the combined training group exhibited the largest improvement in mean digit‑span scores, followed by the dual‑task group, the single working‑memory group, and the control group. The standard deviations indicate that the combined training group had the lowest variability in improvement, suggesting a more consistent effect across participants. These findings suggest that multimodal cognitive training may be more effective than single‑mode training for enhancing working‑memory capacity in young adults.
