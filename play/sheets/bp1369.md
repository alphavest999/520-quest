# BP1369

Researchers investigated how varying doses of caffeine influence reaction time in healthy adults. A randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled study was conducted with 120 participants aged 18–35. Participants were assigned to one of four groups: placebo, 50 mg caffeine, 100 mg caffeine, or 200 mg caffeine. After ingestion, a computerized simple reaction time task was administered, and mean response times were recorded. The study aimed to determine whether higher caffeine doses correlate with faster reaction times and to assess the dose‑response relationship. The findings are summarized in Table 1 below. The researchers noted that while all caffeine groups performed better than placebo, the improvement plateaued at 100 mg, suggesting a threshold effect. These results have implications for optimizing alertness in high‑stakes environments such as aviation and shift work.

Table 1. Mean Reaction Time (s) and Standard Deviation (s) by Caffeine Dose
Group | Mean RT | SD
Placebo | 0.520 | 0.045
50 mg | 0.495 | 0.042
100 mg | 0.480 | 0.040
200 mg | 0.478 | 0.039

## bp1369_q1 Which group exhibited the fastest mean reaction time?
Correct: D
The table shows the mean reaction time for each group. The 200 mg caffeine group has the lowest mean (0.478 s), indicating the fastest reaction time among all groups.
