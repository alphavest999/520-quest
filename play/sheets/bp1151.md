# BP1151

Researchers investigated the effect of four different cognitive training protocols (A, B, C, and D) on reaction time in a simple visual task. Thirty participants were randomly assigned to each protocol. After a 4‑week training period, participants completed a standardized reaction‑time test. The mean reaction times (in seconds) and standard deviations (SD) for each protocol are shown in Table 1. The data suggest that protocol B produced the fastest reaction times, while protocol C was the slowest. The researchers plan to use these findings to recommend a protocol for clinical rehabilitation programs targeting patients with slowed processing speeds.

Treatment | Sample Size | Mean Response Time (s) | SD
A | 30 | 12.5 | 2.3
B | 30 | 10.8 | 1.9
C | 30 | 13.2 | 2.1
D | 30 | 11.5 | 2.0

## bp1151-01 Based on Table 1, which protocol yielded the fastest mean reaction time?
Correct: B
Protocol B has the lowest mean reaction time (10.8 s) compared with A (12.5 s), C (13.2 s), and D (11.5 s).

## bp1151-02 Which experimental design would best determine whether the observed differences in reaction time are caused by the training protocols?
Correct: B
A randomized controlled trial with double‑blinded assessment controls for confounders and bias, allowing causal inference about the effect of each protocol on reaction time.

## bp1151-03 What is the percent improvement in mean reaction time when comparing protocol B to protocol A?
Correct: A
Percent improvement = [(12.5 – 10.8)/12.5] × 100 = 1.7/12.5 × 100 ≈ 13.6%.

## bp1151-04 Which of the following is a common misinterpretation of the data presented in Table 1?
Correct: C
A higher mean reaction time indicates slower responses, so protocol C is actually the least effective, not the most.

## bp1151-05 Which mnemonic could help a clinician remember the correct interpretation of mean reaction time values?
Correct: A
The mnemonic FAST reminds clinicians that a faster (lower) mean reaction time is better, helping avoid the trap of interpreting higher means as superior.
