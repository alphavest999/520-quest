# BP1705

Researchers studied the impact of a novel antioxidant supplement on the endurance performance of 60 trained male cyclists. Participants were randomly assigned to one of three groups: a placebo group (n = 20), a low‑dose group receiving 200 mg/day (n = 20), and a high‑dose group receiving 600 mg/day (n = 20). After a 12‑week supplementation period, all cyclists completed a standardized 30‑km time trial on a calibrated indoor bike. The primary outcome was the change in VO₂max (ml·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹) from baseline to post‑intervention. Secondary outcomes included lactate threshold and perceived exertion. The study also recorded adverse events and compliance rates. Data were analyzed using one‑way ANOVA followed by Tukey post‑hoc tests. The authors concluded that the high‑dose group exhibited a statistically significant greater improvement in VO₂max compared to placebo, while the low‑dose group showed a non‑significant trend toward improvement. The authors suggested that the antioxidant may enhance mitochondrial efficiency, thereby improving endurance capacity.

## bp1705_q1 Which of the following best describes the primary finding of the study?
Correct: B
The table shows a mean ΔVO₂max of 4.3 ml·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹ for the high‑dose group versus 1.2 for placebo, and the authors report a statistically significant difference between these groups. The low‑dose group’s improvement (2.5) was not statistically significant.

## bp1705_q2 If the researchers wanted to determine whether the antioxidant’s effect on VO₂max was dose‑dependent, which experimental design element should be added or modified?
Correct: B
A dose‑response relationship is best evaluated by including intermediate doses to observe a gradient of effects. Adding a 400 mg/day group would allow assessment of whether improvements increase with dose.

## bp1705_q3 What is the approximate percent increase in VO₂max for the high‑dose group relative to the placebo group?
Correct: A
Percent increase = ((4.3 - 1.2) / 1.2) × 100 ≈ 70%. This calculation uses the mean changes reported in the table.

## bp1705_q4 Which of the following statements is a potential trap when interpreting the study’s results?
Correct: B
A non‑significant result does not prove ineffectiveness; it may reflect insufficient power or variability. The other statements are either incorrect (A) or true (C, D).
