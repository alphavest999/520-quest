# BP1669

Researchers studied the impact of a novel cognitive training program on working memory performance in adults aged 25–35. A randomized controlled trial enrolled 120 participants who were randomly assigned to one of four groups: a control group receiving no intervention, Treatment A receiving the training program for 8 weeks, Treatment B receiving the program for 4 weeks, and a placebo group receiving a sham training of equal duration. Working memory was assessed using the n‑back task before and after the intervention. Table 1 summarizes the mean n‑back accuracy and standard deviation for each group at baseline and post‑intervention. The researchers hypothesized that longer training would yield greater improvements, and they planned to analyze the data using repeated‑measures ANOVA. Additionally, they collected demographic variables and assessed adherence rates. The study aimed to determine whether the training effect was dose‑dependent and to evaluate the feasibility of implementing the program in community settings.

## bp1669_q1 Based on Table 1, which group exhibited the greatest absolute improvement in n‑back accuracy from baseline to post‑intervention?
Correct: B
Absolute improvement = Post Mean – Baseline Mean. Treatment A improved from 68.7 to 78.2 (Δ = 9.5), the largest change among all groups.

## bp1669_q2 Which methodological feature of this study most effectively controls for confounding variables?
Correct: A
Random assignment distributes known and unknown confounders evenly across groups, preserving internal validity.

## bp1669_q3 Using the data provided, what is the Cohen's d effect size for the difference between Treatment A and Control groups at post‑intervention?
Correct: D
Cohen's d = (78.2 – 69.0) / √[(4.8² + 5.2²)/2] ≈ 9.2 / 5.0 ≈ 1.8, indicating a very large effect.

## bp1669_q4 Which of the following statements could represent a common misinterpretation of the study's findings?
Correct: A
Although the study is randomized, claiming direct causation without considering other factors (e.g., motivation, testing effect) is a trap. The data support an association, not definitive causation.

## bp1669_q5 The study's use of an intention‑to‑treat analysis is most closely associated with which of the following principles?
Correct: A
Intention‑to‑treat includes all randomized participants regardless of adherence, thereby maintaining the benefits of random assignment and minimizing bias.
