# BP1691

Researchers investigated the influence of light intensity on the rate of photosynthetic oxygen evolution in the model plant Arabidopsis thaliana. Leaf discs (1 cm²) were incubated in a 0.5 M CaCl₂ solution and exposed to monochromatic light at four different intensities: 100, 200, 400, and 800 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹. After a 30‑minute equilibration period, the amount of oxygen produced was measured using a Clark-type electrode. The experiment was replicated three times for each light level, and the mean O₂ evolution rates (µmol O₂ g⁻¹ FW h⁻¹) were recorded. The researchers noted a steep rise in oxygen production up to 400 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹, followed by a plateau at higher intensities, suggesting light saturation of the photosynthetic apparatus. They proposed that the limiting factor beyond 400 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ was the availability of CO₂ rather than photon flux.

## 5 Which concept is most closely linked to the observed light‑saturation phenomenon?
Correct: A
Light saturation reflects the capacity of the light‑dependent reactions (photochemistry) to utilize photons; once saturated, additional light does not increase the rate.
