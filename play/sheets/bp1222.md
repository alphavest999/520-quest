# BP1222

Researchers studied the effect of a novel antihypertensive agent, Hypertensol, on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults with stage 1 hypertension. A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial enrolled 120 participants aged 35-65 years. Participants were randomly assigned to receive either Hypertensol 10 mg once daily or a matching placebo for 4 weeks. Baseline SBP was measured after a 5-minute seated rest using a calibrated sphygmomanometer. After 4 weeks, SBP was reassessed under identical conditions. The investigators also recorded adverse events and compliance. Table 1 summarizes the mean SBP values and changes for both groups. The primary endpoint was the mean change in SBP from baseline to week 4. Secondary endpoints included the proportion of participants achieving SBP < 140 mmHg and the incidence of adverse events. The study aimed to determine whether Hypertensol could provide a clinically meaningful reduction in SBP with an acceptable safety profile.
