Researchers conducted a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive agent, Hypotensol, in adults with stage 1 hypertension. A total of 200 participants were enrolled and randomly assigned to one of four groups: placebo (n=50), low-dose Hypotensol (5 mg, n=50), medium-dose Hypotensol (10 mg, n=50), and high-dose Hypotensol (20 mg, n=50). Over 12 weeks, systolic blood pressure (SBP) was measured at baseline and at the end of the study. Table 1 summarizes the mean change in SBP from baseline to week 12 for each group. The results indicate a dose-dependent reduction in SBP, with the high-dose group exhibiting the greatest mean decrease. The study also monitored adverse events, which were mild and comparable across all groups. These findings suggest that Hypotensol effectively lowers SBP in a dose-responsive manner and warrants further investigation in larger, longer-term trials.
