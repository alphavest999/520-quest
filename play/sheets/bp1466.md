# BP1466

Researchers studied the effect of different concentrations of a novel antioxidant compound on the viability of cultured neuronal cells under oxidative stress. The experiment involved four treatment groups: 0 μM (control), 10 μM, 50 μM, and 100 μM of the compound. Each group was exposed to 200 μM hydrogen peroxide for 2 hours, after which cell viability was assessed using the MTT assay. The results, expressed as a percentage of control viability, are presented in Table 1. The data suggest a dose-dependent protective effect, with the 50 μM group showing the highest viability. However, the 100 μM group exhibited a slight decline compared to the 50 μM group, raising questions about potential toxicity at higher concentrations.

## bp1466-01 Which concentration of the antioxidant compound resulted in the highest cell viability?
Correct: C
The table shows the 50 μM group had a viability of 140% of control, the highest among all groups.

## bp1466-02 Which additional control would most strengthen the study’s conclusions about the antioxidant’s effect?
Correct: C
Including a group treated with the antioxidant alone would show whether the compound has any intrinsic effects on viability independent of oxidative stress.

## bp1466-03 What is the percent increase in viability when the antioxidant concentration increases from 10 μM to 50 μM?
Correct: C
Percent increase = (140-115)/115 × 100 = 25.22%, which rounds to 24%.

## bp1466-04 Which of the following statements is NOT supported by the data presented?
Correct: C
The control group has a viability of 100%, which is higher than the 10 μM group (115%) and lower than the 50 μM group (140%). Thus, it does not show the lowest viability.

## bp1466-05 Which biological concept best explains the observed dose-dependent protective effect of the antioxidant?
Correct: C
The antioxidant likely scavenges reactive oxygen species, reducing oxidative damage in a dose-dependent manner.
