# BP1345

Researchers conducted a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial to evaluate the antihypertensive efficacy of a novel drug, Hypertensol, in adults with mild to moderate hypertension. The study enrolled 200 participants who were randomized to receive either 10 mg, 20 mg, 30 mg, or 40 mg of Hypertensol once daily for 4 weeks. Blood pressure was measured at baseline and at the end of the study. Table 1 summarizes the mean systolic blood pressure (SBP) change (mmHg) and standard deviation (SD) for each dosage group. The investigators hypothesized a dose‑dependent reduction in SBP, with the greatest effect at the highest dose. The data showed a clear trend: the 30 mg group achieved the largest mean reduction, while the 40 mg group had a slightly smaller mean change but greater variability. These findings suggest an optimal dose range and highlight the importance of balancing efficacy with safety in dose‑finding studies.

| Dosage (mg) | Mean SBP change (mmHg) | SD |
|-------------|------------------------|----|
| 10          | 5.0                    | 3.2 |
| 20          | 8.5                    | 3.8 |
| 30          | 12.0                   | 4.0 |
| 40          | 10.5                   | 5.5 |

## bp1345-1 Which dosage group showed the greatest average reduction in systolic blood pressure?
Correct: C
The table indicates that the 30‑mg group had the largest mean SBP reduction of 12.0 mmHg, surpassing all other groups.

## bp1345-2 Which experimental design best controls for placebo effect in this study?
Correct: B
A randomized double‑blind placebo‑controlled design minimizes bias by ensuring neither participants nor investigators know the treatment assignment, effectively controlling for placebo effects.

## bp1345-3 Assuming the 30‑mg group had a mean reduction of 12 mmHg with SD 4 and a sample size of 25, what is the 95% confidence interval for the mean reduction?
Correct: A
Standard error = SD/√n = 4/5 = 0.8. 95% CI = 12 ± 1.96×0.8 = 12 ± 1.568 → (10.432, 13.568), which rounds to 10.5–13.5 mmHg.
