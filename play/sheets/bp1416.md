# BP1416

Researchers investigated how different visual cues affect reaction times in a simple choice task. A total of 120 undergraduate participants were randomly assigned to one of four conditions: Control (no cue), Condition A (a red cue), Condition B (a blue cue), and Condition C (a green cue). Each participant completed 50 trials, and the mean reaction time (RT) and standard deviation (SD) were recorded. The Control group had a mean RT of 650 ms (SD = 45 ms), Condition A had 630 ms (SD = 50 ms), Condition B had 615 ms (SD = 48 ms), and Condition C had 640 ms (SD = 47 ms). The researchers hypothesized that color cues would reduce reaction times compared to the Control. The data were analyzed using a one‑way ANOVA, followed by post‑hoc Tukey tests to identify significant pairwise differences. The results indicated that Condition B produced the fastest responses, significantly faster than Control and Condition C, while Condition A did not differ significantly from Control. The authors concluded that blue visual cues may facilitate faster perceptual processing in this task.
