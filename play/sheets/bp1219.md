Researchers studied the effect of different learning strategies on memory retention in adults. They recruited 120 participants, randomly assigned to four groups: control (no intervention), treatment A (flashcards), treatment B (spaced repetition), and treatment C (dual coding). Each group underwent a 30‑minute training session, followed by a delayed recall test after 24 hours. The mean recall scores and response times are summarized in Table 1. The results suggest that spaced repetition yielded the highest recall accuracy, while flashcards produced the fastest response times. However, the dual coding group showed the greatest variability in scores. These findings have implications for educational practices and cognitive training programs. The researchers also measured response times to assess processing speed and found that while treatment A had the lowest average response time, treatment C had the highest variability, indicating inconsistent performance among participants. Statistical analysis revealed significant differences (p<0.05) between treatment B and the control group, but not between treatment A and C. These results suggest that spaced repetition may be the most effective strategy for enhancing memory retention, whereas flashcards may be beneficial for quick recall but less effective for long‑term retention.
