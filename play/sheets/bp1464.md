# BP1464

Researchers at the University of Newbridge investigated the effect of a novel dietary supplement on insulin sensitivity in adults with prediabetes. The study was a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial enrolling 120 participants (60 per arm). Participants were 45–65 years old, had a BMI of 25–35 kg/m², and had fasting glucose levels of 100–125 mg/dL. Over 12 weeks, the supplement group received 500 mg of the active compound twice daily, while the control group received identical capsules containing inert cellulose. Primary outcomes included changes in the Homeostatic Model Assessment of Insulin Resistance (HOMA‑IR) and fasting insulin levels. Secondary outcomes were changes in waist circumference and lipid profile. At baseline, mean HOMA‑IR was 2.8 (SD 0.4) in both groups. After 12 weeks, the supplement group’s mean HOMA‑IR decreased to 2.2 (SD 0.3), whereas the placebo group’s mean HOMA‑IR decreased to 2.6 (SD 0.4). The authors concluded that the supplement improved insulin sensitivity more than placebo, suggesting potential clinical benefit for prediabetic individuals.
