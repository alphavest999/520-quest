# BP1116

Researchers studied the effect of two cognitive training programs on reaction time in adults aged 20-30. Participants were randomly assigned to one of three groups: a control group that received no training, Treatment A that involved a computer-based speed‑of‑processing task, and Treatment B that involved a mindfulness meditation protocol. After four weeks, participants completed a standardized reaction‑time task. The mean reaction times (in milliseconds) and standard deviations for each group were reported. The study aimed to determine whether the training programs could produce measurable improvements in processing speed and to quantify the magnitude of any observed effects. The authors also discussed potential mechanisms, such as neural plasticity and attentional resource allocation, that might underlie the observed changes. They concluded that Treatment A yielded the greatest improvement, followed by Treatment B, while the control group showed minimal change.

Condition | Mean Response Time (ms) | Standard Deviation
Control   | 350                     | 30
Treatment A | 300                     | 25
Treatment B | 360                     | 35

## bp1116-01 Which condition had the lowest mean reaction time?
Correct: B
Treatment A had a mean of 300 ms, lower than Control (350 ms) and Treatment B (360 ms).

## bp1116-02 Which experimental design best isolates the effect of the training programs?
Correct: B
Randomized assignment to control, Treatment A, and Treatment B controls for confounding variables and isolates the training effect.

## bp1116-03 Calculate Cohen’s d for the difference between Control and Treatment A.
Correct: C
Pooled SD = √[(30²+25²)/2] ≈ 27.6. Difference = 50 ms. Cohen’s d = 50/27.6 ≈ 1.81.

## bp1116-04 Which of the following statements is NOT a valid conclusion from the data?
Correct: B
Treatment B’s mean (360 ms) is higher than Control’s (350 ms), so it is less effective, not more.

## bp1116-05 Which real‑world application best aligns with the study’s findings?
Correct: A
The study directly measured reaction time, a key performance metric for athletes, making this the most relevant application.
