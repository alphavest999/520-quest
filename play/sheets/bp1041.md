# BP1041

Researchers conducted a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of the novel antihypertensive agent, Hypotensol, in adults with stage 1 hypertension. A total of 200 participants (age 35-65 years) were enrolled and randomly assigned to receive either Hypotensol 10 mg daily (n=100) or matching placebo (n=100) for 12 weeks. Baseline characteristics were comparable between groups, with mean systolic blood pressure (SBP) of 145 ± 8 mmHg and diastolic blood pressure (DBP) of 90 ± 6 mmHg. At week 12, the Hypotensol group exhibited a mean reduction in SBP of 12.4 mmHg (SD 5.1), whereas the placebo group showed a reduction of 3.2 mmHg (SD 4.7). The between-group difference was statistically significant (p < 0.001). Secondary outcomes included changes in heart rate and serum creatinine, both of which remained unchanged. Adverse events were mild and comparable across groups. These findings suggest that Hypotensol effectively lowers SBP in hypertensive adults without notable safety concerns.
