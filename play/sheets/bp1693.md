# BP1693

Researchers at the University of Health Sciences conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive agent, Hypertensol, compared with standard therapy in adults with stage 1 hypertension. The study enrolled 200 participants (100 per arm) and measured systolic blood pressure (SBP) at baseline and after 8 weeks of treatment. In addition, adverse events and adherence were recorded. The investigators hypothesized that Hypertensol would produce a greater mean reduction in SBP than standard therapy. The study was double‑blinded, and all participants received a matching placebo capsule to maintain blinding. Data were analyzed on an intention‑to‑treat basis. The results are summarized in Table 1 below. The authors concluded that Hypertensol significantly lowered SBP compared with standard therapy, with a mean difference of 8.4 mm Hg (95% CI 5.1–11.7).

Correct: C
A randomized controlled trial (RCT) with blinding and intention‑to‑treat analysis provides the strongest evidence for causality by minimizing bias and confounding.

## bp1693-04 Which of the following is a potential source of bias that could threaten the validity of the study’s findings?
Correct: A
Even though the study is randomized, if the randomization process was flawed or participants were not truly comparable at baseline, selection bias could occur.

## bp1693-05 The study’s conclusion that Hypertensol significantly lowers SBP compared with standard therapy is most closely linked to which methodological concept?
Correct: C
Intention‑to‑treat analysis preserves the benefits of randomization by including all randomized participants in the analysis regardless of adherence, strengthening causal inference.
