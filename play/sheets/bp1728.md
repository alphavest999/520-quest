# BP1728

Researchers studied the effect of varying concentrations of a novel antimicrobial peptide on the growth of *Pseudomonas aeruginosa* in vitro. The experiment involved 24 wells of a 96‑well microplate, each inoculated with 1×10^5 CFU/mL of bacteria and treated with peptide concentrations of 0, 5, 10, 20, 40, and 80 µg/mL. After 24 hours of incubation at 37 °C, optical density at 600 nm (OD600) was measured. The data revealed a dose‑dependent inhibition, with OD600 values decreasing from 0.95 (control) to 0.12 at 80 µg/mL. The researchers calculated the IC50 value and performed a linear regression of log10(concentration) versus percent inhibition. They also compared the peptide’s efficacy to that of ciprofloxacin under identical conditions. The study concluded that the peptide exhibited superior potency at concentrations below 30 µg/mL, but its activity plateaued at higher doses.

## bp1728-01 Which concentration yielded the highest percent inhibition?
Correct: C
Percent inhibition increases with concentration; the highest value (≈87%) occurs at 80 µg/mL.

## bp1728-02 Which of the following best describes the control used in this experiment?
Correct: B
The control is the well containing bacteria without any peptide, allowing comparison of OD600 to treated wells.

## bp1728-03 If the OD600 at 20 µg/mL is 0.30, calculate the percent inhibition relative to control.
Correct: A
Percent inhibition = (0.95 – 0.30)/0.95 ≈ 0.684 → 68%.

## bp1728-04 Which of the following statements is NOT supported by the data?

## bp1728-05 Which of the following best links the observed plateau in activity to a potential mechanism?
Correct: A
A plateau suggests that all available binding sites on the bacterial membrane are occupied, limiting further inhibition.
