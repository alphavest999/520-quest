# bp003 – Amino Acid Titration

**Passage**  
During a standard titration of an amino acid, a strong base such as NaOH is added to an aqueous solution containing the amino acid. Initially, the titration curve shows a sharp rise in pH when the carboxylate group (\(-\mathrm{COO^-}\)) is protonated to \(-\mathrm{COOH}\). As more base is added, the solution reaches the isoelectric point (pI) where the net charge of the amino acid is zero; at this pH the amino group (\(-\mathrm{NH_3^+}\)) is deprotonated to \(-\mathrm{NH_2}\) while the carboxyl group remains protonated. Beyond the pI, the titration curve rises again as the amino group is fully deprotonated. The shape of the curve depends on the relative pKa values of the carboxyl and amino groups, as well as any ionizable side‑chain groups. Understanding these titration behaviors is essential for predicting protein charge states, designing buffer systems, and interpreting electrophoretic mobility of peptides.

**Table – Representative pKa Values for Selected Amino Acids**

| Amino Acid | Side‑Chain pKa | Carboxyl pKa | Amino pKa | Isoelectric Point (pI) |

| Alanine    | –              | 2.34         | 9.70      | 6.07                   |

## Questions

### 1. Which of the following statements best describes the pH at the isoelectric point (pI) of a typical amino acid that has no ionizable side chain?

A. The solution contains only protonated amino groups.  
B. The carboxyl groups are fully deprotonated, while the amino groups are fully protonated.  
C. The net charge of the amino acid is zero.  
D. The solution is at the pH where the amino group has a pKa of 2.34.  
E. The solution is at a pH lower than the carboxyl pKa.

**Answer: C**

**Explanation:**  
At the isoelectric point the overall charge of the amino acid is zero (equal numbers of positive and negative charges). For an amino acid without an ionizable side chain, the carboxyl group is protonated (neutral) and the amino group is protonated (positive) at pH ≈ pI. The net charge is therefore zero. Options A, B, D, and E mischaracterize the protonation states or the relationship to pKa values.

### 2. During the titration of lysine, the first buffering region (the lowest pH plateau) is primarily associated with which functional group?

A. The α‑carboxyl group  
B. The α‑amino group  
C. The ε‑amino side chain  
D. The α‑hydroxyl group  
E. The carbonyl group of the peptide bond

**Answer: A**

**Explanation:**  
The first buffering region occurs when the α‑carboxyl group (pKa ≈ 2.18) is protonated/deprotonated. The ε‑amino side chain (pKa ≈ 10.53) and the α‑amino group (pKa ≈ 10.53) give buffering regions at higher pH values. Lysine has no α‑hydroxyl or peptide bond carbonyl that act as ionizable groups in the free amino acid.

### 3. Based on the table, which amino acid would have the highest isoelectric point (pI) among the listed options?

A. Glycine  
B. Alanine  
C. Aspartic Acid  
D. Lysine  
E. Arginine

**Answer: E**

**Explanation:**  
The isoelectric point is higher when the amino group has a higher pKa, especially for basic amino acids. Arginine has the highest side‑chain pKa (12.48) and also a high α‑amino pKa, leading to a pI of 10.76, which is higher than that of lysine (9.74) and the neutral amino acids (≈ 6). Aspartic acid, being acidic, has a low pI (≈ 2.77).

### 4. If a buffer is prepared at pH 4.5 using glycine, which form of glycine will dominate in solution?

A. Glycine in its fully protonated form (NH₃⁺–CH₂–COOH)  
B. Glycine in its zwitterionic form (NH₃⁺–CH₂–COO⁻)  
C. Glycine in its fully deprotonated form (NH₂–CH₂–COO⁻)  
D. Glycine in its protonated amino form but deprotonated carboxyl form  
E. Glycine in its deprotonated amino form but protonated carboxyl form

**Answer: B**

**Explanation:**  
At pH 4.5, which is above the carboxyl pKa of glycine (2.34) but below the amino pKa (9.60), the carboxyl group is deprotonated while the amino group remains protonated. Thus, the zwitterionic form (NH₃⁺–CH₂–COO⁻) dominates. Options A and C misrepresent the protonation state of either group; options D and E incorrectly mix protonation states.

### 5. During a titration curve of aspartic acid, why does the pH rise more sharply after the first buffering region compared to glycine?

A. Aspartic acid has a higher α‑carboxyl pKa than glycine.  
B. Aspartic acid lacks an ionizable side chain.  
C. Aspartic acid’s side‑chain carboxyl group introduces an additional buffering region.  
D. Glycine’s α‑amino group has a lower pKa than aspartic acid’s.  
E. Aspartic acid’s α‑amino group is more acidic than glycine’s.

**Answer: C**

**Explanation:**  
Aspartic acid possesses a side‑chain carboxyl group (pKa ≈ 3.65) that provides a second buffering region between the α‑carboxyl and α‑amino groups. Consequently, after the first buffering region (α‑carboxyl), the solution remains buffered until the side‑chain carboxyl group is titrated, delaying the steep rise in pH. Glycine lacks such a side‑chain, so its pH rises more sharply after the single buffering region. Options A, B, D, and E incorrectly describe the chemical features involved.
