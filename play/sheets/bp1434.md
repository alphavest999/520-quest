# BP1434

Researchers studied the effect of three different dietary supplements on the reduction of systolic blood pressure in adults with prehypertension. A randomized controlled trial enrolled 120 participants who were assigned to one of four groups: a control group receiving a placebo, and three treatment groups receiving either supplement A, supplement B, or supplement C. Baseline systolic blood pressure (SBP) was measured for all participants, and SBP was reassessed after four weeks of supplementation. The study found that supplement A lowered SBP by an average of 8.5 mmHg, supplement B by 5.2 mmHg, and supplement C by 3.1 mmHg. The placebo group showed a negligible change of 0.4 mmHg. The authors concluded that supplement A was the most effective in reducing SBP over the short term. The data are summarized in Table 1 below.

## bp1434-01 Based on Table 1, which group experienced the greatest absolute reduction in systolic blood pressure after four weeks?
Correct: B
Supplement A shows the largest absolute decrease of 8.5 mmHg, which is greater than the reductions seen in the other treatment groups and the negligible change in the control group.

## bp1434-02 Which experimental design element best supports the conclusion that supplement A is the most effective?
Correct: D
All three design elements—placebo control, random assignment, and pre/post measurement—contribute to the internal validity of the conclusion that supplement A is most effective.

## bp1434-03 What is the percent reduction in SBP for participants in the supplement B group?
Correct: A
Percent reduction = (Change / Baseline) × 100 = (5.2 / 137) × 100 ≈ 3.8%.

## bp1434-04 Which of the following statements is NOT supported by the data presented?
Correct: B
The placebo group's change was only -0.4 mmHg, which is not clinically significant. The other statements accurately reflect the data.

## bp1434-05 Which of the following best links the study’s findings to broader clinical practice?
Correct: A
The data suggest that supplement A effectively lowers SBP, supporting its consideration as a non-pharmacologic option in prehypertension management.
