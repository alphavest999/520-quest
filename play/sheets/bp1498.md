# BP1498

Researchers studied the effect of a novel antihypertensive drug (Drug X) on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial. A total of 120 adults aged 45–65 were enrolled and randomly assigned to one of four groups: (1) placebo, (2) low‑dose Drug X (25 mg), (3) medium‑dose Drug X (50 mg), and (4) high‑dose Drug X (100 mg). The trial lasted 12 weeks, with SBP measured at baseline and at week 12. Table 1 summarizes the mean change in SBP (mmHg) and standard deviations for each group. The investigators reported a statistically significant dose‑response relationship (p < 0.001).

## 1 What is the mean change in systolic blood pressure for the low‑dose Drug X group?
Correct: B
The table lists the mean SBP change for each group. The low‑dose group (25 mg) shows a mean reduction of –5.8 mmHg.

## 2 Which experimental design feature best supports causal inference that Drug X lowers SBP?
Correct: A
Randomization balances known and unknown confounders across dose groups, providing the strongest evidence for causality.

## 3 Calculate the overall weighted mean change in SBP across all groups.
Correct: A
Weighted mean = Σ(n_i × mean_i) / Σn_i = (30×–1.2 + 30×–5.8 + 30×–9.4 + 30×–13.1) / 120 = –816 / 120 = –6.8 mmHg.

## 4 Which of the following statements is a potential trap based on the reported p‑value?
Correct: B
A p‑value <0.001 means the probability of observing the data (or more extreme) if the null hypothesis is true is <0.1%, not 0.1%. Interpreting it as a 0.1% chance of the effect being real is a common trap.

## 5 How does this study’s finding link to current hypertension management guidelines?
Correct: B
The dose‑response data show greater SBP reduction with higher doses, aligning with guidelines that advocate dose escalation for patients not achieving target BP on lower doses.
