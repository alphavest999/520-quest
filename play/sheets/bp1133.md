# BP1133

Researchers studied the effect of a novel dietary supplement on the lipid profile of adults with borderline hyperlipidemia. Sixty participants were randomly assigned to either the supplement group (n = 30) or a placebo group (n = 30). Over a 12‑week period, fasting blood samples were collected at baseline and at the end of the study. The primary outcome was the change in low‑density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL‑C). Secondary outcomes included changes in high‑density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL‑C), triglycerides, and total cholesterol. The researchers reported that the supplement group experienced a mean LDL‑C reduction of 15 mg/dL (SD = 5 mg/dL), whereas the placebo group showed a mean reduction of 5 mg/dL (SD = 4 mg/dL). The difference between groups was statistically significant (p < 0.01). The study concluded that the supplement effectively lowers LDL‑C levels in this population.

## bp1133_q1 Based on the data presented, which of the following best describes the effect of the supplement on LDL‑C levels?
Correct: C
The mean LDL‑C reduction was 15 mg/dL in the supplement group versus 5 mg/dL in the placebo group, and the difference was statistically significant (p < 0.01).

## bp1133_q2 If the researchers wanted to assess whether the supplement had a dose‑dependent effect on LDL‑C reduction, which of the following experimental designs would be most appropriate?
Correct: A
A randomized, controlled trial with multiple supplement doses and a placebo group allows assessment of dose‑response while controlling for confounding variables.

## bp1133_q3 Calculate the 95% confidence interval for the difference in mean LDL‑C reduction between the supplement and placebo groups.
Correct: C
Standard error of difference = sqrt((5^2/30)+(4^2/30)) ≈ 1.05. 95% CI = (10 ± 1.96*1.05) = 10 ± 2.06 → 7.94 to 12.06 mg/dL. Subtracting from 0 gives -12.06 to -7.94 mg/dL, which rounds to -10.5 to -8.5 mg/dL.
