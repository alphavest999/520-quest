# BP1343

Researchers investigated the effect of varying doses of a novel cognitive enhancer on reaction time in healthy adults. A double‑blind, placebo‑controlled study enrolled 120 participants who were randomly assigned to one of four groups: placebo (n = 30), low dose (5 mg, n = 30), medium dose (10 mg, n = 30), and high dose (20 mg, n = 30). After a single oral administration, participants performed a computerized choice‑reaction task. Mean reaction times (RT) and standard deviations (SD) were recorded. The investigators hypothesized that higher doses would produce faster RTs, but also monitored for potential adverse effects. The study was conducted over a 12‑month period, with interim safety analyses. The results are summarized in Table 3‑4 below. The data provide a basis for dose‑response modeling and future phase‑III trials.

## bp1343_q1 Which of the following best represents the observed trend in mean reaction time across the dose groups?
Correct: B
The mean reaction time decreases from 350 ms in the placebo group to 310 ms in the high‑dose group, indicating faster responses with higher doses.

## bp1343_q2 Which experimental design element best supports the validity of the study’s conclusions about dose‑response?
Correct: B
Random assignment minimizes selection bias and ensures comparable groups, which is essential for attributing differences in reaction time to the dose of the enhancer.

## bp1343_q3 Calculate the absolute difference in mean reaction time between the high‑dose group and the placebo group.
Correct: A
Mean RT for placebo is 350 ms; for high dose it is 310 ms. The absolute difference is 350 – 310 = 40 ms. (Correct answer is 40 ms, option B.)

## bp1343_q4 Which statement is a potential trap when interpreting the study’s findings?
Correct: B
While the study shows an association, the single‑dose design and lack of long‑term follow‑up mean causality cannot be definitively established. The other statements are supported by the data.

The mnemonic REACT (Randomized, Evaluation, Analysis, Control, Timing) captures the essential elements of a robust dose‑response study, aligning with the design described in the passage.
