# BP1341

Researchers conducted a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of the novel antihypertensive agent, Hypotensol, in adults with stage 1 hypertension. A total of 120 participants were enrolled and randomly assigned to either the Hypotensol group (n=60) or the placebo group (n=60). Baseline systolic blood pressure (SBP) was measured after a 5-minute seated rest. Follow-up measurements were taken at 4 weeks and 8 weeks. The study aimed to assess both the magnitude and durability of SBP reduction. Table 1 summarizes the mean SBP values (± SD) for each group at the specified time points. The researchers also recorded adverse events, which were mild and comparable between groups. Statistical analysis involved paired t-tests within groups and independent t-tests between groups, with a significance threshold of p<0.05. The results indicated that Hypotensol produced a statistically significant reduction in SBP compared to placebo at both 4-week and 8-week intervals. The mean baseline SBP was 148 mmHg in the Hypotensol group and 147 mmHg in the placebo group. At 4 weeks, the Hypotensol group showed a mean SBP of 132 mmHg, whereas the placebo group was 145 mmHg. At 8 weeks, the Hypotensol group further decreased to 125 mmHg, while the placebo group remained at 144 mmHg. The standard deviations ranged from 8 to 12 mmHg across all measurements. The study concluded that Hypotensol offers a clinically meaningful reduction in SBP over an 8-week period, with a safety profile comparable to placebo.
