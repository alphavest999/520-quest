Researchers studied the effect of a novel inhibitor, Compound X, on the proliferation of human breast cancer cells in vitro. Cells were exposed to varying concentrations of Compound X (0, 10, 25, 50, and 100 µM) for 48 hours. Cell viability was measured using an MTT assay, and the results were plotted as a dose–response curve. The study also monitored the concentration of Compound X in the culture medium at 0, 12, 24, 36, and 48 hours to assess its stability. The data suggest that Compound X remains largely stable over the 48‑hour period and that its inhibitory effect on cell proliferation follows a sigmoidal dose–response relationship, with an IC50 value near 30 µM. The researchers propose that Compound X could be a promising therapeutic candidate pending further in vivo validation.

## 2 Which of the following best describes an appropriate control for this experiment?
Correct: B
A vehicle‑only control (0 µM Compound X) accounts for any effects of the solvent and establishes the baseline viability against which treated groups are compared.

## 3 If the MTT assay shows a 70% viability at 50 µM Compound X, what percent viability would you expect at 25 µM, assuming a linear interpolation between the 0 µM (100% viability) and 50 µM (70% viability) points?
Correct: A
Linear interpolation: slope = (70-100)/(50-0) = -0.6 per µM. At 25 µM, viability = 100 + (-0.6×25) = 85%.

## 4 Which statement is most likely incorrect regarding the stability of Compound X?
Correct: C
The table shows only a 6 µM drop over 48 hours, indicating that Compound X is not fully degraded by 24 hours; it remains largely stable.

## 5 Which of the following future experiments would best test whether the observed IC50 is due to specific target inhibition rather than nonspecific cytotoxicity?
Correct: B
A rescue experiment that restores the target protein would demonstrate that the effect is mediated through the intended target rather than general toxicity.
