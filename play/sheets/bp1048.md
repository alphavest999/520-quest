# BP1048

Researchers investigated how varying light intensity affects photosynthetic oxygen production in spinach leaves. The study measured oxygen evolution (µmol m⁻² s⁻¹), stomatal conductance (mol m⁻² s⁻¹), and leaf temperature (°C) at five light intensities: 50, 100, 200, 400, and 800 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹. Results indicated that oxygen production increased with light intensity up to 400 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹, after which it plateaued. Stomatal conductance rose steadily across all intensities, while leaf temperature increased modestly, peaking at 800 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹. The data suggest a saturating relationship between light intensity and photosynthetic output, typical of the light‑dependent phase of photosynthesis. These findings are consistent with the Beer‑Lambert law and the concept of photoinhibition at high light levels. The study underscores the importance of optimizing light conditions in controlled‑environment agriculture to maximize yield while avoiding energy waste.
