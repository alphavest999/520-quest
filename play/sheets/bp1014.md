# BP1014

Researchers investigated the effect of different auditory cues on cognitive performance during a sustained attention task. Twenty-four healthy adults (12 males, 12 females, age 22–35) were randomly assigned to one of four groups: Control (no cue), Condition A (continuous low‑frequency tone), Condition B (intermittent high‑frequency tone), and Condition C (continuous white noise). Each participant completed a 30‑minute vigilance task while their reaction times to target stimuli were recorded. The mean reaction time (RT) and standard deviation (SD) for each group are presented in Table 1. The researchers hypothesized that auditory cues would reduce RT variability, thereby improving sustained attention. Data analysis revealed that Condition A produced the fastest mean RT, followed by Condition C, Condition B, and the Control group. Despite the faster mean RT, Condition B exhibited the largest SD, suggesting increased inconsistency. The authors concluded that low‑frequency tones facilitate sustained attention more effectively than high‑frequency tones or white noise, and they suggested future studies examine neural correlates using EEG.

Control | 350 | 30
Condition A | 320 | 25
Condition B | 340 | 35
Condition C | 330 | 28

Correct: B

Correct: B

Correct: B
The findings suggest that low‑frequency auditory cues increase arousal, enhancing sustained attention, aligning with arousal theory.
