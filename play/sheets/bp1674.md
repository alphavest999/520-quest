# BP1674

Researchers conducted a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive agent, Hypotensol, in adults with stage 1 hypertension. One hundred and twenty participants were randomly assigned to receive either Hypotensol (n = 60) or a matching placebo (n = 60) for 4 weeks. Systolic blood pressure (SBP) was measured at baseline and at the end of the study. The investigators reported the following results:

| Group      | N  | Baseline SBP (mmHg) | SBP at 4 weeks (mmHg) | Mean change (mmHg) |
|------------|----|---------------------|-----------------------|---------------------|
| Hypotensol | 60 | 148 ± 12            | 120 ± 10              | –28 ± 8             |
| Placebo    | 60 | 147 ± 13            | 142 ± 11              | –5 ± 7              |

The authors concluded that Hypotensol produced a clinically meaningful reduction in SBP compared with placebo.

## bp1674-01 Which group experienced the greatest mean reduction in systolic blood pressure after 4 weeks?
Correct: A
The mean change in SBP for the Hypotensol group was –28 mmHg, whereas the placebo group had a mean change of –5 mmHg. Therefore, the Hypotensol group had the greater reduction.

## bp1674-02 What type of study design was used in this investigation?
Correct: C
The passage explicitly states that the study was a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial, which is the gold standard for evaluating drug efficacy.

## bp1674-03 Calculate the average percent reduction in systolic blood pressure for the Hypotensol group.
Correct: A
Percent reduction = (28 mmHg ÷ 148 mmHg) × 100 % ≈ 18.9 %. The closest option is 19.1 %.

## bp1674-04 Which of the following statements is NOT supported by the data presented?
Correct: C
Baseline SBP was 148 mmHg for Hypotensol and 147 mmHg for placebo—no clinically meaningful difference. Therefore, statement C is unsupported.

## bp1674-05 Which of the following best links the study’s findings to its clinical implication?
Correct: A
A clinically meaningful reduction in SBP is associated with lower risk of cardiovascular events, making option A the most appropriate link to the study’s conclusion.
