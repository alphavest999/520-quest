# BP1146

Researchers studied the effect of varying environmental parameters on the growth rate of a marine bacterium, *Pseudoalteromonas marina*. The experiment was conducted in triplicate using a controlled incubator. In the control group, the temperature was maintained at 22 °C, the pH at 7.8, the nutrient concentration at 0.5 g L⁻¹, and the incubation time was 48 h. In the experimental group, the temperature was increased to 28 °C, the pH was lowered to 6.5, the nutrient concentration was doubled to 1.0 g L⁻¹, and the incubation time was extended to 72 h. After 72 h, bacterial density was measured using optical density at 600 nm. The control group exhibited an optical density of 0.45, whereas the experimental group reached 1.20. The researchers concluded that the combined changes in temperature, pH, nutrient concentration, and time synergistically enhanced bacterial growth.

## bp1146_q1 Based on the data presented, which environmental parameter exhibited the greatest absolute change between the control and experimental groups?
Correct: D
The absolute change in incubation time is 24 h (48 h to 72 h), which is larger than the changes in temperature (6 °C), pH (1.3 units), or nutrient concentration (0.5 g L⁻¹).

## bp1146_q2 Which modification to the experimental design would most directly test whether temperature alone is responsible for the observed increase in bacterial growth?
Correct: A
Group A isolates temperature as the variable by keeping all other parameters at control levels, allowing assessment of temperature’s independent effect.

## bp1146_q4 Which of the following statements is a potential trap when interpreting the pH data?
Correct: B
A change from 7.8 to 6.5 is a 13‑fold increase in [H⁺] (10^(7.8‑6.5) ≈ 13), not a 13‑fold change in pH value itself.

## bp1146_q5 How could the findings of this study be applied to improve large‑scale production of marine‑derived bioactive compounds?
Correct: B
The study demonstrates that a combination of higher temperature, lower pH, increased nutrients, and longer incubation boosts bacterial growth, which can translate to higher biomass and potentially greater yields of bioactive metabolites in industrial fermentations.
