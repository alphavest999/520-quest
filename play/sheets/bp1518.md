Researchers at the University of Example studied the impact of three distinct instructional interventions on high school students' mathematics achievement. The study involved 240 participants randomly assigned to one of four groups: a control group receiving standard classroom instruction, and three experimental groups each exposed to a different instructional strategy—Intervention A (peer tutoring), Intervention B (technology‑enhanced learning), and Intervention C (flipped classroom). Baseline assessments were administered before the intervention and post‑intervention assessments after an eight‑week period. The table below summarizes the mean scores (out of 100) for each group at both time points. The researchers hypothesized that all experimental groups would outperform the control group, with the greatest gains expected in the technology‑enhanced group. Data analysis included paired‑sample t‑tests and analysis of variance to evaluate within‑group changes and between‑group differences. The findings revealed statistically significant improvements for all experimental groups relative to control, with Intervention B showing the largest mean increase. These results suggest that incorporating technology into mathematics instruction may yield substantial benefits for student learning outcomes.

Control | 68.5 | 70.2
Intervention A | 69.0 | 75.3

## 1 What was the average improvement (post - baseline) for Intervention A?
Correct: B
The mean improvement for Intervention A is calculated as 75.3 – 69.0 = 6.3 points.

## 2 Which experimental design best addresses potential confounding variables in this study?
Correct: B
Randomized controlled trials with random assignment distribute known and unknown confounders evenly across groups, enhancing internal validity.

## 3 Calculate the percent improvement for Intervention B.
Correct: C
Percent improvement = (80.1 – 68.8) / 68.8 × 100 ≈ 16.4%.

## 4 Which statement is false regarding random assignment in this study?
Correct: B
Random assignment reduces confounding but does not eliminate the need for statistical controls; it merely balances them across groups.

## 5 How could the findings of this study inform educational policy?
Correct: A
The study found the largest gains with technology‑enhanced instruction, supporting policy initiatives that promote such interventions.
