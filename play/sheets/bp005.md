# bp005 – DNA Replication  
*(MCAT‑style passage‑based questions – 80 % passage‑based)*  

## Passage  
DNA replication in *E. coli* is a highly coordinated, semi‑conservative process that begins at a single origin of replication (oriC).  The enzyme DnaA first binds to the origin, inducing local unwinding and recruiting the helicase DnaB, which separates the two strands of the double helix.  Single‑strand binding proteins (SSBs) coat the exposed single strands to prevent re‑annealing.  A short RNA primer is synthesized by primase (DnaG) and provides a 3′‑OH group for DNA polymerase III (the main replicative polymerase).  Polymerase III then extends the primer on both the leading and lagging strands, producing a continuous strand and short Okazaki fragments, respectively.  DNA polymerase I removes the RNA primers and replaces them with DNA, while DNA ligase seals the nicks between Okazaki fragments.  The replication fork moves bidirectionally, and the entire chromosome is duplicated in a few minutes under optimal conditions.

| **Enzyme** | **Primary Function** | **Key Feature** |
|------------|----------------------|-----------------|
| DnaA | Initiates replication at oriC | ATP‑dependent binding |
| DnaB (helicase) | Unwinds duplex DNA | 3′→5′ helicase activity |
| SSB | Stabilizes single strands | Prevents secondary structure |
| DnaG (primase) | Synthesizes RNA primers | 5′→3′ RNA polymerase |
| DNA Pol III | Extends primers on both strands | Proofreading 3′→5′ exonuclease |
| DNA Pol I | Removes RNA primers, fills gaps | 5′→3′ polymerase & 5′→3′ exonuclease |
| Ligase | Seals nicks between Okazaki fragments | NAD⁺‑dependent |

## Questions  

### 1.  
Which of the following enzymes is responsible for initiating the unwinding of the DNA duplex at the origin of replication?  

A. DNA polymerase I  
B. DnaB (helicase)  
C. DnaG (primase)  
D. DNA polymerase III  

**Answer:** B. DnaB (helicase)  
**Explanation:** The passage states that “DnaB, the helicase, separates the two strands of the double helix” immediately after DnaA has bound to oriC. DnaB is the enzyme that directly unwinds the DNA.

---

### 2.  
According to the passage, which enzyme performs the proofreading function during strand synthesis?  

A. DnaG (primase)  
B. DNA polymerase I  
C. DNA polymerase III  
D. Ligase  

**Answer:** C. DNA polymerase III  
**Explanation:** The table lists DNA polymerase III as the “main replicative polymerase” and notes its “proofreading 3′→5′ exonuclease” activity. The passage also describes Pol III as the enzyme that extends primers on both strands.

### 3.  
What is the primary role of single‑strand binding proteins (SSBs) during replication?  

A. They synthesize RNA primers.  
B. They prevent the single strands from re‑annealing.  
C. They ligate Okazaki fragments.  
D. They unwind the DNA helix.  

**Answer:** B. They prevent the single strands from re‑annealing.  
**Explanation:** The passage explains that “SSBs coat the exposed single strands to prevent re‑annealing.” None of the other options describe the function of SSBs.

---

### 4.  
Which step in the replication process directly follows the removal of RNA primers by DNA polymerase I?  

A. Synthesis of the leading strand  
B. Ligation of Okazaki fragments  
C. Initiation of replication at oriC  
D. Recruitment of helicase  

**Answer:** B. Ligation of Okazaki fragments  
**Explanation:** After Pol I replaces RNA primers with DNA, the passage notes that “DNA ligase seals the nicks between Okazaki fragments.” Thus, ligation follows primer removal.

### 5.  
The passage describes DNA polymerase III as the “main replicative polymerase.” Which of the following statements best reflects why Pol III is considered essential for efficient replication?  

A. It can synthesize both RNA and DNA primers.  
B. It has high processivity and proofreading capability.  
C. It is the only enzyme that can unwind DNA.  
D. It ligates the final nick between Okazaki fragments.  

**Answer:** B. It has high processivity and proofreading capability.  
**Explanation:** The table highlights Pol III’s high processivity and 3′→5′ exonuclease proofreading, enabling rapid, accurate DNA synthesis. The other enzymes either lack these features or perform different functions.
