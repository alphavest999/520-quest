# BP1634

Researchers studied the effect of three different study techniques on college students’ memory retention. A randomized controlled trial enrolled 120 participants who were assigned to one of four groups: (1) flashcards, (2) spaced repetition, (3) summarization, or (4) control (no structured study). After a 30‑minute study session, participants completed a 20‑item recall test. Table 3‑4 summarizes the mean test scores (out of 20) and standard deviations for each group. The investigators aimed to determine which technique produced the highest average recall and to assess whether the observed differences were statistically significant. The study also collected demographic data to control for potential confounding variables such as prior GPA and study hours per week. The results suggested that spaced repetition yielded the highest mean score, followed by flashcards, summarization, and control. The authors discussed the practical implications for educational settings and proposed future research to explore long‑term retention.
