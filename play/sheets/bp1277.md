# BP1277

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel dietary supplement on blood lipid profiles in adults aged 45–65. In a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial, 120 participants were assigned to either the supplement group (n = 60) or the placebo group (n = 60). After 12 weeks, fasting serum samples were collected and analyzed for total cholesterol (TC), low‑density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL‑C), and high‑density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL‑C). The following table summarizes the mean values (mg/dL) and standard deviations (SD) for each group. The study aimed to determine whether the supplement could significantly lower LDL‑C while maintaining or improving HDL‑C levels compared to placebo.

## bp1277_q1 Which of the following statements best describes the data presented in Table 1?
Correct: A
The supplement group’s mean TC is 190 mg/dL versus 195 mg/dL for placebo, indicating a lower mean TC. LDL‑C and HDL‑C means are slightly lower and higher, respectively, but the question asks for the best overall description, which is the lower TC in the supplement group.

## bp1277_q2 Which of the following best describes an appropriate control group for this study?
Correct: C
A placebo identical in appearance controls for placebo effect and ensures blinding. Option B lacks blinding, A introduces a different dose, and D introduces another active agent.

## bp1277_q3 Calculate the absolute difference in mean LDL‑C between the supplement and placebo groups.
Correct: B
Absolute difference = 125 – 120 = 5 mg/dL.

## bp1277_q4 Which of the following is a common misinterpretation of the data presented?
Correct: D
Overlapping standard deviations do not automatically indicate no effect; statistical testing is required. Options A–C describe reasonable interpretations based on the means.

## bp1277_q5 Which of the following concepts is most directly related to the study’s primary outcome measure?
Correct: B
The study is a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial, a classic example of RCT design, which is the foundational concept for evaluating interventions like the supplement.
