# BP1217

Researchers studied the effect of a novel angiotensin-converting enzyme (ACE) inhibitor on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults with mild hypertension. In a double-blind, randomized controlled trial, 60 participants were assigned to receive either the new drug or placebo for 12 weeks. Baseline SBP was measured after a 5‑minute seated rest, and follow‑up SBP was recorded at week 12. The primary endpoint was the mean change in SBP from baseline to week 12. Secondary endpoints included the proportion of participants achieving a target SBP <140 mmHg and the incidence of adverse events. The study found that the drug group experienced a mean SBP reduction of 12.5 mmHg compared with 3.2 mmHg in the placebo group (p<0.001). No serious adverse events were reported. These findings suggest the new ACE inhibitor effectively lowers SBP in mild hypertension with a favorable safety profile.

## bp1217-004 Which of the following biases could threaten the validity of the study results?
Correct: A
Randomization and blinding minimize selection and observer bias. Recall bias is irrelevant because outcomes are objective measurements of blood pressure.

## bp1217-005 Which mechanism best explains the blood pressure reduction observed with the new drug?
Correct: A
The drug is described as a novel ACE inhibitor, and inhibition of ACE reduces angiotensin II formation, leading to vasodilation and decreased blood pressure.
