# BP1086

Researchers at the National Institute of Sleep Studies investigated the impact of sleep deprivation on cognitive performance. Eighty healthy adults were recruited and randomly assigned to one of five sleep conditions: 8, 6, 4, 2, or 0 hours of sleep the night before testing. All participants completed a computerized psychomotor vigilance task (PVT) the following morning, and mean reaction times (RT) were recorded. The study also measured subjective sleepiness using the Stanford Sleepiness Scale (SSS). The researchers found a clear dose–response relationship: as sleep hours decreased, mean RT increased and SSS scores rose. The 0‑hour group exhibited the slowest RT (mean 520 ms, SD 45 ms) and the highest SSS score (mean 7.8). Conversely, the 8‑hour group had the fastest RT (mean 310 ms, SD 30 ms) and the lowest SSS score (mean 2.1). These findings suggest that even moderate sleep loss can impair reaction time and increase sleepiness, with implications for occupations requiring sustained attention.
