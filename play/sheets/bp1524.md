# BP1524

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel glucose‑lowering peptide on insulin sensitivity in a cohort of 120 overweight adults with pre‑diabetes. Participants were randomly assigned to either the peptide group (n=60) or a placebo group (n=60) and followed for 12 weeks. Baseline characteristics were comparable between groups. Blood samples were collected at baseline, week 4, week 8, and week 12 to measure fasting plasma glucose and insulin levels. The peptide was administered subcutaneously at a dose of 0.5 mg/kg once daily. Adherence was monitored via electronic injection devices. The primary outcome was the change in the Homeostatic Model Assessment of Insulin Resistance (HOMA‑IR) from baseline to week 12. Secondary outcomes included changes in fasting glucose, HbA1c, and lipid profile. Safety was assessed by recording adverse events and monitoring liver function tests. The study was approved by the institutional review board and all participants provided written informed consent.

Correct: D
Randomization, blinding, and a placebo control together minimize bias and confounding, making it most likely that any observed effect is due to the peptide.

## 4 Which of the following statements is a common trap when interpreting HOMA‑IR changes?
Correct: B
HOMA‑IR is calculated from fasting glucose and insulin; it is not independent of fasting glucose. Misinterpreting it as such is a common trap.
