# BP1229

Researchers at the University of Greenfield investigated the effect of a novel dietary supplement, NutriBoost, on the metabolic rate of adult mice. The study enrolled 60 mice, randomly assigned to three groups: a control group receiving standard chow (n=20), a low-dose group receiving 5 mg/kg of NutriBoost (n=20), and a high-dose group receiving 15 mg/kg of NutriBoost (n=20). Over a 12-week period, researchers measured the resting metabolic rate (RMR) of each mouse weekly using indirect calorimetry. The table below summarizes the mean RMR (in kilocalories per day) and standard deviation (SD) for each group at the end of the study.

Table 3-4
| Group          | Mean RMR (kcal/day) | SD (kcal/day) |
|----------------|---------------------|----------------|
| Control        | 12.4                | 1.2            |
| Low-dose       | 13.1                | 1.4            |
| High-dose      | 14.5                | 1.6            |

## bp1229-q1 Based on the data presented, which statement best describes the effect of NutriBoost on the mice’s resting metabolic rate?
Correct: C
The mean RMR values show a clear dose-dependent increase: 12.4 (control) < 13.1 (low-dose) < 14.5 (high-dose). Both experimental groups exceed the control, and the high-dose group has the largest mean difference, indicating a dose-dependent effect.

## bp1229-q2 Which of the following best describes an appropriate control for this experiment?
Correct: A
A placebo group that receives an inert substance identical in appearance controls for the effect of handling and the psychological impact of supplementation, isolating the effect of NutriBoost itself.
