# BP1264

Researchers studied the effect of a novel antibiotic on the growth of *Staphylococcus aureus* in vitro. The experiment involved two groups: a treated group receiving 10 µg/mL of the antibiotic and an untreated control group. Samples were collected at 0, 3, 7, and 14 days, and bacterial counts were measured in colony forming units per milliliter (CFU/mL). The data are summarized in Table 1. The antibiotic-treated group exhibited a marked reduction in bacterial load compared to the control, with the most pronounced effect observed at day 7. By day 14, the treated group’s bacterial count had fallen to near the detection limit, whereas the control group’s count increased exponentially. These findings suggest that the antibiotic is highly effective in suppressing bacterial proliferation over a two-week period. The study’s design, including random assignment and duplicate measurements, supports the reliability of the observed differences. The implications for clinical practice are significant, as the antibiotic could potentially reduce the duration of infection and lower the risk of resistance development.

Table 1: Bacterial counts over time for treated and untreated groups

| Day | Untreated CFU/mL | Treated CFU/mL |
|-----|------------------|----------------|
| 0   | 1.0×10^6         | 1.0×10^6       |
| 3   | 4.0×10^6         | 2.5×10^6       |
| 7   | 1.1×10^7         | 1.1×10^7       |
| 14  | 3.4×10^7         | 5.0×10^3       |

## q3 If the bacterial count on day 3 is 4.0×10⁶ CFU/mL and on day 7 is 1.1×10⁷ CFU/mL, what is the average daily growth rate (assuming exponential growth)?
Correct: B
Growth factor = 1.1×10⁷ / 4.0×10⁶ = 2.75 over 4 days. Daily factor = 2.75^(1/4) ≈ 1.27, corresponding to a 27% daily increase.
