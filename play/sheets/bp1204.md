# BP1204

Researchers studied the effect of caffeine on cognitive performance in a controlled experiment. Thirty healthy adults were randomly assigned to one of four conditions: no caffeine (Placebo), 50 mg caffeine, 100 mg caffeine, and 150 mg caffeine. Each participant completed a series of reaction‑time tasks while their responses were recorded. ## bp1204_q1 Which condition had the fastest average response time?
Correct: C
The mean response time for the 100 mg caffeine condition (395 ms) is lower than all other conditions, indicating the fastest average reaction time.

## bp1204_q2 Which experimental design best controls for placebo effect in this study?
Correct: B
A double‑blind randomized controlled trial ensures that neither participants nor researchers know which dose is administered, thereby minimizing expectancy and observer bias.

## bp1204_q3 What percent improvement in mean response time does the 100 mg caffeine condition represent over the placebo condition?
Correct: D
Improvement = (450 ms – 395 ms) / 450 ms × 100 ≈ 12.2%, which rounds to 12%.

## bp1204_q4 Which statement is NOT supported by the data presented?
Correct: B
While 50 mg and 100 mg doses lower response time, the 150 mg dose does not produce a faster mean than 100 mg, indicating a plateau rather than a continuous improvement.

## bp1204_q5 The findings of this study most closely illustrate which of the following concepts?
Correct: B
The data show a clear relationship between caffeine dose and reaction time up to a point, demonstrating a dose‑response effect.
