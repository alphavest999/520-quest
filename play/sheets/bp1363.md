# BP1363

Researchers studied the effect of a novel dietary supplement on resting metabolic rate (RMR) in healthy adults. Eighty participants were randomly assigned to either the supplement group (n = 40) or a placebo group (n = 40). RMR was measured using indirect calorimetry after an overnight fast. The supplement group received 500 mg of the active ingredient daily for 12 weeks, while the placebo group received an inert capsule. Table 1 summarizes the mean RMR (kcal/day) and standard deviation for each group at baseline and after the intervention. The researchers hypothesized that the supplement would increase RMR by at least 10% compared to placebo.

Table 1. Mean RMR (kcal/day) and SD
Group | Baseline | 12‑week
Supplement | 1500 (±200) | 1650 (±210)
Placebo | 1490 (±190) | 1510 (±195)

## q1 Based on Table 1, what is the absolute increase in mean RMR for the supplement group after 12 weeks?
Correct: B
The supplement group’s mean RMR increased from 1500 to 1650 kcal/day, an absolute increase of 150 kcal/day. Option B reflects the correct calculation of 1650 - 1500 = 150.

## q2 Which of the following best describes the study’s design?
Correct: B
Participants were randomly assigned to supplement or placebo groups, and the outcome (RMR) was measured after a defined intervention period, which is the hallmark of a randomized controlled trial.

## q3 What is the percent increase in mean RMR for the supplement group after 12 weeks?
Correct: B
Percent increase = (1650 – 1500) / 1500 × 100% = 150 / 1500 × 100% = 10%. Option B correctly reflects this calculation.

## q4 Which of the following statements is NOT supported by the data presented?
Correct: D
The passage does not mention blinding; therefore, we cannot conclude the study was double‑blind. The other statements are directly supported by Table 1.

## q5 Which concept best explains why the researchers used a placebo group?
Correct: B
A placebo group serves as a control to account for confounding variables and natural changes over time, allowing the researchers to attribute observed effects specifically to the supplement.
