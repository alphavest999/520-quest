# BP1149

Researchers at the Cognitive Neuroscience Laboratory examined how different auditory cues influence reaction time in a simple go/no-go task. Twenty-four adult volunteers were randomly assigned to one of four conditions: (1) no auditory cue (control), (2) a low-frequency tone (200 Hz), (3) a high-frequency tone (2000 Hz), or (4) a broadband noise burst. Each participant completed 120 trials, with reaction times recorded for correct go responses. Table 1 summarizes the mean reaction times and standard deviations for each group. The investigators hypothesized that the high-frequency tone would produce the fastest responses due to its salience, while the broadband noise would have the least effect. Statistical analysis (one-way ANOVA) revealed a significant main effect of condition (F(3,20)=5.67, p=0.005). Post-hoc comparisons indicated that the high-frequency group was significantly faster than the control (p=0.012) and the broadband group (p=0.021), but not significantly different from the low-frequency group (p=0.34). The authors concluded that frequency-specific auditory cues can modulate attentional readiness, with the high-frequency tone providing the greatest facilitation.
