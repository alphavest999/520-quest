# BP1160

Researchers investigated the antibacterial activity of a novel amphipathic peptide, designated AP‑42, against *Escherichia coli* cultured under controlled laboratory conditions. The study employed a factorial design with two independent variables: peptide concentration (0, 5, 10, 20, 40 µg/mL) and medium pH (5.5, 7.0, 8.5). For each treatment combination, bacterial growth was monitored by measuring optical density at 600 nm (OD600) over a 24‑hour period, and the maximum specific growth rate (μmax) was calculated from the exponential phase. The results, summarized in Table 1, indicate a dose‑dependent inhibition of growth at neutral and alkaline pH, whereas acidic pH attenuates the peptide’s efficacy. The authors concluded that AP‑42 exhibits potent antibacterial activity, particularly at pH 7.0 and 8.5, and suggested its potential as a therapeutic agent for infections in body sites with neutral to slightly alkaline pH. The data also raise questions about the peptide’s mechanism of action and its stability across pH ranges.

## bp1160_q1 Which peptide concentration at pH 7.0 produced the lowest μmax?
Correct: D
At pH 7.0 the μmax values decrease with increasing peptide concentration. The lowest μmax (0.15 h⁻¹) occurs at the highest concentration tested, 40 µg/mL.

## bp1160_q2 Which of the following best describes the experimental design used in this study?
Correct: B
The investigators varied two independent variables (peptide concentration and pH) simultaneously and measured the outcome for each combination, which is the hallmark of a factorial design.

## bp1160_q3 If the μmax at 10 µg/mL and pH 7.0 is 0.45 h⁻¹, what is the percent reduction in μmax compared to the control (0 µg/mL) at the same pH, given the control μmax is 1.20 h⁻¹?
Correct: D
Reduction = (1.20 – 0.45)/1.20 = 0.75/1.20 = 0.625 = 62.5%.

## bp1160_q4 Which statement is most likely incorrect regarding the peptide’s activity?
Correct: B
The data show a clear dose‑dependent inhibition; activity is not independent of concentration.

## bp1160_q5 Which additional experiment would best help elucidate the mechanism of action of AP‑42?
Correct: C
Measuring changes in membrane potential directly probes whether the peptide disrupts bacterial membranes, a common antibacterial mechanism for amphipathic peptides.
