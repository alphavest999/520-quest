# BP1150

Researchers investigated the impact of a novel probiotic strain, *Lactobacillus fermentum* 123, on gut microbiome composition and symptom severity in adults diagnosed with irritable bowel syndrome (IBS). In a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial, 120 participants were assigned to receive either the probiotic or a matched placebo for 12 weeks. Stool samples were collected at baseline, 6 weeks, and 12 weeks, and 16S rRNA sequencing was performed to assess microbial diversity. The primary clinical endpoint was the change in IBS Symptom Severity Score (IBS‑SSS), a validated 100‑point questionnaire. Secondary endpoints included fecal calprotectin levels and quality‑of‑life measures. The probiotic group exhibited a mean reduction of 45 points in IBS‑SSS versus 18 points in the placebo group, with a statistically significant difference (p < 0.01). Microbiome analysis revealed increased relative abundance of *Bifidobacterium longum* and decreased *Clostridium difficile* in the probiotic group. No serious adverse events were reported. These findings suggest that *L. fermentum* 123 may alleviate IBS symptoms by modulating gut microbial communities and reducing intestinal inflammation.

## bp1150-02 Which experimental design feature best ensures that observed symptom improvements are attributable to the probiotic?
Correct: B
Random assignment to probiotic or placebo groups minimizes selection bias and balances known and unknown confounders, allowing attribution of differences to the intervention.

The study shows a decrease in relative abundance of *C. difficile*, not complete elimination. The probiotic’s effect is associative, not a direct eradication.
