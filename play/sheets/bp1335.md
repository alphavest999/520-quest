Researchers conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive agent, CardioX, in adults with stage 1 hypertension. A total of 120 participants were enrolled and randomly assigned to one of three groups: CardioX 10 mg daily (n=40), CardioX 20 mg daily (n=40), or placebo (n=40). The study duration was 12 weeks, with systolic blood pressure (SBP) measured at baseline and at the end of the study. Table 1 summarizes the mean change in SBP and standard deviation for each group. The investigators hypothesized that the higher dose of CardioX would produce a greater reduction in SBP compared to the lower dose and placebo. In addition to SBP, diastolic blood pressure and adverse events were recorded, but the primary outcome remained the change in SBP. The results indicated a dose‑dependent effect, with the 20 mg group showing the largest mean reduction. The safety profile was comparable across all groups, with no serious adverse events reported. These findings suggest that CardioX may be an effective option for managing mild hypertension, pending further studies on long‑term outcomes.
