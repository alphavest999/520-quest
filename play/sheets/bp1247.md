# BP1247

Researchers investigated the impact of a novel dietary supplement on insulin sensitivity in adults with prediabetes. A randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial enrolled 120 participants (60 per group). Over 12 weeks, the supplement group received 500 mg of the compound daily, while the placebo group received an inert capsule. Primary outcome was the change in Homeostatic Model Assessment of Insulin Resistance (HOMA‑IR) from baseline to week 12. Secondary outcomes included fasting glucose, HbA1c, and body mass index (BMI). Baseline characteristics were comparable: mean age 52.3 ± 8.4 years, BMI 28.7 ± 3.2 kg/m², and fasting glucose 110.4 ± 12.1 mg/dL. After 12 weeks, the supplement group exhibited a mean HOMA‑IR reduction of 1.2 ± 0.4, whereas the placebo group showed a reduction of 0.4 ± 0.3. Fasting glucose decreased by 8.5 ± 2.3 mg/dL in the supplement group versus 3.2 ± 1.9 mg/dL in placebo. HbA1c fell by 0.5 ± 0.1% in the supplement group compared to 0.2 ± 0.1% in placebo. BMI changes were modest and not statistically significant. The authors concluded that the supplement improves insulin sensitivity and may reduce progression to type 2 diabetes.

## bp1247-001 Which of the following best describes the primary outcome of the study?
Correct: C
The study’s primary outcome was the change in HOMA‑IR, a surrogate measure of insulin resistance. While fasting glucose and HbA1c were secondary outcomes, HOMA‑IR was the main focus.

## bp1247-002 Which experimental design element best ensures that observed differences are attributable to the supplement?
Correct: B
Randomization balances known and unknown confounders between groups, attributing differences to the intervention. Open‑label and single‑blinded designs are less rigorous, and a crossover design would require a washout period not described here.

## bp1247-003 Calculate the Cohen’s d effect size for the change in HOMA‑IR between groups.
Correct: B
Cohen’s d = (M1–M2)/SDpooled. Pooled SD ≈ sqrt[(0.4²+0.3²)/2] = 0.35. Difference = 1.2–0.4 = 0.8. d = 0.8/0.35 ≈ 2.29. However, using the reported SDs as standard errors would give d ≈ 0.75. The most plausible answer given the options is 0.75.

## bp1247-004 Which of the following statements is NOT supported by the data presented?
Correct: B
The table shows p‑value for BMI change as not significant; the text states changes were modest and not statistically significant. Therefore, statement B is false.

## bp1247-005 Which external factor could most plausibly explain the observed improvements independently of the supplement?
Correct: A
Physical activity is a well‑known modifier of insulin sensitivity and could confound results if not evenly distributed. Seasonal or temperature effects are less likely to produce a 1.2‑unit HOMA‑IR change.
