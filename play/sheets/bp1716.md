Researchers conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the antihypertensive effect of a novel drug, Hypertol, in adults with stage 1 hypertension. Three groups were studied: a placebo group (n = 50), a low‑dose Hypertol group (5 mg daily, n = 50), and a high‑dose Hypertol group (10 mg daily, n = 50). Baseline systolic blood pressure (SBP) averaged 150 mmHg across all participants. After 12 weeks, the mean change in SBP (± SD) was −2 ± 5 mmHg for placebo, −8 ± 6 mmHg for low‑dose, and −15 ± 7 mmHg for high‑dose. The study also measured diastolic BP, heart rate, and adverse events, but the primary endpoint was the mean SBP reduction. The investigators concluded that Hypertol dose‑dependently lowered SBP, with the high‑dose group achieving the greatest reduction and a clinically meaningful effect size. ## q1 Which group experienced the greatest mean reduction in systolic blood pressure?
Correct: C
The high‑dose Hypertol group had the largest mean SBP reduction of −15 mmHg, compared with −8 mmHg for low‑dose and −2 mmHg for placebo.

## q2 Which experimental design feature best controls for confounding variables in this study?
Correct: B
Randomization ensures that known and unknown confounders are evenly distributed across dose groups, reducing bias.

## q3 What is the percent reduction in mean systolic blood pressure for the high‑dose Hypertol group relative to baseline?
Correct: B
Percent reduction = (15 mmHg ÷ 150 mmHg) × 100% = 10%. However, because the change is a decrease, the correct calculation is (15 ÷ 150) × 100% = 10%.
