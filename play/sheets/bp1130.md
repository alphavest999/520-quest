# BP1130

Researchers studied the physiological effects of a novel dietary supplement in 120 healthy adults over a 12‑week period. Participants were randomly assigned to either a low‑dose group (5 g/day) or a high‑dose group (15 g/day). Baseline measurements included resting heart rate, systolic/diastolic blood pressure, fasting glucose, and a standardized cognitive assessment. After 12 weeks, the low‑dose group showed modest improvements in glucose control and cognitive scores, whereas the high‑dose group exhibited a significant drop in heart rate and a modest rise in systolic blood pressure. No serious adverse events were reported. The authors concluded that the supplement may benefit metabolic and cardiovascular health, with dose‑dependent effects on different physiological systems.

## bp1130-01 Which variable exhibited the greatest relative change between baseline and week 12?
Correct: A
The high‑dose group’s heart rate decreased from 72 bpm to 65 bpm, a 9.7% reduction, which is larger than the 3.4% drop in glucose, the 3.4% rise in systolic BP, or the 0.6% change in cognitive score.

## bp1130-02 Which additional control group would best strengthen the study’s internal validity?
Correct: A
A placebo group allows comparison of supplement effects against no active ingredient, controlling for placebo response and natural changes over time.

## bp1130-03 Calculate the percent increase in systolic blood pressure for the high‑dose group relative to baseline.
Correct: B
Percent change = (122 − 118) / 118 × 100 = 4 / 118 × 100 ≈ 3.39%. The closest option is 3.4% (A). However, the table lists 122 mmHg as high‑dose and 118 mmHg as low‑dose; the correct calculation is 4 / 118 ≈ 3.39%. ## bp1130-05 How do these findings relate to the 2019 meta‑analysis on omega‑3 supplementation and cardiovascular outcomes?
Correct: C
The 2019 meta‑analysis reported that omega‑3 fatty acids modestly lower fasting glucose and improve insulin sensitivity; the current supplement’s glucose‑lowering effect mirrors this metabolic benefit, though the mechanisms differ.
