# BP1024

Researchers studied the effect of caffeine on reaction time in a controlled experiment involving 120 healthy adults aged 18‑35. Participants were randomly assigned to one of four groups: a placebo (0 mg caffeine), a low dose (50 mg), a moderate dose (100 mg), or a high dose (150 mg). Each subject performed a computerized visual‑stimulus task in a quiet laboratory setting while their reaction times (RT) were recorded. The mean RTs (in milliseconds) and standard deviations (SD) for each group were as follows: Control: 350 ± 45, 50 mg: 320 ± 40, 100 mg: 350 ± 35, 150 mg: 310 ± 50. The study was double‑blind, and the data were analyzed using one‑way ANOVA followed by post‑hoc Tukey tests. Results indicated a statistically significant reduction in RT for the 150 mg group compared to placebo (p < 0.01). The authors concluded that high‑dose caffeine improves short‑term reaction speed, but caution that the effect may not generalize to older adults or individuals with caffeine sensitivity.

## 1 Which condition had the lowest mean reaction time?
Correct: D
The table shows the 150 mg caffeine group had the lowest mean RT of 310 ms.

## 2 Which of the following best describes the design used in this study?
Correct: A
Participants were randomly assigned to caffeine doses and the study was double‑blind, which are hallmarks of a randomized controlled trial.

## 3 If the standard error of the mean for the 100 mg caffeine group is 5 ms, what is the 95% confidence interval for the mean reaction time?
Correct: C
95% CI = mean ± 1.96 × SE = 350 ± 1.96 × 5 ≈ 350 ± 9.8 → 340.2–359.8 ms, rounded to 340–360 ms.

## 4 Which of the following statements is a common trap when interpreting the results of this study?
Correct: B
The study included only 120 participants, which may be underpowered for broader generalization. Assuming sufficiency is a common misinterpretation.

## 5 Which of the following areas would most benefit from applying the findings of this study?
Correct: A
Improved reaction time from high‑dose caffeine is directly relevant to athletic performance, where rapid responses are critical.
