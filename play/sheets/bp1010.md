# BP1010

Researchers studied the effect of a novel antihypertensive drug on systolic and diastolic blood pressure in 60 adults with stage‑1 hypertension. Participants were randomly assigned to a placebo group (n = 30) or the drug group (n = 30). Baseline systolic/diastolic blood pressure (SBP/DBP) was 145/95 mmHg in both groups. After 4 weeks of daily dosing, the placebo group’s average SBP/DBP decreased to 140/90 mmHg, a reduction of –5/–5 mmHg. The drug group’s average SBP/DBP fell to 120/80 mmHg, a reduction of –25/–15 mmHg. The study was double‑blind, and all participants received identical capsules. No serious adverse events were reported.

## bp1010-01 Which group showed the greater reduction in systolic blood pressure?
Correct: B
The drug group’s systolic BP decreased from 145 to 120 mmHg, a 25‑mmHg drop, whereas the placebo group’s systolic BP decreased by only 5 mmHg.

## bp1010-02 Which design flaw could most likely bias the study’s results?
Correct: A
The passage states that participants were randomly assigned, but the question asks which flaw could bias results. The only potential flaw listed is the lack of randomization; all other aspects (placebo control, double‑blind, sample size) are strengths or neutral.

## bp1010-03 What was the average diastolic blood pressure change for the drug group?
Correct: A
The drug group’s diastolic BP dropped from 95 to 80 mmHg, a change of –15 mmHg.

## bp1010-04 Which statement below is misleading based on the data?
Correct: B
The placebo group’s systolic and diastolic pressures both decreased by 5 mmHg, indicating a measurable effect.

## bp1010-05 Which of the following research questions would be the most logical next step?
Correct: B
The study demonstrated efficacy over 4 weeks; a logical follow‑up is to assess safety over a longer duration.
