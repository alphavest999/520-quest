# BP1196

Researchers studied the effect of varying doses of a novel antihypertensive drug on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial. One hundred and twenty adults with stage‑1 hypertension were assigned to one of four groups: placebo (n = 30), low dose (5 mg, n = 30), medium dose (10 mg, n = 30), or high dose (20 mg, n = 30). After 12 weeks of daily dosing, the mean change in SBP from baseline was measured. The results are summarized in Table 1. The investigators reported a statistically significant dose‑response relationship, with the high‑dose group showing the greatest reduction in SBP. No serious adverse events were observed. The study concluded that the drug is safe and effective, particularly at higher doses.

Table 1. Mean Change in SBP (mmHg) and Standard Deviation

| Treatment | Mean Change | SD |
|-----------|-------------|----|
| Placebo   |  -2.1       | 4.5 |
| Low Dose  |  -5.3       | 4.8 |
| Medium Dose | -9.7     | 5.0 |
| High Dose | -14.2       | 5.2 |

## bp1196-1 Which treatment group had the highest mean reduction in systolic blood pressure?
Correct: D
The table shows the mean change in SBP for each group. The high‑dose group had the largest mean reduction (–14.2 mmHg), indicating the greatest effect.

## bp1196-2 Which experimental design feature best ensures that the observed dose‑response effect is attributable to the drug rather than to other variables?
Correct: A
Random assignment balances known and unknown confounders across groups, isolating the effect of the drug dose. The placebo group (option D) is also important, but randomization is the key design element for causal inference.

## bp1196-3 Calculate the effect size (Cohen’s d) for the difference between the high‑dose and placebo groups. (Use pooled SD).
Correct: C
Pooled SD = √[((29×4.5²)+(29×5.2²))/58] ≈ 4.86. Mean difference = 14.2 – 2.1 = 12.1. Cohen’s d = 12.1 / 4.86 ≈ 0.88.
