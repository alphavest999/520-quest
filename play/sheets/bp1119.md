# BP1119

Researchers studied the effect of a novel antihypertensive drug on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults with hypertension. One hundred participants were randomly assigned to receive either the drug (n=50) or a placebo (n=50) for eight weeks. After the intervention, mean SBP and standard deviation (SD) were recorded. The table below summarizes the findings. The study aimed to determine whether the drug lowered SBP more effectively than placebo and to assess the magnitude of the effect.

## bp1119-01 Which group had the lower mean systolic blood pressure after eight weeks?
Correct: A
The table shows a mean SBP of 128 mmHg for the drug group versus 138 mmHg for placebo, indicating the drug group had the lower mean.

## bp1119-02 Which experimental design best supports causal inference in this study?
Correct: A
Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) are the gold standard for establishing causality because randomization balances known and unknown confounders between groups.

## bp1119-03 What is the absolute difference in mean systolic blood pressure between the drug and placebo groups?
Correct: A
Mean SBP: Drug 128 mmHg, Placebo 138 mmHg. Absolute difference = 138 – 128 = 10 mmHg.

## bp1119-04 Which of the following biases could still threaten the validity of the study’s findings?
Correct: A
Although randomization reduces selection bias, improper randomization or allocation concealment can still introduce selection bias. Confounding and information bias are less likely but not impossible.

## bp1119-05 Which concept is most directly linked to the study’s use of random assignment?
Correct: B
Randomization is the process of assigning participants to groups by chance, which is the key feature highlighted in the passage.
