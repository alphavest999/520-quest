# BP1173

Researchers at the University of Example conducted a randomized controlled trial to investigate the impact of caffeine on simple reaction time. One hundred healthy adults aged 18‑35 were recruited and randomly assigned to either a caffeine group (200 mg) or a placebo group. Each participant completed a computerized reaction‑time task consisting of 120 trials, where they pressed a button as soon as a visual stimulus appeared. The mean reaction time (RT) for the caffeine group was 350 ms (SD = 45 ms, n = 50), whereas the placebo group had a mean RT of 410 ms (SD = 50 ms, n = 50). The researchers also recorded secondary measures such as heart rate and subjective alertness. The study aimed to determine whether caffeine could reliably reduce RT and improve performance in tasks requiring rapid responses. Statistical analysis involved independent‑samples t‑tests and effect‑size calculations. The authors concluded that caffeine produced a statistically significant and practically meaningful reduction in RT, with a Cohen’s d of 1.33. They suggested that caffeine might be beneficial for occupations that demand quick reaction times, such as air‑traffic control or competitive gaming.

## q2 Which experimental design best controls for potential confounding variables in this study?
Correct: A
Randomization balances known and unknown confounders across groups, making it the gold standard for controlling bias.

## q3 Calculate the percent improvement in reaction time from placebo to caffeine.
Correct: C
Percent improvement = (410 – 350) / 410 × 100 = 60 / 410 × 100 ≈ 14.6%.

## q4 Which statistical test would be most appropriate to compare the mean reaction times between the two groups?
Correct: B
Two independent groups with continuous data require an independent‑samples t‑test. A paired t‑test is for matched pairs, and chi‑square is for categorical data.

## q5 Which concept is most closely related to the study’s findings?
Correct: B
The Yerkes‑Dodson law describes the relationship between arousal (e.g., caffeine) and performance, matching the study’s observation that moderate caffeine improves reaction time.
