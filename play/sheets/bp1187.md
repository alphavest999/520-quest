# BP1187

Researchers studied the impact of acute sleep deprivation on cognitive performance in a controlled laboratory setting. Eight healthy adults (4 males, 4 females) were randomly assigned to either a sleep-deprived group (n=4) or a well-rested control group (n=4). The sleep-deprived group was kept awake for 24 hours, while the control group slept normally. After the intervention, all participants completed a computerized psychomotor vigilance task (PVT) measuring reaction time (RT) and a working memory task assessing accuracy. The study aimed to determine whether lack of sleep impairs reaction speed and memory precision. Data were collected, analyzed, and compared between groups. The researchers hypothesized that sleep deprivation would lead to slower reaction times and lower accuracy. Results indicated that the sleep-deprived group had an average RT of 350 ms and 78% accuracy, whereas the control group had an average RT of 280 ms and 92% accuracy. The findings support the hypothesis that acute sleep loss negatively affects both speed and accuracy in cognitive tasks.
