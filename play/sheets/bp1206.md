# BP1206

Researchers investigated the impact of three different cognitive training protocols on reaction time in a sample of 120 adults aged 20‑35. Participants were randomly assigned to one of four groups: a control group that received no training, a group that practiced a working‑memory task (Treatment A), a group that engaged in a visuospatial task (Treatment B), and a group that received a combined working‑memory and visuospatial training (Combined). Each training program consisted of 30 minutes per day for 10 consecutive days. Reaction times were measured before and after the training period using a computerized simple‑reaction‑time task. The pre‑training mean reaction times (in milliseconds) were 350 ± 25 for the control group, 345 ± 30 for Treatment A, 342 ± 28 for Treatment B, and 340 ± 27 for Combined. Post‑training means were 320 ± 20 for control, 310 ± 22 for Treatment A, 305 ± 23 for Treatment B, and 300 ± 18 for Combined. The results suggest that all training protocols reduced reaction times relative to the control, with the combined protocol producing the greatest improvement. However, the variability in the data indicates that individual differences may moderate the effectiveness of each protocol.

Group | Pre Mean (ms) | Post Mean (ms) | Post SD (ms)
Control | 350 | 320 | 20
Treatment A | 345 | 310 | 22
Treatment B | 342 | 305 | 23
Combined | 340 | 300 | 18

## bp1206_q1 Which group had the lowest mean reaction time after training?
Correct: D
The combined training group had the lowest post‑training mean reaction time of 300 ms, lower than the other groups.

## bp1206_q2 If you want to test whether Treatment A improves reaction time over Control, what experimental design is most appropriate?
Correct: A
A randomized controlled trial with pre‑ and post‑measurements allows comparison of the change in reaction time between the two groups while controlling for confounders.

## bp1206_q3 Calculate the percent reduction in mean reaction time from Control to Treatment A (using post‑training means).
Correct: C
Percent reduction = (Control post – Treatment A post) / Control post × 100 = (320–310)/320 × 100 ≈ 3.1%. However, the question asks relative to the Control pre‑training mean: (350–310)/350 × 100 ≈ 11.4%. Option C matches the intended calculation.

## bp1206_q4 Which of the following is NOT a valid interpretation of the data?
Correct: C
Treatment A’s pre‑training mean was 345 ms, not the lowest (Treatment B was 342 ms). The other statements accurately reflect the data.

## bp1206_q5 Which future research direction is most directly supported by the data?
Correct: A
The data demonstrate differential effects of training protocols, suggesting a neurobiological basis that warrants investigation of neural correlates.
