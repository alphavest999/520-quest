# BP996

Researchers studied the impact of ambient temperature on the speed of a simple reflex test among adult volunteers. The study recruited 80 participants (40 males, 40 females) aged 20–35 years. Participants were randomly assigned to one of four temperature conditions: 10 °C, 20 °C, 30 °C, and 40 °C. After a 10‑minute acclimation period, each subject performed a hand‑to‑foot reflex test, and the latency (in milliseconds) was recorded. Table 1 summarizes the mean latency and standard deviation for each temperature group. The researchers hypothesized that higher temperatures would reduce reflex latency due to increased nerve conduction velocity. The data were analyzed using a one‑way ANOVA, followed by post‑hoc Tukey tests to identify pairwise differences. Results indicated a statistically significant decrease in latency at 30 °C and 40 °C compared to 10 °C. However, the difference between 20 °C and 30 °C was not significant. The study concluded that moderate temperature elevations can enhance reflex responsiveness, but extreme heat does not provide additional benefit. These findings have implications for occupational safety and athletic training protocols.

## bp996-01 Which temperature condition had the lowest mean reflex latency?
Correct: C
The table shows that both 30 °C and 40 °C have a mean latency of 310 ms, which is lower than the other conditions. Since 30 °C appears first in the table, the correct answer is C.

## bp996-02 Which variable is the independent variable in this experiment?
Correct: B
The independent variable is the one that researchers manipulate, which in this study is the ambient temperature.

## bp996-03 What is the difference in mean latency between the 10 °C and 30 °C conditions?
Correct: C
Mean latency at 10 °C is 350 ms; at 30 °C it is 310 ms. The difference is 350 ms – 310 ms = 40 ms.

Correct: C
