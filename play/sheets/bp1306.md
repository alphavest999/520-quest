# BP1306

Researchers at the University of Midvale investigated how caffeine intake influences reaction time under varying lighting conditions. Twenty-four healthy adults were randomly assigned to one of three groups: (1) caffeine (200 mg) with bright light (800 lux), (2) caffeine with dim light (200 lux), and (3) placebo with bright light. Each participant completed a computerized simple reaction time task (stimulus presented every 2–3 seconds). Reaction times were recorded in milliseconds. The study aimed to disentangle the effects of caffeine and lighting on cognitive performance. The researchers hypothesized that caffeine would improve reaction times, but that bright lighting would further enhance this effect. After data collection, the team performed a two‑factor ANOVA to assess main effects and interaction. The results suggested a significant caffeine effect (p < .01) but no significant lighting effect or interaction. These findings contribute to the literature on psychostimulant use and environmental factors affecting alertness.
