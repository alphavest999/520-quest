# BP1271

Researchers investigated the impact of sleep deprivation on cognitive performance in adolescents aged 13–17. The study recruited 120 participants and randomly assigned them to one of four sleep conditions: 8 hours, 6 hours, 4 hours, and 2 hours of sleep per night for a week. Each group completed a standardized reaction time task and a memory accuracy test at the end of the week. The researchers recorded mean reaction times (in milliseconds) and accuracy percentages for each group. The data suggested a clear trend: as sleep duration decreased, reaction times increased and accuracy declined. The authors concluded that adequate sleep is essential for optimal cognitive functioning in this age group and recommended policy changes to promote longer sleep durations among adolescents.
