# BP1703

Researchers at the University of Midvale conducted a controlled experiment to examine the impact of acute sleep deprivation on cognitive performance in young adults. Eight healthy volunteers (aged 20–25) were randomly assigned to either a sleep‑restricted group (4 hours of sleep) or a normal‑sleep group (8 hours of sleep) for one night. The next morning, all participants completed a computerized reaction‑time task and a working‑memory test. Reaction times were recorded in milliseconds, and accuracy was expressed as the percentage of correct responses. The researchers also collected subjective sleepiness ratings on a 10‑point Likert scale. The study aimed to determine whether reduced sleep leads to measurable declines in speed and accuracy, and to explore potential dose‑response relationships between sleep duration and performance metrics. The data were analyzed using mixed‑effects models to account for within‑subject variability and to assess group differences while controlling for baseline performance. The findings are expected to inform guidelines for shift workers and students who often face sleep restrictions.

A valid control group should match the experimental group in all respects except the manipulated variable (sleep duration). Participants who sleep 8 hours and perform the same tasks provide a baseline for comparison.
