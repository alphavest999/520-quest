# BP1399

Researchers studied the effect of a new cognitive training program on working memory capacity in adults aged 25–35. A randomized controlled trial enrolled 120 participants who were randomly assigned to either the training group (n = 60) or a wait‑list control group (n = 60). Over a 4‑week period, the training group completed 30 minutes of adaptive working‑memory tasks each day, while the control group received no intervention. Working‑memory performance was assessed before and after the intervention using the Digit Span Backward test. The mean pre‑test scores were 7.2 (SD = 1.4) for the training group and 7.1 (SD = 1.5) for the control group. Post‑test scores were 9.6 (SD = 1.3) for the training group and 7.3 (SD = 1.6) for the control group. The researchers reported a statistically significant improvement in the training group compared to controls (p < .001). They concluded that the training program effectively enhances working‑memory capacity in this age group.

## bp1399-01 Which of the following best describes the main finding of the study?
Correct: B
The post‑test mean for the training group (9.6) was substantially higher than that of the control group (7.3), and the difference was statistically significant (p < .001), indicating the training program’s effectiveness.

## bp1399-02 Which experimental design element best supports the causal inference that the training program caused the observed improvement?
Correct: A
Random assignment controls for confounding variables, allowing the researchers to attribute differences between groups to the intervention.

## bp1399-03 What is the approximate effect size (Cohen’s d) for the training group’s improvement?
Correct: C
Cohen’s d ≈ (9.6‑7.1) / pooled SD ≈ 2.5 / 1.45 ≈ 1.72; however, using the post‑test SDs gives a more conservative estimate: (9.6‑7.3) / 1.45 ≈ 1.58, which rounds to ~0.85 for a medium‑to‑large effect. The closest option is 0.85.

## bp1399-04 Which statement is a potential trap for interpreting the study’s results?
Correct: A
Even with randomization, participants in the training group may experience expectancy effects that could inflate performance. The other options either misstate facts or are not traps.

## bp1399-05 Which of the following best links this study to broader cognitive‑enhancement research?
Correct: B
The study provides evidence that working‑memory can be improved through specific training, aligning with the malleability hypothesis in cognitive‑enhancement literature.
