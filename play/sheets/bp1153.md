# BP1153

Researchers investigated the effect of caffeine on reaction time among adolescents. One hundred twenty participants were randomly assigned to four groups: placebo, low dose (50 mg), moderate dose (100 mg), and high dose (200 mg). In a double‑blind design, each participant’s reaction time to a visual stimulus was recorded. The study also measured heart rate to monitor physiological effects. Results showed a progressive decrease in mean reaction time with increasing caffeine dose up to 100 mg, after which the mean plateaued. The researchers concluded that moderate caffeine intake improves reaction time, but higher doses do not confer additional benefit. The study controlled for baseline caffeine consumption and age but did not assess sleep quality or caffeine tolerance. These factors could influence reaction time outcomes.

Group | Mean Reaction Time (ms) | SD (ms)
--- | --- | ---
Placebo | 350 | 30
Low | 320 | 28
Moderate | 280 | 25
High | 275 | 27

## bp1153-q1 Which group had the lowest mean reaction time?
Correct: C
The table shows the moderate‑dose group (100 mg) had a mean reaction time of 280 ms, lower than all other groups.

## bp1153-q2 Which statement best describes the study design?
Correct: B
Participants were randomly assigned to treatment groups in a double‑blind manner, which is the hallmark of a randomized controlled trial.

## bp1153-q3 What is the percent reduction in mean reaction time from placebo to moderate group?
Correct: C
Reduction = (350 ms – 280 ms) / 350 ms × 100% = 20%.

## bp1153-q4 Which of the following is a potential confounder NOT controlled in the study?
Correct: C
The passage states that age was controlled, but sleep quality was not assessed, making it a potential confounder.

## bp1153-q5 Which mechanism best explains the plateau effect observed at the high‑dose group?
Correct: A
At higher caffeine doses, adenosine receptors become saturated, limiting further improvement in reaction time.
