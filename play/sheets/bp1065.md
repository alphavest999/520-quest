# BP1065

Researchers at the University of Midvale conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the effect of a new antihypertensive medication on systolic blood pressure (SBP). Two hundred adult participants (aged 40–65) with diagnosed stage 1 hypertension were recruited and randomly assigned to either the treatment group (n = 100) receiving the medication or the placebo group (n = 100). Baseline SBP was measured after a 5‑minute seated rest, and follow‑up measurements were taken at 4, 8, and 12 weeks. The study aimed to determine whether the medication could reduce SBP by at least 10 mmHg over 12 weeks and to assess any associated side effects. In addition to SBP, researchers recorded heart rate, serum creatinine, and patient-reported adherence. The trial was double‑blind, and all participants provided informed consent. Data were analyzed using mixed‑effects linear models to account for repeated measures and missing data. The results indicated a statistically significant reduction in SBP in the treatment group compared to placebo, with a mean difference of 12 mmHg at 12 weeks. No serious adverse events were reported.
