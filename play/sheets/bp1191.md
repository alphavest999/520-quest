# BP1191

Researchers conducted a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive agent, HypoX, in adults with stage 1 hypertension. A total of 200 participants were enrolled and randomly assigned to either the HypoX group (n = 100) or a placebo group (n = 100). Baseline systolic blood pressure (SBP) was measured after a 5‑minute seated rest. Follow‑up measurements were taken at 4 weeks and 8 weeks. The investigators recorded mean SBP values and standard deviations for each group at each time point. The primary endpoint was the mean change in SBP from baseline to week 8. Secondary endpoints included the proportion of patients achieving SBP < 140 mmHg and the incidence of adverse events. The study was approved by an institutional review board, and all participants provided written informed consent. Statistical analysis was performed using ANCOVA, adjusting for baseline SBP, with a two‑tailed significance level of 0.05.

## q1 Which of the following best describes the observed change in mean systolic blood pressure for the HypoX group from baseline to week 8?

The mean SBP decreased from 148 mmHg at baseline to 124 mmHg at week 8, a difference of 24 mmHg.

## q2 If the researchers wanted to assess whether HypoX had a dose‑dependent effect on SBP, which of the following study designs would be most appropriate?
Correct: A
A dose‑response study requires multiple treatment arms with different doses to observe a gradient of effect.

## q3 Using the data provided, calculate the absolute risk reduction (ARR) for achieving SBP < 140 mmHg at week 8 between HypoX and placebo. Assume 80% of HypoX patients and 40% of placebo patients reached the target.
Correct: A
ARR = 0.80 – 0.40 = 0.40. However, the question asks for absolute risk reduction, which is the difference in proportions: 0.80 – 0.40 = 0.40. The correct answer is 0.40 (Option B).
