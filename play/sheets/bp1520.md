# BP1520

Researchers investigated the influence of light intensity on the rate of photosynthesis in spinach leaves. A controlled experiment was conducted in a growth chamber where four different light intensities—100, 200, 400, and 800 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹—were applied for 30 minutes to identical leaf samples. Oxygen evolution was measured using a Clark-type electrode, and the data were recorded as micromoles of O₂ produced per gram of leaf tissue per second. The results showed a clear, dose-dependent increase in photosynthetic rate up to 400 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹, after which the rate plateaued, suggesting a saturation point of the photosynthetic apparatus. The authors concluded that moderate light intensities optimize energy capture while minimizing photoinhibition, and they recommended further studies on the role of chlorophyll concentration in this process.

## bp1520-05 Which of the following concepts best links the observed saturation of photosynthetic rate to the underlying biochemical mechanism?
Correct: B
Saturation occurs when the enzymes of the Calvin cycle (e.g., Rubisco) become saturated with CO₂ and ATP, limiting further increases in photosynthetic rate despite higher light intensity.
