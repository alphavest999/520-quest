# BP1226

Researchers studied the effect of a novel dietary supplement on the recovery time of athletes after strenuous exercise. In a randomized controlled trial, 120 participants were assigned to either the supplement group (n=60) or a placebo group (n=60). All participants performed a standardized 30‑minute treadmill run at 70% of their VO₂max. Immediately after the run, they received their assigned treatment and were monitored for 24 hours. Recovery time was defined as the period until heart rate returned to within 5% of resting baseline. The study also measured serum lactate levels at 0, 6, and 24 hours post‑exercise to assess metabolic clearance. Results indicated that the supplement group had a mean recovery time of 4.2 hours (SD = 0.8), whereas the placebo group recovered in 5.6 hours (SD = 1.1). Serum lactate clearance was significantly faster in the supplement group, with a 30% reduction in lactate concentration at 6 hours compared to placebo. The authors concluded that the supplement accelerates cardiovascular and metabolic recovery, potentially enhancing athletic performance and reducing injury risk.

## bp1226-q2 Which experimental design feature best supports the validity of the study’s findings?
Correct: C
Random assignment minimizes selection bias and balances confounding variables, strengthening causal inference. The other options describe measurement methods or reporting practices, not design elements that control bias.

## bp1226-q3 What is the approximate 95% confidence interval for the difference in mean recovery time between the supplement and placebo groups?
Correct: B
Difference in means = 5.6–4.2 = 1.4 h. Standard error = sqrt((0.8²/60)+(1.1²/60)) ≈ 0.18 h. 95% CI = 1.4 ± 1.96×0.18 ≈ 1.4 ± 0.35 → 1.05–1.75 h, closest to option B.
