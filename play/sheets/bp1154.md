# BP1154

Researchers conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the effect of a novel antihypertensive agent, Hypertensol, on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults with stage 1 hypertension. A total of 200 participants were enrolled and randomly assigned to either the Hypertensol group (n = 100) or a placebo group (n = 100). Baseline characteristics were comparable between groups, with mean age 52 ± 8 years and baseline SBP 145 ± 10 mmHg. After 12 weeks of treatment, the Hypertensol group exhibited a mean SBP of 125 ± 8 mmHg, whereas the placebo group’s mean SBP was 138 ± 9 mmHg. The investigators reported a statistically significant difference (p < 0.001) and calculated the absolute risk reduction for achieving SBP < 140 mmHg as 35%. The study also monitored adverse events, noting a mild incidence of dizziness in 5% of the Hypertensol group and none in the placebo group. The authors concluded that Hypertensol effectively lowers SBP with a favorable safety profile, suggesting potential utility in clinical practice.
