# BP1661

Researchers investigated how temperature influences the catalytic efficiency of the enzyme lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) isolated from bovine muscle tissue. The study measured enzyme activity at four distinct temperatures (25 °C, 35 °C, 45 °C, and 55 °C) while maintaining a constant substrate concentration of 10 mM pyruvate. Activity was expressed as units per milligram of protein, and reaction rates were recorded in micromoles of product formed per minute. The results revealed a bell‑shaped curve, with peak activity observed at 45 °C. Beyond this optimal temperature, activity declined sharply, suggesting thermal denaturation. The data provide insight into the enzyme’s thermodynamic stability and potential industrial applications where temperature control is critical.

## bp1661-01 Which temperature yields the highest reaction rate according to the table?
Correct: C
The reaction rate increases from 124 µmol/min at 25 °C to a maximum of 243 µmol/min at 45 °C, then drops to 101 µmol/min at 55 °C. Thus, 45 °C provides the highest rate.

## bp1661-02 If the researchers wanted to test whether substrate concentration affects temperature dependence, which experimental condition should they add?
Correct: C
To isolate the effect of substrate concentration on temperature dependence, the researchers should vary pyruvate levels while holding temperature constant at 35 °C. This allows comparison of activity across substrate concentrations independent of temperature changes.

## bp1661-04 Which statement is a common misconception about enzyme temperature profiles?
Correct: A
The misconception is that activity increases indefinitely with temperature. In reality, activity rises to an optimum then declines due to denaturation.
