# BP1707

Researchers conducted a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial to evaluate the antihypertensive efficacy of the novel compound X-101 in adults with stage 1 hypertension. Eighty-four participants were enrolled and randomly assigned to either the X-101 group (n=42) or a matching placebo group (n=42). Baseline characteristics were comparable: mean age 52.3 ± 9.8 years, 55% male, and baseline systolic blood pressure (SBP) of 138.5 ± 10.2 mmHg. Over a 12‑week treatment period, participants received daily oral doses of X-101 (50 mg) or placebo. The primary endpoint was the change in mean SBP from baseline to week 12. Table 1 summarizes the key outcomes. At week 12, the X‑101 group exhibited a mean SBP of 122.4 ± 8.5 mmHg, whereas the placebo group had 131.7 ± 9.1 mmHg. The between‑group difference of 9.3 mmHg (p < 0.001) indicates a statistically significant reduction attributable to X‑101. Secondary endpoints, including diastolic BP and pulse‑pressure variability, mirrored the primary findings. These results suggest that X‑101 may offer a clinically meaningful benefit in managing mild hypertension.
