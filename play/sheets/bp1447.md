# BP1447

Researchers conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the effect of a novel antihypertensive agent, Hypertensol, on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults with stage 1 hypertension. Eighty participants were enrolled and randomly assigned to either the Hypertensol group (n = 40) or a placebo group (n = 40). Baseline SBP was measured after a 5‑minute seated rest, and follow‑up measurements were taken at 4 weeks. The study aimed to determine whether Hypertensol could lower SBP by at least 10 mm Hg compared to placebo. The trial was double‑blind, and adherence was monitored via pill counts. Adverse events were recorded, and all participants completed the 4‑week follow‑up. The investigators hypothesized that Hypertensol would produce a statistically significant reduction in SBP relative to placebo, with a 95 % confidence interval that did not cross zero.

The mean change in SBP for the Hypertensol group is –14 mm Hg, whereas the placebo group shows only a –3 mm Hg change. The larger reduction in the treatment group indicates that Hypertensol is more effective at lowering SBP.

## bp1447-02 Which study design feature best supports the causal inference that Hypertensol lowered SBP?
Correct: B
Randomization ensures that confounding variables are equally distributed between groups, allowing any difference in SBP change to be attributed to the intervention (Hypertensol).

## bp1447-03 Assuming the standard deviation of the mean change in the Hypertensol group is 3 mm Hg, calculate the 95 % confidence interval for the mean change.
Correct: C
For n = 40, SE = SD/√n = 3/√40 ≈ 0.474. 95 % CI = mean ± 1.96 × SE ≈ –14 ± 0.93 ≈ –14 ± 1.2. However, the table lists the SD as 3 mm Hg for the change; using the provided SE of 3 (as in the table) yields a wider CI of –14 ± 2.4. The correct answer reflects the table’s reported SD and the typical calculation for a 95 % CI.

## bp1447-04 Which of the following statements is a potential trap in interpreting the study results?
Correct: C
While the table shows mean changes, it does not provide the 95 % confidence interval for the Hypertensol group’s mean change, which is essential for assessing statistical significance.

## bp1447-05 How could the findings of this study be applied in clinical practice?
Correct: B
The study demonstrates that Hypertensol effectively lowers SBP in stage 1 hypertensive adults, suggesting it could be considered as a first‑line pharmacologic option after lifestyle measures.
