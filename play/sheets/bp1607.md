# BP1607

Researchers studied the effect of three different auditory stimuli on reaction time in a simple visual task. Forty healthy adults were randomly assigned to one of three conditions: (1) a 440 Hz tone, (2) a 880 Hz tone, or (3) no tone. Each participant completed 60 trials, and mean reaction times (RT) were recorded. The results are summarized in Table 1. The researchers hypothesized that higher frequency tones would facilitate faster responses due to increased arousal. The study also measured subjective arousal ratings to assess the relationship between perceived arousal and RT. Participants reported higher arousal for the 880 Hz condition, but the RT advantage was modest. The authors concluded that while auditory stimuli can influence reaction time, the effect size is small and may be mediated by individual differences in attentional allocation. Future studies should examine the role of stimulus modality and task difficulty to better understand the mechanisms underlying cross-modal facilitation.
