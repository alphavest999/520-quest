Researchers at the University of Health Sciences conducted a 12‑week, randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive drug, Hypotensol, in adults with stage 1 hypertension. A total of 120 participants were enrolled and randomly assigned to receive either Hypotensol (n = 60) or placebo (n = 60). Systolic blood pressure (SBP) was measured at baseline and at weeks 4, 8, and 12. The mean SBP decreased progressively in the treatment group, from 145 mmHg at baseline to 125 mmHg at week 12, while the placebo group showed a modest decline from 146 mmHg to 140 mmHg. Standard deviations decreased over time, reflecting reduced variability. The study reported a statistically significant difference between groups at week 12 (p < 0.01). Adverse events were mild and comparable between groups. The authors concluded that Hypotensol effectively lowers SBP over a 12‑week period and is well tolerated.
