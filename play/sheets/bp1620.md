# BP1620

Researchers investigated the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive agent, Hypertensol, in adults with stage 1 hypertension. In a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial, 100 participants (age 45–65) were assigned to either Hypertensol (n=50) or placebo (n=50) and followed for 12 weeks. Baseline systolic blood pressure (SBP) averaged 150 mmHg in both groups. At week 12, the Hypertensol group exhibited a mean SBP reduction of 8 mmHg (SD = 4), whereas the placebo group showed a 3 mmHg decrease (SD = 5). The study reported a p‑value < 0.01 for the difference between groups. Researchers concluded that Hypertensol significantly lowers SBP compared to placebo. The trial’s design, sample size, and statistical analysis were consistent with guidelines for phase II hypertension studies. The investigators also monitored adverse events, noting no serious complications. These findings suggest Hypertensol could be a viable option for managing mild hypertension, pending further phase III trials.
