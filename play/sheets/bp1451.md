# BP1451

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel cognitive enhancer on reaction time in healthy adults. Eighty participants were randomly assigned to one of four groups: a placebo control, a low‑dose (5 mg) group, a medium‑dose (10 mg) group, and a high‑dose (20 mg) group. After a single dose, participants completed a computerized simple reaction time task. Mean reaction times (ms) and standard deviations (SD) were recorded for each group. The study aimed to determine whether increasing doses would produce a dose‑response improvement in reaction time while monitoring safety and variability. The results suggested a trend toward faster reaction times with higher doses, but the differences were modest. The authors concluded that the high‑dose group showed the greatest improvement, though variability remained similar across groups. This data set provides a basis for evaluating dose‑response relationships, experimental design, and effect size calculations in a controlled setting.
