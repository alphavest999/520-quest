# BP1015

Researchers studied the effect of light intensity on the rate of photosynthesis in spinach leaves. In a controlled laboratory setting, fully expanded spinach leaves were exposed to four different light intensities (200, 400, 600, and 800 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹). Oxygen evolution was measured using a Clark-type electrode, and the rate of photosynthesis was expressed as µmol O₂ g⁻¹ leaf area min⁻¹. The experiment was replicated three times to ensure reproducibility. All other variables—temperature (25 °C), CO₂ concentration (400 ppm), and leaf temperature—were held constant. The data revealed a clear trend of increasing photosynthetic rate with rising light intensity up to 600 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹, after which the rate plateaued, indicating a light-saturated response.

## bp1015-01 Which light intensity produced the highest rate of photosynthesis according to the data?
Correct: C
The highest oxygen evolution rate (7.1 µmol g⁻¹ min⁻¹) was observed at 600 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹. The rate at 800 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹ is slightly higher (7.3) but the difference is within experimental error and the trend indicates a plateau, so 600 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹ is the intensity that produced the peak rate before saturation.

## bp1015-02 Which experimental factor should be controlled to ensure that differences in photosynthetic rate are due to light intensity alone?
Correct: D
To attribute changes in photosynthetic rate solely to light intensity, all other variables that influence photosynthesis—leaf age, CO₂ concentration, leaf temperature, and ambient temperature—must be held constant. The study already controlled temperature and CO₂, so controlling leaf age would be the missing variable, making D the best answer.

## bp1015-03 What is the percent increase in photosynthetic rate when light intensity increases from 200 to 400 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹?

Percent increase = [(5.8 – 3.2) / 3.2] × 100 % = 2.6 / 3.2 × 100 % ≈ 81.25 %. The closest option is 80 %.

## bp1015-04 Which of the following conclusions is NOT supported by the data?
Correct: C
The data show a nonlinear, saturating response: the rate increases steeply at low intensities and then levels off. Therefore, a linear relationship is not supported.

## bp1015-05 How does the observed light‑saturation curve relate to the broader concept of photosynthetic efficiency in natural ecosystems?
Correct: C
The plateau at high light intensities shows that plants have mechanisms (e.g., non‑photochemical quenching) to protect against photoinhibition, a key adaptation in natural ecosystems.
