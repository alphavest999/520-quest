# BP802

Researchers studied the acid–base properties of five common amino acids—lysine, glutamic acid, histidine, tyrosine, and serine—by performing UV absorbance titrations across a pH range of 1–12. The experiment involved preparing 0.1 M solutions of each amino acid in 10 mM phosphate buffer, measuring the absorbance at 280 nm at 1‑unit pH intervals, and fitting the data to the Henderson–Hasselbalch equation to extract pKa values for the side‑chain functional groups. The resulting pKa values were compared to literature values to assess experimental accuracy. Additionally, the researchers calculated the isoelectric point (pI) for each amino acid by averaging the pKa values of the ionizable groups that change charge across the pH range. The study also examined how the presence of a high‑ionic‑strength buffer (150 mM NaCl) affected the apparent pKa shifts. The data are summarized in Table 1 below.

| Amino Acid | Side‑Chain pKa | pI   | Comments |
|------------|----------------|------|----------|
| Lysine     | 10.5           | 9.74 | Basic   |
| Glutamic Acid | 4.25        | 3.22 | Acidic  |
| Histidine  | 6.00           | 7.59 | Neutral |
| Tyrosine   | 10.07          | 5.66 | Neutral |
| Serine     | 13.6           | 5.68 | Neutral |


## 802-1 Which amino acid has the highest side‑chain pKa according to the experiment?
Correct: A
Analogy (10‑yr): Think of a ladder; the highest rung is Lysine.
Official: Lysine’s side‑chain pKa is 10.5, higher than the others.
Why wrong: Glutamic acid (4.25), histidine (6.00), and tyrosine (10.07) all have lower pKa values.

## 802-2 If a 150 mM NaCl buffer at pH 8.5 is added, which amino acid will be predominantly in its protonated form?
Correct: A
Analogy (10‑yr): At pH 8.5, only Lysine’s ladder rung (pKa 10.5) is still above the floor, so it holds a proton.
Official: Since Lysine’s side‑chain pKa (10.5) > 8.5, it remains protonated.
Why wrong: Glutamic acid (pKa 4.25), histidine (6.00), and tyrosine (10.07) are either below or just above 8.5, leading to partial or deprotonation.

## 802-3 Calculate the isoelectric point (pI) of a dipeptide composed of lysine and glutamic acid using the side‑chain pKa values provided.
Correct: A
Analogy (10‑yr): Add the two numbers (10.5 + 4.25) and divide by two.
Official: (10.5 + 4.25)/2 = 7.375 → 7.4.
Why wrong: The other options do not reflect the arithmetic mean of the two pKa values.
