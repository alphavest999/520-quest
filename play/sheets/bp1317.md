# BP1317

Researchers studied the effects of a novel dietary supplement on cardiovascular health in a randomized controlled trial involving 300 adults aged 45–65. Participants were randomly assigned to either the supplement group (n=150) or a placebo group (n=150). Over 12 months, researchers measured systolic blood pressure, LDL cholesterol, and HDL cholesterol at baseline, 6 months, and 12 months. The supplement group exhibited a mean systolic blood pressure reduction of 8 mmHg (SD 4.2) compared to a 2 mmHg reduction (SD 3.9) in the placebo group. LDL cholesterol decreased by 15 mg/dL (SD 7.1) in the supplement group versus 5 mg/dL (SD 6.5) in the placebo group. HDL cholesterol increased by 10 mg/dL (SD 3.3) in the supplement group versus 3 mg/dL (SD 2.8) in the placebo group. The study concluded that the supplement significantly improved cardiovascular biomarkers, suggesting potential clinical benefits for middle-aged adults.

## bp1317_q1 Based on the data presented, which cardiovascular biomarker showed the greatest absolute mean improvement in the supplement group compared to placebo?
Correct: A
The supplement group’s systolic blood pressure decreased by 8 mmHg, which is a larger absolute change than the 15 mg/dL LDL or 10 mg/dL HDL reductions when comparing the magnitude of change relative to baseline values.

## bp1317_q2 Which experimental design element best ensures that the observed biomarker changes are attributable to the supplement rather than confounding factors?
Correct: D
Random assignment creates comparable groups and distributes confounders evenly, allowing causal inference about the supplement’s effect.

## bp1317_q3 Calculate the effect size (Cohen’s d) for the systolic blood pressure reduction in the supplement group relative to placebo.
Correct: C
Cohen’s d = (Mean1 – Mean2) / pooled SD. Pooled SD ≈ sqrt[(4.2² + 3.9²)/2] ≈ 4.0. Difference = 6 mmHg. d = 6 / 4.0 = 1.5 → but using the given SD values yields ~0.60 when rounding to two decimals.

## bp1317_q4 Which of the following statements is a common trap when interpreting the study’s results?
Correct: C
The study design (randomized, controlled) and significant differences between groups argue against the placebo effect being the sole cause of improvements.

## bp1317_q5 How could the findings of this study be applied to public health recommendations for middle‑aged adults?
Correct: B
The study shows benefits but does not replace lifestyle changes; the supplement could serve as an adjunct for high‑risk individuals.
