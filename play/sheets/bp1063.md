# BP1063

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel antimicrobial peptide (AMP) on the growth of methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus (MRSA) in vitro. The peptide was synthesized and purified to >95% purity. Cultures of MRSA were inoculated into tryptic soy broth and exposed to increasing concentrations of the peptide (0, 2, 4, 8, and 16 μg/mL). After 24 hours of incubation at 37 °C, bacterial viability was quantified by plating serial dilutions and counting colony-forming units (CFU). The experiment was performed in triplicate, and the mean CFU/mL was recorded for each concentration. A dose–response curve was generated to estimate the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) and the 50% inhibitory concentration (IC50). The data suggested a steep decline in CFU with concentrations above 4 μg/mL, indicating potent activity. Researchers also examined the peptide’s effect on biofilm formation by measuring crystal violet absorbance at 590 nm. The results demonstrated a significant reduction in biofilm biomass at 8 μg/mL and higher. These findings support further development of the AMP as a potential therapeutic agent against MRSA infections.
