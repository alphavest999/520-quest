# BP1748

Researchers studied the effect of a novel dietary supplement on the recovery time of athletes after a 10‑km run. Twenty‑eight male participants were randomly assigned to either the supplement group (n=14) or a placebo group (n=14). All participants performed a standardized 10‑km time trial, received their assigned intervention immediately after completion, and then recorded their recovery time (time until heart rate returned to 70% of resting value). The table below summarizes the mean recovery times (in minutes) and the standard deviations for each group. The study aimed to determine whether the supplement significantly reduced recovery time compared to placebo.

Group | Mean Recovery Time (min) | Standard Deviation
--- | --- | ---
Supplement | 12.4 | 1.8
Placebo | 14.7 | 2.1

## bp1748-01 Based on the data presented, which statement accurately reflects the study’s findings?
Correct: A
The mean recovery time for the supplement group is 12.4 minutes, which is shorter than the placebo group’s 14.7 minutes, indicating a faster recovery.

## bp1748-02 Which experimental design element is most critical for ensuring the validity of this study’s conclusion?
Correct: A
A double‑blind design prevents both participants and researchers from knowing group assignments, reducing bias and increasing internal validity.

## bp1748-03 What is the relative risk reduction of recovery time in the supplement group compared to placebo?
Correct: C
Relative risk reduction = (14.7 – 12.4) / 14.7 ≈ 0.33 (33%).

## bp1748-04 Which of the following is a common misinterpretation of the study’s results?
Correct: A
The study only demonstrates a short‑term effect; it does not prove a permanent change or long‑term efficacy.

## bp1748-05 Which concept is most directly linked to the study’s outcome measure?
Correct: B
Recovery time measured by heart rate returning to 70% of resting value is an indicator of heart rate variability and autonomic recovery.
