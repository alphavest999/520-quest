# BP1031

Researchers studied the effect of a novel dietary supplement on the recovery time of athletes after strenuous exercise. The study enrolled 120 male runners aged 18–35, randomly assigning them to either the supplement group (n = 60) or a placebo group (n = 60). All participants completed a 10‑km time trial, followed by a standardized resistance‑training session. Recovery time was measured as the duration (in minutes) required to return to baseline heart rate (HR ≤ 80 bpm). Blood samples were collected pre‑exercise, immediately post‑exercise, and at 30‑minute intervals during recovery to assess lactate concentration and inflammatory markers (IL‑6, TNF‑α). The supplement group exhibited a mean recovery time of 42.3 ± 5.1 min, whereas the placebo group’s mean was 48.7 ± 6.4 min. Lactate clearance was faster in the supplement group, with a 30‑minute lactate level of 2.1 ± 0.4 mmol/L compared to 3.0 ± 0.5 mmol/L in the placebo group. Inflammatory markers decreased more rapidly in the supplement group, with IL‑6 levels dropping to 3.2 ± 0.6 pg/mL at 60 min versus 4.5 ± 0.8 pg/mL in the placebo group. These findings suggest that the supplement may enhance metabolic recovery and attenuate inflammation post‑exercise.

## bp1031-03 If the researchers wanted to calculate the effect size (Cohen’s d) for recovery time between groups, which of the following values best approximates the standardized mean difference?
Correct: B
Cohen’s d = (M1 – M2) / SDpooled. M1 = 48.7, M2 = 42.3, difference = 6.4. SDpooled = √[(5.1² + 6.4²)/2] ≈ 5.8. Thus d ≈ 6.4/5.8 ≈ 1.10.
