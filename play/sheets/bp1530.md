# BP1530

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel dietary supplement on glycemic control in adults with prediabetes. Over a 12‑week randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial, 120 participants were assigned to either the supplement (n=60) or placebo (n=60). Baseline fasting blood glucose (FBG) and HbA1c levels were recorded, and these metrics were reassessed at weeks 4, 8, and 12. The supplement group showed a progressive decline in mean FBG, from 110 mg/dL at baseline to 95 mg/dL at week 12, while the placebo group remained relatively stable (108 mg/dL to 107 mg/dL). HbA1c decreased by 0.4% in the supplement group versus 0.1% in the placebo group. Adverse events were mild and comparable between groups. The authors concluded that the supplement may improve glycemic markers in prediabetic individuals, but longer follow‑up is needed to assess sustained effects and safety.

## bp1530-01 Based on the data presented, which of the following statements best describes the trend in fasting blood glucose (FBG) over the 12‑week study period?
Correct: B
The table shows a consistent decline in mean FBG for the supplement group (110 → 95 mg/dL) while the placebo group’s FBG remains near baseline (108 → 107 mg/dL). Thus, the supplement group experienced a decrease, whereas the placebo group did not.

## bp1530-02 If the researchers wanted to determine whether the supplement’s effect on FBG was independent of baseline HbA1c levels, which study design element would be most appropriate to include?
Correct: A
Stratifying by baseline HbA1c ensures balanced distribution of this potential confounder across treatment groups, allowing assessment of the supplement’s effect independent of HbA1c. Sample size, dosing, and center number do not directly address this question.

## bp1530-03 What is the absolute reduction in mean FBG (mg/dL) observed in the supplement group from baseline to week 12?
Correct: C
Baseline FBG was 110 mg/dL; week 12 FBG was 95 mg/dL. The absolute reduction is 110 – 95 = 15 mg/dL.

## bp1530-04 Which of the following statements is a potential trap for interpreting the study’s results?
Correct: A
Because the study was double‑blind and placebo‑controlled, a placebo effect is unlikely to explain the observed FBG decline. This option is a distractor that misattributes the effect to placebo.

## bp1530-05 Which of the following resources would best help a clinician apply the findings of this study to practice?
Correct: A
A systematic review synthesizes multiple studies, providing broader evidence on supplement efficacy and safety, which is more useful for clinical decision‑making than a single study abstract or unrelated resources.
