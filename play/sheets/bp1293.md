# BP1293

Researchers studied the effect of a novel dietary supplement on the prevalence of a chronic fatigue symptom in adults aged 30-50. The study enrolled 400 participants, randomly assigned to one of four groups: a control group receiving no supplement, a low-dose group receiving 100 mg/day, a medium-dose group receiving 200 mg/day, and a high-dose group receiving 400 mg/day. Over a 12-week period, participants reported the frequency of fatigue episodes. The results, summarized in Table 1, show a clear dose-dependent reduction in symptom prevalence, with the high-dose group exhibiting the lowest prevalence. The researchers concluded that the supplement may be effective in reducing chronic fatigue, especially at higher doses, and suggested further large-scale trials to confirm these findings.

| Group | Prevalence of Symptom (%) | p-value |
|-------|---------------------------|---------|
| Control | 12 | 1.00 |
| Low-dose | 9 | 0.45 |
| Medium-dose | 6 | 0.12 |
| High-dose | 3 | 0.02 |

## bp1293_q1 According to Table 1, which group had the lowest prevalence of the chronic fatigue symptom?
Correct: D
The table shows the high-dose group had a 3% prevalence, the lowest among all groups.

## bp1293_q2 Which experimental design feature best controls for potential confounding variables in this study?
Correct: A
Blinding participants prevents placebo effects and reporting bias, which are key confounders in symptom studies.

## bp1293_q3 Calculate the relative risk reduction of fatigue in the high-dose group compared to the control group.
Correct: A
Relative risk reduction = (12% - 3%) / 12% = 9% / 12% = 0.75 = 75%.

## bp1293_q4 Which statement is a common misconception about the p-value reported for the high-dose group?
Correct: D
The p-value is the probability of observing the data assuming the null hypothesis is true; it does not give the probability that the null hypothesis is true.

## bp1293_q5 How could the findings from this study inform public health policy regarding dietary supplement regulation?
Correct: C
While the study shows promise, regulatory bodies would likely require more extensive trials to confirm safety and efficacy before approving supplements for clinical use.
