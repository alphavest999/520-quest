# BP980

Researchers studied the acute effects of a novel antihypertensive agent, HypoPress, on adults with stage 1 hypertension. In a double‑blind, randomized controlled trial, 200 participants were assigned to either HypoPress (n = 100) or placebo (n = 100). Blood pressure was measured at baseline and after 4 weeks of treatment. The mean systolic blood pressure (SBP) change for the HypoPress group was –12.5 mmHg (SD = 4.2), whereas the placebo group exhibited a mean change of –2.3 mmHg (SD = 3.8). Diastolic BP reductions followed a similar pattern. The investigators also recorded adverse events, noting that 8% of the HypoPress group experienced dizziness, compared to 3% in the placebo group. The study concluded that HypoPress significantly lowers SBP and DBP over a 4‑week period, with a tolerable side‑effect profile. The data suggest a dose‑dependent relationship and support further investigation in larger, longer‑term trials.

## bp980-2 What experimental design feature best controls for placebo effects in this study?
Correct: E
A double‑blind design, where both participants and investigators are unaware of group assignment, is the gold standard for controlling placebo effects in clinical trials.

## bp980-3 Calculate the percent reduction in mean systolic blood pressure for the HypoPress group if the baseline mean SBP was 140 mmHg.
Correct: A
Percent reduction = (12.5 mmHg ÷ 140 mmHg) × 100 % ≈ 8.9 %. The negative sign indicates a decrease, so we use the absolute value of the change.
