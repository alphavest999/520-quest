# BP469 - 2B Ketone bodies

**Passage:** Researchers examined the effect of varying concentrations of the essential cofactor NAD+ on the catalytic activity of hepatic HMG‑CoA lyase, the rate‑limiting enzyme in ketogenesis. Using isolated rat liver mitochondria, they measured the rate of acetoacetate production from HMG‑CoA at a fixed substrate concentration of 0.5 mM. Table 1 lists the observed Michaelis‑Menten parameters at three NAD+ concentrations (0.1, 0.5, and 1.0 mM). The data show a marked increase in Vmax with higher NAD+ while Km remains essentially unchanged, consistent with a cofactor‑dependent activation rather than classic inhibition. The researchers also performed a control experiment in which the mitochondria were pre‑treated with the irreversible inhibitor 3‑hydroxybutyrate (3‑HB) to confirm the specificity of the assay. The results support the conclusion that adequate NAD+ is required for optimal ketone body synthesis during prolonged fasting.

**Table:**
| NAD+ (mM) | Vmax (nmol/min/mg) | Km (mM) |
|-----------|--------------------|--------|
| 0.1       | 80                 | 0.45   |
| 0.5       | 200                | 0.44   |
| 1.0       | 300                | 0.43   |

## 469_Q1 - What is the independent variable in Table 1?
Options: {"A":"NAD+ concentration","B":"Vmax","C":"Km","D":"Substrate concentration"}
Correct: A
Explanation: Analogy: Like turning the volume knob on a stereo, the only thing the scientists changed was the amount of NAD+ added. Officially, the independent variable is the factor that the experimenters vary to observe its effect on the dependent variable. In this table, NAD+ concentration is varied while Vmax and Km are measured. Option B is a dependent variable, C is a kinetic constant measured, and D was held constant at 0.5 mM.
Trap: Confusing the dependent variable (Vmax) with the independent variable (NAD+ concentration)

## 469_Q2 - If the researchers wanted to test whether inhibition by 3‑hydroxybutyrate (3‑HB) is competitive, what would they measure?
Options: {"A":"Change in Km while Vmax remains constant","B":"Change in Vmax while Km remains constant","C":"Both A and B","D":"Neither A nor B"}
Correct: A
Explanation: Analogy: Imagine a parking lot where cars (substrate) can only fit if the gates (active sites) are open. A competitive inhibitor is like a person blocking the gate; it increases the apparent difficulty (Km) but does not reduce the maximum number of cars that can park (Vmax). Officially, a competitive inhibitor raises Km without changing Vmax. Option B describes non‑competitive inhibition, C is impossible, and D ignores the hallmark of competitive inhibition.
Trap: Mistaking non‑competitive inhibition (Vmax change) for competitive inhibition

## 469_Q3 - Using the Michaelis‑Menten equation and the data for 0.5 mM NAD+, what is the expected rate of acetoacetate production when the substrate concentration is 0.5 mM?
Options: {"A":"≈106 nmol/min/mg","B":"120 nmol/min/mg","C":"80 nmol/min/mg","D":"200 nmol/min/mg"}
Correct: A
Explanation: Analogy: Think of a factory that can produce a maximum of 200 widgets per hour (Vmax). If only half the required raw material (substrate) is available, the output will be less than the maximum. Using V = (Vmax·[S])/(Km+[S]) = (200·0.5)/(0.44+0.5) ≈ 106 nmol/min/mg. Option B is the Vmax value, C is the Vmax at the lowest NAD+, and D ignores the substrate limitation.
Trap: Using Vmax directly as the rate or mis‑calculating the denominator

## 469_Q5 - Which metabolic pathway is most directly affected by changes in HMG‑CoA lyase activity?
Options: {"A":"Glycolysis","B":"Lipogenesis","C":"Ketogenesis","D":"Urea cycle"}
Correct: C
Explanation: Analogy: HMG‑CoA lyase is the gatekeeper of the ketone factory; if it works faster, more ketone bodies are produced. Ketogenesis is the pathway that generates acetoacetate, β‑hydroxybutyrate, and acetone from fatty acids. Glycolysis and lipogenesis are unrelated, and the urea cycle deals with nitrogen waste.
Trap: Choosing a pathway that uses the same substrates but is not the direct product of HMG‑CoA lyase
