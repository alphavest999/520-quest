# BP1071

Researchers conducted a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive agent, Hypotensol, in adults with stage 1 hypertension. One hundred and twenty participants were enrolled and randomly assigned to one of three groups: placebo (n=40), Hypotensol low‑dose (n=40), or Hypotensol high‑dose (n=40). Baseline systolic blood pressure (SBP) was measured after a 5‑minute seated rest and averaged over three readings. Participants received their assigned treatment daily for 12 weeks. SBP was reassessed at weeks 4, 8, and 12. Table 1 summarizes the mean SBP at baseline and at week 12 for each group. The primary endpoint was the mean change in SBP from baseline to week 12. Secondary endpoints included the proportion of participants achieving target SBP <140 mmHg and the incidence of adverse events. The study was approved by the institutional review board, and all participants provided written informed consent. The investigators also monitored heart rate and serum creatinine to assess safety. Adverse events were recorded and graded according to the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE).

## bp1071-01 Which group experienced the greatest reduction in systolic blood pressure?
Correct: C
The high‑dose group decreased from 140 to 118 mmHg, a 22 mmHg drop, which is larger than the 16 mmHg drop in the low‑dose group and the 4 mmHg drop in placebo.

## bp1071-02 Which design feature best controls for the placebo effect in this study?
Correct: B
Double‑blind design prevents both participants and investigators from knowing treatment allocation, thereby minimizing placebo and observer biases.

## bp1071-03 What was the mean reduction in systolic blood pressure for the low‑dose Hypotensol group?
Correct: A
Baseline 141 mmHg minus week 12 125 mmHg equals a 16 mmHg reduction.

## bp1071-04 Which of the following conclusions is NOT supported by the study data?
Correct: C
The passage does not provide data on adverse event rates for each dose; thus, a conclusion about increased adverse events in the low‑dose group is unsupported.

## bp1071-05 How do the study results align with current hypertension treatment guidelines?
Correct: A
The significant SBP reductions, especially in the high‑dose group, support the potential use of Hypotensol as a first‑line pharmacologic option per guideline recommendations.
