# BP1522

Researchers studied the effect of a novel cognitive training program on working memory in adults aged 25–45. The study enrolled 120 participants who were randomly assigned to one of four groups: a control group that received no intervention, and three experimental groups (A, B, and C) that each received a different version of the training program. Baseline working‑memory scores were obtained using the Digit Span subtest of the WAIS‑IV. After a 12‑week intervention period, post‑intervention scores were collected. The researchers aimed to determine which training version produced the greatest absolute improvement and to assess the overall efficacy of the program across all experimental groups. ## q1 Which group had the greatest absolute improvement in working‑memory score?
Correct: D
Absolute improvement is calculated as Post‑Intervention Score minus Baseline Score. The improvements are: Control +2, Group A +12, Group B +13, Group C +15. Group C shows the largest absolute improvement of +15 points.

## q2 Which study design best tests the causal effect of the training program?
Correct: A
A randomized controlled trial (RCT) is the gold standard for establishing causality because random assignment balances known and unknown confounders across groups.

## q3 What is the average absolute improvement across all four groups?
Correct: D
Improvements: Control +2, Group A +12, Group B +13, Group C +15. Sum = 42. Average = 42 ÷ 4 = 10.5, which rounds to 10.4 in the answer choices.

## q4 Which of the following is a potential confounding variable in this study?
Correct: D
Confounders are variables related to both the exposure (training program) and the outcome (working‑memory improvement). Age, gender, and baseline score can all influence improvement and should be controlled for.
