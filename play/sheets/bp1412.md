Researchers studied the effect of caffeine on reaction time in a controlled laboratory setting. Sixty healthy adults aged 20-35 were randomly assigned to one of four groups: a placebo group (Group C), a low-dose caffeine group (Group A), a moderate-dose caffeine group (Group B), and a high-dose caffeine group (Group D). Each participant completed a computerized simple reaction time task consisting of 100 trials. The mean reaction times (in milliseconds) and standard deviations were recorded for each group. Table 1 summarizes the results. The researchers hypothesized that moderate caffeine would produce the greatest improvement in reaction time, while high doses might lead to overstimulation and slower responses. Statistical analysis revealed a significant main effect of caffeine dose on reaction time (p < 0.01). Post-hoc comparisons indicated that Group B’s mean was 45 ms faster than the placebo, whereas Group D’s mean was 12 ms slower than the placebo. The authors concluded that moderate caffeine intake optimizes cognitive performance, but excessive consumption may impair it.
