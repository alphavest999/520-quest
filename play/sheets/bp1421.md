# BP1421

Researchers conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive agent, Hypertensol, in adults with stage 1 hypertension. Three groups were studied: a placebo group, a low‑dose Hypertensol group (10 mg daily), and a high‑dose Hypertensol group (20 mg daily). Each group consisted of 50 participants. Baseline systolic blood pressure (SBP) was measured after a 5‑minute seated rest. After 12 weeks of treatment, SBP was reassessed under identical conditions. The investigators reported mean SBP values and standard deviations for each group. The primary endpoint was the mean reduction in SBP from baseline to week 12. Secondary analyses examined the proportion of participants achieving a target SBP <140 mmHg. The study concluded that Hypertensol produced dose‑dependent reductions in SBP, with the high‑dose group achieving the greatest mean decrease. The authors discussed potential mechanisms, including enhanced vasodilation and renal sodium excretion, and acknowledged limitations such as the short duration and lack of long‑term outcome data.

## bp1421_q1 Which group experienced the greatest mean reduction in systolic blood pressure?
Correct: C
The high‑dose group’s mean SBP decreased from 151 mmHg to 130 mmHg, a 21 mmHg reduction, which is larger than the 7 mmHg reduction in the low‑dose group and the 2 mmHg reduction in the placebo group.

## bp1421_q2 What type of study design was employed in this research?
Correct: C
Participants were randomly assigned to placebo or drug doses and followed prospectively for 12 weeks, which defines a randomized controlled trial.

## bp1421_q3 Calculate the percent reduction in mean systolic blood pressure for the high‑dose group.
Correct: C
Percent reduction = ((151 − 130)/151) × 100 ≈ 21.0%. The other options are calculated using incorrect denominators or rounding errors.

Correct: C
The study was described as randomized and controlled, implying blinding. The other options were explicitly mentioned as limitations in the passage.
