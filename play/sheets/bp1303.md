# BP1303

Researchers studied the effect of a novel dietary supplement, NutriBoost, on the metabolic rate of adult male rats. In a randomized controlled trial, 60 rats were assigned to either the NutriBoost group (n=30) or a placebo group (n=30). Over a 12-week period, the researchers measured resting metabolic rate (RMR) weekly using indirect calorimetry. Baseline RMR values were similar between groups (mean 45.2 ± 3.1 kcal/day for NutriBoost, 44.9 ± 2.9 kcal/day for placebo). At week 12, the NutriBoost group exhibited a mean RMR of 52.8 ± 3.4 kcal/day, while the placebo group showed 46.1 ± 3.0 kcal/day. The increase in RMR for the NutriBoost group was statistically significant (p < 0.01). Additionally, the researchers recorded body weight changes: the NutriBoost group gained an average of 0.8 kg, whereas the placebo group gained 1.2 kg. The authors concluded that NutriBoost may enhance metabolic rate and modestly reduce weight gain in rats.

## bp1303-1 Which of the following best describes the change in resting metabolic rate (RMR) observed in the NutriBoost group compared to the placebo group at the end of the study?
Correct: C
The table and passage state that the NutriBoost group’s RMR increased to 52.8 kcal/day versus 46.1 kcal/day for placebo, with a p-value < 0.01 indicating statistical significance.

## bp1303-2 Which of the following best describes the experimental design used in this study?
Correct: B
The passage specifies random assignment to NutriBoost or placebo, a 12‑week period, and weekly RMR measurements, which constitutes a randomized controlled trial with repeated measures.

## bp1303-3 What is the absolute increase in resting metabolic rate (RMR) from baseline to week 12 for the NutriBoost group?
Correct: A
Baseline RMR for NutriBoost is 45.2 kcal/day; week 12 RMR is 52.8 kcal/day. The absolute increase = 52.8 – 45.2 = 7.6 kcal/day.
