# BP1346

Researchers studied the effect of three different cognitive training protocols on reaction time in adults aged 20–35. Participants were randomly assigned to one of four groups: a control group that received no training, Treatment A that used a visual-spatial memory task, Treatment B that used a verbal working memory task, and a Combined group that received both tasks. Each training session lasted 30 minutes and was conducted twice a week for six weeks. Reaction times were measured using a computerized Stroop task at baseline, mid-intervention, and post-intervention. The data collected showed that the Combined group had the greatest reduction in reaction time, followed by Treatment A, Treatment B, and the Control group. Researchers concluded that multimodal training may produce the most robust improvements in cognitive processing speed.

## bp1346_q2 Which experimental design would best isolate the effect of multimodal training on reaction time?
Correct: B
A between-subjects randomized controlled trial with four parallel groups allows direct comparison of each training condition while controlling for inter-individual variability.

## bp1346_q3 Calculate the absolute difference in mean reaction time between Treatment A and the Control group.
Correct: A
Treatment A mean = 485 ms; Control mean = 520 ms. Difference = 520 – 485 = 35 ms.

## bp1346_q4 Which of the following is a potential confounding variable that could bias the results?
Correct: D
Baseline fitness, interface color, and testing time could all influence reaction time independently of the training protocol, making them potential confounders.

## bp1346_q5 Which concept best explains the observed greater improvement in the Combined group?
Correct: A
Transfer of training refers to the ability of skills learned in one domain to improve performance in another domain; multimodal training likely enhances multiple cognitive processes, leading to greater overall improvement.
