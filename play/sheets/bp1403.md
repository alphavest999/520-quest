# BP1403

Researchers studied the effect of a novel dietary supplement, NutriBoost, on metabolic rate in adult mice. Twenty-four male C57BL/6 mice, aged 8 weeks, were randomly assigned to two groups: a control group receiving standard chow (n=12) and an experimental group receiving standard chow supplemented with 5% NutriBoost (n=12). Over a 6-week period, daily food intake and body weight were recorded. Metabolic rate was assessed weekly using indirect calorimetry, measuring oxygen consumption (VO2) and carbon dioxide production (VCO2). At the end of the study, mice were euthanized, and liver and adipose tissue samples were collected for histological analysis and gene expression profiling of key metabolic regulators (e.g., PPARα, UCP1). Preliminary data indicated that the experimental group exhibited a 12% increase in VO2 compared to controls, with no significant difference in food intake. Histology revealed increased brown adipose tissue (BAT) activity in the experimental group. Gene expression analysis showed upregulation of UCP1 and PPARα by 2.5-fold and 1.8-fold, respectively. These findings suggest that NutriBoost may enhance basal metabolic rate through activation of BAT and upregulation of thermogenic genes.

## bp1403_q1 Which parameter shows the greatest relative increase in the experimental group compared to the control group?
Correct: A
VO2 increased from 0.45 to 0.50 ml/min, a 11.1% rise, which is larger than the changes in VCO2 (10%), food intake (−3%), or body weight (−1.6%).

## bp1403_q2 Which statement best describes the experimental design used in this study?
Correct: A
The investigators randomly assigned mice to a control or experimental group and manipulated the dietary supplement, fitting the definition of a randomized controlled trial.

## bp1403_q3 What is the percent increase in VO2 from the control to the experimental group?
Correct: C
Percent increase = (0.50 – 0.45) / 0.45 × 100 ≈ 11.1%. The closest answer is 11%.

## bp1403_q4 Which of the following could be a confounding variable that might influence the observed increase in VO2?
Correct: D
All listed factors—age, circadian timing, and genetic background—can affect metabolic rate and thus confound the results if not controlled.

## bp1403_q5 Which mechanism best explains how upregulation of UCP1 contributes to the increased VO2 observed in the experimental group?
Correct: A
UCP1 uncouples oxidative phosphorylation in brown adipose tissue, leading to heat production and increased oxygen consumption (fatty acid oxidation).
