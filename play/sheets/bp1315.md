# BP1315

Researchers at the University of Health Sciences investigated the effect of a novel cognitive training program on working memory performance in adults aged 60-75. The study employed a randomized controlled design with two groups: an intervention group receiving the training and a wait‑list control group. Participants completed the Digit Span Backward test at baseline, immediately after the 8‑week program, and at a 3‑month follow‑up. Table 1 summarizes the mean scores and standard deviations for each group across the three time points. The intervention group showed a mean improvement of 1.8 points from baseline to post‑intervention, while the control group’s mean change was 0.2 points. At the 3‑month follow‑up, the intervention group maintained a mean score 1.5 points higher than baseline, indicating sustained benefits. The researchers concluded that the training program significantly enhances working memory in older adults and may reduce cognitive decline. However, they noted limitations such as the small sample size (n=40) and lack of blinding.
