# BP1123

Researchers at the University of Greenfield investigated the effect of a novel probiotic supplement on gut microbiota composition in adults with irritable bowel syndrome (IBS). The study enrolled 120 participants, randomly assigning them to either the probiotic group (n = 60) or a placebo group (n = 60). Over a 12‑week period, stool samples were collected at baseline, week 6, and week 12. The primary outcome was the relative abundance of Bifidobacterium longum, measured by quantitative PCR. Secondary outcomes included IBS symptom severity scores (IBS‑SSS) and quality of life (IBS‑QOL) questionnaires. Table 1 summarizes the baseline characteristics and primary outcome results at week 12. The probiotic group showed a 35% increase in Bifidobacterium longum relative abundance compared to a 5% increase in the placebo group. IBS‑SSS scores decreased by an average of 45 points in the probiotic group versus 10 points in the placebo group, and IBS‑QOL scores improved by 12 points versus 3 points, respectively.

## 1 Based on Table 1, what is the absolute increase in Bifidobacterium longum relative abundance in the probiotic group from baseline to week 12?
Correct: C
The probiotic group increased from 12.5% to 17.1%, a difference of 4.6%.

## 2 Which study design best supports a causal inference that the probiotic supplement reduces IBS symptoms?
Correct: B
An RCT with randomization and double‑blinding minimizes bias and confounding, providing the strongest evidence for causality.

## 3 Calculate the mean IBS‑SSS change for the entire study population.
Correct: A
Mean change = (−45 + (−10))/2 = −27.5.

## 4 Which of the following statements is a potential trap when interpreting the probiotic study results?
Correct: C
While the improvement is statistically significant, clinical significance requires a predefined minimal clinically important difference, which is not provided.

## 5 How does this probiotic study exemplify the principle of the 'placebo effect' in clinical research?
Correct: C
The placebo group’s modest improvement demonstrates that patient expectations can produce measurable benefits, even without active treatment.
