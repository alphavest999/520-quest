Researchers studied the effect of three different cognitive training programs on working memory performance in adults aged 25-35. In a randomized controlled trial, 120 participants were assigned to one of four groups: a control group that received no training, and three experimental groups that underwent 8 weeks of training with Program A (visual‑spatial), Program B (verbal), and Program C (dual‑task). Working memory was assessed pre‑ and post‑intervention using the n‑back task (2‑back and 3‑back). Table 1 summarizes the mean reaction times and error rates for each group after training. The results indicated that Program A led to the greatest improvement in reaction time, followed by Program B, while Program C showed no significant change compared to control. However, error rates decreased significantly only in Program A. The researchers concluded that visual‑spatial training may be more effective for enhancing working memory speed, but further studies are needed to confirm these findings and explore underlying neural mechanisms.
