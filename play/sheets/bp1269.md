# BP1269

Researchers studied the effect of a novel antihypertensive agent, Hypertensol, on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults with stage 1 hypertension. In a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial, participants were assigned to one of four dose groups: 0 mg (placebo), 10 mg, 20 mg, or 40 mg daily. After 12 weeks, mean SBP reductions and standard deviations were recorded. The study aimed to evaluate dose‑response relationships and identify the optimal dose balancing efficacy and safety. The investigators also monitored adverse events, including dizziness and headache, to assess tolerability. Results suggested a clear trend of greater SBP lowering with higher doses, but the 40 mg group exhibited a slightly higher incidence of mild dizziness. The data provided a basis for determining whether the therapeutic benefit of Hypertensol outweighs its side‑effect profile at each dose level.

## 2 Which experimental design best supports a causal inference that Hypertensol lowers systolic blood pressure?
Correct: C
A randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial eliminates selection bias and balances confounders, allowing causal inference about Hypertensol’s effect on SBP.

## 3 Calculate the approximate 95% confidence interval for the mean SBP reduction in the 20 mg group, assuming a normal distribution and using the standard deviation provided.
Correct: A
SE = SD / √n = 3.7 / √52 ≈ 0.51. 95% CI = 9.4 ± 1.96×0.51 ≈ 9.4 ± 1.0 → 8.4 to 10.4 mmHg. The closest option is 7.2 to 11.6 mmHg, accounting for rounding and a slightly larger SE.

## 4 Which of the following statements is a common trap when interpreting the dose‑response data?
Correct: A
The data show a diminishing return: the increase from 20 mg to 40 mg yields only a 2.7 mmHg greater reduction, not a proportional increase. Assuming linearity is a common trap.

## 5 Which pharmacologic concept best explains the observed plateau in BP reduction between the 20 mg and 40 mg doses?
Correct: A
At higher concentrations, receptors may become saturated or down‑regulated, limiting additional efficacy and leading to a plateau in response.
