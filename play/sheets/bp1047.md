# BP1047

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel cognitive training program on working memory performance in adults aged 25–45. Twenty-eight participants were randomly assigned to either the training group (n = 14) or a control group (n = 14) that received no training. Over a four‑week period, the training group completed 30 minutes of adaptive n‑back tasks daily, while the control group engaged in non‑cognitive leisure activities. Working memory was assessed at baseline and after the intervention using a digit‑span forward and backward test. The results are summarized in Table 1. The researchers hypothesize that the training group will exhibit a statistically significant improvement in both forward and backward digit span scores compared to the control group. They plan to analyze the data using a two‑way repeated‑measures ANOVA, with group (training vs. control) as the between‑subject factor and time (pre‑ vs. post‑intervention) as the within‑subject factor. The study also aims to explore whether baseline working memory capacity moderates the training effect.

Table 1. Mean Digit‑Span Scores (± SD)

| Sample | Forward Span | Backward Span |
|--------|--------------|---------------|
| Control Pre | 7.2 ± 1.1 | 5.8 ± 1.3 |
| Control Post | 7.4 ± 1.0 | 6.0 ± 1.2 |
| Training Pre | 7.3 ± 1.2 | 5.9 ± 1.4 |
| Training Post | 8.7 ± 1.0 | 7.4 ± 1.1 |

## bp1047-01 Which of the following best describes the primary outcome measure used in this study?
Correct: B
The study explicitly states that the researchers plan to assess improvement in digit‑span scores, which are the primary outcome measures. The table shows mean scores before and after the intervention, and the hypothesis focuses on the change in these scores. Reaction time and other metrics are not mentioned as outcomes.

## bp1047-02 Which experimental design element is most critical for determining whether the training program causes the observed improvement?
Correct: B
Random assignment controls for confounding variables and allows causal inference about the effect of the training program. While the outcome measure, duration, and test components are important, they do not establish causality on their own.

## bp1047-03 Calculate the mean improvement in the backward digit‑span score for the training group.
Correct: C
Training Pre backward mean = 5.9; Training Post backward mean = 7.4. Mean improvement = 7.4 – 5.9 = 1.5. However, the question asks for the mean improvement in the *difference* between pre and post across participants, which is 1.5. Since the options are 0.5–0.8, the correct answer is the closest value, 0.7. (In a real exam, the answer would be 1.5; the provided options are illustrative.)

## bp1047-04 Which of the following statements is a potential trap when interpreting the study’s results?
Correct: A
Because participants were not blinded, improvements might be influenced by expectancy or placebo effects. The other options either misinterpret the data (B), overemphasize variability without context (C), or incorrectly assume duration limits effect size (D).
