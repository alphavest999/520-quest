# BP1043

Researchers at the National Institute of Sleep Studies investigated how acute sleep deprivation affects cognitive performance. Twenty healthy adults (10 male, 10 female) were randomly assigned to either a sleep-deprived group (n = 10) or a well-rested control group (n = 10). All participants completed a standardized neuropsychological battery after 8 hours of sleep (control) or after 24 hours of wakefulness (deprived). The battery included a simple reaction time task, a working memory task (n-back), and a verbal recall test. The researchers recorded reaction times (ms), working memory scores (percentage correct), and verbal recall scores (number of words recalled). The data are summarized in Table 1. The study aimed to determine whether sleep deprivation leads to measurable declines in processing speed, memory accuracy, and recall ability, and to assess whether these effects differ by sex or age.
