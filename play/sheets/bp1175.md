# BP1175

Researchers studied the effect of a novel antihypertensive agent on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults with stage‑1 hypertension. A randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial enrolled 240 participants, who were assigned to one of four groups: placebo, low‑dose (25 mg), medium‑dose (50 mg), or high‑dose (100 mg) of the drug. Baseline SBP was measured after a 5‑minute seated rest. After 4 weeks of daily dosing, SBP was reassessed. The investigators reported mean SBP values and the mean change from baseline for each group. The study aimed to determine dose‑response relationships and to evaluate safety signals. The primary outcome was the mean reduction in SBP at week 4. Secondary outcomes included the proportion of patients achieving target SBP <140 mmHg and incidence of adverse events. The trial’s design allowed for comparison of dose‑dependent efficacy while maintaining blinding and randomization integrity. Data analysis used analysis of covariance adjusting for baseline SBP. The results suggested a clear dose‑response curve, with the high‑dose group showing the largest mean reduction.

## bp1175-01 Which group experienced the greatest mean reduction in systolic blood pressure after 4 weeks?
Correct: D
The high‑dose group’s mean SBP decreased from 152 mmHg to 131 mmHg, a 21 mmHg reduction, which is larger than the reductions seen in the other groups.

## bp1175-02 Which of the following design elements is missing from the study that would best strengthen the causal inference between the drug and blood pressure reduction?
Correct: A
The study already uses randomization, blinding, and a placebo control. A crossover design would further reduce inter‑subject variability and strengthen causal inference.
