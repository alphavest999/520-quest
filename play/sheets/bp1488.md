# BP1488

Researchers investigated the effect of varying concentrations of a novel antioxidant, Compound X, on the oxidative stress response in cultured human fibroblasts. Cells were exposed to 0, 10, 25, 50, and 100 µM of Compound X for 24 hours. After treatment, intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels were quantified using a fluorescent probe, and cell viability was assessed via an MTT assay. Additionally, the expression of the antioxidant enzyme superoxide dismutase 2 (SOD2) was measured by quantitative PCR. ## bp1488-1 Which concentration of Compound X resulted in the lowest ROS fluorescence intensity?
Correct: C
The table shows that 50 µM Compound X yielded the lowest ROS fluorescence intensity (120 AU) compared to all other concentrations.

## bp1488-2 Which experimental control would best account for potential confounding by light exposure during fluorescence measurement?
Correct: B
Adding a light‑exposure control group allows the experimenter to determine whether light exposure itself alters ROS readings, thereby controlling for this confounder.

## bp1488-3 If the mean ROS intensity at 50 µM is 120 units and at 0 µM is 200 units, what percent reduction in ROS does 50 µM Compound X produce?
Correct: B
Percent reduction = (200 − 120)/200 × 100 = 80/200 × 100 = 40%.

## bp1488-4 Which statement reflects a common misconception about antioxidant supplementation?
Correct: A
The belief that antioxidants universally lower oxidative stress is misleading; many studies show dose‑dependent pro‑oxidant effects and context‑specific outcomes.

## bp1488-5 Which future study would best extend the findings of this experiment?
Correct: A
An in vivo animal model would assess the therapeutic relevance and pharmacokinetics of Compound X beyond the in vitro fibroblast system.
