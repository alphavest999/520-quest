# BP1115

Researchers studied the effect of caffeine on reaction time in a controlled laboratory setting. Twenty-four healthy adults were randomly assigned to one of three groups: a placebo group (n=8), a low-dose caffeine group (n=8), and a high-dose caffeine group (n=8). Each participant completed a computerized Go/No-Go task while their reaction times were recorded. The mean reaction time and standard deviation for each group were summarized in Table 1 below. The researchers hypothesized that caffeine would reduce reaction time, with a greater effect at the higher dose. The study aimed to quantify the dose-response relationship and assess variability among individuals. The data were collected over a single session to minimize confounding factors such as circadian rhythm and prior caffeine intake. The authors noted that while the mean differences were statistically significant, individual differences were substantial, suggesting that caffeine’s effect may vary across people.

Table 1: Reaction Time by Group

| Group            | Mean Reaction Time (ms) | Standard Deviation (ms) |
|------------------|-------------------------|-------------------------|
| Placebo          | 350                     | 30                      |
| Low-dose Caffeine| 310                     | 25                      |
| High-dose Caffeine| 280                    | 20                      |

## bp1115_q1 Which group exhibited the highest mean reaction time?
Correct: A
The placebo group had a mean reaction time of 350 ms, which is higher than the 310 ms (low-dose) and 280 ms (high-dose) groups.
