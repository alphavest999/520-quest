# BP1650

Researchers at the University of Midvale investigated the effect of a new cognitive training program on working memory capacity in adults aged 25 to 45. Sixty participants were randomly assigned to either the training group (n = 30) or a control group that received no training (n = 30). Over a 6‑week period, the training group completed 45 minutes of adaptive working‑memory tasks each week, while the control group engaged in unrelated leisure activities. Pre‑ and post‑intervention assessments measured working‑memory capacity using the n‑back task (0‑back, 1‑back, 2‑back, 3‑back). Table 1 summarizes the mean scores and standard deviations for each group at both time points. The researchers hypothesized that the training group would show a statistically significant improvement in 3‑back performance relative to controls. Data were analyzed using a mixed‑design ANOVA with group (training vs. control) as the between‑subject factor and time (pre vs. post) as the within‑subject factor. Results indicated a significant interaction (p < .01), suggesting the training program effectively enhanced working‑memory capacity.

## 1 Which of the following best describes the main finding of the study?
Correct: C
The mixed‑design ANOVA revealed a significant interaction, indicating that the training group’s 3‑back scores increased from 48 to 61, while the control group’s scores changed minimally from 47 to 49.

## 2 Which experimental design best matches the study described?
Correct: B
Participants were randomly assigned to training or control groups and followed over time, fitting a randomized controlled trial.

## 4 Which of the following statements is a common trap when interpreting the study’s results?
Correct: A
Randomized assignment controls for confounders, but the study still assumes causality; a trap is to overlook other possible influences such as motivation or test‑retest effects.
