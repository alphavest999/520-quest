# BP1109

Researchers studied the effects of a novel antioxidant compound, Antiox-7, on oxidative stress markers in cultured human endothelial cells. The cells were exposed to 0, 5, 10, and 20 µM concentrations of Antiox-7 for 24 hours. After treatment, intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels were quantified using a fluorescent probe, and malondialdehyde (MDA) concentrations were measured via TBARS assay. The results are summarized in Table 1. The data indicate a dose‑dependent reduction in ROS and MDA levels, with the most pronounced effect observed at 20 µM. Statistical analysis revealed that the decrease in ROS at 20 µM was significant (p < 0.01) compared to the control group.

## bp1109-04 Which of the following is a potential confounding factor that could invalidate the conclusion that Antiox-7 reduces ROS?
Correct: A
If the probe responds to pH changes, observed fluorescence changes might reflect pH shifts rather than true ROS alterations, confounding the results.

Correct: B
MDA is a marker of lipid peroxidation; its reduction suggests less oxidative damage to lipids, which is implicated in atherogenesis.
