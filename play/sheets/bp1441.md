# BP1441

Researchers conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive agent, Hypertenso, in reducing systolic blood pressure among adults with stage 1 hypertension. Two hundred and forty participants were enrolled and randomly assigned to either the Hypertenso group (n=120) or a placebo group (n=120). Baseline mean systolic blood pressure (SBP) was 145.2 mmHg (SD 10.5) in the Hypertenso group and 144.8 mmHg (SD 10.3) in the placebo group. After a 4‑week treatment period, the Hypertenso group’s mean SBP decreased to 130.4 mmHg (SD 9.8), while the placebo group’s mean SBP fell to 141.6 mmHg (SD 10.1). The mean change in SBP was −14.8 mmHg (SD 3.2) for Hypertenso and −3.2 mmHg (SD 3.5) for placebo. The study also recorded adverse events, with 5% of participants in the Hypertenso group reporting dizziness compared to 2% in placebo. These findings suggest Hypertenso produces a clinically meaningful reduction in systolic blood pressure over a short period, with a tolerable safety profile.

## 2 What best describes the study design used in this trial?
Correct: A
Participants were randomly assigned to Hypertenso or placebo, which is the hallmark of a randomized controlled trial.

## 3 What is the percent reduction in mean systolic blood pressure for the Hypertenso group over the 4‑week period?
Correct: A
Percent reduction = (145.2 − 130.4) / 145.2 × 100 ≈ 10.2%.
