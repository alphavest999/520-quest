# BP1126

Researchers at the University of Metropolis investigated the effect of caffeine on simple reaction time in a controlled laboratory setting. Forty healthy adults were randomly assigned to one of four groups: a placebo (Control), 50 mg, 100 mg, or 200 mg of caffeine. Each participant completed a computerized reaction time task after ingesting their assigned dose. The mean reaction time (RT) in milliseconds, the standard deviation (SD), and the sample size (n) for each group are presented in Table 1. The study aimed to determine whether increasing caffeine dosage leads to a linear decrease in RT and to assess the magnitude of this effect. Participants were blinded to their dose, and the experimenter was unaware of group assignments to reduce expectancy bias. The researchers hypothesized that the 200 mg group would exhibit the fastest RT, followed by the 100 mg, 50 mg, and Control groups, reflecting a dose‑response relationship.
