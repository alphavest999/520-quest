# BP1176

Researchers studied the effect of a novel dietary supplement on resting metabolic rate (RMR) in adults aged 20–35. Sixty participants were randomly assigned to either the supplement group (n = 30) or a placebo group (n = 30). Baseline RMR was measured after an overnight fast using indirect calorimetry. Participants then received their assigned intervention daily for 12 weeks, after which RMR was reassessed under identical conditions. The supplement group exhibited a mean RMR increase of 120 kcal/day (SD = 35) compared to a 30 kcal/day increase (SD = 25) in the placebo group. Body composition changes were minimal in both groups. The authors concluded that the supplement modestly elevates RMR, potentially aiding weight management. The study’s strengths include randomization and a double‑blind design; limitations involve a relatively short follow‑up and lack of long‑term weight data.

## bp1176_q3 Assuming the mean RMR increase in the supplement group is 120 kcal/day with SD = 35, calculate the 95% confidence interval for the mean increase using a t‑distribution (df = 29).
Correct: B
t = t_{0.975,29} ≈ 2.045. SE = 35/√30 ≈ 6.39. Margin = 2.045 × 6.39 ≈ 13.07. 120 ± 13.07 → 106.93 – 133.07, rounded to 108 – 132 kcal/day. Option B is the closest.
