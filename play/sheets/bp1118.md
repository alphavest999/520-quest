# BP1118

Researchers at the Institute for Cognitive Enhancement examined the effects of two novel mnemonic techniques on short‑term memory retention. Participants (N = 120) were randomly assigned to one of four groups: (1) Control (no mnemonic training), (2) Technique A (the method of loci), (3) Technique B (semantic clustering), and (4) Combined (both techniques applied sequentially). Each participant was given a list of 20 unrelated words to memorize and then tested immediately and again after a 30‑minute delay. The table below summarizes the mean recall scores (out of 20) and standard deviations for each group at the two time points.

Table: Mean Recall Scores by Group and Time Point

| Group      | Time Point | Mean Score | SD |
|------------|------------|------------|----|
| Control    | Immediate  | 10.2       | 2.5 |
| Control    | Delayed    | 8.7        | 2.8 |
| Technique A| Immediate  | 13.5       | 2.1 |
| Technique A| Delayed    | 11.9       | 2.4 |
| Technique B| Immediate  | 12.8       | 2.3 |
| Technique B| Delayed    | 11.2       | 2.6 |
| Combined   | Immediate  | 15.4       | 1.9 |
| Combined   | Delayed    | 13.7       | 2.0 |

The researchers hypothesized that mnemonic training would improve both immediate and delayed recall, with the combined approach yielding the greatest benefit. They also noted that the standard deviations were smaller in the combined group, suggesting more consistent performance among participants who used both techniques.

## bp1118-01 Based on the data presented, which statement most accurately reflects the effect of mnemonic training on delayed recall?
Correct: B
The delayed recall mean for the Combined group (13.7) is higher than for Technique A (11.9) and Technique B (11.2), and also higher than the Control (8.7). This indicates that using both mnemonic strategies together provides additive benefits for delayed recall.

## bp1118-02 If you were to design a follow‑up study to further investigate the combined mnemonic technique, which of the following would be the most appropriate experimental design?
Correct: B
A double‑blind, randomized controlled trial is the gold standard for testing the efficacy of an intervention. Blinding reduces bias, and randomization ensures comparable groups. A placebo mnemonic would control for expectancy effects.

## bp1118-03 Calculate the absolute risk reduction (ARR) in forgetting (difference between immediate and delayed recall) for the Combined group compared to the Control group.
Correct: C
For the Control group, forgetting = 10.2 – 8.7 = 1.5. For the Combined group, forgetting = 15.4 – 13.7 = 1.7. ARR = 1.7 – 1.5 = 0.2. Since the question asks for the difference in forgetting (not risk reduction), the correct calculation is 1.7 – 1.5 = 0.2, which corresponds to option C when expressed in the same units as the table (points).

## bp1118-04 Which of the following statements is a common trap when interpreting the standard deviations reported in the table?
Correct: A
A smaller SD in the Combined group suggests more consistent performance, but it does not automatically mean the technique is superior. Consistency is one aspect; overall mean performance is also critical.

## bp1118-05 Which concept is most closely linked to the observed additive effect of the Combined mnemonic technique?
Correct: B
Dual‑Coding Theory posits that information is better remembered when encoded both visually and verbally. The Combined technique likely engages both spatial (method of loci) and semantic (clustering) pathways, leading to additive benefits.
