# BP1108

Researchers conducted a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of two novel antihypertensive agents, Drug A and Drug B, on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults with mild hypertension. A total of 150 participants were enrolled and randomly assigned to one of three groups: placebo (n=50), Drug A (n=50), or Drug B (n=50). Baseline SBP was measured after a 5‑minute seated rest period. Participants received their assigned treatment daily for 4 weeks. SBP was reassessed at the end of the study. Table 1 summarizes the mean SBP values and the mean change from baseline for each group. The results showed that both drugs reduced SBP, with Drug A producing a greater average reduction than Drug B. The placebo group exhibited a modest decrease, likely reflecting regression to the mean. These findings suggest that Drug A may be more effective in lowering SBP in this population, but further studies are needed to confirm long‑term safety and efficacy.
