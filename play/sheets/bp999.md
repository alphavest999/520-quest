# BP999

Researchers at the Greenhouse Institute conducted a controlled experiment to investigate how varying light intensity affects the growth of the common bean (Phaseolus vulgaris). Over a 30‑day period, seedlings were exposed to four levels of light intensity: 100, 200, 400, and 800 lux. Each intensity group contained 20 plants, randomly assigned to minimize bias. Measurements taken at the end of the trial included average plant height, total above‑ground biomass, and flower count. The results, summarized in Table 1, revealed a clear trend: as light intensity increased, average height and biomass rose, while flower production peaked at intermediate levels before declining at the highest intensity. The researchers hypothesize that photosynthetic photon flux density, coupled with photoinhibition at extreme lux levels, underlies these patterns. Their findings suggest practical implications for optimizing greenhouse lighting to balance vegetative growth and reproductive output.

Table 1. Effect of Light Intensity on Bean Growth

| Light Intensity (lux) | Avg Height (cm) | Biomass (g) | Flower Count |
|-----------------------|-----------------|-------------|--------------|
| 100                   | 12.4            | 3.1         | 2            |
| 200                   | 18.7            | 5.0         | 5            |
| 400                   | 27.3            | 12.0        | 7            |
| 800                   | 32.1            | 14.5        | 4            |

## bp999-1 Which light intensity level produced the highest average plant height?
Correct: D
The table shows average heights of 12.4, 18.7, 27.3, and 32.1 cm for 100, 200, 400, and 800 lux, respectively. The greatest height is at 800 lux.

## bp999-2 Which experimental design best controls for potential confounding variables in this study?
Correct: C
A randomized block design groups plants with similar initial conditions (e.g., seedling size) into blocks and then randomizes treatments within each block, reducing variability from confounders such as soil heterogeneity or micro‑climate differences.

## bp999-3 What is the percent increase in biomass when light intensity rises from 200 lux to 400 lux?
Correct: B
Biomass at 200 lux is 5.0 g; at 400 lux it is 12.0 g. Percent increase = [(12.0‑5.0)/5.0] × 100% = 140%.

## bp999-4 Which of the following statements is NOT a valid interpretation of the data presented?
Correct: A
While biomass increases with light intensity up to 800 lux, the data do not show a plateau or decline, but the statement is too absolute; other factors could limit biomass beyond the studied range.

## bp999-5 Which statement best links the observed growth patterns to underlying photosynthetic mechanisms?
Correct: C
The decline in flower count at 800 lux suggests photoinhibition, where excessive light damages photosystem II, reducing photosynthetic efficiency and reproductive output.
