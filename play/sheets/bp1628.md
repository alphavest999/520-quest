# BP1628

Researchers studied the effect of a novel antioxidant on oxidative stress markers in cultured human fibroblasts. They exposed cells to varying concentrations (0, 10, 20, 40 µM) of the compound for 24 hours and measured intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels, glutathione (GSH) concentration, and cell viability. The results are summarized in Table 1.

Table 1: Concentration (µM) | ROS (fluorescence units) | GSH (µM) | Viability (%)
0 | 100 | 8.0 | 95
10 | 70 | 9.5 | 93
20 | 50 | 10.8 | 90
40 | 30 | 12.2 | 85

## bp1628-01 Which concentration resulted in the lowest ROS level?
Correct: D
ROS values decrease with increasing antioxidant concentration; the lowest ROS (30 units) occurs at 40 µM.

## bp1628-02 To determine whether the antioxidant effect is dose‑dependent, which experimental design would best test this hypothesis?
Correct: B
A dose‑response in vitro study directly assesses the relationship between concentration and effect, which is ideal for testing dose dependence.

## bp1628-03 Calculate the percent decrease in ROS from 0 to 40 µM.
Correct: D
ROS decreases from 100 to 30 units, a reduction of 70 units. Percent decrease = (70/100) × 100 = 70%.

## bp1628-04 Which of the following statements is NOT supported by the data?
Correct: D
The data show ROS lowest at 40 µM, not 0 µM; thus statement D is false.

## bp1628-05 Which additional measurement would best help link antioxidant concentration to cellular metabolic activity?
Correct: A
ATP production directly reflects cellular metabolic activity and would best correlate antioxidant concentration with metabolic status.
