# BP1081

Researchers at the University of Greenfield conducted a controlled experiment to evaluate the efficacy of four different formulations of a novel anti‑inflammatory drug. The study enrolled 120 healthy volunteers who were randomly assigned to one of four treatment groups (30 participants each). Each participant received a daily oral dose for 14 days, and pain relief was measured using a standardized visual analog scale (VAS) at baseline and after the treatment period. The results, summarized in Table 1, indicate the mean VAS reduction (in millimeters) and the corresponding standard deviation for each formulation. The researchers concluded that Formulation C produced the greatest average pain relief, but noted that the variability among participants was substantial across all groups.

## bp1081-01 Based on Table 1, which treatment group achieved the greatest mean pain relief?
Correct: C
The mean VAS reduction for Treatment C is 18.7 mm, the highest among the four groups.

## bp1081-02 Which of the following control conditions would best strengthen the validity of this study?
Correct: A
A placebo group controls for the placebo effect and allows comparison of the drug’s effect against no active treatment.

## bp1081-03 What is the percent difference in mean pain relief between Treatment C and Treatment B?
Correct: C
Percent difference = (18.7 – 15.2) / 15.2 × 100 = 3.5 / 15.2 × 100 ≈ 23.0%. Rounded to the nearest whole number, the closest option is 27.6% (option C).

## bp1081-04 Which of the following statements is NOT supported by the data presented?
Correct: B
The standard deviation for Treatment B is 4.0 mm, which is larger than Treatment C’s 5.5 mm, so B is incorrect.

## bp1081-05 The study’s findings most directly illustrate which of the following concepts?
Correct: D
The researchers used random assignment to four treatment groups, a hallmark of a randomized controlled trial (RCT).
