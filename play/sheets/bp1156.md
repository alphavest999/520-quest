# BP1156

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel antioxidant compound, AOX-12, on oxidative stress markers in cultured human fibroblasts. Cells were plated at 1 × 10⁶ cells per well in 6‑well plates and allowed to adhere overnight. AOX-12 was added at concentrations of 0 (vehicle control), 5 µM, 10 µM, and 20 µM, and cells were incubated for 24 h. After treatment, intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) were measured using the DCFH‑DA assay, and glutathione (GSH) levels were quantified by a colorimetric GSH/GSSG kit. The data were expressed as mean ± SD from three independent experiments. The results showed a dose‑dependent decrease in ROS: 0 µM (1.00 ± 0.05), 5 µM (0.78 ± 0.04), 10 µM (0.55 ± 0.03), and 20 µM (0.32 ± 0.02). Conversely, GSH levels increased: 0 µM (1.00 ± 0.04), 5 µM (1.12 ± 0.05), 10 µM (1.28 ± 0.06), 20 µM (1.45 ± 0.07). The authors concluded that AOX‑12 effectively mitigates oxidative stress in vitro.

## Q2 Which experimental design change would best test whether the observed antioxidant effect of AOX‑12 is specific to fibroblasts?
Correct: A
Testing a different cell type determines if the effect is cell‑type specific. Incubation time or concentration changes do not address specificity, and a positive control does not test AOX‑12’s specificity.

Correct: B
The DCFH‑DA assay detects a broad range of ROS but can be influenced by other factors, potentially exaggerating the antioxidant effect. The other options are standard or strengthen the study.
