# BP1035

Researchers at the University of Health Sciences conducted a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial to evaluate the antihypertensive efficacy of a novel angiotensin‑II receptor blocker, Hypertensol, in adults with stage 1 hypertension. A total of 200 participants (mean age 48 ± 12 years; 52 % female) were randomly assigned to receive either Hypertensol (10 mg once daily) or matching placebo for 28 days. Systolic blood pressure (SBP) was measured at baseline and at days 7, 14, 21, and 28 using an automated cuff. Table 1 summarizes the mean SBP (± SD) for each group and the associated two‑tailed p‑values comparing treatment to placebo. The Hypertensol group exhibited a progressive decline in SBP, reaching a 15 % reduction by day 28 (127.5 mm Hg) compared with baseline (150 mm Hg). In contrast, the placebo group showed a modest, non‑significant rise in SBP over the same period. No serious adverse events were reported. These findings suggest that Hypertensol may provide clinically meaningful blood‑pressure control within a month of initiation, warranting further investigation in larger, longer‑term studies.
