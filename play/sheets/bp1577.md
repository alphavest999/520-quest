# BP1577

Researchers at the University of Midwest conducted a controlled laboratory study to investigate how varying amounts of sleep affect cognitive performance in healthy adults. A total of 60 participants (30 males, 30 females, aged 20–35) were randomly assigned to one of three sleep conditions: 4 hours, 6 hours, or 8 hours of sleep per night for three consecutive nights. After the final night, participants completed a battery of cognitive tasks, including a simple reaction time test and a word list recall task. The researchers recorded reaction times (milliseconds) and memory scores (number of words correctly recalled out of 20). Table 1 summarizes the mean values for each sleep condition. The study aimed to determine whether sleep deprivation leads to measurable declines in processing speed and memory retention, and whether the relationship is linear across the sleep duration spectrum. Findings could inform guidelines for shift workers, students, and clinicians managing sleep disorders.

Sleep Hours | Mean Reaction Time (ms) | Mean Memory Score (out of 20)
4 | 350 | 12
6 | 320 | 15
8 | 300 | 18

## bp1577_q1 According to Table 1, what is the mean reaction time for participants who slept 4 hours?
Correct: A
Table 1 lists the mean reaction time for the 4‑hour sleep condition as 350 ms.

## bp1577_q2 Which of the following best represents an appropriate control group for this study?
Correct: C
An appropriate control group would be participants who are not sleep‑restricted but receive the same amount of sleep as the experimental group (8 hours), allowing comparison of sleep deprivation effects.

## bp1577_q3 What is the percent improvement in memory score from the 4‑hour sleep condition to the 8‑hour sleep condition?
Correct: B
Improvement = (18 – 12) / 12 = 0.5 = 50%.

## bp1577_q4 Which of the following statements could mislead a reader into thinking that the study proves sleep causes better memory?
Correct: B
A correlation indicates association but does not establish causation; claiming causality would be misleading.

## bp1577_q5 How might the findings of this study be applied to improve performance among night‑shift nurses?
Correct: B
Sleep hygiene education can help nurses maintain adequate sleep before night shifts, thereby improving cognitive performance during work.
