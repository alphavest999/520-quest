# BP1111

Researchers investigated the effects of a novel dietary supplement on resting metabolic rate (RMR) in adults aged 25–45. A randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial enrolled 120 participants who were assigned to either the supplement group (n = 60) or the placebo group (n = 60). RMR was measured using indirect calorimetry at baseline and after 12 weeks of intervention. The study also collected data on body composition, dietary intake, and physical activity to control for confounding variables. The primary outcome was the change in RMR (kcal/day). Secondary outcomes included changes in lean body mass and fat mass. The investigators hypothesized that the supplement would increase RMR by at least 5% relative to placebo. Statistical analyses involved paired t‑tests within groups and ANCOVA between groups, adjusting for baseline RMR and age. The study aimed to provide evidence for a clinically meaningful metabolic benefit of the supplement.

## bp1111-01 Based on the table, which group experienced the greatest absolute increase in mean RMR over the 12‑week period?
Correct: C
The supplement group’s mean RMR increased from 1650 to 1725 kcal/day, a rise of 75 kcal/day. The placebo group increased from 1640 to 1660 kcal/day, a rise of 20 kcal/day. Thus, the supplement group had the greatest absolute increase.

## bp1111-02 Which experimental design best controls for individual differences when testing the supplement’s effect on RMR?
Correct: A
A crossover design allows each participant to serve as their own control, reducing variability due to individual differences in metabolism, age, and lifestyle. The parallel group design also controls for confounding but does not eliminate between‑subject variability as effectively.

## bp1111-05 How does the observed increase in RMR relate to the concept of energy balance?
Correct: B
RMR constitutes a major portion of total daily energy expenditure. An increase in RMR raises the total calories burned, which, if caloric intake remains constant, can shift the energy balance toward a deficit and support weight loss.
