# BP1455

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel antihypertensive agent, CardioX, on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults with mild hypertension. A randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial enrolled 120 participants (60 per arm). Baseline SBP was measured after a 5‑minute seated rest. Participants received either CardioX (10 mg daily) or matched placebo for 4 weeks. SBP was reassessed at week 4. Table 1 summarizes mean SBP (± SD) at baseline and after 4 weeks for both groups. The study aimed to determine whether CardioX significantly lowers SBP compared with placebo and to estimate the magnitude of change. The investigators also recorded adverse events to assess tolerability.

## bp1455_q1 Which of the following best describes the data presented in Table 1?
Correct: A
The mean SBP decreased by 13.2 mmHg in the CardioX group versus only 1.3 mmHg in the placebo group, indicating a substantial treatment effect. The difference is both statistically and clinically meaningful.

## bp1455_q2 If you were designing a follow‑up study to evaluate CardioX’s long‑term safety, which of the following study designs would be most appropriate?
Correct: B
A prospective cohort allows systematic collection of safety data over time in a defined group, providing incidence rates and temporal relationships. Cross‑sectional and retrospective designs are limited by recall bias and lack of temporal clarity.

Percent reduction = (13.2 mmHg ÷ 138.5 mmHg) × 100 % ≈ 9.5 %. However, the calculation uses the baseline mean (138.5). The correct answer is 9.5 %. (Note: The provided options were illustrative; the correct calculation yields 9.5 %.)

## bp1455_q4 Which of the following statements is a common trap when interpreting the p‑value reported for the difference between groups?
Correct: A
A p‑value <0.05 only indicates statistical significance, not clinical importance. The other options are partially correct: B is a misinterpretation (it indicates insufficient evidence to reject the null), C is correct, D is incorrect.

## bp1455_q5 How does the reduction in SBP observed with CardioX translate to clinical benefit for patients?
Correct: A
Meta‑analyses show that each 10 mmHg reduction in SBP lowers the risk of major cardiovascular events by ~20 %. Thus a 13.2 mmHg decrease confers a clinically meaningful benefit.
