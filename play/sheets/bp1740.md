# BP1740

Researchers studied the effect of a new dietary supplement on blood pressure in adults aged 30-50. In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, 120 participants were assigned to either the supplement group (n=60) or the placebo group (n=60). Baseline systolic blood pressure (SBP) was measured for all participants. After 12 weeks, SBP was measured again. The table below summarizes the mean change in SBP (in mmHg) and the standard deviation for each group. The supplement group showed a mean reduction of 8.5 mmHg (SD = 4.2) while the placebo group showed a mean reduction of 2.1 mmHg (SD = 3.9). The researchers concluded that the supplement significantly lowered SBP compared to placebo. They also reported that 30% of participants in the supplement group achieved a target SBP of <120 mmHg versus 15% in the placebo group. The study suggests that the supplement may be a useful adjunct in managing hypertension, but further research is needed to confirm long-term safety and efficacy.

## bp1740-1 Which of the following statements best describes the change in systolic blood pressure (SBP) for the supplement group relative to the placebo group?
Correct: A
The table shows a mean reduction of 8.5 mmHg for the supplement group versus 2.1 mmHg for placebo, indicating a greater mean reduction in the supplement group.

## bp1740-2 Which factor is most important to control in this study to reduce potential confounding?
Correct: A
Baseline SBP is a key predictor of follow-up SBP; randomization should balance it, but controlling or adjusting for it reduces confounding.

## bp1740-3 If 30% of the supplement group achieved a target SBP of <120 mmHg and 15% of the placebo group did, what is the absolute risk reduction (ARR)?
Correct: A
ARR = 30% – 15% = 15%, which expressed as a proportion is 0.15.

## bp1740-4 Which of the following is a common misinterpretation of the study’s results?
Correct: C
The study only included adults aged 30‑50; generalizing to all age groups is a trap. Options A, B, and D are supported by the data.

## bp1740-5 Which physiological mechanism is most likely responsible for the observed reduction in SBP with the supplement?
Correct: A
Many supplements that lower BP act by promoting natriuresis, increasing renal sodium excretion, which reduces intravascular volume and BP.
