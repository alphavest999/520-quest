# BP1033

Researchers conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive agent, HypoX, in adults with stage 1 hypertension. Eighty participants were enrolled and randomly assigned to either the HypoX group (n=40) or a placebo group (n=40). Baseline systolic blood pressure (SBP) and diastolic blood pressure (DBP) were recorded after a 24‑hour ambulatory monitoring period. After 12 weeks of daily dosing, follow‑up blood pressure measurements were obtained. The table below summarizes the data for a subset of 5 patients from the HypoX group, illustrating individual changes in SBP. The researchers hypothesized that HypoX would produce a clinically significant reduction in SBP compared to placebo. Statistical analysis was planned using paired t‑tests and intention‑to‑treat principles. The study also collected secondary outcomes such as heart rate variability and patient‑reported quality of life scores. Preliminary results suggested a mean SBP reduction of 12 mm Hg in the treatment arm, with a 95 % confidence interval of 8–16 mm Hg. The investigators intend to publish their findings in a peer‑reviewed journal.

## 5 Which concept best explains the use of a placebo group in this study?
Correct: C
A placebo group helps quantify the placebo effect, the improvement patients experience due to expectation rather than the active drug.
