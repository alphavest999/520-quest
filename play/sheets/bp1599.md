# BP1599

Researchers at a university investigated the effect of caffeine on short-term memory retention among college students. A total of 120 participants were randomly assigned to one of three groups: caffeine (n=40), placebo (n=40), and no caffeine (n=40). All participants underwent a standardized memory test consisting of 50 word pairs presented visually. After a 30‑minute break, recall performance was measured. The mean recall scores (out of 100) for each group were recorded. The study aimed to determine whether caffeine ingestion prior to the test would enhance memory retention compared to a placebo or no caffeine condition. Results suggested a graded effect, with the caffeine group outperforming the placebo group, which in turn outperformed the no‑caffeine group. The authors discussed potential mechanisms such as increased arousal and neurotransmitter modulation, while noting limitations including a homogeneous sample and short follow‑up period. They concluded that moderate caffeine intake may transiently improve memory recall in young adults, but further research is needed to assess long‑term effects and applicability to other populations.

Correct: A
Participants were randomly assigned to caffeine, placebo, or no‑caffeine groups, which is characteristic of a randomized controlled trial.

## bp1599-q3 Calculate the mean difference in recall scores between the caffeine and no‑caffeine groups.
Correct: A
Mean difference = 78 (caffeine) – 68 (no caffeine) = 10.

## bp1599-q4 Which of the following statements is a common misinterpretation of the study's results?
Correct: A
The study was limited to college students; generalizing to all populations is a misinterpretation.

## bp1599-q5 How could the findings be applied in educational settings?
Correct: A
The study suggests moderate caffeine intake can enhance short‑term memory, so providing caffeine before exams could be beneficial.
