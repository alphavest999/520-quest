# BP1208

Researchers investigated the impact of two novel cognitive training programs on working memory performance in adults aged 18-35. Participants were randomly assigned to one of three groups: a control group that received no training, Intervention A that used a visuospatial task, and Intervention B that used a verbal task. Baseline working memory was assessed using the Digit Span Forward and Backward tests. After a 4-week training period, post-intervention scores were re-evaluated. The study aimed to determine whether task modality influences the magnitude of working memory improvement and to assess the durability of training effects over a 3-month follow-up. Results were analyzed using repeated-measures ANOVA, and effect sizes were reported as Cohen’s d. The authors discussed potential neural mechanisms underlying observed gains and suggested implications for educational and occupational settings.

## bp1208-001 Which group demonstrated the greatest absolute improvement in working memory scores?
Correct: B
Intervention A improved from 12.2 to 15.8, a gain of 3.6 points, which is larger than the 1.7-point gain in Intervention B and the 0.4-point gain in Control.

## bp1208-002 Which experimental design feature best controls for confounding variables in this study?
Correct: A
Random assignment ensures that participant characteristics are evenly distributed across groups, minimizing confounding effects.

## bp1208-003 Calculate the percent improvement for Intervention A.
Correct: A
Percent improvement = ((15.8‑12.2)/12.2) × 100 ≈ 23.7%.

## bp1208-004 Which statement is a potential trap in interpreting the study’s results?
Correct: D
The 4-week duration is a design choice that may limit generalizability; it is not a direct result of the data and can mislead readers about the training’s efficacy.

## bp1208-005 Which theoretical framework best explains the superior performance of Intervention A?
Correct: C
Intervention A’s visuospatial tasks likely engage multisensory integration, enhancing working memory beyond verbal-only training.
