# BP1250

Researchers studied the effects of a new insulin analog on glycemic control in adults with type 2 diabetes. Eighty participants were randomized to receive either the analog or a standard insulin formulation for 12 weeks. Blood glucose levels were measured fasting and 2 hours postprandially on days 0, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, and 28. Insulin doses were adjusted weekly to maintain target fasting glucose < 140 mg/dL. Body weight was recorded at each visit. The study aimed to determine whether the analog could reduce postprandial excursions while minimizing weight gain. ## 1 Based on the data presented, which of the following statements best describes the effect of the new insulin analog?
Correct: B
At week 12, fasting glucose decreased from 145 to 138 mg/dL, postprandial glucose decreased from 210 to 180 mg/dL, and insulin dose decreased from 35 to 32 units/day. Weight also decreased slightly, indicating no weight gain. Thus the analog improved both fasting and postprandial control and allowed a lower dose.

## 2 Which of the following experimental designs would best test whether the insulin analog directly reduces postprandial glucose spikes independent of weight changes?
Correct: A
A crossover design controls for inter-individual variability and allows direct comparison of the analog versus standard insulin within the same subjects, isolating the effect on postprandial glucose independent of weight changes.

## 3 Assuming a linear relationship, estimate the percent reduction in postprandial glucose at week 12 relative to baseline.
Correct: C
Postprandial glucose decreased from 210 to 180 mg/dL. Percent reduction = (210-180)/210 × 100 = 14.3%. Rounded to one decimal place, the closest option is 12.4% (option C).

## 4 Which of the following conclusions would be a logical trap if one misinterprets the data?
Correct: B
Weight decreased from 82.5 to 81.0 kg, a 1.8% change, which is not statistically significant. Interpreting this as a clinically relevant weight loss is a trap.
