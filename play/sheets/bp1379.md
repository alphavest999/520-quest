# BP1379

Researchers studied the effect of antibiotic X on the growth of *Escherichia coli* strain K12 in a controlled laboratory setting. The experiment was conducted over a 48‑hour period, with samples collected every 12 hours. Bacterial counts were measured by plating serial dilutions on agar and counting colony‑forming units (CFU). The antibiotic concentration was maintained at 10 µg/mL throughout the experiment. The data are summarized in Table 1. The investigators observed a sharp decline in CFU after the first 12 hours, followed by a plateau and a slight rebound at 48 hours. The authors concluded that antibiotic X is effective in the early phase but that resistance mechanisms may emerge over time. The study design included a vehicle‑only control (no antibiotic) and a positive control with a known bactericidal agent (ampicillin). The authors also measured the expression of the *bla* gene by qPCR to assess the potential for β‑lactamase‑mediated resistance.

Table 1. Bacterial count and antibiotic concentration over time

| Time (h) | CFU/mL | Antibiotic X (µg/mL) |
|----------|--------|----------------------|
| 0        | 1.0×10⁶ | 10                   |
| 12       | 2.5×10⁴ | 10                   |
| 24       | 1.2×10⁴ | 10                   |
| 36       | 1.0×10⁴ | 10                   |
| 48       | 1.5×10⁴ | 10                   |

## q1379-01 Based on the data presented in Table 1, which of the following best describes the trend in bacterial population over the 48‑hour period?
Correct: C
The CFU/mL values show a dramatic drop from 1.0×10⁶ at 0 h to 2.5×10⁴ at 12 h, followed by a plateau around 1.0–1.2×10⁴ up to 36 h, and a modest rise to 1.5×10⁴ at 48 h, indicating a rebound.

## q1379-02 Which of the following experimental controls would best allow the researchers to attribute changes in CFU specifically to antibiotic X?
Correct: A
A vehicle‑only control provides a baseline for bacterial growth in the absence of any antibiotic, allowing direct comparison to the antibiotic‑treated group. While the positive control (ampicillin) is useful for benchmarking, it does not isolate the effect of antibiotic X alone.

## q1379-03 Calculate the average percent reduction in CFU from 0 h to 12 h.
Correct: A
Percent reduction = [(1.0×10⁶ – 2.5×10⁴) / 1.0×10⁶] × 100 = (9.75×10⁵ / 1.0×10⁶) × 100 ≈ 97.5 %.

## q1379-04 Which of the following statements is a potential pitfall when interpreting the rebound in CFU at 48 h?
Correct: A
A rebound in viable counts after an initial kill suggests that the antibiotic may only inhibit growth (bacteriostatic) and that surviving bacteria resume proliferation once the drug’s effect wanes. Contamination or concentration changes are less likely given the controlled conditions, and gene expression data are needed to confirm resistance.

## q1379-05 Which concept best explains the observed rebound in bacterial counts at 48 h?
Correct: A
The rebound is most plausibly due to the emergence of antibiotic‑resistant mutants that survive the initial kill and proliferate once the drug’s effect diminishes. This aligns with the study’s measurement of *bla* gene expression as a marker of resistance.
