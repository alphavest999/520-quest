# BP1329

Researchers conducted a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial to evaluate the antihypertensive effects of a novel compound, Hypertensol, at three different daily doses (10 mg, 20 mg, and 30 mg). A total of 150 adults with stage 1 hypertension were enrolled and randomly assigned to one of the four groups: placebo (n = 30), 10 mg (n = 60), 20 mg (n = 50), or 30 mg (n = 40). Systolic blood pressure (SBP) was measured at baseline and after 12 weeks of treatment. The mean SBP reductions, standard deviations, and sample sizes are summarized in Table 1. The investigators hypothesized a dose‑dependent response, with the 30 mg group expected to show the greatest reduction. Adverse events were mild and comparable across groups. The study’s primary endpoint was the mean change in SBP from baseline to week 12.

## bp1329-1 What is the mean systolic blood pressure (SBP) reduction for the 20 mg dose?
Correct: B
Table 1 lists the mean SBP reduction for the 20 mg group as 10.2 mmHg. The other options correspond to the 10 mg dose (7.8 mmHg), the 30 mg dose (12.4 mmHg), or an implausible value.

## bp1329-2 Which statement best describes the control group used in this study?
Correct: A
The study was placebo‑controlled; the control group received an inert tablet that matched Hypertensol in size, shape, and color, ensuring blinding.

## bp1329-3 What is the overall mean SBP reduction across all dose groups, weighted by sample size?
Correct: A
Weighted mean = (10.2×50 + 7.8×60 + 12.4×40) / (50+60+40) = 1474 / 150 ≈ 9.83 mmHg, rounded to 9.8 mmHg.

## bp1329-4 Which of the following is NOT a potential confounding variable in this study?
Correct: D
Sample size is a design parameter, not a confounder. Age, measurement device, and placebo effect can bias results if unevenly distributed.

## bp1329-5 Which statement best summarizes the study’s conclusion?
Correct: A
The data show a clear dose‑dependent trend: 10 mg → 7.8 mmHg, 20 mg → 10.2 mmHg, 30 mg → 12.4 mmHg, supporting a dose‑response relationship.
