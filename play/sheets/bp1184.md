# BP1184

Researchers at the University of Greenfield investigated the effect of a novel probiotic on weight gain in laboratory mice. A total of 80 mice were randomly assigned to either a control group (n = 40) receiving a standard diet or a treatment group (n = 40) receiving the same diet supplemented with the probiotic. The study spanned 28 days, with body weights recorded weekly. At the end of the study, the mean body weight of the control group was 30.2 g (SD = 1.5 g), whereas the treatment group averaged 32.8 g (SD = 1.3 g). A two‑tailed t‑test yielded a p‑value of 0.001, indicating a statistically significant difference between groups. The researchers also measured serum short‑chain fatty acids (SCFAs) and found higher acetate levels in the treatment group, suggesting a potential mechanistic link. The study concluded that probiotic supplementation may promote modest weight gain in mice, possibly mediated by altered gut microbiota metabolism.

## q1 What was the percent increase in mean body weight of the treatment group compared to the control group at the end of the study?
Correct: C
Percent increase = ((32.8 g – 30.2 g) / 30.2 g) × 100% ≈ 8.3%. Option C matches this calculation.

## q2 Which experimental design feature best ensures that the observed weight difference is attributable to the probiotic?
Correct: B
Random assignment controls for confounding variables, making it more likely that the probiotic is the cause of the weight difference.

## q3 Calculate the 95% confidence interval for the difference in mean weight between the treatment and control groups at day 28, assuming equal variances and using the provided standard deviations.
Correct: C
Difference = 32.8 g – 30.2 g = 2.6 g. Standard error ≈ √[(1.5²/40)+(1.3²/40)] = 0.30 g. 95% CI = 2.6 ± 1.96×0.30 = (1.8, 3.2). Option C matches.

## q4 Which of the following statements is a potential trap in interpreting the study’s findings?
Correct: D
While the study had 80 mice, the sample may still be underpowered to detect very small effects or to generalize beyond the specific strain used. Option D is a trap because it assumes adequacy without evidence.

## q5 Which mnemonic best helps students remember the key steps of a randomized controlled trial as illustrated by this study?
Correct: A
The mnemonic RANDOM captures the essential steps of a randomized controlled trial: Randomize groups, Assign interventions, Monitor outcomes, Observe results, Number (sample size), and Document findings. Option A aligns with the study’s design.
