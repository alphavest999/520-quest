# BP1431

Researchers investigated the effect of light intensity on the rate of photosynthesis in spinach leaves. Using a controlled growth chamber, they exposed leaf discs to five different light intensities: 100, 200, 400, 600, and 800 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹. After a 30‑minute acclimation period, the rate of CO₂ uptake was measured using a gas exchange system. The experiment was repeated three times for each intensity to account for biological variability. The results indicated a sigmoidal relationship, with photosynthetic rates increasing rapidly at lower intensities, plateauing around 600 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹, and showing a slight decline at 800 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹, likely due to photoinhibition. The researchers concluded that optimal light intensity for maximum photosynthetic efficiency in spinach occurs near 600 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹ under the conditions tested.
