# BP1575

Researchers studied the effect of a novel weight‑loss supplement on adults aged 25–55 years. Participants were randomly assigned to one of five groups: a control group receiving no supplement, a low‑dose group (10 mg/day), a medium‑dose group (20 mg/day), a high‑dose group (40 mg/day), and a placebo group receiving an inert capsule identical in appearance to the supplement. Over 12 weeks, participants recorded their daily caloric intake and engaged in a standardized 30‑minute aerobic exercise routine three times per week. Body weight was measured at baseline and at the end of the study. The study aimed to determine whether the supplement produced a dose‑dependent reduction in body weight while controlling for dietary and exercise variables.

## bp1575_q1 Which group achieved the greatest mean weight loss according to Table 1?
Correct: C

## bp1575_q2 What type of study design was used in this research?
Correct: A
Participants were randomly assigned to treatment groups and followed over time, which is the hallmark of a randomized controlled trial.

Correct: A
Mean difference = 3.4 kg (high dose) – 0.5 kg (control) = 2.9 kg.

## bp1575_q4 Which of the following factors could most likely confound the observed weight‑loss effect?
Correct: D
All listed factors—age, diet, and exercise—could influence weight loss and thus act as confounders if not adequately controlled.

## bp1575_q5 The pattern of increasing weight loss with higher supplement doses best illustrates which pharmacological concept?
Correct: B
A dose‑response relationship occurs when greater doses produce greater physiological effects, as seen with the supplement’s incremental weight‑loss benefits.
