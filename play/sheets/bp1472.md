# BP1472

Researchers studied the effect of a novel antihypertensive drug on systolic blood pressure in 60 adults with stage 1 hypertension. Participants were randomly assigned to either a placebo group (n=30) or the treatment group receiving 10 mg of the drug daily (n=30). Blood pressure was measured at baseline (Day 0) and then every two days for six days. The following table summarizes the mean systolic blood pressure (±SD) for each group.

Table 1: Mean systolic blood pressure (mmHg) for control and treatment groups
Day | Control (±SD) | Treatment (±SD)
0   | 140 ± 5       | 140 ± 5
2   | 138 ± 4       | 133 ± 4
4   | 137 ± 5       | 129 ± 5
6   | 136 ± 4       | 125 ± 4

## Q1 According to the table, which day shows the greatest reduction in mean systolic blood pressure in the treatment group relative to baseline?
Correct: C
The treatment group’s mean systolic BP decreases from 140 mmHg at Day 0 to 125 mmHg at Day 6, a reduction of 15 mmHg, which is larger than the reductions on Days 2 and 4.

## Q2 Which of the following best describes the experimental design used in this study?
Correct: A
Participants were randomly assigned to placebo or drug groups, a hallmark of a randomized controlled trial.

## Q3 Calculate the percent reduction in mean systolic blood pressure in the treatment group from Day 0 to Day 6.
Correct: B
Percent reduction = [(140 – 125) / 140] × 100 ≈ 10.7%, which rounds to about 10%.

## Q4 Which of the following statements is a common trap when interpreting this data?
Correct: A
A 15‑mmHg drop may be clinically meaningful, but without context (e.g., patient risk profile) it can be a trap to assume clinical significance solely from the magnitude.

## Q5 Which concept is most directly illustrated by the observed change in systolic blood pressure in the treatment group?
Correct: D
The drug produced a measurable, time‑dependent reduction in systolic BP, which is the definition of a therapeutic effect.
