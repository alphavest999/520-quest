# BP1395

Researchers studied the effect of a high‑protein diet on the growth of laboratory rats over a 5‑week period. Forty male Sprague‑Dawley rats were randomly assigned to either a control diet (10% protein) or a high‑protein diet (30% protein). Body weights were recorded weekly. The researchers reported mean weights and standard deviations for each group at each time point. The data suggest that the high‑protein group gained weight more rapidly than the control group, with the most pronounced difference observed in the final week of the study. The authors concluded that increased protein intake accelerates growth in adolescent rats and recommended further investigation into the underlying metabolic mechanisms.
