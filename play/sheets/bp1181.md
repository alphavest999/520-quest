# BP1181

Researchers at the Institute for Biomedical Research investigated the effect of a novel compound on cellular proliferation. In a controlled laboratory setting, 120 cultured cell lines were randomly assigned to one of four treatment groups: a control group receiving no compound, and three experimental groups receiving 1, 2, and 3 mg/mL of the compound, respectively. After 48 hours of exposure, the mean proliferation rate was measured using a standardized colorimetric assay. The results, summarized in Table 1, indicate a dose-dependent increase in proliferation. The control group exhibited a mean response of 5.2 units (SD 0.8), while the 1 mg/mL group showed 6.1 units (SD 0.9), the 2 mg/mL group 7.3 units (SD 1.0), and the 3 mg/mL group 8.0 units (SD 1.1). The researchers concluded that the compound enhances cell growth in a concentration-dependent manner, suggesting potential therapeutic applications but also raising concerns about uncontrolled proliferation. Future studies were recommended to explore the mechanism of action and to assess long-term effects in vivo.

The researchers randomly assigned cell lines to treatment groups and measured outcomes, which is characteristic of a randomized controlled trial.

Correct: D
Increase = (8.0 – 5.2) / 5.2 ≈ 0.5385, or about 54%. The closest option is 50%.

## bp1181_q4 Which of the following statements is most likely to be a confounding variable in this study?
Correct: A
If cell lines were derived from donors of varying ages, age could influence baseline proliferation rates, acting as a confounder.

## bp1181_q5 Which of the following future research directions would best build on the findings of this study?
Correct: A
Exploring lower doses could identify a therapeutic window and help delineate safety margins, directly extending the dose–response relationship observed.
