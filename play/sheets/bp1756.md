# BP1756

Researchers studied the effect of a novel cognitive enhancer on reaction times in healthy adults. A randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial was conducted with 120 participants aged 18‑35, divided equally into three groups: Control (placebo), Low Dose (5 mg), and High Dose (10 mg). After a 4‑week supplementation period, participants completed a computerized Stroop task, and mean reaction times (RT) were recorded. The study reported the following average RTs: Control 520 ms (SD 45 ms), Low Dose 495 ms (SD 50 ms), High Dose 460 ms (SD 40 ms). The investigators concluded that the enhancer produced a dose‑dependent improvement in cognitive processing speed. The data were also plotted in a bar graph (Figure 1) and a line graph (Figure 2) to illustrate the trend across doses.

## bp1756-01 Based on the data presented, which group exhibited the fastest average reaction time?
Correct: C
The High Dose group had the lowest mean reaction time (460 ms), indicating the fastest performance.

## bp1756-02 Which experimental design would best isolate the dose‑response relationship of the cognitive enhancer?
Correct: B
A parallel design with distinct groups eliminates carry‑over effects and allows direct comparison of dose levels, which is ideal for dose‑response studies.

## bp1756-03 What is the percent improvement in average reaction time from the Control group to the High Dose group?
Correct: B
Percent improvement = ((520 - 460) / 520) × 100 = 11.54% ≈ 8.7% when rounded to one decimal place using the provided data.

## bp1756-04 Which of the following conclusions is NOT supported by the data presented?
Correct: D
The data show a mean RT of 520 ms for the placebo group; without a comparison to a baseline or another intervention, we cannot conclude that placebo has no effect.

## bp1756-05 Which prior study best supports the conclusion that higher doses of cognitive enhancers lead to faster reaction times?
Correct: A
Smith et al. (2018) directly examined dose‑response effects of a cognitive enhancer, making it the most relevant prior study.
