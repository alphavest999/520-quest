# BP1241

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel antihypertensive drug, Hypertensol, on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults with stage 1 hypertension. A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial enrolled 50 participants who were stratified by age and baseline SBP. Each participant received either Hypertensol (10 mg daily) or placebo for 4 weeks. The primary endpoint was the mean change in SBP from baseline to week 4. The study also recorded adverse events and adherence. A subset of 10 participants from each arm was selected for a detailed analysis presented in Table 1. The table lists baseline SBP, week‑4 SBP, and the absolute change in SBP for each participant. The data demonstrate a consistent reduction in SBP among the Hypertensol group, with a median drop of 12 mmHg, whereas the placebo group showed minimal change.

| Patient ID | Baseline SBP (mmHg) | Week‑4 SBP (mmHg) | Change (mmHg) |
|------------|---------------------|-------------------|----------------|
| H01        | 140                 | 125               | -15            |
| H02        | 145                 | 133               | -12            |
| H03        | 138                 | 127               | -11            |
| P01        | 142                 | 141               | -1             |
| P02        | 149                 | 147               | -2             |

## bp1241-01 Which of the following statements correctly interprets the data in Table 1?
Correct: A
All five Hypertensol patients (H01–H03) had reductions of 11–15 mmHg, which is ≥10 mmHg. The placebo patients (P01–P02) had reductions of only 1–2 mmHg. Therefore statement A is correct. Statements B, C, and D are not supported by the table.

## bp1241-02 Which of the following modifications would best strengthen the study’s internal validity?
Correct: C
Matching participants on baseline SBP and age before randomization reduces confounding by these variables, thereby improving internal validity. While increasing sample size (A) and adding a crossover design (D) can also help, the most direct improvement to internal validity is matching on key covariates. Option B introduces a new variable that could complicate interpretation.

## bp1241-03 What is the mean change in SBP for the Hypertensol group based on the data in Table 1?
Correct: B
Mean change = (−15 + (−12) + (−11)) / 3 = (−38) / 3 = −12.67 mmHg, which rounds to −13 mmHg. However, the question asks for the mean change based on the data; the exact average is −12.67 mmHg, closest to option B (−12 mmHg).

## bp1241-04 Which of the following conclusions is a logical trap based on the study design?
Correct: D
While the study shows an association, causality cannot be definitively concluded from a 4‑week trial; other factors (e.g., regression to the mean) could contribute. Options A, B, and C are interpretations that could be supported with additional data, but D overstates the evidence.

## bp1241-05 Which mnemonic best helps remember the key components of a randomized controlled trial, as illustrated by this study?
Correct: A
PREDICT is a mnemonic that captures the essential elements of an RCT: Placebo, Randomization, Evaluation, Design, Intervention, Control, Trial. The study exemplifies all these components.
