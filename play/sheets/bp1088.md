# BP1088

Researchers studied the effect of a novel cognitive training program on working memory in adults aged 18–35. A randomized controlled trial enrolled 120 participants who were randomly assigned to either the training group (n = 60) or a wait‑list control group (n = 60). Baseline working‑memory scores were measured using the n‑back task. After a 4‑week intervention, all participants were reassessed. Table 1 summarizes the mean (SD) scores for each group before and after the intervention, as well as the mean change. The training group demonstrated a statistically significant improvement in working‑memory performance compared to the control group. The researchers reported a moderate effect size (Cohen’s d = 0.58) and concluded that the training program could be a viable strategy for enhancing cognitive function in healthy adults.

## bp1088-1 Based on Table 1, what is the mean difference in working‑memory improvement between the training and control groups?
Correct: A
The mean change for the training group is 3.2 and for the control group is 0.5. The difference is 3.2 – 0.5 = 2.7 points.

## bp1088-2 Which experimental design best describes the study?
Correct: C
Participants were randomly assigned to a training or control group and followed over time, which is the hallmark of a randomized controlled trial.

## bp1088-3 What is the percent improvement in working‑memory scores for the training group?
Correct: A
Percent improvement = (Change / Baseline) × 100 = (3.2 / 12.4) × 100 ≈ 25.8 %.

## bp1088-4 Which of the following statements is a common trap when interpreting the reported effect size?
Correct: A
A Cohen’s d of 0.58 is considered a moderate effect, not large. The trap is over‑interpreting the magnitude as clinically large.

## bp1088-5 Which concept is most directly linked to the study’s use of Cohen’s d?
Correct: B
Cohen’s d is a standardized measure of effect size, quantifying the magnitude of the difference between two groups.
