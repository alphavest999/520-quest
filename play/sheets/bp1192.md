# BP1192

Researchers conducted a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive agent, Hypertenso, in adults with stage 1 hypertension. Participants (n = 200) were randomized to receive either Hypertenso (n = 100) or placebo (n = 100) for 12 weeks. Blood pressure was measured at baseline and at the end of the study. The investigators reported the mean systolic blood pressure (SBP) reduction and standard deviation (SD) for each group. The Hypertenso group experienced a mean SBP reduction of 12.4 mmHg (SD = 6.8), whereas the placebo group had a mean reduction of 3.1 mmHg (SD = 5.9). The authors concluded that Hypertenso significantly lowered SBP compared with placebo and suggested that the drug could be an effective treatment option for patients with mild hypertension.

## bp1192-001 What does the table most directly indicate about the effect of Hypertenso on systolic blood pressure?
Correct: A
The mean reduction in the Hypertenso group (12.4 mmHg) exceeds that of the placebo group (3.1 mmHg) by 9.3 mmHg, indicating a greater effect of the drug.

## bp1192-002 Which study design feature best supports the conclusion that Hypertenso caused the observed SBP reduction?
Correct: A
Randomization minimizes selection bias and balances known and unknown confounders, strengthening the causal inference that the drug caused the greater SBP reduction.

## bp1192-003 What percent of the Hypertenso group’s mean SBP reduction is represented by the placebo group’s mean reduction?
Correct: B
Percent = (3.1 / 12.4) × 100 % ≈ 24.8 %. This shows that the placebo reduction is roughly a quarter of the drug’s effect.

## bp1192-004 Which statement most likely reflects a misinterpretation of the standard deviation reported for the Hypertenso group?
Correct: B
An SD of 6.8 mmHg is moderate relative to the mean; it does not indicate low variability. Options A, C, and D correctly interpret the SD in terms of distribution ranges.

## bp1192-005 How does the study’s finding support the clinical relevance of Hypertenso for patients with mild hypertension?
Correct: A
A mean SBP reduction of 12.4 mmHg is well above the commonly cited minimal clinically important difference (~5 mmHg), indicating a clinically meaningful benefit.
