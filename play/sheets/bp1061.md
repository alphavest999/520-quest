# BP1061

Researchers studied the effect of caffeine on reaction time in 120 participants. Participants were randomly assigned to one of three groups: control (no caffeine), low‑dose caffeine (50 mg), and high‑dose caffeine (200 mg). Over a 30‑minute period, participants completed a computerized Stroop task. Reaction times were recorded and summarized in Table 1. The data indicate that the high‑dose group had the fastest mean reaction time, while the control group had the slowest. Standard deviations suggest moderate variability within each group. The researchers hypothesize that caffeine improves cognitive performance in a dose‑dependent manner. However, they also note that individual differences in metabolism may affect the outcome. The study design was a double‑blind, randomized controlled trial with a between‑subject factor of caffeine dose. The authors plan to analyze the data using ANOVA and post‑hoc comparisons. The Stroop task required participants to name the color of the ink in which the word was printed, ignoring the word itself. Each participant completed 60 trials, and the average reaction time was calculated across correct responses. Table 1 presents the mean reaction times and standard deviations for each group. The researchers also collected subjective alertness ratings on a 7‑point Likert scale, but those results are not shown in the table. They intend to examine the correlation between caffeine dose, reaction time, and alertness. The study was conducted at a university laboratory between 9:00 a.m. and 12:00 p.m. on a single day to control for circadian effects.
