# BP1337

Researchers investigated the inhibitory effects of two novel kinase inhibitors, KIN-A and KIN-B, on the proliferation of cultured human melanoma cells. The study employed a time‑course design, measuring drug concentrations and cellular response over a 7‑day period. Cells were treated with varying concentrations of each inhibitor, and the percent inhibition of proliferation was assessed using an MTT assay. The data revealed a dose‑dependent decrease in cell viability for both drugs, with KIN-A achieving 80% inhibition at 10 µM after 5 days, whereas KIN-B required 20 µM to reach the same level of inhibition. The researchers noted that the combination of sub‑IC50 concentrations of KIN-A and KIN-B produced a synergistic effect, reducing proliferation by 90% at 5 µM each after 7 days. These findings suggest potential therapeutic benefits of combination therapy in melanoma treatment, warranting further in‑vivo studies.
