# BP1003

Researchers conducted a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive agent, Hypertensol, in adults with stage 1 hypertension. Three hundred and sixty participants were enrolled and randomly assigned to one of three arms: placebo (n = 120), low‑dose Hypertensol (5 mg daily, n = 120), or high‑dose Hypertensol (10 mg daily, n = 120). After 12 weeks of treatment, mean systolic blood pressure (SBP) and diastolic blood pressure (DBP) were measured in a standardized setting. Table 1 summarizes the mean changes from baseline and the associated p‑values for each comparison. The investigators reported that both Hypertensol doses produced statistically significant reductions in SBP compared with placebo (p < 0.001), while the high‑dose group also achieved a greater reduction in DBP (p = 0.02). The study concluded that Hypertensol is a safe and effective option for managing mild hypertension, with dose‑dependent benefits on systolic control.
