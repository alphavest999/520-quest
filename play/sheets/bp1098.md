# BP1098

Researchers studied the effect of a novel cognitive training program on reaction times in adults aged 25-35. The study employed a randomized controlled design with two groups: a training group that received 30 minutes of the program daily for four weeks, and a control group that engaged in non-cognitive leisure activities for the same duration. Reaction times were measured using a computerized simple reaction time task at baseline, immediately after the intervention, and at a 4‑week follow‑up. The training group exhibited a mean reduction of 45 ms (SD = 12 ms) immediately post‑intervention, whereas the control group’s mean change was 5 ms (SD = 10 ms). At follow‑up, the training group’s mean reaction time remained 30 ms faster than baseline (SD = 15 ms), while the control group showed no significant change. The study concluded that the training program produces a short‑term improvement in reaction time that partially sustains over a month. The authors suggest that the program may enhance attentional resource allocation, potentially benefiting occupations requiring rapid decision making.

## 2 Which experimental design best controls for potential confounding variables in this study?
Correct: B
A randomized controlled trial (RCT) randomly assigns participants to intervention and control groups, thereby balancing known and unknown confounders across groups. This design is the gold standard for testing causal effects of interventions.
