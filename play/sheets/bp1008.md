# BP1008

Researchers studied the influence of light intensity on the growth of a common garden plant, *Arabidopsis thaliana*. Four separate growth chambers were set up, each receiving a constant light intensity: 1,000 lux, 2,000 lux, 4,000 lux, and 8,000 lux. Over a six‑week period, the plants were monitored for average stem height, growth rate (percentage increase in height per week), and total seed yield (grams per plant). The data collected are summarized in Table 1. The results indicate a clear trend: as light intensity increases, average height and yield both rise, while the growth rate peaks at 4,000 lux and then slightly declines at 8,000 lux, suggesting a possible saturation effect. The researchers hypothesize that beyond a certain intensity, photoinhibition may limit further growth, but the data also show that seed yield continues to increase, implying that the plants allocate more resources to reproduction under higher light. These findings could inform greenhouse lighting strategies for maximizing crop output.

## bp1008_q1 Which light intensity produced the highest average stem height?
Correct: D
The table shows that the average height increases with light intensity, reaching its maximum of 28.9 cm at 8,000 lux.

## bp1008_q2 Which variable should the researchers keep constant to isolate the effect of light intensity on plant growth?
Correct: D
To attribute observed differences solely to light intensity, all other environmental factors—temperature, watering, and soil nutrients—must remain unchanged across chambers.

## bp1008_q3 Calculate the percent increase in seed yield when light intensity increases from 1,000 lux to 8,000 lux.
Correct: B
Yield at 1,000 lux = 3.1 g; at 8,000 lux = 10.2 g. Percent increase = ((10.2‑3.1)/3.1)×100 ≈ 236%.

## bp1008_q4 Which of the following statements is NOT supported by the data?
Correct: D
The data show seed yield increases from 8.4 g at 4,000 lux to 10.2 g at 8,000 lux, so statement D is false.

## bp1008_q5 Which biological concept best explains the observed increase in seed yield with higher light intensity?
Correct: B
Higher light intensity increases photosynthetic photon flux, boosting carbohydrate production and thus seed yield. Photoinhibition only affects growth rate at the highest intensity.
