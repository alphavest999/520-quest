# BP1295

Researchers conducted a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive agent, Hypotensol, in adults with stage 1 hypertension. Eighty participants were enrolled and randomly assigned to either the Hypotensol group (n=40) or a placebo group (n=40). Baseline systolic and diastolic blood pressures (BP) were measured after a 5‑minute seated rest. Participants received daily oral Hypotensol (20 mg) or identical placebo tablets for 4 weeks. BP readings were taken at the end of the study period. Table 1 summarizes the mean BP values and changes for both groups. The Hypotensol group exhibited a mean systolic BP reduction of 20 mmHg and a diastolic reduction of 10 mmHg, whereas the placebo group showed modest reductions of 3 mmHg systolic and 2 mmHg diastolic. The data suggest that Hypotensol effectively lowers BP compared to placebo, supporting its potential use as an adjunct therapy in hypertension management.

## bp1295-01 Which group experienced the greater absolute reduction in systolic blood pressure after 4 weeks of treatment?
Correct: A
The treatment group’s systolic BP decreased by 20 mmHg (from 150 to 130), whereas the placebo group’s systolic BP decreased by only 3 mmHg (from 148 to 145).

## bp1295-02 Which statement best describes the study design used in this research?
Correct: A
The investigators randomly assigned participants to treatment or placebo, blinded both participants and investigators, and used a placebo control, which defines a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial.

## bp1295-05 Which clinical implication is most directly supported by the study findings?
Correct: B
The trial demonstrates a significant BP reduction with Hypotensol, suggesting it could be added to existing regimens for patients who remain hypertensive. It does not support replacing all first‑line agents or claiming ineffectiveness.
