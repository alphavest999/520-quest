# BP1435

Researchers studied the effect of light intensity on the rate of photosynthesis in spinach leaves. In a controlled laboratory setting, fresh leaves were exposed to four different light intensities: 200 lux, 400 lux, 600 lux, and 800 lux. The oxygen production rate was measured in micromoles per minute (µmol/min) over a 10‑minute interval. The experiment was repeated three times for each intensity to ensure reproducibility. Results indicated a positive correlation between light intensity and oxygen production, with the highest rate observed at 800 lux. The study aimed to quantify this relationship and assess whether the increase in light intensity led to a linear or plateaued response in photosynthetic output. The data were recorded in a table for subsequent analysis and graphing.
