# BP1573

Researchers investigated the impact of sleep deprivation on short‑term memory performance in healthy adults. A total of 120 participants (aged 18–35) were randomly assigned to either a control group (normal sleep) or a sleep‑deprived group (restricted to 4 hours of sleep for 48 hours). After the intervention, all subjects completed a standardized word‑list recall test. The data are summarized in Table 1. The study aimed to determine whether reduced sleep leads to measurable deficits in memory retrieval and to assess the magnitude of this effect. The researchers also collected demographic variables (age, sex, baseline cognitive score) to control for potential confounders. The results were intended to inform workplace policies and public health recommendations regarding sleep hygiene.

Table 1. Memory Test Scores by Group

Group | N | Mean Score (SD)
Control | 60 | 85.4 (7.2)
Sleep‑Deprived | 60 | 72.1 (9.5)

## bp1573-q1 Which of the following best describes the main finding of the study?
Correct: C
The mean memory score for the sleep‑deprived group (72.1) is markedly lower than that of the control group (85.4), indicating a significant reduction in performance. The standard deviations overlap only partially, supporting a real effect.

## bp1573-q2 Which experimental design best matches the study’s methodology?
Correct: C
Participants were randomly assigned to two conditions (control vs. sleep‑deprived) and then measured on the same outcome, which is the hallmark of a randomized controlled trial.

## bp1573-q3 Calculate the absolute difference in mean memory scores between the two groups.
Correct: C
Absolute difference = 85.4 – 72.1 = 13.3 points.

## bp1573-q4 Which of the following statements is a common trap when interpreting the study’s results?
Correct: C
Although the study is a randomized controlled trial, the reported difference is a statistical association; causation is inferred but not proven without ruling out all confounders and ensuring the study is adequately powered.

## bp1573-q5 Which concept is most directly linked to the study’s findings?
Correct: D
Sleep deprivation directly disrupts circadian rhythm regulation, which is closely tied to memory consolidation processes observed in the study.
