# BP1170

Researchers conducted a randomized controlled trial to investigate the impact of evening light exposure on sleep quality among rotating shift workers. One hundred twenty‑eight participants (age 24–48, 60% male) were assigned to either a control group (standard indoor lighting, 5–7 lux) or an experimental group (intense amber LED lighting, 200 lux, for 2 hours before bedtime) for 6 weeks. Sleep quality was assessed weekly using the Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index (PSQI), with lower scores indicating better sleep. Baseline PSQI scores were comparable between groups (control: 7.2 ± 1.8; experimental: 7.4 ± 1.9). After 6 weeks, the experimental group’s mean PSQI decreased to 4.3 ± 1.5, a statistically significant improvement (p < 0.001) relative to the control group, which changed to 6.9 ± 1.6. Secondary outcomes included total sleep time and sleep latency, measured via actigraphy. The study concluded that moderate evening light exposure can enhance sleep quality in shift workers, potentially reducing occupational fatigue.
