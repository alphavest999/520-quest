# BP1236

Researchers investigated the impact of caffeine on reaction time in a randomized controlled trial involving 120 healthy adults. Participants were randomly assigned to one of four groups: Placebo, 50 mg caffeine, 100 mg caffeine, and 200 mg caffeine. After ingestion, each subject completed a computerized reaction‑time task, and the mean reaction time (RT) and standard deviation (SD) were recorded. The study aimed to determine whether increasing caffeine dosage would systematically improve reaction speed and reduce variability. The findings suggested a dose‑dependent decrease in mean RT, with the 200 mg group achieving the fastest average response. Additionally, the SD of RT decreased across higher doses, indicating more consistent performance. These results provide preliminary evidence that caffeine can enhance cognitive processing speed, but further research is needed to confirm the optimal dosage and to assess long‑term effects.

## bp1236_q1 Which group had the fastest average reaction time?
Correct: D
The mean reaction time for the 200 mg caffeine group was 300 ms, the lowest (fastest) among all groups.

## bp1236_q2 Which of the following best describes the study’s design?
Correct: B
Participants were randomly assigned to dosage groups and the effect of caffeine on reaction time was measured, which is the hallmark of a randomized controlled trial.

## bp1236_q3 What is the overall mean reaction time across all groups?
Correct: A
With equal sample sizes (30 per group), the overall mean is the simple average of the four means: (350 + 330 + 310 + 300)/4 = 330 ms.

## bp1236_q4 Which of the following statements is NOT supported by the data?
Correct: C
The standard deviation actually decreases with higher caffeine doses, indicating reduced variability, not increased.

## bp1236_q5 Which future study would best build on these findings?
Correct: D
A crossover design allows each participant to serve as their own control, reducing inter‑individual variability and providing a stronger test of dose effects.
