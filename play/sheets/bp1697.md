Researchers studied the effect of a novel antihypertensive agent, Hypotensol, on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults with stage 1 hypertension. A double‑blind, randomized, placebo‑controlled trial enrolled 200 participants (100 per arm). Baseline SBP was measured after a 5‑minute seated rest. Participants received either 10 mg Hypotensol daily or placebo for 4 weeks. SBP was reassessed at week 4 using the same protocol. The study aimed to determine whether Hypotensol could produce a clinically meaningful reduction in SBP compared to placebo. Secondary outcomes included changes in diastolic blood pressure (DBP) and incidence of adverse events. The investigators hypothesized a mean SBP reduction of at least 10 mmHg in the treatment group, with a standard deviation of 12 mmHg. Data were analyzed using an intention‑to‑treat approach, and significance was set at p < .05. The trial was conducted across three academic centers, and all participants provided written informed consent.
