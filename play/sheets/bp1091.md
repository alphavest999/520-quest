# BP1091

Researchers conducted a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of three novel antihypertensive agents (Drug A, Drug B, and Drug C) in adults with stage 1 hypertension. A total of 400 participants were enrolled and randomly assigned to one of four groups: placebo (n=100), Drug A (n=100), Drug B (n=100), or Drug C (n=100). Baseline systolic blood pressure (SBP) was measured after a 24‑hour ambulatory monitoring period. After 12 weeks of treatment, mean SBP and standard deviation were recorded for each group. The study aimed to determine which drug produced the greatest reduction in SBP while maintaining safety and tolerability. In addition, secondary outcomes included diastolic BP, pulse pressure, and incidence of adverse events. The trial was registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT01234567) and approved by the institutional review board. Results were analyzed using intention‑to‑treat principles, and a mixed‑model repeated measures approach was applied to account for missing data. The investigators reported significant differences among groups, with Drug A showing the largest mean reduction. These findings suggest that Drug A may be a promising option for patients who cannot tolerate first‑line agents.

Correct: B
Randomization, blinding, and placebo control minimize selection bias, observer bias, and placebo effects, providing the strongest control for confounding.
