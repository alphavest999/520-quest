# BP1676

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel dietary supplement on the recovery of skeletal muscle after resistance training. Twenty healthy adults (10 males, 10 females) were randomly assigned to either the supplement group (n = 10) or a placebo group (n = 10). Participants performed a standardized 8‑week strength‑endurance program (3 sessions per week). Muscle soreness was recorded on a 0–10 visual analog scale (VAS) immediately after each session, and creatine kinase (CK) levels were measured in venous blood samples taken at baseline, week 4, and week 8. Table 1 summarizes the mean VAS scores and CK concentrations for both groups at each time point. The researchers hypothesized that the supplement would reduce subjective soreness and lower CK elevations, indicating less muscle damage. Statistical analysis involved repeated‑measures ANOVA with group and time as factors. The results showed a significant group × time interaction for VAS scores (p < 0.01) but not for CK levels (p = 0.12). The authors concluded that the supplement may attenuate perceived soreness during training but did not provide evidence of reduced biochemical muscle injury.

## bp1676-01 Which of the following best describes the statistical evidence supporting the supplement’s effect on muscle soreness?
Correct: B
The study reports a significant group × time interaction for VAS scores (p < 0.01), indicating that the change in soreness over time differed between the supplement and placebo groups. This is the correct statistical evidence for the supplement’s effect on soreness. The other options either refer to non‑significant findings or incorrect interpretations.

## bp1676-02 Which of the following experimental designs would best allow the researchers to determine whether the supplement directly influences muscle recovery mechanisms?
Correct: A
A crossover design controls for inter‑subject variability by having each participant serve as his/her own control, thereby increasing the power to detect the supplement’s effect on recovery. The parallel design (B) would not address individual differences; the factorial design (C) adds complexity unrelated to the primary question; and a case‑control study (D) cannot establish causality in an intervention context.

## bp1676-03 Assuming the standard deviation of VAS scores for the supplement group at week 8 is 0.5 and for the placebo group is 0.6, calculate the pooled standard deviation.
Correct: A
The pooled SD for two independent samples of equal size (n = 10 each) is sqrt[(SD₁² + SD₂²)/2] = sqrt[(0.5² + 0.6²)/2] = sqrt[(0.25 + 0.36)/2] = sqrt[0.305] ≈ 0.552, which rounds to 0.55. The other options are larger than the correct value.

## bp1676-04 Which of the following statements is a potential trap when interpreting the study’s findings?
Correct: A
The study reports no significant difference in CK levels (p = 0.12), so claiming a reduction in CK is incorrect and constitutes a trap. The other statements accurately reflect the data or acknowledge limitations.

## bp1676-05 Which of the following resources would best help a clinician apply the findings of this study to practice?
Correct: A
A systematic review aggregates evidence from multiple studies, providing a broader context for clinical decision‑making. The textbook (B) offers basic science but not specific evidence; the news article (C) may lack depth; and the forum (D) is anecdotal.
