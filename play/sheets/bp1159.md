# BP1159

Researchers studied the effect of caffeine on short-term memory recall in a sample of 150 healthy adults aged 18-35. Participants were randomly assigned to one of three groups: a control group receiving a placebo, a low‑dose caffeine group receiving 100 mg, and a high‑dose caffeine group receiving 200 mg. After a 30‑minute absorption period, all participants completed a standardized word‑list recall test. The study aimed to determine whether caffeine dosage influences recall accuracy and to assess the dose‑response relationship. Results indicated that the high‑dose group achieved a mean recall accuracy of 85% (SD 5%), the low‑dose group achieved 78% (SD 7%), and the control group achieved 70% (SD 8%). The researchers concluded that caffeine has a dose‑dependent positive effect on short‑term memory recall, with higher doses producing greater improvements. They noted that while the effect size was statistically significant, the practical significance for everyday memory tasks remains modest.
