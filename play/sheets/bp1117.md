# BP1117

Researchers at the University of Midvale investigated the effects of a new dietary supplement on the gut microbiome of adult mice. Over a 12‑week period, 48 mice were randomly assigned to one of four groups: a control diet, a diet supplemented with 0.5% of the compound, 1.0%, or 2.0%. Fecal samples were collected at baseline, week 6, and week 12. Using 16S rRNA sequencing, the relative abundance of major bacterial phyla (Firmicutes, Bacteroidetes, Proteobacteria, Actinobacteria) was quantified. Table 1 summarizes the average relative abundances (in %) for each group at week 12. The researchers hypothesized that higher supplement concentrations would shift the Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio toward a healthier profile. They also monitored body weight and glucose tolerance as secondary outcomes. The study aimed to determine whether the supplement could modulate gut microbiota composition and improve metabolic markers without adverse effects.

## bp1117_q1 According to Table 1, which of the following statements best describes the trend in the Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio across the supplement groups?
Correct: B
The Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio is calculated by dividing the Firmicutes percentage by the Bacteroidetes percentage. For Control: 55.2/35.1 ≈ 1.57. For 0.5%: 52.8/37.6 ≈ 1.41. For 1.0%: 49.5/40.2 ≈ 1.23. For 2.0%: 46.0/42.5 ≈ 1.08. The ratio steadily decreases as supplement concentration increases.

## bp1117_q2 Which experimental design element would best control for potential confounding by baseline microbiome composition?
Correct: B
Stratification by baseline ratio ensures that each group has a comparable distribution of initial microbiome profiles, reducing confounding. A crossover design (A) would introduce carry‑over effects, while simply increasing sample size (C) does not address baseline imbalance. Selecting only high‑ratio mice (D) introduces selection bias.

## bp1117_q3 If the researchers report that the 2.0% supplement group had a 12% reduction in fasting glucose compared to the control, what is the absolute change in glucose (mg/dL) if the control group's fasting glucose was 140 mg/dL?
Correct: B
12% of 140 mg/dL = 0.12 × 140 = 16.8 mg/dL. A 12% reduction means the glucose level is 140 – 16.8 = 123.2 mg/dL, so the absolute change is 16.8 mg/dL.

## bp1117_q4 Which of the following statements is a common trap when interpreting the Firmicutes/Bacteroidetes ratio?
