# BP1055

Researchers studied the effect of varying doses of a new cognitive enhancer on reaction time in healthy adults. Sixty participants were randomly assigned to one of four groups: Control (no drug), Low Dose (10 mg), Medium Dose (20 mg), and High Dose (30 mg). Each participant performed a computerized reaction time task, and the mean response time (in seconds) along with the standard deviation and sample size (N) were recorded. The data suggested a dose‑dependent improvement, with the High Dose group showing the fastest responses. The study aimed to identify the optimal dose that maximizes benefit while minimizing side effects. The researchers also collected safety data, but the primary outcome was reaction time. The findings could inform dosage recommendations for clinical use and guide future trials on cognitive enhancement.

## bp1055-2 Which experimental design would best determine the dose‑response relationship?
Correct: A
A randomized controlled trial (RCT) is the gold standard for establishing causal dose‑response effects because it controls for confounding variables and assigns participants to dose groups randomly.

## bp1055-4 Which of the following is NOT a valid reason to use the mean as a measure of central tendency?
Correct: B
The mean is sensitive to skewness and outliers; it is appropriate for symmetric, normally distributed data (option A) and for interval/ratio scales (option D). Option B is incorrect because the mean is not robust to heavy skew.

## bp1055-5 How can the findings from this study be applied to improve workplace ergonomics?
Correct: D
The study demonstrates that cognitive performance can be enhanced by pharmacologic means; similarly, ergonomic interventions such as proper desk height, monitor angle, and frequent breaks can improve reaction time and overall productivity.
