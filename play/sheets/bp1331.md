# BP1331

Researchers investigated how varying light intensity influences the rate of photosynthesis in the model plant *Arabidopsis thaliana*. Leaf discs were exposed to five different light intensities (200, 400, 600, 800, and 1000 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹) for 30 minutes while maintaining constant temperature (25 °C) and CO₂ concentration (400 ppm). Oxygen evolution was measured using a Clark-type electrode and expressed as µmol O₂ g⁻¹ FW h⁻¹. The results, summarized in Table 1, indicate a nonlinear response: photosynthetic rate increases sharply up to 600 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ and then plateaus, suggesting a saturation point beyond which additional light does not enhance O₂ production. These findings align with the light‑dependent phase of the photosynthetic light reactions, where electron transport capacity limits further increases in rate.
