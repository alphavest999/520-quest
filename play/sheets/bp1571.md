# BP1571

Researchers at the University of Health Sciences conducted a pilot study to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive agent, Hypertensol, in reducing systolic blood pressure (SBP). Twenty adult volunteers with mild to moderate hypertension were enrolled and randomly assigned to receive either Hypertensol (n=10) or a placebo (n=10) for four weeks. Baseline SBP was measured after a 5‑minute seated rest using an automated cuff. After the treatment period, SBP was reassessed under identical conditions. The investigators recorded the change in SBP for each participant and performed a paired t‑test to determine statistical significance. The following table summarizes the SBP values for the Hypertensol group. The data suggest a mean reduction of 11 mmHg, with the largest individual drop observed in patient P5. While the study demonstrates potential benefit, limitations such as the small sample size and short duration warrant cautious interpretation.

Patient ID | Baseline SBP (mmHg) | Post-treatment SBP (mmHg) | Change (mmHg)
P1 | 120 | 110 | -10
P2 | 140 | 130 | -10
P3 | 130 | 125 | -5
P4 | 150 | 140 | -10
P5 | 160 | 140 | -20
