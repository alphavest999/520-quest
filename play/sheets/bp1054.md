# BP1054

Researchers at Midvale University studied how chronic sleep deprivation affects spatial memory in adult male mice. Twenty-eight mice were randomly assigned to four groups (n=7 each). Group A had unrestricted sleep (12 h/day), Group B moderate deprivation (8 h), Group C severe deprivation (4 h), and Group D total deprivation (0 h) for 14 consecutive days. After the deprivation period, all mice performed a Barnes maze test to locate an escape box using spatial cues. The average escape latency (seconds) and number of errors were recorded. Table 1 summarizes the key findings. Results show a dose‑response relationship: mice with less sleep performed progressively worse, with Group D exhibiting the longest latencies and most errors. Researchers controlled for confounders by keeping identical housing, diet, and handling across groups. These findings underscore the importance of adequate sleep for cognitive function and lay groundwork for further studies on sleep‑memory mechanisms.
