# BP1125

Researchers at the University of Example investigated the impact of sleep deprivation on cognitive performance among college students. The study recruited 90 participants, randomly assigned to three groups: a control group that received a full 8 hours of sleep, a sleep‑deprivation group that slept only 4 hours, and a partial‑sleep group that slept 6 hours. Over a one‑week period, each participant completed a battery of reaction‑time tasks and accuracy tests. The results, summarized in Table 1, reveal that the control group exhibited the fastest average reaction time (420 ms) and highest accuracy (95 %). The sleep‑deprivation group had the slowest reaction time (480 ms) and lowest accuracy (85 %). The partial‑sleep group fell in between with a reaction time of 450 ms and accuracy of 90 %. These findings suggest that reduced sleep duration impairs both speed and precision of cognitive processing, supporting existing literature on the neurocognitive costs of sleep loss.

## bp1125_q1 Based on Table 1, which group exhibited the fastest average reaction time?
Correct: A
The control group had the lowest reaction time (420 ms) compared to the sleep‑deprivation (480 ms) and partial‑sleep (450 ms) groups.

## bp1125_q2 Which experimental design best controls for confounding variables in this study?
Correct: A
Random assignment to groups in a randomized controlled trial minimizes confounding variables, ensuring that differences in outcomes are attributable to the intervention.

## bp1125_q3 What is the percent difference in reaction time between the sleep‑deprivation group and the control group?
Correct: A
Percent difference = (480 ms – 420 ms) / 480 ms = 60/480 = 0.125 = 12.5 %. The sleep‑deprivation group’s reaction time is 12.5 % slower than the control.

## bp1125_q4 Which of the following statements is a common misconception about sleep deprivation?
Correct: A
Sleep deprivation impairs cognitive performance, leading to slower reaction times and lower accuracy, not improvement.

## bp1125_q5 Which physiological concept is most closely related to the study’s findings?
Correct: B
Sleep deprivation disrupts circadian rhythms, which regulate alertness and cognitive performance.
