# BP1136

Researchers investigated the effect of varying concentrations of a novel antioxidant compound on oxidative stress markers in cultured human fibroblasts. The study employed a randomized, double‑blind design with four experimental groups: a vehicle control and three treatment groups receiving 10, 50, and 100 µM of the compound. After 48 hours of exposure, intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) were quantified using a fluorescent probe, and the expression of the antioxidant enzyme superoxide dismutase (SOD) was measured by qPCR. The results, summarized in Table 1, show a dose‑dependent decrease in ROS levels and a corresponding increase in SOD mRNA. The authors concluded that the antioxidant compound effectively mitigates oxidative stress in a concentration‑dependent manner, suggesting potential therapeutic benefits for age‑related cellular damage.

Correct: C
Randomization minimizes confounding by evenly distributing unknown factors across groups, strengthening the causal link between the compound and ROS reduction.
