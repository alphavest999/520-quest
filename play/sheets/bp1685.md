# BP1685

Researchers investigated how varying light intensity affects the rate of photosynthesis in the model plant Arabidopsis thaliana. Using a controlled growth chamber, seedlings were exposed to four light intensities (200, 400, 600, and 800 µmol photons m‑2 s‑1) for 30 minutes. Oxygen evolution was measured as a proxy for photosynthetic activity, expressed in µmol O₂ g⁻¹ fresh weight h⁻¹. Two genotypes were compared: the wild‑type (WT) and a mutant line (MT) with a known deficiency in light‑harvesting complex proteins. The data showed a clear increase in photosynthetic rate with higher light intensity for both genotypes, but the WT consistently outperformed the MT across all intensities. At the lowest intensity (200 µmol photons m‑2 s‑1), the WT produced 12.5 µmol O₂ g⁻¹ h⁻¹, while the MT produced 8.3 µmol O₂ g⁻¹ h⁻¹. At the highest intensity (800 µmol photons m‑2 s‑1), the WT reached 45.6 µmol O₂ g⁻¹ h⁻¹, whereas the MT plateaued at 28.4 µmol O₂ g⁻¹ h⁻¹. These results suggest that the MT’s impaired light‑harvesting machinery limits its capacity to increase photosynthetic output under high light conditions.

## bp1685-01 Which genotype shows the higher photosynthetic rate at the highest light intensity (800 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹)?
Correct: A
At 800 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹, the WT rate is 45.6 µmol O₂ g⁻¹ h⁻¹, whereas the MT rate is 28.4 µmol O₂ g⁻¹ h⁻¹. Thus, the WT shows the higher rate.

## bp1685-02 Which experimental design best isolates the effect of light intensity on photosynthetic rate while controlling for genotype?
Correct: B
A split‑plot design allows light intensity (main plot) to be varied across blocks while genotype (subplot) is randomized within each light level, controlling for genotype effects while testing light intensity.

Correct: B
