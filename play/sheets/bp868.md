# BP868

Researchers investigated the acid–base properties of three common amino acids—glycine, alanine, and lysine—by performing standard titrations with 0.1 M NaOH. Each amino acid was dissolved to a final concentration of 0.1 M in deionized water, and the pH was recorded continuously with a calibrated glass electrode as NaOH was added in 0.5 mL increments. The resulting titration curves exhibited two distinct buffering regions corresponding to the carboxyl and amino functional groups. From the curves the researchers calculated the first and second pKa values (pKa1 and pKa2) and the isoelectric point (pI) for each amino acid. The data are summarized in Table 1. The experiment demonstrates how the relative strengths of the acidic and basic groups determine the pI, and it provides a practical example of the Henderson–Hasselbalch equation in action.

| Amino Acid | pKa1 | pKa2 | pI |
|------------|------|------|----|
| Glycine | 2.34 | 9.60 | 6.97 |
| Alanine | 2.35 | 9.69 | 7.00 |
| Lysine | 2.18 | 9.06 | 10.79 |
