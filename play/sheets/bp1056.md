# BP1056

Researchers investigated the effect of a new antihypertensive drug on systolic blood pressure in a randomized controlled trial. Eighty adults aged 45–65 with mild hypertension were enrolled and randomly assigned to either the drug (n=40) or placebo (n=40). Baseline systolic blood pressure (SBP) was recorded for all participants. After 12 weeks of daily dosing, SBP was measured again. Table 1 presents the mean SBP change (in mmHg) and standard deviation for each group. The drug group exhibited a mean reduction of 12.5 mmHg (SD 4.3), whereas the placebo group showed a mean reduction of 3.2 mmHg (SD 5.1). The researchers concluded that the drug significantly lowered SBP compared to placebo. The study also reported that 25% of drug recipients experienced mild dizziness, while 10% of placebo recipients reported the same symptom. No serious adverse events were noted. The trial was double-blind, with neither participants nor investigators aware of group assignments. Adherence was monitored via pill counts, achieving an average compliance rate of 92% in the drug arm and 94% in placebo. Secondary outcomes included diastolic blood pressure and heart rate, which showed parallel trends to systolic changes.

## bp1056-002 Which design feature best supports the validity of the study's conclusion?
Correct: A
Random assignment reduces selection bias and balances confounders between groups.

## bp1056-003 If a patient in the drug group had a baseline SBP of 150 mmHg, what is their expected SBP after 12 weeks?
Correct: A
150 mmHg – 12.5 mmHg = 137.5 mmHg.

## bp1056-004 Which of the following statements is a common misinterpretation of the study results?

The mean reduction reflects an average; individual responses vary.
