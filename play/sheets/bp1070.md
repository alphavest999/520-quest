# BP1070

Researchers investigated how varying light intensity influences photosynthetic oxygen production in spinach leaves. In a controlled greenhouse experiment, five light intensity levels (200, 400, 600, 800, 1000 lux) were applied to potted spinach plants for 30 minutes each. Oxygen production was measured using a dissolved oxygen probe, stomatal conductance was recorded with a porometer, and leaf temperature was monitored with a thermocouple. The results indicated a nonlinear increase in oxygen output, peaking at 600 lux, while stomatal conductance rose steadily with light intensity. Leaf temperature remained relatively constant across treatments, suggesting that temperature did not confound the photosynthetic response. These findings imply that spinach leaves achieve optimal photosynthetic efficiency at moderate light levels, beyond which additional light does not further enhance oxygen production and may induce photoinhibition.
