# BP1085

Researchers studied the effect of a novel antihypertensive drug on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults with mild to moderate hypertension. A randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial enrolled 200 participants, 100 assigned to the drug and 100 to placebo. Baseline SBP was measured after a 5‑minute seated rest. After 4 weeks of daily dosing, SBP was reassessed using the same protocol. The study reported the following summary statistics: the placebo group’s mean SBP decreased from 150 mmHg to 148 mmHg (a mean change of –2 mmHg), whereas the drug group’s mean SBP decreased from 150 mmHg to 130 mmHg (a mean change of –20 mmHg). The investigators concluded that the drug produced a clinically meaningful reduction in SBP compared with placebo. The study also collected data on adverse events and adherence but did not report these in the primary analysis.

## bp1085-01 Which group experienced the greatest mean reduction in systolic blood pressure?
Correct: B
The mean change in SBP for the drug group was –20 mmHg, whereas the placebo group’s mean change was only –2 mmHg. Therefore, the drug group had the greatest mean reduction.

## bp1085-02 Which methodological flaw could most threaten the internal validity of this study?
Correct: B
Although the study was randomized, the lack of blinding can introduce expectation bias, potentially influencing how participants report symptoms or how investigators record outcomes. This is a more serious threat to internal validity than a modest sample size or short duration.

## bp1085-03 What is the percent reduction in SBP for the drug group over 4 weeks?
Correct: A
Percent reduction = (baseline – 4‑week) / baseline × 100 = (150 – 130) / 150 × 100 = 20 / 150 × 100 = 13.3 %. Option A is correct.

## bp1085-04 Which of the following conclusions is NOT supported by the data presented?
Correct: C
The placebo group’s mean SBP decreased only from 150 mmHg to 148 mmHg, a change of –2 mmHg, not >10 mmHg. Therefore, conclusion C is not supported.

## bp1085-05 Which concept is most directly related to the study’s primary outcome measure?
Correct: C
The primary outcome is the mean change in SBP, and the investigators discuss whether this change is clinically meaningful. Thus, the concept of clinical significance is directly linked to the outcome measure.
