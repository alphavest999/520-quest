# BP1414

Researchers at the University of Health Sciences conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive agent, Hypertensol, in adults with stage 1 hypertension. One hundred and fifty patients were enrolled and randomly assigned to either a placebo group (n = 75) or the Hypertensol group (n = 75). Blood pressure was measured at baseline and after 12 weeks of therapy using a standardized automated cuff. The mean systolic blood pressure (SBP) at 12 weeks was 148 mmHg (SD 12) in the placebo group and 134 mmHg (SD 10) in the Hypertensol group. The investigators reported a statistically significant reduction in SBP in the drug group (p < 0.001). No serious adverse events were observed. The study suggests that Hypertensol may be a promising addition to the antihypertensive armamentarium, but larger trials are warranted to confirm its safety and efficacy across diverse populations.
