# BP1274

Researchers studied the effects of three different dietary supplements on the growth rate of laboratory mice over a 12-week period. The study involved 90 mice, divided equally into three groups: Group A received supplement X, Group B received supplement Y, and Group C received a placebo. All mice were housed under identical conditions with a 12-hour light/dark cycle, and their body weights were recorded weekly. The primary outcome measure was the average weekly weight gain, expressed in grams. At the end of the study, the researchers also measured serum levels of protein Z, a biomarker associated with metabolic efficiency. The data collected suggested that supplement X significantly increased weight gain compared to the placebo, while supplement Y showed a modest but statistically non-significant improvement. Additionally, serum protein Z levels were elevated in both supplement groups relative to the placebo, indicating a potential mechanism of action. The researchers concluded that supplement X may be more effective in promoting healthy weight gain in mice, but further studies are needed to confirm these findings in humans.

Variable | Group A | Group B | Group C
Average weekly weight gain (g) | 2.5 | 1.8 | 1.2
Serum protein Z (mg/dL) | 120 | 110 | 90

## 1 Which group had the highest average weekly weight gain?
Correct: A
The table shows Group A’s average weekly weight gain as 2.5 g, which is higher than Group B’s 1.8 g and Group C’s 1.2 g.

## 2 Which experimental design best controls for individual variation in this study?
Correct: A
Randomizing mice to supplement or placebo groups distributes individual differences evenly, providing the strongest control for variation.

## 3 What is the percent increase in average weekly weight gain of Group A compared to Group C?
Correct: A
Percent increase = ((2.5 g – 1.2 g) / 1.2 g) × 100 ≈ 108.3%.

## 4 Which statement is NOT supported by the data presented?
Correct: B
The passage states that supplement Y’s improvement was modest and statistically non-significant, so it did not increase weight gain significantly.

## 5 Which broader concept does this study illustrate?
Correct: B
The study compares different supplements and shows varying effects on weight gain, exemplifying a dose-response relationship between supplement type and physiological outcome.
