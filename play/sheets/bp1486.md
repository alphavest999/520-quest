# BP1486

Researchers studied the impact of sleep deprivation on cognitive performance among shift workers over a five-day period. Participants were assigned to different sleep conditions ranging from 8 hours to 0 hours of sleep per night. Cognitive performance was assessed using a standardized reaction time task and an error rate measurement. The study found a clear trend: as sleep hours decreased, both reaction time and error rate increased. The data suggest that sleep deprivation significantly impairs quick decision-making and accuracy in tasks requiring sustained attention. These findings have implications for occupations that involve irregular schedules, such as healthcare, transportation, and emergency services, where maintaining optimal cognitive function is critical for safety and efficiency.

Sleep Hours | Avg Reaction Time (ms) | Error Rate (%)
8 | 320 | 2.1
6 | 350 | 3.5
4 | 410 | 5.8
2 | 480 | 9.3
0 | 560 | 14.7

## bp1486-01 Which sleep condition had the lowest error rate?
Correct: A
The table shows that 8 hours of sleep correspond to an error rate of 2.1%, the lowest among all conditions.

## bp1486-02 Which of the following best describes the study design?
Correct: C
Participants were followed over five consecutive days, measuring performance at each sleep condition, which characterizes a longitudinal cohort design.

## bp1486-03 What is the percent increase in reaction time from 8 to 0 sleep hours?
Correct: A
Increase = (560‑320)/320 = 240/320 = 0.75 → 75%.

## bp1486-04 Which of the following statements is NOT supported by the data?
Correct: C
The 0‑hour condition actually had the highest error rate (14.7%); 4‑hour had 5.8%.

## bp1486-05 Which real‑world scenario best illustrates the study’s findings?
Correct: A
Pilots often operate on irregular schedules and face sleep deprivation, mirroring the study’s shift‑worker context.
