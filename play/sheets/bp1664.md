# BP1664

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel antibiotic, X‑123, on the growth of Staphylococcus aureus in vitro. The study employed a time‑kill assay over a 48‑hour period. Cultures were inoculated at 1×10^6 CFU/mL and exposed to varying concentrations of X‑123 (0, 2, 4, 8, and 16 mg/L). Samples were taken at 0, 6, 12, 24, 36, and 48 hours, and bacterial counts were determined by serial dilution and plating. The results, summarized in Table 1, indicate a concentration‑dependent bactericidal effect: at 8 mg/L, bacterial counts fell by >3 log10 CFU/mL within 24 hours, whereas 2 mg/L produced only a modest 1 log10 reduction after 48 hours. The authors concluded that X‑123 achieves rapid bacterial killing at concentrations above the minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) of 4 mg/L. However, the study did not assess the potential for resistance development or the pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic (PK/PD) profile of the drug in vivo. The data suggest that dosing strategies aiming for peak concentrations of ≥8 mg/L may be most effective for treating severe S. aureus infections.
