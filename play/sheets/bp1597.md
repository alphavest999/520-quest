# BP1597

Researchers studied the effect of a novel dietary supplement on glucose tolerance in a cohort of 120 adults aged 30–60 years. Participants were randomly assigned to either the supplement group (n = 60) or a placebo group (n = 60). Baseline fasting glucose levels were measured, followed by an oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT) at 30, 60, and 120 minutes. The supplement group received 500 mg of the compound daily for 12 weeks, while the placebo group received an identical inert capsule. After the intervention period, fasting glucose and OGTT results were compared between groups. The researchers reported a mean reduction of 0.8 mmol/L in fasting glucose for the supplement group versus a 0.1 mmol/L reduction in the placebo group, with a p-value of 0.02. The OGTT curves showed a steeper decline in plasma glucose levels in the supplement group at 60 and 120 minutes. The study concluded that the supplement improves insulin sensitivity and glucose handling in adults with impaired glucose tolerance.

## bp1597_q1 Which of the following best describes the primary outcome measured in this study?
Correct: A
The study explicitly reports a mean reduction in fasting glucose levels as the main result, indicating that fasting glucose was the primary outcome.

## bp1597_q2 Which of the following best describes the experimental design used in this study?
Correct: B
Participants were randomly assigned to supplement or placebo groups, and outcomes were measured after a defined intervention period, which is characteristic of a randomized controlled trial.

## bp1597_q3 If the mean fasting glucose in the supplement group decreased from 6.2 mmol/L to 5.4 mmol/L, what is the percent reduction?
Correct: B
Percent reduction = (6.2 – 5.4) / 6.2 × 100 = 0.8 / 6.2 × 100 ≈ 12.9 %. The closest option is 13.3% (option B).

## bp1597_q4 Which of the following statements is a potential trap for interpreting the study’s results?
Correct: C
While the supplement group improved, the placebo group also had a small reduction (0.1 mmol/L), which could be a confounding factor and is a trap for attributing all improvement solely to the supplement.
