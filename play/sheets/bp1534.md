# BP1534

Researchers studied the effect of a novel antihypertensive drug on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in 200 adults with stage‑1 hypertension. Participants were randomized into four groups: a placebo group (n = 50), a low‑dose group (0.5 mg, n = 50), a medium‑dose group (1.0 mg, n = 50), and a high‑dose group (2.0 mg, n = 50). Baseline SBP was measured after a 24‑hour ambulatory monitoring period. After 12 weeks of daily dosing, SBP was re‑measured under identical conditions. The study aimed to evaluate dose‑response relationships and identify the optimal dose that maximizes efficacy while minimizing adverse events. In addition to SBP, the investigators recorded heart rate, serum potassium, and incidence of dizziness. The data were analyzed using ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post‑hoc test. The study was approved by the institutional review board, and all participants provided written informed consent. The results are summarized in Table 1 below.

## bp1534-01 Which treatment group experienced the greatest absolute reduction in systolic blood pressure?
Correct: D
The high‑dose group had the largest drop from 152 mmHg to 125 mmHg, an absolute reduction of 27 mmHg, which exceeds the reductions seen in the other groups.

## bp1534-02 Which of the following study designs best supports a causal inference that the drug lowers systolic blood pressure?
Correct: B
A randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial eliminates selection bias and confounding, providing the strongest evidence for causality.
