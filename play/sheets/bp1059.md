# BP1059

Researchers investigated the effect of varying doses of a novel cognitive enhancer on reaction time in healthy adults. A double‑blind, randomized controlled trial enrolled 120 participants, who were assigned to one of four groups: a placebo control, a low‑dose (10 mg), a medium‑dose (20 mg), or a high‑dose (40 mg) treatment. Each participant completed a computerized Stroop task before and after a single dose. Mean reaction times (ms) and standard deviations (SD) were recorded. The control group had a mean pre‑dose reaction time of 650 ms (SD 45). The low‑dose group improved to 620 ms (SD 50), the medium‑dose group to 590 ms (SD 55), and the high‑dose group to 560 ms (SD 60). The researchers noted a dose‑dependent trend, with the high‑dose group showing the greatest reduction in reaction time. However, the high‑dose group also exhibited the largest variability. The authors suggested that while the enhancer improves cognitive speed, higher doses may introduce inconsistent effects, warranting further investigation into optimal dosing and safety.

## bp1059-01 Which group had the lowest mean reaction time after receiving the dose?
Correct: D
The high‑dose group (40 mg) had the lowest mean reaction time of 560 ms, indicating the greatest improvement.

## bp1059-02 Which variable is the independent variable in this study?
Correct: B
The independent variable is the dose of the cognitive enhancer, which the researchers manipulated to observe its effect on reaction time.

## bp1059-03 What is the percent reduction in mean reaction time from the control group to the high‑dose group?
Correct: B
Percent reduction = (650 - 560) / 650 × 100 ≈ 13.8%.

## bp1059-04 Which group had the highest standard deviation?
Correct: D
The high‑dose group had an SD of 60 ms, the largest among all groups.

## bp1059-05 Which statement best links the study’s findings to a potential clinical application?
Correct: A
The dose‑dependent improvement in reaction time suggests potential utility in disorders characterized by slowed cognitive processing, such as ADHD.
