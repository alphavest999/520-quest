Researchers investigated the effect of light wavelength on the photosynthetic rate of the freshwater alga *Chlorella vulgaris*. Cultures were grown under monochromatic LEDs at 400 nm (violet), 500 nm (green), 600 nm (orange), and 700 nm (red). Photosynthetic rates were measured using a dissolved oxygen electrode, and chlorophyll‑a concentrations were quantified spectrophotometrically. The results are summarized in Table 1.

Table 1. Photosynthetic rate (µmol CO₂ m⁻² s⁻¹) and chlorophyll‑a content (µg mL⁻¹) for *C. vulgaris* under different wavelengths.

| Wavelength (nm) | Photosynthetic rate (µmol CO₂ m⁻² s⁻¹) | Chlorophyll‑a (µg mL⁻¹) |
|-----------------|----------------------------------------|------------------------|
| 400             | 12.4                                   | 8.3                    |
| 500             | 18.7                                   | 10.1                   |
| 600             | 24.9                                   | 12.5                   |
| 700             | 9.2                                    | 6.7                    |

The authors concluded that photosynthetic efficiency peaks at 600 nm, coinciding with the highest chlorophyll‑a concentration, suggesting an optimal absorption spectrum for *C. vulgaris* in the orange region of the spectrum.
