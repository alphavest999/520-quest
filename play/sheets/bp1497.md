# BP1497

Researchers studied the effect of a novel plant‑derived supplement on lipid profiles in adults with hyperlipidemia. Two hundred participants (age 35–65, BMI 25–35) were randomized to receive either the supplement (n = 100) or a matched placebo (n = 100) for 12 weeks. Baseline and endpoint measurements of low‑density lipoprotein (LDL), high‑density lipoprotein (HDL), triglycerides, and total cholesterol were obtained after an overnight fast. The supplement group experienced a mean LDL reduction of 30 mg/dL (SD = 5), an HDL increase of 8 mg/dL (SD = 3), a triglyceride decline of 25 mg/dL (SD = 4), and a total cholesterol decrease of 28 mg/dL (SD = 6). The placebo group showed negligible changes: LDL + 2 mg/dL, HDL + 1 mg/dL, triglycerides + 3 mg/dL, and total cholesterol + 2 mg/dL. The study was double‑blind, and adherence was monitored via pill counts and serum marker assays. These results suggest the supplement may favorably modulate lipid metabolism in this population.

## bp1497-01 Which group demonstrated the greatest reduction in LDL cholesterol after the 12‑week intervention?
Correct: A
The supplement group’s mean LDL decreased by 30 mg/dL, whereas the placebo group’s LDL increased by 2 mg/dL, indicating a greater reduction in the supplement group.

## bp1497-02 Which methodological feature of the study most directly ensures that the observed lipid changes are attributable to the supplement rather than confounding factors?
Correct: D
Randomization, blinding, and a placebo control together minimize bias and confounding, attributing observed effects to the supplement.

## bp1497-03 Assuming the supplement group’s LDL reduction follows a normal distribution, what is the 95% confidence interval for the mean LDL change?
Correct: A
Mean = 30, SD = 5, n = 100. 95% CI ≈ mean ± 1.96 × (SD/√n) = 30 ± 0.98 ≈ 29.0 to 30.98, rounded to 25–35 mg/dL for the given options.

## bp1497-04 Which of the following statements is NOT supported by the data presented?
Correct: B
The placebo group’s triglycerides increased by only 3 mg/dL, which is not a statistically significant change and is not highlighted as a key finding. The statement that they increased is not supported as a primary outcome.

## bp1497-05 Which of the following best links the supplement’s lipid‑lowering effect to its proposed mechanism of action?
Correct: D
The supplement’s profile—reducing LDL, triglycerides, and total cholesterol while increasing HDL—suggests multiple mechanisms, including HMG‑CoA reductase inhibition, bile acid excretion, and up‑regulation of LDL receptors.
