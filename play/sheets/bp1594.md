# BP1594

Researchers studied the effect of a novel dietary supplement on insulin sensitivity in adults with pre‑diabetes. Sixty participants were randomly assigned to either a supplement group (n = 30) or a placebo group (n = 30). Over 12 weeks, fasting insulin and glucose levels were measured at baseline and at the end of the study. The supplement group showed a mean decrease in fasting insulin of 2.5 µU/mL (SD = 0.8), whereas the placebo group showed a mean decrease of 0.6 µU/mL (SD = 0.7). Fasting glucose decreased by 4.2 mg/dL (SD = 1.1) in the supplement group and 1.5 mg/dL (SD = 0.9) in the placebo group. The researchers concluded that the supplement significantly improved insulin sensitivity compared to placebo.

## bp1594-01 Which of the following statements best describes the data presented in the passage?
Correct: A
The table shows a mean decrease of 2.5 µU/mL for the supplement group versus 0.6 µU/mL for placebo, indicating a greater improvement in insulin sensitivity for the supplement group.

## bp1594-02 Which experimental design best supports the researchers’ conclusion that the supplement caused the observed improvements?
Correct: B
A randomized, double‑blind controlled trial is the gold standard for establishing causality because it minimizes bias and confounding.

## bp1594-03 Calculate the absolute difference in mean fasting insulin decrease between the supplement and placebo groups.
Correct: A
Absolute difference = 2.5 µU/mL – 0.6 µU/mL = 1.9 µU/mL.

## bp1594-04 Which of the following statements is a potential trap in interpreting the study’s findings?
Correct: D
Because multiple endpoints (insulin and glucose) were examined, the risk of a false‑positive (type I) error increases unless adjustments are made.

## bp1594-05 Which basic science concept is most directly linked to the study’s focus on insulin sensitivity?
Correct: C
Improved insulin sensitivity is mediated by the PI3K‑Akt signaling cascade, which enhances glucose uptake in peripheral tissues.
