# BP1330

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel antihypertensive agent, CardioX, on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults with stage‑1 hypertension. In a double‑blind, randomized controlled trial, 200 participants were assigned to either CardioX (n = 100) or placebo (n = 100). Baseline mean SBP was 145 mmHg (SD = 8) for both groups. After 12 weeks of therapy, the CardioX group exhibited a mean SBP reduction of 12 mmHg (SD = 4), whereas the placebo group showed a mean reduction of 3 mmHg (SD = 5). The study reported a statistically significant difference (p < 0.001) favoring CardioX. Secondary outcomes included heart rate, lipid profile, and adverse events, all of which were comparable between groups. The investigators concluded that CardioX effectively lowers SBP with a favorable safety profile.

## bp1330-02 Which methodological feature best ensures that the observed blood‑pressure reduction is attributable to CardioX?
Correct: B
Randomization and double‑blinding minimize bias and confounding, strengthening causal inference.

## bp1330-03 If a patient’s baseline SBP is 150 mmHg, what is the expected SBP after 12 weeks of CardioX therapy?
Correct: A
150 mmHg – 12 mmHg = 138 mmHg.

## bp1330-04 Which statement most accurately reflects a potential limitation of this study?
Correct: B
A 12‑week trial may not capture long‑term efficacy or rare adverse events.

## bp1330-05 How might the findings of this study be integrated into a guideline for treating stage‑1 hypertension?
Correct: B
The study shows efficacy and safety over 12 weeks, supporting use after lifestyle measures, but long‑term data are needed before labeling it first‑line.
