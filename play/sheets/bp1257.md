# BP1257

Researchers conducted a double‑blind, randomized controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of drug A in reducing systolic blood pressure (SBP) among adults with stage 1 hypertension. Two hundred and thirty‑two participants were enrolled and randomly assigned to either drug A (n = 116) or placebo (n = 116). Baseline characteristics were comparable between groups. The mean (±SD) SBP at baseline was 142 ± 12 mmHg in the drug A group and 141 ± 13 mmHg in the placebo group. After 12 weeks of treatment, the mean SBP decreased by 10.2 mmHg (±5.1 mmHg) in the drug A group versus 3.4 mmHg (±4.8 mmHg) in the placebo group, yielding a between‑group difference of 6.8 mmHg (95 % CI = 4.2 to 9.4 mmHg). Secondary outcomes included diastolic BP, heart rate, and adverse events. The study concluded that drug A significantly lowered SBP compared with placebo, with a favorable safety profile.

## 2 Which design feature best ensures internal validity in this study?
Correct: D
Blinding of both participants and investigators reduces performance and detection bias, strengthening internal validity.
