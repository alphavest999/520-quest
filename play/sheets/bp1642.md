# BP1642

Researchers studied the effect of a novel dietary supplement on fasting glucose levels in adults with prediabetes. A randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial enrolled 180 participants, who were randomly assigned to one of three groups: (1) supplement (n = 60), (2) placebo (n = 60), and (3) no intervention (n = 60). After 12 weeks, fasting glucose was measured. Table 1 summarizes the mean fasting glucose (mg/dL), standard deviation (SD), and sample size for each group. The investigators hypothesized that the supplement would reduce fasting glucose relative to placebo and no‑intervention controls. The study also collected demographic data (age, sex, BMI) to assess potential confounding variables. The authors concluded that the supplement significantly lowered fasting glucose compared to placebo (p < .01) and that the effect was independent of age and BMI. These findings suggest a promising adjunctive therapy for prediabetes management.

## bp1642-01 Which group had the highest mean fasting glucose level?
Correct: D
The mean fasting glucose for the placebo group is 105 mg/dL and for the no‑intervention group is 107 mg/dL, both higher than the supplement group’s 98 mg/dL. Thus, both placebo and no‑intervention groups have the highest means.

## bp1642-02 Which experimental design best tests the effect of the supplement on fasting glucose?
Correct: A
A randomized controlled trial (RCT) is the gold standard for testing an intervention’s efficacy, as it minimizes bias and confounding by random assignment.

## bp1642-04 Which of the following is a potential confounder that the authors attempted to control for in their analysis?
Correct: A
Age is a known risk factor for glucose metabolism and could confound the relationship between supplement use and fasting glucose; the authors collected age data to adjust for it.

## bp1642-05 Which statement best summarizes the clinical implication of this study?
Correct: B
The study shows a significant reduction in fasting glucose with the supplement compared to placebo, but the authors note the need for larger, longer‑term trials to confirm efficacy and safety.
