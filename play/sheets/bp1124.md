# BP1124

Researchers investigated the effect of light intensity on the photosynthetic performance of *Arabidopsis thaliana* under controlled laboratory conditions. Fifteen-day-old seedlings were exposed to four discrete light intensities (200, 400, 600, and 800 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹) for 4 hours each day over a 7‑day period. Gas exchange measurements were recorded using a portable photosynthesis system, yielding simultaneous data for net photosynthetic rate (A), stomatal conductance (gs), and respiration rate (R). The study aimed to identify the light saturation point and to evaluate whether increased light intensity led to a proportional rise in photosynthetic rate or triggered photoinhibition. The authors noted that while A increased markedly from 200 to 600 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹, the rise plateaued at 800 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹, suggesting a saturation threshold. Stomatal conductance mirrored this trend, rising until 600 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ before declining slightly at 800 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹, indicating possible stomatal limitations. Respiration rates increased modestly across all intensities, with the highest value observed at 800 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹. These findings provide insight into the optimal light conditions for maximizing photosynthetic efficiency in *A. thaliana*.

Correct: A
