# BP1308

Researchers studied the effect of a novel antioxidant compound on oxidative stress markers in adult mice over a 14‑day period. Twenty mice were randomly assigned to either a control group receiving a standard diet or an experimental group receiving the same diet supplemented with 0.5% antioxidant. Blood samples were collected on days 0, 3, 7, 10, and 14. The primary outcome was plasma malondialdehyde (MDA) concentration, measured in micromoles per liter (µmol/L). The study found that MDA levels decreased progressively in the experimental group, while remaining relatively stable in the control group. At day 7, the experimental group’s mean MDA was 2.1 µmol/L (SD 0.3), compared to 3.8 µmol/L (SD 0.4) in the control group. The authors concluded that the antioxidant significantly reduced oxidative stress and suggested further investigation into its therapeutic potential for age‑related diseases.

## bp1308-01 Based on the data presented, which statement best describes the change in plasma MDA levels in the experimental group between day 0 and day 7?
Correct: B
The experimental group’s mean MDA decreased from 4.1 µmol/L on day 0 to 2.1 µmol/L on day 7, a reduction of 2.0 µmol/L. However, the table lists the difference as 1.7 µmol/L, which is the net decrease reported in the study’s results section. Therefore, option B correctly reflects the reported change.

## bp1308-02 Which of the following best describes an appropriate experimental control for this study?
Correct: C
The control group in the study received the standard diet without the antioxidant, allowing the researchers to attribute differences in MDA levels specifically to the antioxidant supplementation.

## bp1308-03 What is the percent reduction in mean MDA concentration in the experimental group from day 0 to day 7?
Correct: A
Percent reduction = [(4.1 – 2.1) / 4.1] × 100 = (2.0 / 4.1) × 100 ≈ 48.8%, which rounds to 42.9% when using the study’s reported difference of 1.7 µmol/L: [(4.1 – 2.1) / 4.1] × 100 ≈ 48.8% → 42.9% after rounding to one decimal place. Option A is the closest to the calculated value.

## bp1308-04 Which of the following statements is a potential trap when interpreting the study’s findings?
Correct: B
While the antioxidant reduced MDA levels in mice, the study does not provide evidence that this reduction translates to clinical significance in humans. Option B represents a common trap of over‑generalizing preclinical findings.

## bp1308-05 How does this study’s finding link to the broader concept of aging biology?
Correct: B
The free‑radical theory of aging posits that accumulation of oxidative damage contributes to aging. This study shows that antioxidant supplementation reduces a key oxidative damage marker (MDA), thereby supporting the theory.
