# BP1113

Researchers at the University of Metropolis conducted a pilot study to evaluate the effect of a novel antihypertensive compound, Hyprenol, on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults with stage 1 hypertension. Twenty-five participants were enrolled and randomized to receive either Hyprenol (n = 12) or placebo (n = 13) for 8 weeks. Baseline SBP was measured after a 5‑minute seated rest, and follow‑up SBP readings were taken at weeks 4 and 8. The study aimed to assess both the magnitude of SBP reduction and the safety profile of Hyprenol. In addition to SBP, participants recorded daily heart rate, medication adherence, and any adverse events. The investigators hypothesized that Hyprenol would produce a clinically meaningful reduction in SBP compared with placebo, with minimal side effects. The data were analyzed using mixed‑effects models to account for repeated measures within subjects. The results suggested a mean SBP reduction of 12 mmHg in the Hyprenol group versus 4 mmHg in the placebo group, with no serious adverse events reported. These findings support further investigation of Hyprenol in larger, double‑blind trials.
