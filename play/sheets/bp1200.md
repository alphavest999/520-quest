# BP1200

Researchers investigated the impact of visual complexity on reaction time in a simple target detection task. Participants were randomly assigned to one of three display conditions: (1) minimal visual clutter, (2) moderate clutter, and (3) high clutter. Each participant completed 60 trials, and mean reaction times were recorded. The study aimed to quantify how increasing visual complexity affects cognitive processing speed. The findings are intended to inform interface design guidelines for high‑stakes environments such as air traffic control and surgical navigation systems.

## BP1200-001-Q1 Which display condition produced the fastest mean reaction time?
Correct: A
The table shows the mean reaction time for each condition. Minimal clutter has the lowest mean (350 ms), indicating the fastest responses.

## BP1200-001-Q2 Which of the following would be the most appropriate control condition for a follow‑up study investigating the effect of color contrast on reaction time?
Correct: A
A control condition should match the original minimal clutter setup but vary only the factor of interest (color contrast). Low contrast serves as the baseline to compare against high contrast.

## BP1200-001-Q5 How do the findings of this study relate to the broader principle of cognitive load theory?
Correct: A
Cognitive load theory posits that extraneous load (e.g., visual clutter) consumes working memory resources, thereby increasing overall cognitive load and reducing processing speed.
