Researchers at the National Institute of Health conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel anti‑inflammatory drug, Inflammex, in patients with moderate rheumatoid arthritis. The study enrolled 240 adults aged 30–65 years, randomly assigning them to either Inflammex (n = 120) or placebo (n = 120) for 12 weeks. Baseline disease activity was measured using the Disease Activity Score 28 (DAS28). At week 12, the mean DAS28 reduction in the Inflammex group was 2.3 (SD 0.8) versus 1.1 (SD 0.9) in the placebo group, yielding a between‑group difference of 1.2 (95% CI 0.9–1.5, p < 0.001). Secondary outcomes included patient global assessment, functional status (Health Assessment Questionnaire), and safety profile. Adverse events were mild and comparable between groups. The authors concluded that Inflammex provides a statistically significant improvement in disease activity over placebo, supporting its potential use as an adjunct therapy for rheumatoid arthritis.
