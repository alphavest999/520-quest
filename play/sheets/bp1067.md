# BP1067

Researchers studied the effect of a new dietary supplement on blood pressure in adults aged 40 to 60 years. In a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial, 240 participants were assigned to either the supplement group (n = 120) or the placebo group (n = 120). Baseline systolic blood pressure (SBP) was measured for all participants and recorded as the mean ± SD. Participants received their assigned intervention daily for 12 weeks. SBP was reassessed at 4, 8, and 12 weeks. The supplement group showed a gradual decline in mean SBP, reaching a 12‑week mean of 122 ± 9 mm Hg, whereas the placebo group’s mean SBP remained stable at 128 ± 10 mm Hg. The study also recorded diastolic blood pressure, heart rate, and adverse events. Statistical analysis employed a mixed‑effects model, yielding a p‑value of 0.003 for the between‑group difference at 12 weeks. The investigators concluded that the supplement significantly reduces SBP in this age group, with a favorable safety profile.

## bp1067-04 Which statement is a common misconception about the study’s p‑value?
Correct: B
The p‑value reflects the probability of observing data as extreme as those seen, assuming the null hypothesis is true; it does not convey effect size or magnitude.

Correct: B
