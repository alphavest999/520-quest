# BP1377

Researchers studied the effect of three different auditory stimuli on participants’ reaction times during a simple visual detection task. The study recruited 90 healthy adults aged 18–35, randomly assigned to one of three groups: Control (no auditory stimulus), Treatment A (high‑frequency tone), and Treatment B (low‑frequency tone). Each participant completed 60 trials, and the mean reaction time (RT) and standard deviation (SD) were recorded for each group. The Control group had a mean RT of 350 ms (SD = 45 ms), Treatment A had a mean RT of 310 ms (SD = 38 ms), and Treatment B had a mean RT of 320 ms (SD = 42 ms). The researchers reported that both auditory stimuli reduced mean RT compared to the Control, with Treatment A showing the greatest improvement. The data were analyzed using one‑way ANOVA followed by post‑hoc Tukey tests. The study concluded that high‑frequency tones may facilitate faster visual processing in healthy adults.
