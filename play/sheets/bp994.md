# BP994

Researchers studied the effect of a novel antihypertensive drug on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults with stage 1 hypertension. One hundred twenty participants were randomly assigned to receive either the drug (n = 60) or a placebo (n = 60) for eight weeks. Baseline SBP was measured after a 5‑minute seated rest. After the treatment period, SBP was reassessed. The study aimed to determine whether the drug lowered SBP more effectively than placebo and to assess the magnitude of change. The investigators also collected data on adverse events and adherence. ## bp994-1 Which group experienced the greater reduction in systolic blood pressure after 8 weeks?
Correct: A
The drug group’s mean SBP decreased from 120 mmHg to 110 mmHg, a 10 mmHg drop, whereas the placebo group’s mean SBP decreased only 1 mmHg. Therefore, the drug group had the greater reduction.

## bp994-2 What type of study design was employed in this research?
Correct: A
Participants were randomly assigned to the drug or placebo group, and the effect was measured over time, which defines a randomized controlled trial.

## bp994-3 Calculate the percent reduction in systolic blood pressure for the drug group.
Correct: A
Percent reduction = [(120 – 110)/120] × 100 = 8.33 %. The other options are incorrect calculations.

## bp994-4 Which of the following is a potential source of bias in this study?
Correct: C
A small sample size can limit the study’s power and increase the risk of random error, which is a source of bias. Randomization, blinding, and placebo control are methods to reduce bias.

## bp994-5 Which statement best links the study findings to clinical practice?
Correct: A
An 8 % reduction in SBP is clinically meaningful for patients with stage 1 hypertension, supporting the drug’s potential use. The other statements either misinterpret the data or are trivial observations.
