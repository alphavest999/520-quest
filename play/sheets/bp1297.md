# BP1297

Researchers studied the effect of a novel cognitive enhancer on reaction time in healthy adults. A double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial enrolled 120 participants who were randomly assigned to one of four groups: placebo (control), low dose (10 mg), medium dose (20 mg), or high dose (40 mg). Each participant completed a computerized simple reaction time task 30 minutes after drug administration. The mean reaction times (RT) and standard deviations (SD) for each group are presented in Table 1. The study aimed to determine whether increasing doses would produce a dose‑response improvement in RT without compromising safety. All participants reported no adverse events, and the study was approved by the institutional review board.

Table 1. Mean Reaction Time (ms) by Dose

Condition | Mean RT | SD
Control (Placebo) | 350 | 15
Low Dose (10 mg) | 340 | 12
Medium Dose (20 mg) | 330 | 10
High Dose (40 mg) | 320 | 8

## bp1297_q1 Based on Table 1, which statement best describes the observed dose‑response relationship?
Correct: B
The mean RT decreases from 350 ms (control) to 320 ms (high dose), indicating faster responses with higher doses.

## bp1297_q2 Which experimental design element best ensures that the observed changes in reaction time are attributable to the drug dose?
Correct: A
Random assignment minimizes confounding variables, allowing attribution of differences to the dose.

## bp1297_q3 What percent reduction in mean reaction time does the high dose produce relative to the control?
Correct: B
Percent reduction = ((350-320)/350)*100 = 8.57% ≈ 5.7% when rounding to one decimal place.

## bp1297_q4 Which of the following statements is a common misinterpretation of the standard deviation values in Table 1?
Correct: D
SD reflects variability, not speed; option D incorrectly equates SD with mean performance.

## bp1297_q5 Which real‑world application could most directly benefit from the dose‑response findings of this study?
Correct: B
The study demonstrates that higher doses of the enhancer improve reaction time, which could be applied to athletic performance.
