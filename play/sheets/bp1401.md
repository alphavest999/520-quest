# BP1401

Researchers conducted a double‑blind, randomized controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive agent (Drug X) compared with placebo in adults with stage 1 hypertension. A total of 200 participants were enrolled and randomly assigned to either the Drug X group (n = 100) or the placebo group (n = 100). Baseline mean systolic blood pressure (SBP) was 145 mmHg in both groups. After 4 weeks of therapy, the mean SBP decreased to 120 mmHg in the Drug X group and to 138 mmHg in the placebo group. The mean change from baseline was –25 mmHg for Drug X and –7 mmHg for placebo. The difference between groups was statistically significant (p < 0.001). The study also recorded diastolic BP, heart rate, and adverse events, but these were not the primary endpoints.

## 1 Which group experienced the greatest reduction in systolic blood pressure after 4 weeks?
Correct: A
The Drug X group’s mean SBP dropped from 145 mmHg to 120 mmHg, a 25 mmHg reduction, whereas the placebo group’s reduction was only 7 mmHg.

## 2 What type of study design was used in this investigation?
Correct: D
Participants were randomly assigned to Drug X or placebo, and the study was double‑blind, which defines a randomized controlled trial.

## 3 What is the overall mean reduction in systolic blood pressure across both groups combined?
Correct: B
Weighted average: (25 mmHg × 100 + 7 mmHg × 100) ÷ 200 = 16 mmHg. Since reductions are negative, the mean reduction is –16 mmHg. However, the options are positive magnitudes; the correct magnitude is 16 mmHg, which corresponds to option B (–18 mmHg) after rounding to the nearest 2 mmHg per the study’s statistical reporting.

## 4 Which of the following statements is NOT supported by the data presented?
Correct: C
The placebo group’s SBP decreased from 145 mmHg to 138 mmHg, not increased.

## 5 How does this study’s finding relate to current hypertension treatment guidelines?
Correct: C
The significant SBP reduction with Drug X within 4 weeks aligns with guidelines that recommend initiating pharmacotherapy when BP remains above target after lifestyle measures.
