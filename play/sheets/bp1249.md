# BP1249

Researchers at the University of Health Sciences investigated the effect of a new antihypertensive medication on systolic blood pressure. Forty-eight adults aged 45-65 with mild hypertension were randomly assigned to one of four groups: a placebo control, a low-dose group (25 mg), a medium-dose group (50 mg), and a high-dose group (75 mg). Baseline systolic blood pressure was measured for all participants. Over a four-week period, participants took their assigned medication daily. After the intervention, systolic blood pressure was measured again. The results are summarized in Table 1. The data show a dose-dependent decrease in systolic blood pressure, with the high-dose group experiencing the greatest reduction. No serious adverse events were reported. These findings suggest that the medication may be effective in lowering blood pressure in a dose-responsive manner, but further studies are needed to assess long-term safety and efficacy. The study employed a double-blind, randomized controlled design to minimize bias. Blood pressure readings were taken using calibrated sphygmomanometers at baseline and at the end of the study. Statistical analysis revealed that the high-dose group had a mean reduction of 22 mmHg (p<0.01) compared to the placebo. The low- and medium-dose groups showed reductions of 8 mmHg and 16 mmHg, respectively, both statistically significant (p<0.05). No participants reported dizziness or headache, suggesting a favorable tolerability profile.
