# BP1711

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel antihypertensive agent on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults with stage 1 hypertension. In a double‑blind, randomized controlled trial, 120 participants were assigned to either the drug or a placebo. Baseline SBP was measured for all participants, followed by assessments at 4 and 8 weeks. The mean SBP values (± SD) for each group are presented in Table 1. The study aimed to determine whether the drug could achieve a clinically meaningful reduction in SBP compared to placebo while monitoring for adverse events. Results indicated a progressive decline in SBP in the treatment arm, with the most pronounced difference observed at 8 weeks. The investigators concluded that the drug may be a viable option for patients who do not achieve target BP with standard therapy.

Table 1: Mean systolic BP (mmHg) by group and timepoint

Timepoint | Control (Placebo) | Treatment (Drug)
Baseline | 140 ± 12 | 140 ± 11
4 weeks | 138 ± 13 | 130 ± 10
8 weeks | 136 ± 14 | 120 ± 9

## bp1711_q1 Which timepoint shows the greatest difference in mean systolic blood pressure between the treatment and control groups?
Correct: C
At baseline the groups were identical (140 mmHg). The difference increases to 8 mmHg at 4 weeks and to 16 mmHg at 8 weeks, making 8 weeks the timepoint with the greatest difference.

## bp1711_q2 Which study design best controls for confounding variables in this investigation?
Correct: A
Randomization distributes known and unknown confounders evenly between groups, making the randomized controlled trial the gold standard for controlling confounding.

## bp1711_q3 What is the percent reduction in mean systolic blood pressure in the treatment group from baseline to 8 weeks?
Correct: B
Percent reduction = [(140 – 120)/140] × 100 = 14.3%, which rounds to 14%.

## bp1711_q4 Which of the following is a potential source of bias in this study?
Correct: C
Randomization and blinding reduce bias; a small sample size can increase random error and reduce the precision of effect estimates, thus introducing bias.

## bp1711_q5 Which statement best links the study findings to clinical practice?
Correct: B
The drug produced a clinically meaningful SBP reduction in patients with stage 1 hypertension, suggesting it could be useful for those who do not reach targets with standard therapy (i.e., resistant hypertension).
