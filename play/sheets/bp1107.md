# BP1107

Researchers studied the effect of varying doses of a novel antihypertensive agent on systolic blood pressure in a cohort of 120 adults with mild hypertension. The study was conducted over a 12‑week period and employed a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled design. Participants were randomly assigned to one of three dosage groups—10 mg, 20 mg, or 40 mg daily—or to a placebo group. Baseline systolic blood pressure (SBP) was recorded for all subjects, and subsequent measurements were taken at weeks 4, 8, and 12. Table 1 summarizes the mean SBP reduction (in mmHg) observed at the study’s conclusion, along with the standard deviation and sample size for each dosage. The 10‑mg group showed a modest reduction of 5 mmHg (SD = 3.2, n = 35), the 20‑mg group achieved a 10‑mmHg reduction (SD = 4.1, n = 30), and the 40‑mg group experienced the largest decrease of 20 mmHg (SD = 5.6, n = 25). The placebo group’s mean reduction was 1 mmHg (SD = 2.5, n = 30). These findings suggest a dose‑dependent relationship between the drug and SBP lowering, with higher doses yielding greater reductions but also greater variability. Further studies are needed to assess long‑term safety and optimal dosing.

## bp1107-1 Which dosage produced the greatest mean reduction in systolic blood pressure?
Correct: C
The table shows that the 40‑mg group had the largest mean reduction of 20 mmHg, compared with 10 mmHg for 20 mg and 5 mmHg for 10 mg.

## bp1107-2 Which experimental design best controls for confounding variables in this study?
Correct: A
The study used random assignment to dosage groups and was double‑blind and placebo‑controlled, characteristics of a randomized controlled trial, which is the gold standard for controlling confounders.
