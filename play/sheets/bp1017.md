# BP1017

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel dietary supplement on resting metabolic rate (RMR) in healthy adults. Twenty-four participants (12 male, 12 female) were randomly assigned to either the supplement group (n=12) or a placebo group (n=12). Baseline RMR was measured using indirect calorimetry after an overnight fast. Participants then received either the supplement (500 mg/day) or placebo for 8 weeks. RMR was reassessed at week 4 and week 8. Table 1 summarizes the mean RMR (kcal/day) and standard deviation (SD) for each group at each time point. In addition, body composition (lean mass and fat mass) was measured by dual‑energy X‑ray absorptiometry. The researchers hypothesized that the supplement would increase RMR and reduce fat mass relative to placebo.

## bp1017-1 What was the mean RMR of the supplement group at week 8?
Correct: B
The table lists the supplement group mean RMR at week 8 as 1755 kcal/day.

## bp1017-2 Which of the following best describes the study design?
Correct: A
Participants were randomly assigned to supplement or placebo, making this a randomized controlled trial.

## bp1017-3 What is the percent increase in mean RMR from baseline to week 8 for the supplement group?
Correct: B
Increase = 1755 – 1650 = 105. Percent = (105/1650) × 100 ≈ 6.36% → 6.4%.

## bp1017-4 Which of the following statements is NOT supported by the data?
Correct: D
The data show an increase, not a decrease, in the supplement group.

## bp1017-5 Which of the following is a potential mechanism by which the supplement could increase RMR?
Correct: A
Thyroid hormones are a well‑known regulator of basal metabolic rate; a supplement that increases thyroid activity could raise RMR.
