# BP1762

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel dietary supplement on insulin sensitivity in adults with prediabetes. In a double‑blind, randomized controlled trial, 120 participants were assigned to either the supplement group (n=60) or a placebo group (n=60) for 12 weeks. Baseline fasting insulin levels and HOMA‑IR scores were recorded. At the end of the trial, the supplement group exhibited a mean reduction in HOMA‑IR of 1.2 units (SD 0.5), while the placebo group showed a mean reduction of 0.3 units (SD 0.4). The difference between groups was statistically significant (p<0.001). Additionally, the supplement group had a 15% greater improvement in fasting glucose levels compared to placebo. Subgroup analysis indicated that participants aged 45‑60 years derived the greatest benefit, with a mean HOMA‑IR reduction of 1.5 units. No serious adverse events were reported. The authors concluded that the supplement improves insulin sensitivity and could be a valuable adjunct therapy for prediabetes management.

## bp1762_q1 Which group had the greatest mean reduction in HOMA‑IR according to the study?
Correct: C
The 45‑60 years subgroup showed a mean HOMA‑IR reduction of 1.5 units, which is higher than the 1.2 units for the supplement group and the 0.3 units for the placebo group.

## bp1762_q2 What type of study design was used in this research?
Correct: C
The passage explicitly states that the study was a double‑blind, randomized controlled trial, which is a gold‑standard design for evaluating interventions.

## bp1762_q3 What is the percent difference in mean HOMA‑IR reduction between the supplement and placebo groups?
Correct: C
Percent difference = [(1.2 – 0.3) / 0.3] × 100 = 300%. This shows the supplement group’s reduction is three times larger than the placebo group’s.
