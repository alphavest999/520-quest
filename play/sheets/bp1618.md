# BP1618

Researchers conducted a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive agent, Hypertensol, in adults with stage 1 hypertension. Eighty participants were enrolled and randomly assigned to receive either Hypertensol (n = 40) or a matching placebo (n = 40) for 8 weeks. Baseline systolic blood pressure (SBP) was measured using an automated cuff after a 5‑minute seated rest. Follow‑up SBP readings were taken at week 4 and week 8. The primary endpoint was the mean change in SBP from baseline to week 8. Secondary endpoints included diastolic BP change and incidence of adverse events. The study found that participants receiving Hypertensol experienced a mean SBP reduction of 20 mmHg (SD 5), whereas the placebo group had a mean reduction of 1 mmHg (SD 4). The difference between groups was statistically significant (p < 0.001). No serious adverse events were reported. These results suggest that Hypertensol may provide a clinically meaningful reduction in SBP in patients with mild hypertension.
