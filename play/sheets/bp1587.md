# BP1587

Researchers studied the effect of a novel antiviral compound, AV‑42, on the replication of influenza A virus in MDCK cell cultures. In a controlled experiment, cells were infected at multiplicity of infection (MOI) 0.1 and then treated with varying concentrations of AV‑42 (0, 0.5, 1, 2, 4 µM). Viral RNA levels were quantified by qRT‑PCR at 24, 48, and 72 hours post‑infection. Additionally, cell viability was assessed using an MTT assay to evaluate cytotoxicity. The data indicated a dose‑dependent reduction in viral RNA, with 4 µM achieving a 95% decrease at 72 h. However, viability dropped to 70% at the same concentration, suggesting a narrow therapeutic window. Researchers also performed a time‑of‑addition assay, revealing that AV‑42 most effectively inhibited viral replication when added within 2 hours of infection. These findings support further preclinical development of AV‑42 as a potential antiviral agent, but underscore the need for optimization to reduce host cell toxicity.
