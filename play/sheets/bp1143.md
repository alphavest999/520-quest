# BP1143

Researchers studied the effect of two novel cognitive enhancers on reaction time in a controlled laboratory setting. Thirty healthy adults were randomly assigned to one of three groups: a control group receiving a placebo, Treatment A receiving drug X, and Treatment B receiving drug Y. Each participant completed a standardized computer-based reaction time task consisting of 60 trials. The mean reaction times (in milliseconds) and standard deviations were recorded, along with the sample size for each group. The results are summarized in Table 1 below. The data suggest that both treatments reduced reaction times relative to the control, with Treatment B showing the largest reduction. However, the variability in responses differed across groups. Researchers plan to conduct a follow-up study to assess long‑term effects and potential side effects. The current findings provide preliminary evidence that drug Y may be more effective at improving cognitive speed, but further investigation is needed to confirm these results and determine clinical relevance.

Table 1: Mean reaction times (ms) and standard deviations for each group
| Group | Mean RT (ms) | SD (ms) | N |
| Control | 350 | 20 | 30 |
| Treatment A | 320 | 25 | 30 |
| Treatment B | 310 | 22 | 30 |

## bp1143_q1 Which of the following best describes the pattern of reaction times across groups?
Correct: A
The table shows mean reaction times of 350 ms (control), 320 ms (Treatment A), and 310 ms (Treatment B). Thus the order from highest to lowest is Control > Treatment A > Treatment B.

## bp1143_q2 What is the most appropriate next step to further investigate the findings?
Correct: C
A double‑blind crossover design would control for individual differences and allow each participant to serve as their own control, providing stronger evidence of causality.

## bp1143_q3 Calculate the approximate Cohen’s d effect size between the Control and Treatment B groups (assume equal SD of 20 ms).
Correct: C
Cohen’s d = (Mean_control – Mean_TreatmentB) / SD = (350 – 310) / 20 = 40 / 20 = 2.0.

## bp1143_q4 Which of the following statements is a common misinterpretation of the study’s results?
Correct: A
The study is observational and does not establish causality; the observed association may be due to confounding factors.

## bp1143_q5 Which concept best links the observed reduction in reaction times to potential clinical applications?
Correct: A
Pharmacodynamics explains how the drugs influence reaction time by interacting with neural targets, providing a mechanistic basis for clinical use.
