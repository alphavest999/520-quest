# BP979

Researchers studied the effect of varying light intensity on photosynthetic rate in spinach leaves. They measured CO₂ uptake over a 10‑minute period at four light intensities (200, 400, 600, and 800 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹). The mean CO₂ uptake (µmol CO₂ min⁻¹) and standard deviation for each intensity are reported in Table 1. The results show a clear increase in uptake with light intensity up to 600 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹, after which the rate plateaus at 800 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹. These findings suggest that photosynthetic capacity is limited by factors other than light at higher intensities.

## bp979-001 Which light intensity produced the highest average CO₂ uptake?
Correct: D
The mean CO₂ uptake at 800 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹ (24.1 µmol min⁻¹) is the highest among the four intensities.

## bp979-002 Which experimental design would best test whether light intensity affects photosynthesis?
Correct: C
A controlled experiment that systematically varies light while holding all other variables constant is the gold standard for testing causality.

Correct: B
Percent increase = [(22.7‑18.3)/18.3] × 100 ≈ 24.1%, which rounds to 24.6%.

## bp979-004 Which statement is a common misconception about photosynthesis based on the data?
Correct: A
The data show a linear increase up to 600 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹, after which the rate plateaus, contradicting a strictly linear relationship.

## bp979-005 Which physiological limitation best explains the observed plateau in CO₂ uptake at high light intensities?
Correct: B
At high light, the Calvin‑cycle enzymes (e.g., Rubisco) become saturated, limiting further increases in CO₂ fixation despite more available light.
