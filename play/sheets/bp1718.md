# BP1718

Researchers investigated the effect of caffeine on working memory performance in a controlled laboratory setting. Twenty-eight healthy adults (age 20-35) were randomly assigned to one of four conditions: (1) placebo, (2) low-dose caffeine (50 mg), (3) moderate-dose caffeine (100 mg), and (4) high-dose caffeine (200 mg). Each participant completed a 2‑back task after ingesting their assigned beverage. Reaction times (RT) and accuracy were recorded. The researchers hypothesized that moderate caffeine would yield the fastest RTs and highest accuracy, with performance declining at the high dose due to overstimulation. Table 1 presents the mean RTs and standard deviations for each group. The study also measured subjective alertness using a 10‑point Likert scale. Results indicated a U‑shaped relationship between caffeine dose and RT, supporting the hypothesis. However, accuracy did not vary significantly across groups, suggesting caffeine primarily affected speed rather than correctness.

## bp1718_q1 Which group had the fastest mean reaction time?
Correct: C
The moderate‑dose group (100 mg) had the lowest mean RT of 520 ms, the fastest among all groups.

## bp1718_q2 Which experimental design best controls for placebo effects in this study?
Correct: C
A cross‑over design would have each participant receive all four doses in a randomized order, eliminating individual differences and controlling for placebo effects.

## bp1718_q3 What percent improvement in mean RT does the moderate‑dose group show relative to placebo?
Correct: B
Improvement = (650 – 520)/650 = 130/650 ≈ 0.20 = 20 %.

## bp1718_q4 Which of the following statements is most likely due to chance?
Correct: D
The data show the high‑dose group’s RT (580 ms) is slower than the moderate group (520 ms); selecting it as fastest is a mistake likely caused by random misreading.

## bp1718_q5 Which psychological principle is most directly illustrated by the study’s findings?
Correct: C
The Yerkes‑Dodson law predicts an optimal level of arousal (moderate caffeine) for peak performance, with performance declining at lower and higher arousal levels, matching the U‑shaped RT curve.
