Researchers conducted a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive agent in adults with mild to moderate hypertension. One hundred participants were randomly assigned to one of four groups: placebo, low‑dose (25 mg), medium‑dose (50 mg), or high‑dose (100 mg). All patients received their assigned treatment for 12 weeks while maintaining their usual diet and exercise habits. Systolic blood pressure (SBP) was measured at baseline and at the end of the study using a calibrated automated cuff. The investigators reported mean SBP values, standard deviations, and sample sizes for each group. The data suggested a dose‑dependent reduction in SBP, with the high‑dose group exhibiting the greatest mean decrease. The study concluded that the new agent is safe and effective, particularly at the highest dose, and recommended further research to confirm long‑term outcomes.

## 1 Which group had the lowest mean systolic blood pressure at the end of the study?
Correct: D
The table shows the mean SBP for each group; the high‑dose group recorded 120 mmHg, the lowest value.

## 2 What type of study design best controls for confounding variables in this investigation?
Correct: A
Randomization distributes known and unknown confounders evenly across treatment groups, which is the hallmark of a randomized controlled trial.

## 3 What is the percent reduction in mean systolic blood pressure from the placebo group to the high‑dose group?
Correct: C
Reduction = 150 mmHg – 120 mmHg = 30 mmHg. Percent reduction = (30/150) × 100 = 20%. Option C is correct.
