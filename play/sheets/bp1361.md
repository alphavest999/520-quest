# BP1361

Researchers investigated the acute effect of a novel antihypertensive agent, Hypril, on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in a cohort of 20 adults with stage‑1 hypertension. Twenty participants were randomly assigned to receive either Hypril (n = 10) or a placebo (n = 10). Baseline SBP was recorded after a 5‑minute seated rest. Participants then received a single oral dose of the assigned treatment and SBP was reassessed at 30 minutes post‑dose. The investigators focused on the magnitude of SBP reduction, hypothesizing that Hypril would produce a clinically significant drop (≥ 10 mm Hg) compared with placebo. The study also monitored heart rate and reported no adverse events.
