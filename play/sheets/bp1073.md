# BP1073

Researchers at the University of Health Sciences conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive drug, Hypotensol, on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults with stage 1 hypertension. Ten participants (5 males, 5 females, ages 45–62) were enrolled and received a daily dose of 10 mg Hypotensol for 4 weeks. Baseline SBP values were recorded after a 5‑minute seated rest. After the 4‑week treatment period, SBP was measured again under identical conditions. The study aimed to determine whether Hypotensol could produce a clinically meaningful reduction in SBP compared to baseline. All participants provided informed consent, and the study was approved by the institutional review board. The investigators also collected data on potential side effects, including dizziness and headache. The results indicated a modest but statistically significant mean reduction in SBP of 8.2 mmHg (p < 0.05). However, variability among subjects was high, with reductions ranging from 2 to 15 mmHg. These findings suggest that Hypotensol may be effective for some patients, but further research is needed to identify predictors of response and to assess long‑term safety.
