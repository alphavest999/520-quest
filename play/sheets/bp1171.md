# BP1171

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel antioxidant compound, Oxidex, on oxidative stress markers in a mouse model of diet‑induced atherosclerosis. Male C57BL/6J mice were randomly assigned to one of three groups: (1) standard chow (control), (2) high‑fat diet (HFD), and (3) HFD plus Oxidex (10 mg/kg/day). After 12 weeks, plasma malondialdehyde (MDA) levels, a marker of lipid peroxidation, were measured. The study found that HFD increased MDA levels by 45 % relative to control, whereas the Oxidex group showed a 30 % reduction in MDA compared with the HFD group. Histological analysis of the aortic root revealed fewer lipid‑laden macrophages in the Oxidex group. The authors concluded that Oxidex mitigates oxidative damage and may slow atherosclerotic plaque formation.

## bp1171-01 Which statement best describes the effect of Oxidex on plasma MDA levels?
Correct: C
The table shows MDA in the HFD + Oxidex group (12.7 µmol/L) versus HFD (18.2 µmol/L), a ~30 % reduction. The value is not below the control group (12.3 µmol/L), so option D is incorrect.

## bp1171-02 If you were to design a follow‑up study to test whether Oxidex’s effect is dose‑dependent, which experimental design would be most appropriate?
Correct: A
A randomized controlled trial with multiple Oxidex doses directly tests dose‑dependence while controlling for confounders. Observational and cross‑sectional designs cannot establish causality or dose response.

## bp1171-03 Calculate the percent reduction in MDA levels achieved by Oxidex compared with the HFD group.
Correct: C
Percent reduction = [(18.2 – 12.7)/18.2] × 100 ≈ 30 %. Options A and B are too low; D reflects the HFD vs. control difference.

## bp1171-04 Which of the following is a potential flaw in interpreting the study’s conclusion that Oxidex ‘may slow atherosclerotic plaque formation’?
Correct: A
The conclusion about plaque formation is inferred from MDA reduction and macrophage counts, but no direct measurement of plaque area was reported. Option B misstates the effect of large samples; option C incorrectly describes the control; option D is not indicated.

## bp1171-05 Which concept best links the study’s use of a high‑fat diet to the development of atherosclerosis?
Correct: A
A high‑fat diet induces endothelial dysfunction, a key early event in atherogenesis. While inflammation is involved, endothelial dysfunction is the most direct link described in the passage.
