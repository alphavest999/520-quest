# BP983

Researchers studied the effect of varying doses of the antihypertensive drug Lisinopril on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in a cohort of 200 adults with stage‑1 hypertension. Participants were randomized to receive either 5 mg, 10 mg, 20 mg, or 40 mg daily for 12 weeks. Baseline SBP was measured after a 5‑minute seated rest. The study employed a double‑blind, placebo‑controlled design. Adherence was monitored via pill counts and plasma drug levels. The primary outcome was mean change in SBP from baseline to week 12. Secondary outcomes included diastolic BP, heart rate, and adverse events. The investigators hypothesized a dose‑dependent reduction in SBP, with the highest dose yielding the greatest benefit but potentially more side effects. Data were analyzed using ANCOVA adjusting for baseline SBP and age. The results indicated a statistically significant linear trend (p < 0.001) across dose groups. The 20 mg group achieved the largest mean reduction of 18 mmHg, while the 40 mg group showed a 15 mmHg reduction, suggesting a plateau in efficacy at higher doses. Adverse events were mild and comparable across groups.
