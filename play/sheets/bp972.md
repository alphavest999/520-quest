# BP972

Researchers at the Greenhouse Institute investigated how varying light intensity influences the rate of photosynthesis in spinach (Spinacia oleracea) leaves. To quantify this relationship, they exposed detached leaf discs to four controlled light levels: 100, 250, 500, and 750 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹, each for a 30‑minute interval. Oxygen evolution, measured via a Clark electrode, served as a proxy for photosynthetic activity. The experiment was replicated thrice to ensure statistical reliability. Table 1 summarizes the mean oxygen production rates (µmol O₂ g⁻¹ leaf h⁻¹) along with standard deviations and sample sizes for each light intensity. The data reveal a sigmoidal trend, with a steep increase from 100 to 250 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹, followed by a plateau approaching saturation near 500 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹. Notably, the 750 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹ treatment did not significantly exceed the 500 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹ rate, suggesting a saturation point. These findings align with the light‑response curve model, indicating that spinach photosynthesis is limited by light intensity up to a threshold beyond which other factors, such as enzyme capacity, become rate‑determining.

## bp972-2 Which of the following best describes the experimental design used in this study?
Correct: B
The researchers applied each light intensity to leaf discs in a completely randomized manner, with no blocking factors or multiple factors being varied simultaneously.

## bp972-3 If the standard deviation at 500 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹ is 1.8 µmol O₂ g⁻¹ leaf h⁻¹, what is the coefficient of variation (CV) expressed as a percentage?
Correct: B
CV = (SD / Mean) × 100 = (1.8 / 25.4) × 100 ≈ 7.1%, which is closest to 10% among the provided options.

## bp972-4 Which of the following statements is NOT supported by the data presented?
Correct: A
The data show a sigmoidal, not linear, increase; the rate plateaus after 250 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹ and does not continue rising to 750 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹.

## bp972-5 Which concept best links the observed saturation of photosynthesis to the underlying biochemical process?
Correct: D
Saturation occurs when the light‑dependent reactions produce more ATP and NADPH than the Calvin cycle can use; thus the limiting step is the carboxylation capacity of Rubisco.
