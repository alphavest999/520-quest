# BP1195

Researchers studied the effect of a novel antihypertensive drug on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults aged 40–65. The randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial enrolled 100 participants, 50 receiving the drug and 50 receiving placebo. Baseline characteristics were comparable between groups. After 12 weeks, mean SBP decreased by 12 mmHg in the drug group and by 4 mmHg in the placebo group. The study also recorded adverse events, with 6 participants in the drug group reporting mild headaches versus 2 in the placebo group. The authors concluded that the drug significantly lowers SBP with a tolerable safety profile.

## bp1195_q1 Based on Table 3, what is the absolute risk reduction (ARR) for headache events between the drug and placebo groups?
Correct: A
ARR = incidence in placebo – incidence in drug = (2/50) – (6/50) = 0.04 – 0.12 = -0.08, but absolute risk reduction is the absolute difference in event rates: 6% (drug) vs 2% (placebo) gives 4% reduction in risk.

## bp1195_q2 Which study design best describes this investigation?
Correct: C
Participants were randomly assigned to drug or placebo, and both groups were followed prospectively for 12 weeks, fitting the definition of a randomized controlled trial.

## bp1195_q3 What is the relative risk (RR) of headache in the drug group compared to placebo?
Correct: A
RR = incidence in drug / incidence in placebo = (6/50) / (2/50) = 0.12 / 0.04 = 3.00. However, since the drug group has higher incidence, RR >1. ## bp1195_q4 Which of the following statements is a potential trap in interpreting the study’s safety data?
Correct: B
The drug group actually had a higher incidence of headaches (12% vs 4%), so the relative risk is >1, not <1. This is a common misinterpretation trap.
