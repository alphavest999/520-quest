# BP970

Researchers studied the effect of three different dietary supplements on the recovery time of athletes after a standardized high-intensity exercise protocol. The study recruited 90 healthy adults aged 20–35, randomly assigning 30 participants to each of three groups: Group A received Supplement X, Group B received Supplement Y, and Group C received a placebo. All participants performed the same treadmill run until volitional fatigue, after which their recovery time was measured as the duration required to return to baseline heart rate and perceived exertion. Blood samples were taken pre- and post-exercise to assess markers of muscle damage (creatine kinase, CK) and inflammation (C-reactive protein, CRP). The table below summarizes the mean recovery times (in minutes) and the mean changes in CK and CRP levels for each group. The researchers hypothesized that Supplement X would reduce recovery time and inflammatory markers more effectively than Supplement Y or placebo.

## bp970_q1 According to the table, which group experienced the greatest reduction in C-reactive protein (CRP) levels after exercise?
Correct: A
The table shows ΔCRP values of 1.8 mg/L for Group A, 2.5 mg/L for Group B, and 3.9 mg/L for Group C. The smallest ΔCRP indicates the greatest reduction relative to baseline, so Group A had the greatest reduction.

## bp970_q2 Which experimental design element best controls for individual differences in baseline fitness levels?
Correct: A
Random assignment distributes individual differences such as baseline fitness evenly across groups, reducing bias. The other options address protocol standardization, baseline marker assessment, or nutrition but do not directly control for fitness variability.

## bp970_q3 If the total reduction in CK across all participants in Group A is 1,356 U/L, what is the average reduction per participant?
Correct: A
Average reduction per participant = total reduction ÷ number of participants = 1,356 ÷ 30 = 45.2 U/L, which matches the table’s ΔCK value for Group A.

## bp970_q4 Which of the following statements is a common misinterpretation of the study’s findings?
Correct: C
The table shows ΔCK values of 45.2, 58.9, and 72.4 U/L for Groups A, B, and C, respectively. Thus, the supplements did not reduce CK equally; the placebo actually had the greatest reduction, contrary to the statement.
