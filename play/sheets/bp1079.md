# BP1079

Researchers studied the effects of a novel dietary supplement on glucose metabolism in adult mice. The study employed a randomized controlled design with 60 male C57BL/6 mice divided into three groups: a control group receiving a standard diet, a low-dose supplement group receiving 50 mg/kg/day, and a high-dose supplement group receiving 200 mg/kg/day. Over 12 weeks, researchers measured fasting blood glucose, insulin tolerance, and body weight weekly. At the end of the study, glucose tolerance tests (GTT) were performed, and insulin signaling pathways were examined via Western blot analysis of the Akt pathway in liver tissue. The data suggested that the high-dose group exhibited a significant reduction in fasting blood glucose (mean 120 mg/dL vs 180 mg/dL in controls) and improved insulin sensitivity, while the low-dose group showed intermediate effects. However, the high-dose group also experienced a slight increase in liver weight, raising concerns about potential hepatic side effects. These findings highlight a dose-dependent response but also underscore the importance of monitoring organ-specific toxicity when evaluating new metabolic interventions.
