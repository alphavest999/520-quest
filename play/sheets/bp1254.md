# BP1254

Researchers at the Greenhouse Institute investigated how varying light intensity affects the growth of Arabidopsis thaliana seedlings. Over a 30‑day period, plants were grown under four controlled light regimes: Low (200 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹), Medium (400 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹), High (600 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹), and Very High (800 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹). Each condition had 10 replicates. The average stem height at the end of the experiment was recorded, along with standard deviations. The researchers hypothesized that moderate light would optimize growth, while excessive light might induce photoinhibition. The results showed a non‑linear response: the Medium intensity produced the tallest average height, while Very High intensity led to a slight reduction in height compared to High intensity. These findings suggest that Arabidopsis thaliana can tolerate a range of light intensities but has an optimal window for maximal growth. The data are summarized in Table 1 below.
