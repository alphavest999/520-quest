Researchers studied the effect of a novel dietary supplement on cognitive performance in 120 older adults (age 65–80). Participants were randomly assigned to either the supplement group (n = 60) or a placebo group (n = 60). After 12 weeks, all participants completed the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA). The mean MoCA scores and standard deviations for each group are presented in Table 1. The study aimed to determine whether the supplement could improve global cognition compared to placebo. The researchers also recorded adverse events and adherence rates, which were reported as percentages. The sample was stratified by baseline cognitive status to ensure comparable groups. The authors noted that the supplement contains a combination of omega‑3 fatty acids, phosphatidylserine, and B‑vitamins, hypothesizing synergistic neuroprotective effects.

## bp1449_q1 Based on Table 1, which group achieved the higher mean MoCA score?
Correct: A
The mean MoCA score for the supplement group is 26.4, which is higher than the placebo group’s mean of 25.7.

## bp1449_q2 Which study design would best establish a causal relationship between the supplement and improved cognition?
Correct: B
A randomized controlled trial (RCT) with double‑blinding controls for confounding variables and bias, providing the strongest evidence for causality.

Correct: B
Cohen’s d = (26.4 – 25.7) / √[(2.1² + 2.3²)/2] ≈ 0.70 / 1.55 ≈ 0.45.
