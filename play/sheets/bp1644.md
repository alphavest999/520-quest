# BP1644

Researchers at the National Sleep Research Institute conducted a randomized controlled study to examine the impact of acute sleep deprivation on executive function in healthy adults. Eighty participants (40 male, 40 female, ages 20–35) were randomly assigned to either a sleep‑deprivation group (n = 40) or a control group (n = 40). The deprivation group was restricted to 4 hours of sleep the night before testing, while the control group slept their usual 8 hours. All participants completed a battery of cognitive tasks the following morning, including the Stroop test, the N‑back working‑memory task, and a simple reaction‑time task. The primary outcome was mean reaction time (ms) on the Stroop task. Secondary outcomes included accuracy on the N‑back task and subjective sleepiness measured by the Karolinska Sleepiness Scale (KSS). The researchers hypothesized that sleep deprivation would significantly increase reaction time and decrease working‑memory accuracy, and that these effects would be more pronounced in participants with higher baseline KSS scores. Data were analyzed using mixed‑effects ANOVA, with group and time as fixed effects and subject as a random effect.
