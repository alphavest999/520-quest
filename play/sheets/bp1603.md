# BP1603

Researchers investigated the impact of sleep deprivation on cognitive performance in a controlled laboratory setting. Eight healthy adults (ages 20–35) were randomly assigned to either a 4‑hour sleep condition or a 8‑hour sleep control condition for one night. The next morning, all participants completed a computerized reaction‑time task (simple visual stimulus) and a working‑memory accuracy test. The study aimed to quantify how reduced sleep affects both speed and accuracy of cognitive processing. Data were recorded for each participant’s hours slept, mean reaction time (ms), and percentage accuracy. The researchers hypothesized that participants with fewer hours of sleep would exhibit slower reaction times and lower accuracy scores compared to the well‑rested group. The findings could inform guidelines for occupations requiring sustained attention, such as healthcare and transportation, and contribute to the broader understanding of sleep’s role in cognitive function.

Correct: A
The 8‑hour sleep condition serves as a baseline for normal sleep, allowing comparison with the 4‑hour deprivation group to isolate the effect of reduced sleep.

## q4 Which of the following is most likely a confounding variable in this study?
Correct: A
Caffeine intake could influence reaction time and accuracy independently of sleep duration, thus confounding the results.

## q5 Which concept is most relevant to interpreting the study’s results?
Correct: A
Cognitive load theory explains how limited cognitive resources are affected by sleep deprivation, directly relating to reaction time and accuracy outcomes.
