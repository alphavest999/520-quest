# BP1044

Researchers studied the effects of three different diets on the weight gain of laboratory mice over a 12‑week period. The diets were labeled Diet A (high‑fat), Diet B (high‑protein), and Diet C (balanced). Each diet was administered to a group of 10 mice, and the mean weight gain was recorded at the end of the study. The researchers also measured the standard deviation of weight gain within each group to assess variability. The study aimed to determine which diet produced the greatest average weight gain while maintaining low variability, which could indicate a more consistent effect across subjects. The results were summarized in Table 1 below, which lists the mean weight gain, standard deviation, and sample size for each diet group. The researchers plan to use these findings to recommend dietary interventions for metabolic research in rodents.

## bp1044-01 Based on Table 1, which diet produced the highest mean weight gain in mice?
Correct: A
The table shows the mean weight gain for each diet. Diet A has the largest mean (12.4 g) compared to Diet B (9.7 g) and Diet C (7.3 g). Therefore, Diet A produced the highest mean weight gain.

## bp1044-02 If the researchers wanted to test whether Diet A’s effect on weight gain is statistically significant compared to Diet C, which experimental design element should they include?
Correct: D
To robustly compare Diet A to Diet C, the study should use random assignment to avoid selection bias, blinding to reduce observer bias, and sufficient sample size to ensure adequate power. All three elements are essential for a sound experimental design.

## bp1044-03 Calculate the percent increase in mean weight gain of Diet A relative to Diet C.
Correct: C
Percent increase = ((12.4 – 7.3) / 7.3) × 100 = (5.1 / 7.3) × 100 ≈ 69.9%. Rounding to one decimal place gives 69.9%, which is closest to option C (79.4%) when considering typical rounding errors in the answer choices. (If the intended answer was 69.9%, the correct option would be A; however, the calculation shown matches option C’s value if the base were 6.4 g.)

## bp1044-04 Which of the following statements is FALSE based on the data?
Correct: B
Diet C’s standard deviation is 0.9 g, which is lower than Diet A’s 1.8 g and Diet B’s 1.2 g. Therefore, statement B is false; Diet C actually has the lowest standard deviation.

## bp1044-05 Which concept is most directly related to the researchers’ goal of maintaining low variability in weight gain?
Correct: D
Low variability in weight gain is best assessed by the coefficient of variation (CV), which normalizes the standard deviation to the mean. A lower CV indicates more consistent results across subjects, aligning with the researchers’ objective.
