# BP1273

Researchers studied the effect of a novel dietary supplement on cognitive performance in adults aged 30-50. The study was a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial conducted over 12 weeks. Participants were randomly assigned to one of four groups: (1) high-dose supplement, (2) low-dose supplement, (3) placebo, and (4) active control (standard cognitive enhancer). Cognitive performance was assessed using a battery of neuropsychological tests, including the Digit Symbol Substitution Test (DSST), Stroop Color-Word Test, and Working Memory Span. Baseline scores were comparable across groups. At week 12, mean DSST scores increased by 12.5 points in the high-dose group, 7.8 points in the low-dose group, 2.1 points in the placebo group, and 9.3 points in the active control group. Standard deviations ranged from 4.2 to 6.1. The authors concluded that the high-dose supplement significantly improved processing speed and working memory relative to placebo, with effects comparable to the active control. They suggested further studies to evaluate long‑term safety and efficacy.
