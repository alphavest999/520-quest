# BP1139

Researchers conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive agent, Hypertensol, in adults with stage 1 hypertension. Two hundred participants were enrolled and randomly assigned to either Hypertensol (n=100) or placebo (n=100). Baseline systolic blood pressure (SBP) averaged 145 mmHg in both groups. After 12 weeks of daily dosing, the Hypertensol group exhibited a mean SBP of 123 mmHg, whereas the placebo group averaged 140 mmHg. The study also recorded diastolic BP, heart rate, and adverse events. No serious adverse events were reported. The investigators concluded that Hypertensol significantly lowered SBP compared to placebo, with a mean reduction of 22 mmHg. The authors suggested that Hypertensol could be a viable alternative to existing therapies, pending further long‑term safety studies.

## bp1139_q1 Which of the following best describes the change in systolic blood pressure in the Hypertensol group?
Correct: B
The Hypertensol group’s mean SBP dropped from 145 to 123 mmHg, a decrease of 22 mmHg.

## bp1139_q2 Which of the following design features would most strengthen causal inference in this study?
Correct: D
Double‑blinding minimizes bias from participants and investigators, thereby strengthening causal inference.

## bp1139_q3 What percent reduction in systolic blood pressure did Hypertensol produce relative to baseline?
Correct: A
Percent reduction = (145‑123)/145 × 100 ≈ 15.2%, which rounds to 15%.

## bp1139_q4 Which of the following statements is a potential trap in interpreting the study results?
Correct: B
The study was described as randomized and double‑blind; claiming it was not blinded misleads readers.

## bp1139_q5 Which concept is most directly related to the study’s primary outcome?
Correct: C
The study’s design and outcome measurement are classic examples of a randomized controlled trial.
