Blood samples were collected at 0.5, 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, and 24 hours post‑dose. Plasma concentrations were quantified by LC‑MS/MS. The study aimed to compare the area under the concentration‑time curve (AUC), maximum concentration (Cmax), and half‑life (t½) of the two compounds. ## 1 Based on the data presented in Table 1, which of the following statements best describes the pharmacokinetic profile of Compound X relative to Compound Y?
Correct: C
The peak concentration (Cmax) for Compound X is 45.2 mg/L at 4 h, whereas Compound Y peaks at 48.1 mg/L at the same time point, so Cmax is slightly lower for Compound X. However, the concentration of Compound X remains above 30 mg/L for 8 h, while Compound Y falls below 30 mg/L after 6 h, indicating a longer half‑life for Compound X. Thus, Compound X has a longer half‑life but a slightly lower Cmax compared to Compound Y.

## 2 If the researchers wanted to design a new study to compare the efficacy of Compound X and Compound Y in a chronic infection model, which experimental design element would be most critical to include?
Correct: C
To assess efficacy in a chronic infection, the study must include infected subjects and a parallel‑group design to compare the therapeutic effects of each compound under clinically relevant conditions. Sample size and washout period are important but secondary to the need for an infection model.

## 3 Using the trapezoidal rule, estimate the AUC from 0 to 24 hours for Compound X. Which of the following values is closest to the calculated AUC?
Correct: B
