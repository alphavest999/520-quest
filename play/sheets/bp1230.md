# BP1230

Researchers investigated the effect of varying concentrations of the inhibitor X on the activity of enzyme Y. The enzyme activity was quantified by measuring the absorbance at 340 nm, which correlates with product formation. Five concentrations of inhibitor X (0, 5, 10, 20, and 40 µM) were tested in triplicate. The results are summarized in Table 1 below. The data show a clear dose‑dependent decrease in absorbance, suggesting inhibition of enzyme Y. The researchers noted that at concentrations above 20 µM, the absorbance values plateaued, indicating possible saturation of the inhibitory effect. The study aims to determine the IC50 value of inhibitor X and to evaluate whether the inhibition follows a competitive or non‑competitive mechanism.

## bp1230_q1 Based on Table 1, which concentration of inhibitor X results in the greatest reduction in enzyme activity compared to the control?
Correct: C
The absorbance at 20 µM (0.70) is the lowest among the tested concentrations, indicating the greatest reduction in enzyme activity relative to the control (1.25).

## bp1230_q2 Which experimental control is essential to determine whether the observed decrease in absorbance is due to inhibitor X and not to a change in enzyme concentration?
Correct: B
An enzyme-only sample (no inhibitor) provides a baseline activity level, allowing comparison to samples containing inhibitor X to attribute changes specifically to the inhibitor.

## bp1230_q3 Calculate the percent inhibition of enzyme Y at 10 µM inhibitor X relative to the control.
Correct: B
Percent inhibition = [(1.25 - 0.95) / 1.25] × 100 = 24%/1.25 × 100 = 30%.

## bp1230_q4 Which of the following statements is a common misconception about interpreting the plateau observed at 40 µM?
Correct: A
The plateau at 40 µM does not mean complete inhibition; absorbance remains at ~0.68, indicating residual activity. Assuming complete inhibition is a trap.

## bp1230_q5 Which broader pharmacological concept does this study exemplify?
Correct: C
The study demonstrates a classic dose–response curve, where increasing inhibitor concentration leads to progressively greater inhibition of enzyme activity.
