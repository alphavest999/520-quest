Researchers studied the effect of a new dietary supplement on blood glucose levels in adults with prediabetes. In a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial, 120 participants were assigned to either the supplement group (n = 60) or placebo group (n = 60) for 12 weeks. Baseline fasting glucose levels were recorded, followed by weekly measurements. Table 1 shows the mean fasting glucose (mg/dL) and standard deviation at baseline, week 6, and week 12 for both groups. The supplement group showed a mean reduction of 12 mg/dL at week 12, whereas the placebo group had a mean reduction of 3 mg/dL. Secondary outcomes included HbA1c, weight, and adherence rates. Adherence was high (>90 %) in both groups. The authors concluded that the supplement significantly improved glycemic control compared to placebo. The study’s strengths include randomization, blinding, and high adherence, but limitations such as short duration and the use of a single supplement dose were noted.
