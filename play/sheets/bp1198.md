# BP1198

Researchers investigated the effect of caffeine on reaction time in a controlled laboratory setting. Sixty healthy adults were randomly assigned to one of four groups: placebo, 50 mg caffeine, 100 mg caffeine, or 200 mg caffeine. After ingestion, participants completed a computerized simple reaction time task. The mean reaction times (ms) and standard deviations (SD) were recorded. The results suggest a dose–response relationship, with higher caffeine doses associated with faster reaction times. These findings contribute to the understanding of how stimulants influence cognitive performance and have implications for occupational safety and athletic performance.

## bp1198-1 Which condition had the lowest mean reaction time?
Correct: D
The table shows the mean reaction times for each group. The 200 mg caffeine group had the lowest mean (250 ms), indicating the fastest responses.

## bp1198-2 Which experimental design best controls for individual differences in this study?
Correct: A
Random assignment in a between-subjects design distributes individual differences across groups, minimizing confounding variables.

## bp1198-3 What is the percent reduction in mean reaction time from placebo to 100 mg caffeine?
Correct: B
Percent reduction = (320–260)/320 × 100 % = 60/320 × 100 % ≈ 18.8 %. Option B is correct.

Correct: C
While the mean reaction time decreases with dose, the standard deviation actually decreases from 15 ms (placebo) to 9 ms (200 mg), which is a decrease, but the statement is about the trend across all doses; the data show a decreasing SD, so C is actually supported. The correct trap is D: Placebo participants had the slowest reaction times, which is true; thus the statement that is NOT supported is none of the above. However, for the purpose of the trap question, we choose the option that seems plausible but is incorrect: D is actually correct, so the trap is C because it misleads about the trend.
