# BP1734

Researchers studied the effect of a novel compound X on the proliferation of cultured human fibroblasts. They cultured cells in 96‑well plates, treating them with varying concentrations of X (0, 0.1, 0.5, 1, 5 µM) for 48 hours. Cell viability was assessed using an MTT assay, and absorbance at 570 nm was recorded. The data are summarized in Table 1. The authors reported a dose‑dependent decrease in viability, with the lowest viability at 5 µM. They also measured reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels using a fluorescent probe, and found increased ROS at concentrations above 1 µM. Statistical analysis was performed using one‑way ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post‑hoc test. The study concluded that compound X induces cytotoxicity via oxidative stress.

## bp1734-1 Which concentration shows a statistically significant decrease in viability compared to the control?
Correct: D
Only the 5 µM concentration has a p‑value (0.001) indicating a significant difference from the 0 µM control.

## bp1734-2 Which statement best describes the control used in this experiment?
Correct: B
The 0 µM concentration (no compound X) serves as the negative control, providing a baseline for comparison.

## bp1734-3 What is the percent decrease in viability at 5 µM relative to the control?
Correct: B
Percent decrease = (100 % – 45 %) = 55 %. This is the correct calculation.

## bp1734-4 Which of the following is a potential confounding factor in this study?
Correct: A
Different passage numbers can affect cell proliferation and sensitivity to compounds, thereby confounding the results.

## bp1734-5 Which of the following statements best links the observed increase in ROS to the cytotoxic effect?
Correct: B
If ROS mediates cytotoxicity, scavenging ROS should mitigate the decrease in viability, thereby increasing cell survival.
