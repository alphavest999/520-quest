# BP1474

Researchers studied the effect of light intensity on the photosynthetic performance of *Arabidopsis thaliana* under controlled laboratory conditions. The experiment involved exposing 6‑week‑old plants to three different light intensities—200, 400, and 600 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹—using LED panels. Gas exchange measurements were taken with a portable photosynthesis system, recording CO₂ uptake, O₂ production, and net photosynthetic rate (µmol CO₂ m⁻² s⁻¹). The data were collected after a 30‑minute acclimation period at each light level. Results indicated a linear increase in CO₂ uptake and O₂ production up to 400 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹, after which the net photosynthetic rate plateaued, suggesting a light saturation point. The researchers hypothesized that the limiting factor at higher intensities was the Calvin cycle’s enzymatic capacity rather than photon availability. This study provides insight into optimizing light regimes for maximal biomass accumulation in controlled‑environment agriculture.

## bp1474-1 Which light intensity produced the highest net photosynthetic rate?
Correct: C
The table shows that the net photosynthetic rate increases from 4 at 200 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ to 8 at 400 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ and then to 9 at 600 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹. Thus, 600 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ yields the highest rate.

## bp1474-2 Which of the following best describes a control condition for this experiment?
Correct: B
A dark control (0 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹) provides a baseline for respiration and ensures that measured CO₂ uptake is due to photosynthesis, not other metabolic processes.

## bp1474-3 Calculate the average increase in net photosynthetic rate per 200 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ increase in light intensity between 200 and 600 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹.
Correct: C
Net rate increases from 4 to 9 over 400 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ (200→400→600). The total increase is 5 µmol over two 200‑unit steps, so 5/2 = 2.5 µmol per 200 µmol. Correct: A
