# BP1761

Researchers at the University of Midvale investigated the impact of caffeine on short‑term memory recall among college students. Eighty participants were randomly assigned to one of four groups: (1) a control group received a placebo beverage; (2) a low‑caffeine group received 50 mg of caffeine; (3) a moderate‑caffeine group received 150 mg; and (4) a high‑caffeine group received 300 mg. All participants were given a 20‑word list to memorize, and after a 30‑minute delay, they were asked to recall as many words as possible. The study aimed to determine whether caffeine dosage correlates with recall performance and whether there is a threshold beyond which additional caffeine yields diminishing returns. The researchers controlled for variables such as prior caffeine consumption, sleep quality, and baseline memory ability by administering questionnaires before the experiment.
