# BP1049

Researchers at the University of Northbridge investigated the effect of different auditory stimuli on reaction time in a visual task. Participants were randomly assigned to one of four groups: a control group exposed to silence, and three experimental groups exposed to (A) a low‑frequency tone, (B) a high‑frequency tone, and (C) white noise. Each participant performed 50 trials, during which they pressed a button as soon as a target stimulus appeared on a screen. The researchers recorded the mean reaction time (RT) and standard deviation (SD) for each group. The study aimed to determine whether specific auditory cues could improve or impair visual processing speed, potentially informing design of alert systems in high‑stakes environments such as aviation and medicine. The data were analyzed using a one‑way ANOVA to assess differences among groups, followed by post‑hoc Tukey tests for pairwise comparisons. Results suggested that the high‑frequency tone group had a significantly faster mean RT than the control, while the low‑frequency tone and white noise groups did not differ from control. The authors concluded that certain auditory frequencies may enhance visual attention, but further research is needed to generalize findings across populations.
