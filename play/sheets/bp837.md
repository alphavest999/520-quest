# BP837

Researchers at the University of Metropolis investigated how ionic strength influences the acid–base behavior of the basic amino acid lysine. Using a 1H NMR titration protocol, they measured the protonation equilibria of lysine in buffer solutions containing 0.01 M, 0.1 M, and 1.0 M NaCl. The experiment monitored the chemical shift of the ε‑amino protons as the pH was incrementally lowered from 9.5 to 2.0. The resulting titration curves were fitted to a Henderson–Hasselbalch equation to extract the apparent pKa values for the side‑chain amino group. In parallel, the researchers recorded the pKa of the α‑carboxyl and α‑amino groups under the same ionic‑strength conditions. The data are summarized in Table 1 below. The authors noted that increasing ionic strength generally stabilizes the charged species, thereby shifting the pKa of the ε‑amino group to higher values. These findings have implications for protein folding studies where salt concentrations vary.

| Amino Acid | Side‑chain pKa | α‑Carboxyl pKa | α‑Amino pKa |
|------------|----------------|----------------|--------------|
| Lysine     | 10.53          | 2.18           | 9.06         |
| Aspartic Acid | 3.65          | 1.88           | 9.60         |
| Glutamic Acid | 4.25          | 2.19           | 9.47         |
| Phenylalanine | –            | 1.83           | 9.24         |

## bp837-1 Which ionic strength condition yielded the highest pKa for lysine’s ε‑amino group?
Correct: C
Analogy 10‑yr: Think of the ε‑amino group as a ball that likes to stay on a hill. More salt (higher ionic strength) makes the hill steeper, so the ball stays higher (higher pKa). Official: Higher ionic strength stabilizes the charged form, shifting pKa up. Why wrong: If you chose a lower ionic strength, you’d miss the stabilizing effect and incorrectly think the pKa is lower.

## bp837-2 If the temperature during the titration were increased, what would be the expected effect on the ε‑amino pKa of lysine?
Correct: B
Analogy 10‑yr: Imagine the ε‑amino group as a balloon that expands with heat. Higher temperature weakens the attraction to protons, so the pKa drops. Official: Temperature increases the equilibrium constant for deprotonation, lowering pKa for basic groups. Why wrong: Choosing a higher pKa would ignore the endothermic nature of protonation for bases.

## bp837-3 Calculate the isoelectric point (pI) of lysine using the pKa values in Table 1.
Correct: B
Analogy 10‑yr: Picture lysine as a seesaw; the pI is where the seesaw balances. Official: pI = (pKa of α‑amino + pKa of ε‑amino)/2 = (9.06+10.53)/2 ≈ 9.8, closest to 9.6. Why wrong: Picking 10.1 or 10.5 overestimates the balance point because it ignores the lower α‑amino pKa.
