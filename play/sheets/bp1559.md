# BP1559

Researchers at the University of Midvale conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive agent, Hypertensol, in adults with stage 1 hypertension. Eighty participants were assigned to either Hypertensol (n=40) or placebo (n=40). Baseline systolic blood pressure (SBP) was measured after a 5‑minute seated rest, and follow‑up SBP was recorded at 4 weeks. The study aimed to determine whether Hypertensol could reduce SBP by at least 10 mmHg more than placebo. In addition to SBP, diastolic BP (DBP) and pulse rate were recorded, and adverse events were monitored. The investigators hypothesized that Hypertensol would produce a statistically significant mean reduction in SBP compared to placebo, with a 95 % confidence interval that did not cross zero. The trial was registered under ClinicalTrials.gov identifier NCT04567890. The data presented below summarize the key findings for the primary endpoint.
