# BP1605

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel dietary supplement on insulin sensitivity in adults with prediabetes. A randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial enrolled 120 participants (age 45–65, BMI 25–35 kg/m²). Subjects were randomized to receive either the supplement (n=60) or a matched placebo (n=60) for 12 weeks. Baseline assessments included fasting glucose, fasting insulin, HOMA‑IR, and oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT). After 12 weeks, the supplement group exhibited a mean reduction in HOMA‑IR of 0.8 units (95% CI 0.5–1.1), whereas the placebo group showed a non‑significant change of 0.1 units (95% CI –0.2 to 0.4). OGTT results revealed a 15% greater decline in 2‑hour glucose levels in the supplement group compared to placebo (p=0.003). Adverse events were mild and comparable between groups. The investigators concluded that the supplement improves insulin sensitivity in prediabetic adults, potentially delaying progression to type 2 diabetes.
