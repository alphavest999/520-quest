# BP1542

Researchers at XYZ University conducted a randomized controlled trial to investigate the impact of caffeine intake on cognitive performance, specifically reaction time in a simple visual task. Twenty healthy adults were recruited and randomly assigned to one of five groups, each receiving a different caffeine dose (0, 50, 100, 150, or 200 mg). After a 30‑minute absorption period, participants performed a computerized go‑no‑go task, and their reaction times (RT) were recorded. The study aimed to determine whether higher caffeine doses correlate with faster RTs and to assess the dose–response relationship. The researchers also monitored heart rate and subjective alertness to control for potential confounding variables. The data were analyzed using ANOVA to compare mean RTs across dose groups and regression to model the dose–response curve. Findings suggested a modest but statistically significant improvement in RT at 100 mg, with diminishing returns at higher doses. These results contribute to understanding how caffeine modulates neural processing speed and inform guidelines for optimal dosing in cognitive tasks.

## bp1542-02 Which experimental design best isolates the effect of caffeine on reaction time while controlling for individual differences?
Correct: B
A randomized controlled trial with a placebo group controls for individual differences and placebo effects, allowing causal inference about caffeine’s impact.

## bp1542-03 Calculate the overall mean reaction time across all participants.
Correct: B
Sum of mean RTs = 350+335+320+322+330 = 1657 ms. Divide by 5 participants = 331.4 ms, rounded to 332 ms.

## bp1542-05 The improvement in reaction time at 100 mg of caffeine is most closely linked to which neurotransmitter system?
Correct: C
Caffeine blocks adenosine receptors, leading to increased dopamine release, which enhances alertness and reaction speed.
