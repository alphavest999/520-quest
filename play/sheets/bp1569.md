# BP1569

Researchers conducted a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive agent, Hypertensol, in adults with stage 1 hypertension. Eighty patients were enrolled and randomly assigned to one of three groups: placebo (n = 27), low‑dose Hypertensol (5 mg daily, n = 27), or high‑dose Hypertensol (10 mg daily, n = 26). Systolic blood pressure (SBP) was measured at baseline and after 4 weeks of therapy. The investigators recorded the mean SBP and standard deviation for each group. The study aimed to determine whether Hypertensol could produce a clinically significant reduction in SBP compared to placebo and to assess dose‑response relationships. The trial was registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT01234567) and received institutional review board approval. Data were analyzed using ANOVA followed by post‑hoc Tukey tests. The results suggested a dose‑dependent decrease in SBP, with the high‑dose group exhibiting the largest mean reduction. No serious adverse events were reported. The authors concluded that Hypertensol may be a promising therapeutic option for patients with mild hypertension.

The high‑dose group’s SBP decreased from 140.3 to 123.7 mmHg, a reduction of 16.6 mmHg, which is larger than the reductions seen in the placebo (1.3 mmHg) and low‑dose (7.7 mmHg) groups.

Correct: C

Correct: A
Percent reduction = (Baseline – 4‑week)/Baseline × 100 = (140.3 – 123.7)/140.3 × 100 ≈ 11.8%.
