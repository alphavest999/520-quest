# BP1161

Researchers studied the effect of a novel dietary supplement on exercise performance in healthy adults. In a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial, 120 participants were assigned to either the supplement group (n=60) or the placebo group (n=60). Over 8 weeks, participants performed a standardized treadmill test at baseline and after the intervention. The primary outcome was peak VO2 (ml·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹). Table 1 summarizes the mean peak VO2 values, standard deviations, and sample sizes for each group at both time points. The supplement group showed a mean increase of 2.5 ml·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹ (SD 1.8) compared to a 0.8 ml·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹ (SD 1.5) increase in the placebo group. Secondary outcomes included time to exhaustion and lactate threshold, which also favored the supplement group but were not statistically significant. The authors concluded that the supplement may enhance aerobic capacity, though larger studies are needed to confirm efficacy and safety.
