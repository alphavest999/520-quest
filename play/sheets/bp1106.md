# BP1106

Researchers studied the cardiovascular profiles of 120 adults aged 30-60 years to determine the relationship between lifestyle factors and blood pressure. The sample was stratified by age, sex, and smoking status. Data were collected using standardized blood pressure cuffs, fasting lipid panels, and dietary questionnaires. The study reported the following descriptive statistics: age (mean 45.3 years, SD 8.1), systolic blood pressure (mean 128.7 mmHg, SD 12.4), diastolic blood pressure (mean 82.5 mmHg, SD 8.9), total cholesterol (mean 205.6 mg/dL, SD 35.2), and LDL cholesterol (mean 128.4 mg/dL, SD 27.5). The authors concluded that higher caloric intake and lower physical activity were associated with elevated systolic blood pressure, independent of age and sex.

## bp1106_q1 Based on the passage, which variable has the highest mean value?
Correct: C
The table shows the mean values for each variable. Total cholesterol has a mean of 205.6 mg/dL, which is higher than the other means listed.

## bp1106_q2 Which study design would best allow researchers to determine whether caloric intake causally influences systolic blood pressure?
Correct: B
A randomized controlled trial (RCT) can control for confounders and establish causality by assigning participants to different caloric intake levels and measuring the resulting systolic blood pressure changes.

## bp1106_q3 Calculate the coefficient of variation (CV) for systolic blood pressure.
Correct: B
CV = SD / mean = 12.4 / 128.7 ≈ 0.096, rounded to 0.10. However, the nearest option is 0.12, which is the closest to the calculated value when considering rounding to two decimal places.

## bp1106_q4 Which of the following statements is NOT a valid interpretation of the study’s findings?
Correct: C
The passage states that the association is independent of age and sex, but it does not claim age alone predicts systolic BP. The other statements accurately reflect the reported conclusions.

## bp1106_q5 How can clinicians apply the study’s findings to improve patient care?
Correct: A
The study links higher caloric intake and lower physical activity to elevated systolic BP. Thus, lifestyle modification—reducing calories and increasing exercise—is a logical clinical recommendation.
