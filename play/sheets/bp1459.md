# BP1459

Researchers studied the effect of a novel antihypertensive drug on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults with mild hypertension. Sixty participants were randomized to receive either the drug or a placebo for six weeks. Baseline SBP was measured for all participants, followed by a repeat measurement after the intervention period. The study aimed to assess the mean change in SBP and determine whether the drug produced a clinically meaningful reduction compared to placebo. The results are summarized in Table 1 below.

## bp1459_q1 Which group experienced a greater reduction in systolic blood pressure?
Correct: B
The drug group’s mean SBP decreased from 140 to 120 mmHg, a reduction of 20 mmHg, whereas the placebo group’s reduction was only 2 mmHg. Thus, the drug group had a greater reduction.

## bp1459_q2 Which design element most effectively minimizes bias in this study?
Correct: A
Randomization ensures that known and unknown confounding factors are evenly distributed between groups, reducing selection bias. While blinding also reduces bias, the question asks for the most effective single element, and randomization is the foundational design choice.

## bp1459_q3 What is the percent reduction in systolic blood pressure for the drug group?
Correct: C
Percent reduction = (Baseline – 6‑Week)/Baseline × 100 = (140–120)/140 × 100 = 20/140 × 100 ≈ 14.3%. The closest option is 14%.

## bp1459_q4 Which statement is a trap based on the data presented?
Correct: D
Both groups had identical baseline SBP values (140 mmHg). Statement D incorrectly asserts a higher baseline for the drug group, making it a trap.

## bp1459_q5 Which statement best links the study’s findings to clinical practice?
Correct: A
The data demonstrate a 20‑mmHg mean reduction in SBP for the drug group versus a 2‑mmHg reduction for placebo, indicating clinical efficacy for mild hypertension.
