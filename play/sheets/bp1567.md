# BP1567

Researchers studied the effect of different concentrations of a novel drug on the proliferation of cultured human fibroblasts. They used a 96‑well plate, seeding 5,000 cells per well, and treated cells with drug concentrations of 0, 0.5, 1.0, 2.5, and 5.0 µM for 72 hours. Cell viability was assessed by MTT assay, and absorbance at 570 nm was recorded. The results were expressed as percent viability relative to the untreated control. The data are summarized in Table 1. The researchers hypothesized that the drug would inhibit cell proliferation in a dose‑dependent manner. They also planned to evaluate the effect of a second compound, an antioxidant, by adding it to the highest drug concentration to assess potential rescue of viability. The study was conducted in triplicate, and the mean ± SD was reported. The investigators noted that the lowest drug concentration did not significantly differ from control (p > 0.05), whereas the 5 µM concentration reduced viability to 35 % (p < 0.01). They concluded that the drug has potent antiproliferative activity at higher concentrations.
