# BP1485

Researchers at the University of Health Sciences conducted a double-blind, placebo-controlled study to evaluate the antihypertensive effects of a novel compound, Hypertol, across a range of doses. Fifty healthy volunteers were randomly assigned to receive either a placebo or Hypertol at 10, 20, or 30 mg daily for four weeks. Systolic blood pressure (SBP) was measured at baseline and after the treatment period. The results, summarized in Table 1, show a clear dose-dependent decrease in mean SBP. The placebo group (0 mg) had a mean SBP of 120 mmHg (SD 5). At 10 mg, the mean SBP dropped to 115 mmHg (SD 4.5). The 20 mg dose further reduced SBP to 110 mmHg (SD 4), and the 30 mg dose achieved a mean SBP of 105 mmHg (SD 3.5). All groups had equal sample sizes of 50 participants. These findings suggest that Hypertol effectively lowers SBP in a dose-responsive manner, supporting its potential as a new antihypertensive agent.

Dose (mg) | Mean Systolic BP (mmHg) | SD | Sample Size
0 | 120 | 5 | 50
10 | 115 | 4.5 | 50
20 | 110 | 4 | 50
30 | 105 | 3.5 | 50

## bp1485-1 What is the mean systolic blood pressure observed in participants receiving 20 mg of Hypertol?
Correct: C
The table lists the mean SBP for each dose. For the 20 mg dose, the mean SBP is 110 mmHg.

## bp1485-2 Which study design best describes the Hypertol trial?
Correct: A
Participants were randomly assigned to treatment groups and the study was double‑blind and placebo‑controlled, which defines a randomized controlled trial.

## bp1485-3 What is the percent reduction in mean SBP from placebo to the 30 mg dose?
Correct: C
Percent reduction = ((120 - 105) / 120) × 100 = 15%. The 30 mg dose lowered SBP by 15% relative to placebo.

## bp1485-4 Which dose had the largest standard deviation?
Correct: A
The standard deviation values are 5, 4.5, 4, and 3.5 for 0, 10, 20, and 30 mg respectively. The largest SD is 5 at the placebo (0 mg) dose.

## bp1485-5 Which physiological mechanism best explains the dose‑dependent reduction in SBP observed with Hypertol?
Correct: A
Hypertol is designed to inhibit the renin‑angiotensin system, a key regulator of blood pressure. The dose‑dependent BP reduction aligns with the pharmacodynamic profile of an RAAS inhibitor.
