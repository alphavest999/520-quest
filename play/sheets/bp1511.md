# BP1511

Researchers studied the effect of a novel antihypertensive drug, Hypertensol, on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults with stage 1 hypertension. In a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial, 120 participants were assigned to either Hypertensol (n = 60) or placebo (n = 60) and followed for 12 weeks. Baseline characteristics were similar across groups (mean age = 52 ± 9 years; mean baseline SBP = 140 ± 8 mm Hg). At week 12, the Hypertensol group exhibited a mean SBP reduction of 12.4 mm Hg (SD = 4.6), whereas the placebo group showed a reduction of 4.1 mm Hg (SD = 5.2). The difference in mean change between groups was statistically significant (p < 0.001). Adverse events were mild and comparable between groups. The authors concluded that Hypertensol provides a clinically meaningful SBP reduction with a favorable safety profile.

## bp1511-02 Which experimental design feature is most critical for ensuring that the observed SBP reduction is attributable to Hypertensol?
Correct: B
Randomization balances known and unknown confounders between groups, making it the most essential feature for attributing effects to the intervention.

Correct: C
Mean change Hypertensol = -12.4 mmHg; Placebo = -4.1 mmHg. Difference = (-12.4) - (-4.1) = -8.3 mmHg, meaning Hypertensol reduced SBP 8.3 mmHg more than placebo. The absolute value is 8.3 mmHg.

## bp1511-04 Which of the following is a common statistical trap when interpreting the trial’s results?
Correct: A
A p‑value < 0.001 shows statistical significance but does not automatically imply the effect is clinically meaningful. Clinical relevance depends on effect size and patient context.

## bp1511-05 Which concept is most closely linked to the trial’s use of a placebo group?
Correct: C
A placebo group serves as a control, allowing comparison of the intervention’s effect against no active treatment.
