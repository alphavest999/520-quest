# BP1557

Researchers conducted a randomized controlled trial to assess the efficacy of two novel cognitive enhancers on reaction time in healthy adults. One hundred sixty participants were randomly assigned to one of four groups: a control group receiving a placebo, Treatment A (a selective NMDA antagonist), Treatment B (a dopamine agonist), or a combined therapy group receiving both agents. Over a four‑week period, participants completed a standardized visual‑motor reaction time task twice weekly. Mean reaction times (in milliseconds) and standard deviations were recorded at baseline and after the intervention. The data revealed that both Treatment A and Treatment B produced modest reductions in mean reaction time compared with placebo, while the combined therapy yielded the greatest improvement. However, the combined group also exhibited the largest variability in response times. These findings suggest that while each agent individually enhances cognitive speed, their combination may introduce unpredictable effects that could limit clinical utility. Further investigation is warranted to delineate optimal dosing strategies and to assess long‑term safety.

Group | Mean RT (ms) | SD
Control | 400 | 30
Treatment A | 350 | 50
Treatment B | 360 | 45
Combined | 320 | 70

## Q1 Which group had the lowest mean response time?
Correct: D
The combined therapy group had the lowest mean reaction time of 320 ms, which is lower than the means for Control (400 ms), Treatment A (350 ms), and Treatment B (360 ms).

## Q2 Which of the following best describes the study design?
Correct: A
Participants were randomly assigned to treatment groups, which is the hallmark of a randomized controlled trial.

## Q3 If the mean response time for Treatment A is 350 ms and the standard deviation is 50 ms, what is the coefficient of variation?
Correct: A
Coefficient of variation = SD / mean = 50 ms / 350 ms ≈ 0.1429, which rounds to 0.143.

## Q4 Which group had the highest standard deviation?
Correct: D
The combined therapy group had an SD of 70 ms, which is higher than Control (30 ms), Treatment A (50 ms), and Treatment B (45 ms).

## Q5 Which of the following statements best links the study findings to potential clinical application?
Correct: A
Treatment A produced a modest but statistically meaningful reduction in reaction time, implying potential benefits for clinical populations requiring faster cognitive processing.
