Researchers investigated the antihypertensive efficacy of a novel compound, Hypertensol, in a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial. Eighty adults (age 45‑68) with stage 1 hypertension were enrolled and assigned to one of three arms: placebo (n = 27), low‑dose Hypertensol (5 mg daily, n = 27), or high‑dose Hypertensol (10 mg daily, n = 26). Baseline characteristics were comparable across groups (mean age 56 ± 7 years; baseline systolic BP 148 ± 9 mmHg). Blood pressure was measured using an automated cuff at baseline and after 28 days of therapy. The primary endpoint was the mean change in systolic BP from baseline to week 4. Table 1 summarizes the results. The low‑dose group showed a modest reduction (− 6 mmHg, p = 0.08), whereas the high‑dose group exhibited a statistically significant decrease (− 12 mmHg, p < 0.001). The placebo group had a negligible change (+ 1 mmHg). These findings suggest a dose‑dependent antihypertensive effect of Hypertensol, warranting further investigation in larger, longer‑term studies.
