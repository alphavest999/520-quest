# BP1666

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel anti‑inflammatory compound on leukocyte migration in a murine model of acute peritonitis. Male C57BL/6 mice were randomly assigned to four treatment groups receiving either vehicle (0 mg kg⁻¹) or escalating doses of the compound (10, 20, 30, or 40 mg kg⁻¹). Inflammation was quantified by counting polymorphonuclear neutrophils (PMNs) in peritoneal lavage fluid at 24 h post‑induction. The study employed a double‑blind, randomized controlled design with 10 animals per group. Data were recorded at days 0 (baseline), 1, 2, 3, and 4 following drug administration. The table below summarizes the mean percentage inhibition of PMN migration relative to the vehicle group, along with standard deviation values. The results demonstrate a clear dose‑dependent suppression of leukocyte infiltration, with maximal inhibition observed at the highest dose. These findings suggest the compound’s potential as a therapeutic agent for inflammatory disorders. Future studies will assess long‑term safety and pharmacokinetics.
