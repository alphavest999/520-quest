# BP998

Researchers conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive drug in reducing systolic blood pressure (SBP) among adults with stage‑1 hypertension. Four hundred participants were enrolled and stratified into four age groups: 30–39, 40–49, 50–59, and 60–69 years. Baseline SBP values were recorded, followed by a 12‑week treatment period during which the drug was administered daily. Post‑treatment SBP was measured at week 12. The results, summarized in Table 1, show that all age groups experienced a reduction in SBP. The magnitude of the reduction varied across age groups, with younger participants showing a slightly higher percentage decline. Baseline SBP increased with age, but the absolute reduction in SBP was remarkably similar across the groups, ranging from 18 to 20 mmHg. These findings suggest that the drug’s antihypertensive effect is consistent across age ranges, though the relative benefit appears greater in younger adults.

## bp998-1 Which age group experienced the greatest absolute reduction in systolic blood pressure?
Correct: A
Absolute reduction is calculated as Baseline SBP minus Post‑treatment SBP. For the 30–39 group, the reduction is 150 – 130 = 20 mmHg, which is the largest among all groups.

## bp998-2 Which study design would best determine whether the drug’s effect differs by age group?
Correct: A
A randomized controlled trial that stratifies participants by age ensures balanced allocation within each age group and allows direct comparison of drug effects across ages.

Absolute reductions are 20 mmHg for 30–39, 18 mmHg for 40–49, 20 mmHg for 50–59, and 20 mmHg for 60–69. Thus, the absolute reduction is not higher in older patients; it is similar or slightly lower in the 40–49 group.
