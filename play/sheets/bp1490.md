# BP1490

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel antioxidant compound, X-100, on oxidative stress markers in cultured human endothelial cells. In a controlled laboratory study, cells were exposed to varying concentrations of X-100 (0 µM, 10 µM, 50 µM, 100 µM) for 24 hours. After treatment, intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) were quantified using a fluorescent probe, and levels of glutathione peroxidase (GPx) activity were measured spectrophotometrically. The study also assessed cell viability via MTT assay. Results indicated a dose‑dependent reduction in ROS with increasing X‑100 concentration, accompanied by a proportional increase in GPx activity. At 100 µM, ROS levels fell by 65% relative to the untreated control, while GPx activity rose by 120%. Cell viability remained above 90% across all doses, suggesting minimal cytotoxicity. These findings suggest that X‑100 may enhance antioxidant defenses in endothelial cells, potentially mitigating vascular oxidative damage. Further in vivo studies are warranted to evaluate therapeutic efficacy and safety.
