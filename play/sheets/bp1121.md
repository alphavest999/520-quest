# BP1121

Researchers conducted a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial to evaluate the effect of a novel dietary supplement on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in 200 adults aged 40–65 years. Participants were randomly assigned to one of four groups: Placebo (n=50), Low dose (25 mg, n=50), Medium dose (50 mg, n=50), or High dose (100 mg, n=50). Baseline SBP was measured after a 12‑hour fast and again after 12 weeks of daily supplementation. The study aimed to determine whether increasing doses of the supplement produce a dose‑dependent reduction in SBP. The investigators also monitored diastolic blood pressure (DBP) and recorded adverse events. Results indicated a progressive decrease in SBP across the dose groups, with the High dose group showing the greatest reduction. No serious adverse events were reported. The study concluded that the supplement has a dose‑dependent antihypertensive effect in the studied population.

## bp1121-q1 Which group experienced the greatest reduction in systolic blood pressure?
Correct: D
The High dose group’s SBP decreased from 140 to 118 mmHg, a 22‑mmHg reduction, which is larger than the reductions in the other groups.

## bp1121-q2 Which statement best describes the study design?
Correct: A
The study randomized participants to supplement or placebo, blinded both participants and investigators, and used a control group, meeting the criteria for a randomized double‑blind controlled trial.

## bp1121-q4 Which of the following could be a confounding variable in this study?
Correct: A
Age could influence baseline blood pressure and response to the supplement; if not evenly distributed, it could confound the results. Sample size, randomization, and blinding are study design features, not confounders.
