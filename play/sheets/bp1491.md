# BP1491

Researchers studied the effect of different cognitive training programs on reaction time in adults aged 30–50. Participants were randomly assigned to one of four groups: a control group that received no training, and three experimental groups that underwent distinct training protocols (A, B, and C). Each participant performed a computerized Stroop task before and after a four-week training period. The primary outcome was the mean reaction time (in seconds) on incongruent trials. The study aimed to determine which training protocol most effectively reduced reaction time and to assess the magnitude of improvement relative to the control group. The researchers also recorded standard deviations to evaluate variability within each group. The data were analyzed using ANOVA followed by post-hoc comparisons. The results indicated that all training groups showed significant improvements, but the magnitude varied across protocols. These findings have implications for designing targeted interventions to enhance cognitive processing speed in middle-aged adults.
