Researchers conducted a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of the novel antihypertensive agent, Hypotensol, in adults with stage 1 hypertension. A total of 120 participants were enrolled and randomly assigned to either the Hypotensol group (n=60) or the placebo group (n=60). Baseline systolic blood pressure (SBP) was measured after a 5-minute seated rest. Participants received either Hypotensol 10 mg once daily or matching placebo for 8 weeks. SBP readings were taken at baseline and at week 8. Table 1 summarizes the mean SBP values and changes for each group. The Hypotensol group showed a mean reduction of 12.3 mmHg, whereas the placebo group exhibited a mean reduction of 3.1 mmHg. The study concluded that Hypotensol significantly lowered SBP compared to placebo, with a 9.2 mmHg greater mean reduction. The investigators suggested that Hypotensol could be a valuable addition to first-line antihypertensive therapy.
