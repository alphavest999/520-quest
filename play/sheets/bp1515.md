# BP1515

Researchers at the Cognitive Neuroscience Institute investigated how sleep deprivation affects simple reaction time (RT). A double‑blind, within‑subject design was used: 30 healthy adults were tested after 8 h, 4 h, and 0 h of sleep on separate days. Each participant completed a computerized Go/No‑Go task, and mean RTs were recorded. The study aimed to quantify the impact of sleep loss on basic cognitive processing speed. Results indicated a clear trend: as sleep hours decreased, RT increased, suggesting impaired processing. The researchers also measured subjective sleepiness using the Karolinska Sleepiness Scale, finding a strong correlation between reported sleepiness and RT. The study’s implications extend to occupations requiring rapid responses, such as driving and air‑traffic control. The authors concluded that even moderate sleep loss (4 h) significantly slows reaction time, with potential safety ramifications.

## bp1515-q4 Which of the following statements is a common misconception about the study’s findings?
Correct: B
Correlation does not prove causation; the study shows association but cannot definitively state that sleepiness causes slower RT without ruling out other factors.

## bp1515-q5 How do the study’s findings relate to real‑world driving safety?
Correct: B
The data demonstrate that reduced sleep increases reaction time; in driving, slower reaction can delay braking and increase crash likelihood, especially after moderate sleep loss.
