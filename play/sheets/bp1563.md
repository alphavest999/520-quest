# BP1563

Researchers conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive agent, Hypertensol, in adults with stage 1 hypertension. Eighty participants were randomly assigned to one of four groups: placebo (n=20), low-dose Hypertensol (25 mg daily, n=20), medium-dose Hypertensol (50 mg daily, n=20), or high-dose Hypertensol (100 mg daily, n=20). Baseline systolic blood pressure (SBP) was measured after a 5‑minute seated rest using an automated cuff. After 12 weeks of treatment, SBP was reassessed under identical conditions. Table 1 summarizes the mean SBP values and standard deviations for each group. The placebo group showed a modest mean reduction of 3 mm Hg (SD = 4). In contrast, the low‑dose group experienced a 9 mm Hg reduction (SD = 5), the medium‑dose group a 15 mm Hg reduction (SD = 6), and the high‑dose group a 22 mm Hg reduction (SD = 7). The authors concluded that Hypertensol dose‑dependently lowers SBP, with the high‑dose regimen producing the greatest clinically significant effect.
