Researchers at the University of Midvale conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the effect of three different cognitive training programs on reaction time in adults aged 35–55. Participants were randomly assigned to one of four groups: a control group that received no training, Treatment A (visual-spatial drills), Treatment B (verbal memory drills), and a Combined group that received both types of drills. Each participant completed a standardized reaction time task before and after a 4‑week intervention period. The table below summarizes the average reaction times (in seconds) and standard deviations for each group immediately post‑intervention. The researchers hypothesized that the Combined group would exhibit the greatest improvement, followed by Treatment A, then Treatment B, with the control group showing minimal change. Their findings were intended to inform the design of workplace training programs aimed at enhancing cognitive performance.

Random assignment ensures that any differences between groups are due to the intervention rather than pre‑existing differences, providing the strongest evidence for causality.
