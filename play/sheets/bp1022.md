# BP1022

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel antihypertensive drug, Hypotensol, on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults with stage 1 hypertension. A double‑blind, randomized controlled trial enrolled 200 participants, who were assigned to either Hypotensol (n = 100) or placebo (n = 100). Baseline SBP was measured after a 5‑minute seated rest and recorded as mean ± SD. Participants received the study medication daily for 4 weeks. SBP was reassessed at week 4 using the same protocol. The investigators reported that the mean SBP in the Hypotensol group decreased from 138 ± 12 mm Hg to 122 ± 10 mm Hg, whereas the placebo group decreased from 139 ± 11 mm Hg to 136 ± 12 mm Hg. The study concluded that Hypotensol significantly lowered SBP compared with placebo, with a mean difference of 14 mm Hg (p < 0.001). The authors noted that the drug was well tolerated, with no serious adverse events reported. The trial was registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT01234567).

Correct: C
The investigators randomized participants to Hypotensol or placebo, blinded both participants and investigators, and followed them prospectively for 4 weeks. This is the definition of a randomized controlled trial (RCT).

Baseline BMI could influence blood pressure response and, if unevenly distributed, could confound the treatment effect. Randomization, blinding, and placebo use are study strengths, not confounders.

## 5 How should the findings of this study be applied in clinical practice?
Correct: C
The study shows significant BP reduction in stage 1 hypertensives, but it was short‑term and excluded patients with comorbidities. Therefore, Hypotensol can be considered as an adjunct to lifestyle changes in mild hypertension, not as a universal first‑line agent or contraindicated outright.
