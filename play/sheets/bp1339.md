# BP1339

Researchers studied the effect of varying concentrations of a novel antimicrobial peptide on the growth of Escherichia coli in vitro. In a controlled experiment, cultures were inoculated with a standardized bacterial load and then exposed to peptide concentrations of 0 µM (control), 5 µM, 10 µM, and 20 µM. After 24 hours of incubation at 37°C, optical density at 600 nm (OD600) was measured to assess bacterial growth. The results, summarized in Table 1, indicate a dose-dependent reduction in OD600, with the highest concentration yielding the greatest inhibition. Additionally, the researchers performed a time-course analysis at the 10 µM concentration, measuring OD600 at 0, 6, 12, and 24 hours to evaluate the kinetics of inhibition. The data suggest that the peptide exerts its bacteriostatic effect most rapidly within the first 6 hours, after which the growth rate plateaued. These findings support further investigation into the peptide’s potential as a therapeutic agent.
