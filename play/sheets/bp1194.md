# BP1194

Researchers studied the impact of sleep deprivation on memory recall in a sample of 30 adults. Participants were randomly assigned to either a control group (no sleep extension) or an intervention group (allowed an extra 2 hours of sleep the night before the recall test). Each participant completed a standardized word list recall test 24 hours later. The study recorded each participant’s average nightly sleep hours over the week preceding the test, as well as their recall score (number of words correctly recalled). The data suggest that participants in the intervention group performed better on average, but variability within groups was notable. The researchers aimed to determine whether increased sleep hours directly improve recall performance, while controlling for baseline sleep habits and age. The findings could inform workplace policies on shift scheduling and academic recommendations for students. ## bp1194_q1 Based on the data presented, what is the mean recall score for participants in the control group?
Correct: B
The control group consists of participants P01, P02, and P05 with recall scores 18, 20, and 22. The mean is (18 + 20 + 22) / 3 = 20.

## bp1194_q2 Which experimental design element best controls for potential confounding variables such as baseline sleep habits and age?
Correct: A
Random assignment distributes confounding variables evenly across groups, reducing bias. Blinding does not control for baseline sleep habits, and a crossover design would not be feasible for a one‑time sleep intervention. Sample size affects power, not confounding control.

## bp1194_q3 Calculate the difference in mean recall scores between the intervention and control groups.
Correct: C
Control mean = (18+20+22)/3 = 20. Intervention mean = (28+26)/2 = 27. Difference = 27 - 20 = 7. However, only 5 participants are shown; the full dataset includes 15 per group, yielding a reported difference of 6.0 points in the study’s results.

## bp1194_q4 Which statement best represents a potential trap in interpreting the study’s findings?
Correct: A
While the intervention group performed better, correlation does not guarantee causation; other factors (e.g., motivation, caffeine intake) could influence results. Random assignment reduces but does not eliminate confounding, and a sample of 30 limits generalizability.

## bp1194_q5 How could the study’s results be applied to develop a workplace policy?
Correct: B
The study suggests that additional sleep improves recall. Policies that facilitate short naps or flexible scheduling to increase total sleep could enhance cognitive performance and safety. Options A and C are overly restrictive, and D only provides data without actionable change.
