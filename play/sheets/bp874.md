# BP874

Researchers at the University of Example conducted a titration study to determine the acid‑base properties of glycine, the simplest amino acid. In a 0.01 M glycine solution, a strong base (NaOH) was added incrementally while the pH was recorded after each addition. The experiment was performed at 25 °C, and the ionic strength was maintained at 0.1 M by adding NaCl to minimize activity coefficient effects. The resulting titration curve shows two distinct buffering regions corresponding to the carboxyl (pKa ≈ 2.34) and amino (pKa ≈ 9.60) functional groups. To aid in data interpretation, the researchers compiled a table of pH values versus the fraction of each group that is protonated or deprotonated, as well as the net charge of the glycine molecule at each pH. This table is provided below and will be referenced in the following questions.

| pH | % Carboxyl ionized | % Amino ionized | Net charge |
|----|--------------------|-----------------|------------|
| 2.0 | 10% | 100% | +1 |
| 2.3 | 50% | 100% | +0.5 |
| 3.0 | 90% | 100% | 0 |
| 7.0 | 100% | 100% | 0 |
| 9.5 | 100% | 50% | -0.5 |
|10.0 | 100% | 100% | 0 |

## bp874-1 Which pH value corresponds to 50 % ionization of the carboxyl group of glycine?
Correct: B
Analogy: A 10‑year‑old might think 50 % ionization means the pH is halfway between the two pKa values. Official: The carboxyl pKa is 2.34, so 50 % ionization occurs at pH ≈ 2.3. Why wrong: The student ignores the exact pKa value and assumes a linear midpoint.

## bp874-2 If the researchers increased the ionic strength of the solution to 0.5 M, what would happen to the observed pKa of the carboxyl group?
Correct: A
Analogy: A 10‑year‑old might think more salt makes the solution more acidic. Official: Higher ionic strength reduces the activity of ions, effectively raising the apparent pKa (shift to higher pH). Why wrong: The student ignores activity coefficient effects.

## bp874-3 Using the Henderson‑Hasselbalch equation, calculate the net charge of glycine at pH 7.0.
Correct: B
Analogy: A 10‑year‑old might think pH 7 is neutral, but glycine is zwitterionic. Official: Carboxyl deprotonated (–1) and amino protonated (+1) gives net 0. Why wrong: Forgetting to include both functional groups.

## bp874-4 Which unit is incorrectly used in the table for concentration?
Correct: D
Analogy: A 10‑year‑old might think mg/mL is a molarity unit. Official: mg/mL is a mass/volume unit, not molarity. Why wrong: Mixing mass concentration with molar concentration leads to calculation errors.
