# BP1288

Researchers at the University of Health Sciences conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive drug, CardioRelief, on systolic blood pressure (SBP). The study enrolled 40 adults with stage 1 hypertension, randomly assigning them to one of four groups: Placebo (n=10), Low Dose (5 mg, n=10), Medium Dose (10 mg, n=10), and High Dose (20 mg, n=10). After 8 weeks of daily administration, the mean SBP and standard deviation (SD) for each group were recorded. The researchers also monitored adverse events and adherence rates. The trial aimed to determine dose‑response relationships and safety profiles, providing preliminary data for larger phase III studies.

## bp1288-01 Based on the table, which group exhibited the lowest mean systolic blood pressure after 8 weeks?
Correct: D
The table shows the mean SBP values for each group. The High Dose group (20 mg) has the lowest mean SBP of 128.9 mmHg, indicating the greatest reduction among the groups.

## bp1288-02 What type of experimental design was employed in this study to assess the effect of CardioRelief on systolic blood pressure?
Correct: C
The passage states that participants were randomly assigned to four groups, a hallmark of a randomized controlled trial (RCT). This design allows for causal inference about the drug’s effect.

## bp1288-03 Calculate the absolute mean difference in systolic blood pressure between the High Dose and Placebo groups.
Correct: A
Mean SBP for Placebo = 145.2 mmHg; for High Dose = 128.9 mmHg. Difference = 145.2 – 128.9 = 16.3 mmHg. Option A reflects this calculation.

## bp1288-04 Which of the following could be a potential confounding variable that might bias the results of this trial?
Correct: C
Baseline dietary sodium intake can influence blood pressure independently of the drug, acting as a confounder if not evenly distributed across groups. Randomization and blinding reduce bias, while a standardized exercise program would be a controlled variable.

## bp1288-05 How might the findings of this study inform future research on antihypertensive therapies?
Correct: B
The observed dose‑response (lower SBP with higher doses) supports advancing to larger trials and helps determine appropriate dosing ranges. The study is not definitive, and placebo does have an effect.
