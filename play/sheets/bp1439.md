# BP1439

Researchers at the Coastal Marine Institute investigated how different light wavelengths affect the growth and photosynthetic efficiency of the marine alga *Ulva lactuca*. Cultures were grown in identical nutrient media under controlled temperature and light intensity conditions, with only the wavelength of the light source varied. Four monochromatic LED lights (400 nm, 470 nm, 530 nm, and 600 nm) were used, each maintained at 200 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹. Over a 14‑day period, daily samples were taken to measure chlorophyll‑a concentration (µg mL⁻¹), cell density (cells mL⁻¹), and photosynthetic efficiency (ΦPSII). The results indicated a peak in chlorophyll‑a and growth rate at 470 nm, while 530 nm produced the highest photosynthetic efficiency. The 600 nm light yielded the lowest values across all measured parameters, suggesting limited utilization by *U. lactuca* in the blue‑green spectrum. These findings provide insight into optimizing light conditions for large‑scale algal cultivation and bio‑fuel production.
