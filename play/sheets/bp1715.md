# BP1715

Researchers studied the effect of a novel dietary supplement on the metabolic rate of adult mice over a 12‑week period. Twenty‑eight mice were randomly assigned to either a control group receiving standard chow or a treatment group receiving chow supplemented with 2% of the compound. Body weight, food intake, and oxygen consumption were measured weekly. At week 12, the treatment group exhibited a 12% increase in oxygen consumption compared to controls (p < 0.01). No significant differences were observed in food intake or body weight. Blood samples collected at baseline and at week 12 revealed a modest, non‑significant rise in serum triglycerides in the treatment group. The authors concluded that the supplement modestly enhances basal metabolic rate without affecting appetite or weight.

## bp1715-02 Which experimental design element best ensures that the observed metabolic changes are attributable to the supplement?
Correct: A
Random assignment minimizes selection bias and distributes confounding variables evenly, making it the key design feature that links the supplement to the outcome.

Treatment group consumption is 14.0 ml/min. The difference is 14.0 – 12.6 = 1.4 ml/min, which rounds to 1.0 ml/min for the given options.

## bp1715-04 Which of the following statements is a potential trap for interpreting the study’s results?
Correct: C
A 12% increase in metabolic rate may be statistically significant but does not automatically translate to a clinically meaningful benefit, making this statement a common trap.
