# BP1415

Researchers investigated the impact of sleep deprivation on short‑term memory recall in a controlled laboratory setting. Forty healthy adults were randomly assigned to one of three groups: a control group that slept 8 hours, a sleep‑deprived group that slept 4 hours, and a partial‑deprivation group that slept 6 hours. After the sleep period, all participants completed a standardized word‑list recall test. The results are summarized in Table 1 below. The researchers found that the mean recall score decreased with reduced sleep duration, while error rates increased. These findings suggest a dose‑response relationship between sleep duration and memory performance, supporting the hypothesis that adequate sleep is essential for optimal cognitive function.

Table 1. Summary of Sleep Duration and Memory Performance

| Group            | Mean Sleep Hours | Mean Recall Score | Error Rate (%) |
|------------------|------------------|-------------------|----------------|
| Control          | 8                | 85                | 5              |
| Sleep‑Deprived   | 4                | 60                | 15             |
| Partial‑Deprived | 6                | 75                | 10             |

## bp1415_q1 What was the mean recall score for participants in the sleep‑deprived group?
Correct: C
The table lists a mean recall score of 60 for the sleep‑deprived group. This value directly answers the question.

## bp1415_q2 Which experimental design best allows the researchers to infer a causal relationship between sleep duration and recall performance?
Correct: B
Randomization of participants to sleep conditions controls for confounding variables, enabling causal inference about the effect of sleep duration on recall.

## bp1415_q3 If the error rate for the sleep‑deprived group is 15 % and the group contains 20 participants, how many errors were observed?
Correct: A
15 % of 20 participants equals 0.15 × 20 = 3 errors.

## bp1415_q4 Which of the following variables is the most likely confounder that could bias the observed relationship between sleep duration and recall score?
Correct: A
Age could influence both sleep patterns and memory performance, acting as a confounder. Sleep duration, recall score, and error rate are part of the experimental manipulation or outcome.

## bp1415_q5 Which statement best describes the relationship between sleep duration and recall score?
Correct: B
The data show that as sleep duration increases, recall score also increases, indicating a positive linear relationship.
