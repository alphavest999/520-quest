# BP1701

Researchers conducted a double-blind, randomized controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of a novel antihypertensive agent, Hypertensol, in reducing systolic blood pressure among adults with stage 1 hypertension. Participants (n = 200) were randomly assigned to one of four groups: placebo, low-dose Hypertensol (25 mg), medium-dose Hypertensol (50 mg), and high-dose Hypertensol (100 mg). Over a 12-week treatment period, systolic blood pressure was measured at baseline and at the end of the study. The results, summarized in Table 1, indicate a clear dose-dependent reduction in systolic pressure. The high-dose group experienced the greatest mean reduction (20 mmHg, SD 6), followed by the medium-dose group (14 mmHg, SD 4), the low-dose group (8 mmHg, SD 5), and the placebo group (4 mmHg, SD 3). All treatment groups showed statistically significant reductions compared to placebo (p < 0.01). These findings suggest that Hypertensol effectively lowers blood pressure in a dose-responsive manner, supporting its potential use in clinical practice.

## bp1701_q1 Which treatment group experienced the largest mean reduction in systolic blood pressure?
Correct: D
The table shows the high-dose group had a mean reduction of 20 mmHg, the greatest among all groups.

## bp1701_q2 Which experimental design element best supports the validity of the study’s conclusions?
Correct: B
The study randomly assigned participants to treatment groups and used a double-blind design, which are hallmarks of a randomized controlled trial, the gold standard for assessing causality.

Correct: C
High-dose mean reduction = 20 mmHg; placebo = 4 mmHg; difference = 20 – 4 = 16 mmHg.
