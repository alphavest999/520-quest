# BP1202

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel antihypertensive agent, Hypto, on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults with stage 1 hypertension. Eighty participants were enrolled and randomly assigned to receive either Hypto (n = 40) or a matching placebo (n = 40). Baseline SBP was measured after a 24‑hour ambulatory monitoring period. After 4 weeks of daily dosing, SBP was reassessed using the same protocol. The Hypto group exhibited a mean SBP of 140 mmHg at 4 weeks, compared with 150 mmHg at baseline, whereas the placebo group’s mean SBP rose from 140 mmHg to 145 mmHg. The mean change in SBP for Hypto was a reduction of 10 mmHg, while the placebo group experienced a mean increase of 5 mmHg. These results suggest Hypto may effectively lower SBP in this population, though the study’s short duration limits conclusions about long‑term efficacy and safety.
