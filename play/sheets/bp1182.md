# BP1182

Researchers investigated the effect of three different dietary supplements on the resting metabolic rate (RMR) of healthy adults. A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study enrolled 120 participants (30 per group). Participants were assigned to one of four groups: a placebo (Group P), supplement A (Group A), supplement B (Group B), or supplement C (Group C). After a 12-week intervention, RMR was measured using indirect calorimetry. Table 1 summarizes the mean RMR (kcal/day) and standard deviation for each group. The investigators hypothesized that supplements A and B would increase RMR relative to placebo, while supplement C would have no effect. The data were analyzed using one-way ANOVA followed by Tukey's post-hoc test. Results indicated significant differences between Groups A and P (p<0.01) and between Groups B and P (p<0.05), but no significant difference between Groups A and B (p=0.45). The authors concluded that both supplements A and B effectively increase RMR compared to placebo, with no superiority between them.

## bp1182_Q1 Which group had the highest mean RMR?
Correct: B
Supplement A had the highest mean RMR (1900 kcal/day) compared to the other groups.

## bp1182_Q2 Which study design best controls for participant variability?
Correct: A
A randomized controlled trial (RCT) randomizes participants to groups, minimizing confounding and controlling for variability.

## bp1182_Q3 What is the approximate percent increase in mean RMR from placebo to supplement A?
Correct: A
Percent increase = (1900-1800)/1800 × 100 ≈ 5.6%, closest to 5%.

Correct: C

Correct: C
