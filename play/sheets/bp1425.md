# BP1425

Researchers at the University of Greenfield investigated the effect of two different light spectra on the growth of the fast-growing lettuce variety, L. crispum. Over a 30‑day period, seedlings were exposed to either a blue‑rich spectrum (Treatment A) or a red‑rich spectrum (Treatment B). Growth was measured by leaf area (cm²) at days 10, 20, and 30. The data, summarized in Table 1, indicate that Treatment A consistently produced larger leaf areas, with a notable increase between days 20 and 30. The researchers hypothesize that blue light enhances photosynthetic efficiency, leading to accelerated growth. They plan to conduct further experiments to test the underlying physiological mechanisms, including chlorophyll fluorescence and stomatal conductance measurements.

## bp1425-02 Which of the following experimental designs would best test the hypothesis that blue light enhances photosynthetic efficiency in lettuce?
Correct: B
Random assignment to blue or red light for the full period and simultaneous measurement of leaf area and chlorophyll fluorescence provides the most direct test of the hypothesis while controlling for time and other variables.

## bp1425-05 The researchers’ hypothesis that blue light enhances photosynthetic efficiency is most closely related to which of the following concepts?
Correct: B
Blue light influences the light-dependent reactions, affecting electron transport and ATP/NADPH production, directly impacting photosynthetic efficiency.
