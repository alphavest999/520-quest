Researchers at the University of X conducted a randomized controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy of two novel antihypertensive agents, Drug A and Drug B, compared with a standard antihypertensive and a placebo. One hundred and twenty adults with stage‑1 hypertension were randomly assigned to one of four groups (30 participants per group). Over a 12‑week period, systolic blood pressure (SBP) was measured at baseline and at the end of the study. Table 1 summarizes the mean SBP reduction (in mmHg) and standard deviation for each group. The control group received the standard medication, while Drug A and Drug B were administered at the same dosage. The placebo group received inert tablets. The results showed that both experimental drugs produced greater reductions in SBP than the standard medication, with Drug B achieving the largest mean reduction. The study also reported comparable safety profiles across all groups. These findings suggest that Drug B may offer superior blood‑pressure control for patients who do not respond adequately to conventional therapy.

| Drug A    | 15.8                      | 1.5     | 30          |
| Drug B    | 18.3                      | 1.7     | 30          |
| Placebo   | 13.0                      | 1.3     | 30          |

## 1 Which treatment group achieved the highest mean reduction in systolic blood pressure?
Correct: C
