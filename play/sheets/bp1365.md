# BP1365

Researchers studied the impact of a novel dietary supplement on blood pressure among adults aged 40–60. Sixty participants were randomly assigned to either the supplement group (n = 30) or a placebo group (n = 30). After 12 weeks, systolic and diastolic blood pressure readings were recorded. The data showed that the supplement group had an average systolic pressure of 122 mm Hg (SD = 8) and a diastolic pressure of 78 mm Hg (SD = 6), whereas the placebo group averaged 128 mm Hg systolic (SD = 9) and 82 mm Hg diastolic (SD = 7). The researchers concluded that the supplement led to a modest but statistically significant reduction in both systolic and diastolic pressures. The study also collected data on heart rate, cholesterol, and adherence rates, but the primary outcome was blood pressure. The authors suggested that further research should examine long‑term effects and potential interactions with antihypertensive medications.

## bp1365-01 Based on Table 1, which of the following best describes the mean systolic blood pressure difference between the supplement and placebo groups?
Correct: B
The supplement group’s mean systolic pressure is 122 mm Hg, which is lower than the placebo group’s 128 mm Hg. Therefore, the placebo group had the higher mean.

## bp1365-02 Which of the following best describes the experimental design used in this study?
Correct: B
The passage states that participants were randomly assigned to supplement or placebo groups, indicating a randomized controlled trial. The mention of a placebo suggests blinding, making it a double‑blind design.

## bp1365-03 Calculate the absolute mean difference in systolic blood pressure between the supplement and placebo groups.
Correct: A
Absolute mean difference = 128 mm Hg (placebo) – 122 mm Hg (supplement) = 6 mm Hg.
