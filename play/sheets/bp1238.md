# BP1238

Researchers studied the effect of a novel cognitive enhancer on reaction time in healthy adults. A randomized controlled trial was conducted with 180 participants divided equally into three groups: a control group receiving a placebo, a low-dose group receiving 50 mg of the enhancer, and a high-dose group receiving 150 mg. After a 4-week intervention, participants performed a computerized Stroop task. The mean reaction times (in milliseconds) and standard deviations for each group were recorded. The results suggested a dose-dependent improvement in reaction time, with the high-dose group showing the greatest reduction. The study also monitored adverse events, noting mild headaches in 10% of the high-dose group and no significant side effects in the other groups. The authors concluded that the enhancer could be a promising therapeutic agent for cognitive decline, pending further large-scale trials.

## q1 Which group had the lowest mean reaction time?
Correct: C
The high‑dose group had the lowest mean reaction time (480 ms) compared to the control (600 ms) and low‑dose (520 ms) groups.

## q2 Which study design best controls for confounding variables in this experiment?
Correct: A
Randomization ensures that known and unknown confounders are evenly distributed across groups, making the randomized controlled trial the gold standard for controlling bias.

## q3 What is the percent difference in mean reaction time between the high‑dose group and the control group?
Correct: B
Percent difference = (600 ms – 480 ms) / 600 ms × 100% = 20 ms / 600 ms × 100% = 13.3%.

## q4 Which of the following is NOT a potential source of bias in this study?
Correct: A
Randomization is a method to reduce bias, not a source of bias. The other options can introduce bias if not properly controlled.

## q5 Which concept is most directly illustrated by the relationship between dose and reaction time in this study?
Correct: A
The study demonstrates a clear dose‑response relationship: higher doses of the enhancer are associated with greater reductions in reaction time.
