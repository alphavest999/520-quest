# BP1615

Researchers investigated how temperature influences the activity of a purified enzyme involved in carbohydrate metabolism. The enzyme was incubated at five discrete temperatures (20°C, 30°C, 40°C, 50°C, and 60°C) in a buffer of constant pH. Activity was measured spectrophotometrically by monitoring the formation of a colored product over a fixed time period. Each temperature condition was run in triplicate to assess experimental variability. The resulting data revealed a bell‑shaped curve, with activity rising from 20°C to a peak at 40°C, followed by a decline at higher temperatures. The decline at 50°C and 60°C is consistent with partial denaturation of the enzyme, reducing catalytic efficiency. The study also noted that the enzyme’s activity was unaffected by the presence of a competitive inhibitor under these conditions, suggesting that temperature is the primary variable influencing catalytic turnover. The findings provide insight into the optimal operating conditions for this enzyme and have implications for industrial applications where temperature control is critical.
