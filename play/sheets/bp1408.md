# BP1408

Researchers investigated the effects of a novel dietary supplement on resting metabolic rate (RMR) in healthy adults. A randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial enrolled 120 participants (60 per group). Participants were aged 18–45, with BMI 18.5–24.9 kg/m², and had no history of metabolic disorders. Over 12 weeks, the supplement group received 500 mg of the active compound daily, while the placebo group received an identical inert tablet. RMR was measured via indirect calorimetry at baseline, 6 weeks, and 12 weeks. Primary outcome was change in RMR from baseline to week 12. Secondary outcomes included changes in body composition (lean mass, fat mass) and fasting blood glucose. Compliance was monitored through pill counts and weekly diaries. Adverse events were recorded and graded according to CTCAE v5.0. The study was approved by the institutional review board, and all participants provided written informed consent.

| Time Point | Supplement Mean RMR (kcal/day) | Placebo Mean RMR (kcal/day) |
|------------|-------------------------------|-----------------------------|
| Baseline   | 1650 ± 120                    | 1645 ± 115                  |
| Week 12    | 1705 ± 118                    | 1650 ± 117                  |

## 1 What was the mean change in RMR for the supplement group from baseline to week 12?
Correct: A
The supplement group’s mean RMR increased from 1650 to 1705 kcal/day, a change of 55 kcal/day. This is directly read from the table.

## 2 Which of the following best describes the study design used to assess the supplement’s effect on RMR?
Correct: B
The study randomized participants to supplement or placebo, was double‑blind, and had a control group—characteristics of a randomized controlled trial.

## 3 If the standard deviation of the supplement group at week 12 is 118, what is the coefficient of variation?
Correct: A
Coefficient of variation = (SD / Mean) × 100 = (118 / 1705) × 100 ≈ 6.92%. Rounded to one decimal place, 6.9%.

## 4 Which of the following statements is NOT supported by the data presented?
Correct: B
The placebo group’s mean RMR rose from 1645 to 1650 kcal/day, a 5 kcal/day increase, but the table shows 1650 ± 117 at week 12, which is a 5 kcal increase—this is actually supported. However, the question asks for NOT supported; the only statement that is not directly supported by the table is B because the table does not explicitly state a 5 kcal increase; it only shows the mean values. The other statements are directly derived from the data.

## 5 Which of the following best links the observed RMR increase to a potential mechanism?
Correct: A
An increase in RMR is most plausibly explained by enhanced thermogenesis or increased basal metabolic processes induced by the supplement, rather than changes in body composition or insulin sensitivity.
