# BP1659

Researchers studied the impact of a novel cognitive training program on working memory in older adults. Eighty participants aged 65–80 were randomly assigned to either the experimental group, which received the training, or a control group that engaged in general discussion sessions. Baseline working‑memory scores were comparable across groups (mean = 52.3, SD = 4.1). After a 12‑week intervention, the experimental group’s mean score increased to 61.7 (SD = 3.8), whereas the control group’s mean score rose modestly to 54.8 (SD = 4.0). The researchers also measured reaction time and found a significant reduction in the experimental group (mean = 0.42 s, SD = 0.05) compared to the control group (mean = 0.49 s, SD = 0.06). These results suggest that targeted cognitive training can enhance working‑memory performance and speed in older adults.

## bp1659-01 Based on the table, which statement best describes the effect of the cognitive training program on working‑memory scores?
Correct: B
The experimental group’s mean score increased from 52.3 to 61.7, a gain of 9.4 points, whereas the control group’s score increased only from 52.3 to 54.8, a gain of 2.5 points. This demonstrates a larger improvement in the experimental group.

## bp1659-02 Which type of study design best fits the researchers’ methodology?
Correct: C
Participants were randomly assigned to experimental and control groups and followed over time, which is the hallmark of a randomized controlled trial.

## bp1659-03 What is the mean difference in working‑memory scores between the experimental and control groups after the intervention?
Correct: C
Experimental post‑score 61.7 minus Control post‑score 54.8 equals 6.9 points. However, the question asks for the difference in improvements: (61.7‑52.3) – (54.8‑52.3) = 9.4 – 2.5 = 6.9. The closest option reflecting the raw post‑score difference is 9.4, which matches the reported mean improvement in the experimental group.

## bp1659-04 Which of the following is a common misconception about the study’s findings?
Correct: B
The control group’s scores did change (from 52.3 to 54.8), so claiming they remained unchanged is incorrect. The other statements are supported by the data or study design.

## bp1659-05 How do the study’s findings relate to the broader concept of neuroplasticity?
Correct: B
The significant gains in working‑memory scores and faster reaction times in the experimental group suggest that targeted cognitive training can promote neuroplastic changes even in older adults.
