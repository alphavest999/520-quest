# BP992

Researchers investigated the effect of a novel antihypertensive agent on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults with stage 1 hypertension. The study enrolled 120 participants, stratified into four age groups (18–29, 30–39, 40–49, 50–59 years). All participants received the drug once daily for four weeks. Baseline SBP and diastolic blood pressure (DBP) were recorded, and follow‑up measurements were taken at the end of the study period. The investigators reported mean SBP reductions and calculated the percent change from baseline for each age group. The results suggested that younger adults experienced a greater relative reduction in SBP compared to older adults, although absolute reductions were similar across groups. The study concluded that the drug is effective across age ranges, with a slightly enhanced relative benefit in younger patients.
