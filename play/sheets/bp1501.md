# BP1501

Researchers studied the effect of a novel dietary supplement on cognitive performance in adults aged 50–65. In a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial, 120 participants were assigned to one of four groups: placebo, low dose (50 mg), medium dose (100 mg), or high dose (200 mg). Each participant underwent a standardized neuropsychological battery before and after a 12‑week intervention period. The primary outcome was the change in composite memory score. Table 1 summarizes the mean (± SD) change scores for each group. The high‑dose group exhibited the largest mean improvement, followed by medium, low, and placebo. Secondary outcomes included executive function and processing speed, which showed similar dose‑response patterns. No serious adverse events were reported. The authors concluded that the supplement may enhance cognitive function in older adults, particularly at higher doses, and recommend further studies to confirm efficacy and safety.

## bp1501_q1 Which group demonstrated the greatest mean improvement in composite memory score?
Correct: D
The table shows the mean change scores: 1.2 (placebo), 2.4 (low), 3.8 (medium), and 5.1 (high). The highest mean improvement is in the high‑dose group.

## bp1501_q2 What is the independent variable in this study?
Correct: C
The independent variable is what the researchers manipulate. Here, they vary the dosage of the supplement (placebo, 50 mg, 100 mg, 200 mg).

## bp1501_q3 Calculate the absolute difference in mean change between the high‑dose and placebo groups.
Correct: A
High‑dose mean change = 5.1; placebo mean change = 1.2. Difference = 5.1 – 1.2 = 3.9 points.

## bp1501_q4 Which statement is a potential trap when interpreting the results?
Correct: C
Large SDs alone do not determine statistical significance; significance depends on sample size and variability relative to the mean difference. The other statements are correct interpretations or study design strengths.

## bp1501_q5 Which concept is most relevant to interpreting the study’s findings in the context of future research?
Correct: A
An intention‑to‑treat analysis preserves the benefits of randomization by including all participants in the groups to which they were originally assigned, which is essential for generalizing efficacy results to real‑world settings.
