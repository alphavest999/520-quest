# BP1687

Researchers studied the effect of varying doses of a novel cognitive enhancer on reaction time in healthy adults. A randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial enrolled 120 participants who were assigned to one of four groups: a control group receiving placebo, and three treatment groups receiving low (10 mg), medium (20 mg), or high (30 mg) doses. Each participant performed a computerized visual‑reaction time task, and mean response times (RT) were recorded. The study aimed to determine whether higher doses would produce a dose‑response relationship, with the hypothesis that increased dosage would reduce RT. The researchers also monitored adverse events to assess safety. Data were collected over a 4‑week period, with baseline RT measured prior to dosing. The results suggested a modest improvement in RT with increasing dose, but the variability was substantial, especially at the high‑dose level. These findings provide preliminary evidence that the cognitive enhancer may enhance processing speed, though further research is needed to confirm efficacy and safety.
