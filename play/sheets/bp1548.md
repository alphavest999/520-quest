# BP1548

Researchers investigated the effects of a new antihypertensive drug on systolic blood pressure (SBP) in adults aged 40–65. In a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial, participants were assigned to one of three groups: (A) placebo, (B) low‑dose drug (50 mg/day), and (C) high‑dose drug (100 mg/day). After 12 weeks, mean SBP reductions and variability were recorded. The study aimed to determine dose‑response relationships, safety profiles, and statistical significance of SBP changes compared to placebo. The trial enrolled 180 participants, 60 per group, ensuring adequate power to detect clinically meaningful differences. Data were analyzed using ANOVA followed by Tukey’s post‑hoc test. The results showed a dose‑dependent decrease in SBP, with the high‑dose group achieving the greatest reduction. Adverse events were mild and evenly distributed across groups. These findings suggest the drug’s efficacy and tolerability, supporting further phase III trials.

Correct: B
A randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial eliminates selection bias, balances confounders, and ensures that differences in SBP are due to the drug dose rather than external factors.

Standard error = SD/√n = 3.8/√60 ≈ 0.49. 95% CI = mean ± 1.96*SE = 8.3 ± 0.96 → (7.34, 9.26). Option B (6.5–9.9) is the closest rounded interval provided.

## bp1548_q4 Which group’s data would most likely lead to a Type I error if the researchers incorrectly concluded significance without correcting for multiple comparisons?
Correct: D
Multiple pairwise comparisons (placebo vs. low‑dose, placebo vs. high‑dose, low‑dose vs. high‑dose) increase the chance of a false positive. Without adjustment (e.g., Bonferroni), any of the comparisons could produce a Type I error.
