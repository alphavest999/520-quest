# BP1754

Researchers studied the effect of three different cognitive training programs on reaction time in adults aged 30-45. Participants were randomly assigned to one of four groups: a control group that received no training, Group A that practiced a working‑memory task, Group B that engaged in a visuospatial task, and Group C that performed a combined working‑memory and visuospatial task. After a 4‑week intervention, reaction times were measured using a computerized Stroop task. The table below summarizes the mean reaction times (in seconds) and standard deviations for each group.

Group | Mean RT (s) | SD (s)
Control | 0.68 | 0.07
Group A | 0.61 | 0.05
Group B | 0.64 | 0.06
Group C | 0.58 | 0.04

## bp1754-01 Which group had the lowest average reaction time after the intervention?
Correct: D
The table shows Group C had the lowest mean reaction time (0.58 s).

## bp1754-02 Which experimental design would best determine if the observed differences in reaction time are caused by the specific training programs?
Correct: B
A randomized controlled trial with pre‑ and post‑tests controls for baseline differences and allows causal inference about the training interventions.

## bp1754-03 Calculate the pooled standard deviation for Groups A and B.
Correct: B
Pooled SD = sqrt(((nA-1)*SD_A² + (nB-1)*SD_B²)/(nA+nB-2)). Assuming equal sample sizes (nA=nB), SD_A=0.05, SD_B=0.06 → pooled ≈ 0.057.

## bp1754-04 Which of the following statements is NOT supported by the data?
Correct: D
Group C’s SD is 0.04, which is smaller than the SDs of the other groups; thus statement D is false.

## bp1754-05 The findings of this study are most closely related to which of the following concepts?
Correct: A
The improvement in reaction time after targeted cognitive training suggests brain plasticity—the ability of neural circuits to reorganize in response to experience.
