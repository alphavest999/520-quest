# BP1326

Researchers studied the effect of a novel anti‑inflammatory compound on joint pain in a randomized, double‑blind, placebo‑controlled trial. One hundred and twenty patients with osteoarthritis were assigned to one of four groups: a placebo control, a low‑dose group (10 mg), a medium‑dose group (20 mg), and a high‑dose group (40 mg). Pain was measured using the Visual Analog Scale (VAS) at baseline and after 12 weeks of treatment. The mean VAS scores (0–100 mm) and standard deviations (SD) at week 12 are presented in Table 1. The study aimed to determine whether a dose‑dependent reduction in pain could be observed and to assess the safety profile of the compound.

Table 1. Mean VAS Scores at Week 12

| Group          | Treatment | Mean VAS | SD |
|----------------|-----------|----------|----|
| Control        | Placebo   | 68.4     | 12.3 |
| Low Dose       | 10 mg     | 55.2     | 10.8 |
| Medium Dose    | 20 mg     | 42.7     | 9.5 |
| High Dose      | 40 mg     | 31.1     | 8.9 |


## bp1326-01 Which group had the lowest mean VAS score at week 12?
Correct: D
The high‑dose group (40 mg) had the lowest mean VAS score of 31.1, indicating the greatest pain reduction.

## bp1326-02 Which experimental design best tests for a dose‑response relationship in this study?
Correct: B
A parallel‑group randomized controlled trial with multiple dose levels allows direct comparison of each dose against placebo and among doses to assess a dose‑response curve.

## bp1326-03 What is the percent reduction in mean VAS score from the control group to the high‑dose group?
Correct: A
Percent reduction = ((68.4 - 31.1) / 68.4) × 100 ≈ 54.7%.

## bp1326-04 Which of the following statements is FALSE regarding the study results?
Correct: C
The low‑dose group (55.2) had a higher mean VAS than the medium‑dose group (42.7), so statement C is false.

## bp1326-05 How might these findings influence clinical decision‑making for patients with osteoarthritis?
Correct: B
The dose‑response data show greater pain reduction with higher doses, but clinicians often aim for the lowest effective dose to reduce adverse effects.
