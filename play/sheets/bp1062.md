# BP1062

Researchers studied the effect of different lighting conditions on cognitive task performance in a controlled laboratory setting. The experiment involved 120 adult participants randomly assigned to one of four lighting conditions: (1) standard office lighting (400 lux), (2) dimmed lighting (200 lux), (3) bright LED lighting (800 lux), and (4) natural daylight (variable lux). Each participant completed a 30‑minute working memory task while their reaction times and accuracy were recorded. The researchers hypothesized that brighter lighting would improve task performance, while dim lighting would impair it. Data were collected over a two‑week period, and participants were blinded to the study’s purpose to reduce expectancy effects. The study also measured subjective mood ratings after each session to assess potential confounding variables. Statistical analysis involved ANOVA to compare mean reaction times across conditions, followed by post‑hoc Tukey tests to identify specific group differences. The results indicated a significant main effect of lighting on reaction time (p < .01), with the bright LED condition yielding the fastest responses and the dimmed condition the slowest. Accuracy rates did not differ significantly across conditions, suggesting that lighting primarily affected speed rather than correctness.
