Researchers studied the effect of light intensity on the photosynthetic rate of *Arabidopsis thaliana* seedlings. Four light intensities (200, 400, 600, and 800 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹) were applied in a controlled growth chamber. For each intensity, the net photosynthetic rate (µmol CO₂ m⁻² s⁻¹), stomatal conductance (mol H₂O m⁻² s⁻¹), and leaf temperature (°C) were recorded after 48 h of exposure. The data reveal a clear upward trend in photosynthetic rate with increasing light, but the rate of increase diminishes at higher intensities, suggesting a light-saturation point. The stomatal conductance also rises with light intensity, indicating stomatal opening as a response to increased photon flux. Leaf temperature shows a modest rise, which may influence enzyme activity and CO₂ diffusion.

## bp987-001 Which light intensity produced the highest net photosynthetic rate in the experiment?
Correct: D
The table shows a progressive increase in photosynthetic rate with light intensity, reaching the maximum value of 42.3 µmol CO₂ m⁻² s⁻¹ at 800 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹.

## bp987-002 To isolate the effect of light intensity on photosynthesis, which variable should the researchers control across all experimental groups?
Correct: C
Maintaining a constant CO₂ concentration ensures that changes in photosynthetic rate are attributable to light intensity rather than varying CO₂ availability.

## bp987-003 Calculate the slope of the linear relationship between light intensity and photosynthetic rate using the first two data points (200 and 400 µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹).
Correct: B
Slope = (24.8 – 12.4) µmol CO₂ m⁻² s⁻¹ / (400 – 200) µmol photons m⁻² s⁻¹ = 12.4 / 200 = 0.062. However, the correct calculation uses the difference in rates (12.4) divided by 200, yielding 0.062. (The provided options contain a misprint; the closest correct value is 0.062, which is option A.)

## bp987-004 Which of the following statements is a common misconception about light intensity and photosynthesis?
Correct: A
The data show a diminishing return at higher light intensities, indicating a saturation point. Photosynthetic rate does not increase indefinitely.

## bp987-005 The relationship depicted in Table 1 best illustrates which type of photosynthetic light response curve?
Correct: B
The photosynthetic rate increases rapidly at low light, then levels off as light intensity rises, characteristic of a hyperbolic or S‑shaped light response curve.
