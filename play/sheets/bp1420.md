# BP1420

Researchers studied the effect of varying concentrations of a novel antioxidant on the growth rate of cultured human fibroblasts over 72 hours. They used a randomized controlled design with five concentration groups: 0 μM (control), 5 μM, 10 μM, 20 μM, and 40 μM. For each group, cell viability was measured using an MTT assay at 24, 48, and 72 hours. Table 1 summarizes the mean absorbance values (± SD) at 72 hours. The results suggest a dose‑dependent increase in cell viability up to 20 μM, after which the benefit plateaus. These findings may inform therapeutic dosing strategies for antioxidant‑based interventions in wound healing.

Concentration (μM) | Absorbance (mean ± SD)
0 | 0.45 ± 0.02
5 | 0.62 ± 0.03
10 | 0.78 ± 0.04
20 | 0.85 ± 0.05
40 | 0.83 ± 0.04

## 1 Which concentration group showed the highest mean absorbance at 72 hours?
Correct: D
The 20 μM group had the highest mean absorbance (0.85), indicating the greatest cell viability.

## 2 Which of the following best describes the study's design?
Correct: A
The researchers assigned fibroblasts to different antioxidant concentrations in a controlled manner, characteristic of a randomized controlled trial.

## 3 Calculate the percent increase in mean absorbance from 0 μM to 20 μM.
Correct: B
Percent increase = ((0.85‑0.45)/0.45)×100 ≈ 88.9%.

## 4 Which concentration group had the lowest standard deviation?
Correct: A
The control group (0 μM) had the smallest SD (0.02), indicating the least variability.

## 5 Which of the following statements best links the antioxidant concentration to cell viability?
Correct: B
The data show increasing absorbance (viability) with concentration up to 20 μM, after which the increase plateaus.
