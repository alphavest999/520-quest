# BP787 - 3A PCR experiment

**Passage:** Researchers investigated the effect of annealing temperature on the efficiency of a PCR amplification targeting a 500‑base pair region of the human β‑actin gene. Using a standard Taq polymerase protocol (1 µL 10 × buffer, 1.5 mM MgCl₂, 200 nM dNTPs, 0.2 µM forward and reverse primers, 1 U Taq, 30 cycles), they varied the annealing temperature (50 °C, 55 °C, 60 °C, 65 °C) while keeping all other parameters constant. After amplification, the reaction products were quantified by agarose gel electrophoresis and a calibrated DNA ladder, yielding the following product amounts: 50 °C – 10 ng; 55 °C – 80 ng; 60 °C – 300 ng; 65 °C – 120 ng. The data indicate an optimal annealing temperature near 60 °C, where the product yield is maximized. The researchers also recorded the number of cycles, primer concentration, and extension time for each experiment to assess their impact on amplification efficiency. These observations provide a foundation for optimizing PCR conditions in diagnostic and research settings. Control reactions without template DNA were included to confirm specificity, and a melt‑curve analysis was performed to verify the purity of the amplified product. The results suggest that annealing temperatures below 55 °C lead to non‑specific amplification, whereas temperatures above 65 °C reduce primer binding efficiency.

## 787_Q2 - If the goal is to increase product yield, which experimental variable should be adjusted first?
Options: {"A":"Increase MgCl₂ concentration","B":"Increase primer concentration","C":"Increase annealing temperature beyond 65 °C","D":"Increase extension time to 2 min"}
Correct: B
Explanation: Analogy: Imagine trying to grow a plant; adding more fertilizer (primer) often boosts growth more than adjusting the light (temperature). Official definition: primer concentration directly influences the number of template molecules that can be primed each cycle, thus increasing yield. A raises ionic strength but can also increase non‑specific binding. C would further reduce primer annealing. D may help with long fragments but has minimal effect on yield for a 500‑bp product.

## 787_Q4 - Why does product yield decrease at 65 °C?
Options: {"A":"Primer‑dimer formation increases","B":"Non‑specific binding is enhanced","C":"Primer annealing efficiency decreases","D":"Taq polymerase denatures at 65 °C"}
Correct: C
Explanation: Analogy: Think of a key that is too hot to fit into a lock; it won’t turn. Official definition: at too high an annealing temperature, primers fail to bind to the template, limiting extension. A and B are more likely at low temperatures. D is incorrect because Taq remains stable up to ~95 °C; denaturation occurs at the extension temperature, not the annealing step.
Trap: Confusing high temperature with enzyme denaturation instead of primer binding
