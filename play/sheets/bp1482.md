# BP1482

Researchers studied the impact of varying sleep durations on reaction time among healthy adults. A randomized controlled trial was conducted with 200 participants aged 20–35. Participants were assigned to one of four groups: Control (no sleep restriction), 4‑hour sleep, 6‑hour sleep, and 8‑hour sleep. After a 24‑hour period of assigned sleep duration, each participant completed a computerized simple reaction time task. The mean reaction times (RT) and standard deviations (SD) were recorded as follows:

Table 1: Reaction Time by Sleep Duration

| Group          | Mean RT (ms) | SD (ms) |
|----------------|--------------|---------|
| Control        | 250          | 15      |
| 4‑hour sleep   | 310          | 20      |
| 6‑hour sleep   | 280          | 18      |
| 8‑hour sleep   | 260          | 16      |

The researchers hypothesized that reduced sleep would impair reaction time. The results indicated a clear trend: as sleep duration decreased, mean reaction time increased, suggesting a dose‑response relationship between sleep loss and cognitive performance.

## bp1482_q1 Which group exhibited the fastest average reaction time?
Correct: A
The Control group had the lowest mean reaction time (250 ms), indicating the fastest average response among the groups.

## bp1482_q2 Which variable is the independent variable in this experiment?
Correct: C
The independent variable is the one that the researchers manipulate, which is sleep duration in this study.

## bp1482_q3 What is the percent increase in mean reaction time from the Control group to the 4‑hour sleep group?
Correct: B
Percent increase = [(310 – 250)/250] × 100 = 60/250 × 100 = 24 %. Option B is correct.

## bp1482_q4 Which group had the highest standard deviation of reaction time?
Correct: B
The 4‑hour sleep group had an SD of 20 ms, which is greater than the SDs of the other groups (15, 18, and 16 ms).

## bp1482_q5 How could these findings inform workplace policies regarding shift work?
Correct: A
The data show that reduced sleep duration leads to slower reaction times. Policies that promote adequate sleep before night shifts can help maintain alertness and safety.
